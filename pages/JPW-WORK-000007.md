#### 4. POLICY ANALYSIS

**Background**: \[FACT\] The Spanish pension system is founded on a pay-as-you-go structure (*sistema de reparto*) and the constitutional principle of intergenerational solidarity, which binds current active workers to current pensioners. Since the 2010s, concern has grown over the baby-boom generation reaching retirement age (beginning around 2027), declining birth rates, and rising life expectancy. This was compounded by austerity policies during 2008–2014 and the introduction of the *Sustainability Factor* (Law 23/2013), which fueled younger generations' doubts about the future viability of their pensions.

**Policy Problem**: \[FACT\]

1.  *Risk of Intergenerational Rupture*: A conflict-inducing narrative has emerged suggesting that older generations' pensions are funded by imposing excessive sacrifice on younger workers (a "win-lose narrative").
2.  *Financial and Demographic Deficit*: Rising pension expenditure burden due to a surge in high-value pensions among the highly educated baby-boom generation.
3.  *Weaknesses of the Sustainability Factor*: The automatic pension-reduction mechanism based on life expectancy cut spending without regard for social adequacy (*sostenibilidad social*) or pensioners' standard of living.

**Legal/Policy Gap**: \[FACT\] The existence of contribution ceilings (*topes máximos de cotización*) limiting contributions from high salaries, without a solidarity contribution mechanism for income above the maximum threshold. Additionally, high-earning self-employed workers (*autónomos*) are exempted from the solidarity contribution scheme.

**Objectives**: \[FACT\] Restore younger generations' trust in the public pension system; strengthen social security system revenue to address the surge in baby-boom pension costs through 2050; and ensure a balance between contributivity (*contributividad*) and pension adequacy (*suficiencia económica*) in accordance with Articles 41 and 50 of the Spanish Constitution.

**Implementation**: \[FACT\] Through Royal Decree-Law 2/2023 and Component 30 of the PRTR:

- *Intergenerational Equity Mechanism (MEI)*: A finalized contribution rate of 0.80% in 2025 (0.67% employer, 0.13% employee), allocated entirely to the Reserve Fund.
- *Additional Solidarity Contribution (CAS)*: A graduated rate (5.5%, 6%, and 7% by 2045) applied to the portion of salary exceeding the maximum contribution ceiling.
- *Increase in Maximum Contribution Ceiling*: Raising the annual monthly maximum contribution ceiling based on CPI plus 1.2 percentage points per year from 2024 through 2050.

**Evidence & Impact**: \[FACT\] Projected to increase Social Security revenue by an additional 0.6% of GDP by 2030 and 0.9% of GDP by 2050. Bank of Spain simulations indicate a small potential impact on employment (a decrease of approximately 0.25% in filled positions if the rate were raised by a full percentage point over four years), offset by stronger social investment and pensioner consumption.

**Reform & Unresolved Issues**: \[FACT\] The absence of an explicit provision directly allocating Additional Solidarity Contribution proceeds to the Reserve Fund (though doctrinally recommended); the exemption of self-employed workers/company directors from CAS; and the need for periodic evaluation of the contribution burden's impact on employers.

#### 6. MEDIAWIKI OUTPUT

## Summary

**The Principle of Intergenerational Solidarity in Social Security and Its Renewal as a Core Element of Our Pay-As-You-Go System** is a Spanish social security law study written by Prof. Dr. Francisca Moreno Romero of Universidad Complutense de Madrid (UCM). This scholarly work analyzes the constitutional principle of intergenerational solidarity as the core pillar of the pay-as-you-go system (\*sistema de reparto\*). The study evaluates Spanish pension law reforms from 2019–2023, particularly the replacement of the automatic pension-reduction mechanism (\*Sustainability Factor\*) with the Intergenerational Equity Mechanism (MEI), the introduction of the Additional Solidarity Contribution (CAS), and the gradual increase of the maximum contribution base (\*base máxima de cotización\*) through 2050.

## Legal Basis

Spain's public Social Security system and the reforms analyzed are grounded in the following constitutional and statutory provisions:

- **Spanish Constitution (CE)**: Article 41 (public social security), Article 50 (adequacy of pensions for the elderly), Article 31 (fair allocation of public resources), and Articles 1.1, 9.2, 9.3, and 14.
- **Law 27/2011, of 1 August**: Reform and modernization of the social security system, raising the retirement age to 67.
- **Law 23/2013, of 23 December**: Establishment of the Sustainability Factor and the Revaluation Index.
- **Royal Legislative Decree 8/2015 (TRLGSS)**: Consolidated text of the General Social Security Law (particularly Articles 2, 19, 19 bis, 127.2 bis, Transitional Provision 38, and Transitional Provision 42).
- **Royal Decree-Law 2/2023, of 16 March**: Pension reform integrating Component 30 of the Recovery, Transformation and Resilience Plan.
- **Royal Decree 322/2024, of 26 March**: Implementing provisions for the additional solidarity contribution.

## Background

Spain's pension system relies on contributions from active workers to fund current pensioners' benefits. Demographic challenges have emerged alongside rising life expectancy, low birth rates, and the onset of baby-boom-generation retirement beginning in 2027. Austerity policies during the 2008–2014 crisis and the implementation of the automatic pension-reduction mechanism (Sustainability Factor) raised widespread concern among younger workers that they would not receive adequate pensions in the future.

## Objectives

1\. Restore and strengthen younger workers' trust in the sustainability of the public pension system. 2. Increase Social Security system revenue to anticipate the peak in baby-boom-generation pension expenditure through 2050. 3. Maintain a balance between the principles of contributivity (\*contributividad\*) and pension adequacy (\*suficiencia económica\*) as mandated by Articles 41 and 50 of the Spanish Constitution.

## Scope

The study covers analysis of three main contribution instruments: 1. The *Intergenerational Equity Mechanism* (MEI) as a replacement for the Sustainability Factor. 2. The *Additional Solidarity Contribution* (CAS) for income exceeding the maximum contribution ceiling. 3. The gradual increase of the maximum contribution ceiling (\*base máxima de cotización\*), adjusted by CPI + 1.2% per year.

## Key Provisions

- **Intergenerational Equity Mechanism (MEI)**: A finalized contribution rate of 0.80% in 2025 (0.67% employer, 0.13% employee), allocated directly to the Reserve Fund without possibility of reduction or deduction.
- **Additional Solidarity Contribution (CAS)**: A three-tier progressive contribution (5.5%, 6%, and 7%) on income above the maximum contribution ceiling, phased in gradually through 2045.
- **Increase in Maximum Contribution Ceiling**: Approved annually through the National Budget (PGE) from 2024 through 2050, using the pension revaluation formula plus 1.2 percentage points.

## Implementation

Reform implementation proceeds continuously through coordination between the Spanish Government, the Toledo Pact Commission, and social partners. MEI contribution proceeds are allocated to strengthen the Social Security Reserve Fund to ensure long-term liquidity.

## Policy Problems

- A narrative of intergenerational conflict positioning pensioners as winners and younger workers as the disadvantaged party (a "win-lose narrative").
- High-earning self-employed workers (\*autónomos\*) are not reached by the Additional Solidarity Contribution scheme.
- Potential impact on employer labor costs, though economic simulations indicate minimal impact on employment levels.

## Reform

- Repeal of the Sustainability Factor (Law 23/2013), judged to be purely a cost-cutting instrument that disregarded social adequacy.
- Implementation of a new progressive contribution scheme through RDL 2/2023 and RD 322/2024.

## Relationships with Other Entities

- **Replaces**: The Sustainability Factor (Law 23/2013).
- **Implements**: Component 30 of the Recovery, Transformation and Resilience Plan & Recommendation 11 of the Toledo Pact.
- **Interprets**: Articles 41 and 50 of the Spanish Constitution of 1978.
