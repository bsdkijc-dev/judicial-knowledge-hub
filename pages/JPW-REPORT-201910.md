## 4. Policy Analysis

Background  
Korea's Civil Act adheres to a strict binary model of legal personality: only natural persons (*자연인*) and incorporated entities (*법인*) possess capacity of enjoyment of rights (*권리능력*, Civil Act Art. 3). However, millions of socially active organizations — such as clan associations (*종중*), religious bodies (*교회/사찰*), village/neighborhood associations (*동/리/마을회/노인회*), and alumni groups — operate as unincorporated associations (*법인 아닌 사단*) without registering as corporations. To accommodate practical economic necessity, procedural statutes explicitly accord them party capacity in litigation (Civil Procedure Act Art. 52) and real estate registration capacity (Registration of Real Estate Act Art. 26).

<!-- -->

Policy Problem  
Unincorporated associations hold immense national wealth: official 2018 government data shows 383,720 registered associations owning 8,392 km² of land (representing 8.3% of Korea's total landmass), with clan associations owning 78.1% (6,096 km²) and religious bodies owning 12.8% (999 km²). Despite this massive economic footprint, there is **no public register** (*공시제도*) verifying their actual existence, corporate substance, or legal representatives. Consequently:

1.  *Litigation Inefficiency*: Courts frequently conduct full trials only for appellate courts to overturn rulings due to lack of party capacity or invalid representative authority, causing severe waste of judicial resources.
2.  *Transaction Insecurity & Invalidation*: Real estate transactions or mortgages executed under an association's name are routinely invalidated retroactively when lower-tier representative appointments or general meeting resolutions (*총유물 처분결의*) are later proven fraudulent or defective.
3.  *Non-Contentious Hazards*: Non-contentious procedures like court deposits (*공탁*) or compulsory execution (*강제집행*) risk releasing funds to fraudulent self-proclaimed representatives without reliable verification tools.

<!-- -->

Legal/Policy Gap  
Korea's Civil Act enforces an administrative permission system (*허가주의*, Art. 32) for non-profit incorporation, giving administrative agencies broad discretion. Associations actively avoid incorporation to evade state supervision, asset dissolution restrictions (Art. 80), and mandatory filing burdens. Furthermore, Civil Act Art. 275 mandates that association property be owned collectively (*총유*, *chong-yu*), requiring general meeting resolutions for management and disposition (Art. 276). This creates an inherent structural conflict between procedural statutes treating them as quasi-corporations and property law treating their assets as collective member ownership.

<!-- -->

Evaluation of Alternative Reform Models  

:# *Civil Act Incorporation Reform (준칙주의 / Normative Principle)*: Shifting non-profit incorporation from permission (*허가주의*) to normative registration (*준칙주의* / *인가주의*), as proposed in the 2004 and 2013 Civil Act Revision drafts. **Evaluated as ineffective** because as long as procedural laws grant real estate ownership and litigation standing without incorporation, associations have no financial or operational incentive to undergo corporate oversight.

:# *Single Special Law / Intermediate Corporation Model (중간법인법)*: Enacting a standalone special law modeled after Japan's 2001 Intermediate Corporation Act. **Evaluated as a failure** based on empirical evidence from Japan, where only 2,619 intermediate corporations were formed over four years due to compliance burdens and corporate tax exposure.

:# *Special Laws for Specific Groups (종중관리법 / 종교법인법)*: Enacting specific regulation for clan or religious bodies. **Evaluated as politically complex** due to deep-seated social resistance and constitutional issues surrounding religious freedom.

Preferred Solution — Public Disclosure via Real Estate Registration Act Amendment  
The report formulates a highly practical, immediate reform centered on the existing real estate registration identification number system:

1.  *Statutory Enactment*: Insert **Article 26-2** into the Registration of Real Estate Act (*부동산등기법 제26조의2*).
2.  *Court-Administered Register*: Establish an official **Unincorporated Association Register** (*법인 아닌 사단 등록부*) managed directly by court registration officers (*등기관*) rather than local municipal heads (*시장/군수/구청장*).
3.  *Mandatory Notary Attendance*: Require notary public attendance and certification (*공증인 참석인증*) for general membership meeting resolutions and articles of association to guarantee procedural truthfulness and representative authenticity.
4.  *Public Register Certificate*: Issue official **Unincorporated Association Registration Certificates** (*법인 아닌 사단 등록사항증명서*) disclosing: (a) Association Name, (b) Principal Office Address, (c) Registration Identification Number, (d) Representative Name and Address, and (e) Whether property disposition requires a general assembly resolution under Civil Act Art. 276(1).
5.  *Protection of Bona Fide Third Parties*: Establish statutory protection ensuring that registered representative information cannot be contested against bona fide third parties (*선의의 제3자 보호*).

<!-- -->

Evidence  
Official 10-year court registration statistics (2009–2018) reveal that unincorporated associations engage in **18,719 to 29,585 real estate ownership registrations per year** (accumulating 223,915 registrations over the decade). Implementation of the proposed court register would immediately capture nearly 20,000 active associations annually, creating an exhaustive public disclosure network covering virtually all property-holding unincorporated associations in South Korea.

**A Study on the Methods of Public Disclosure of Unincorporated Associations** is an official judicial policy research report published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of South Korea's Supreme Court.

## Summary

This document is an official judicial policy research report published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of South Korea's Supreme Court. It analyzes the legal status, comparative law (Germany, France, Japan), and practical problems concerning unincorporated associations (법인 아닌 사단), and formulates a concrete legal reform: the establishment of a court-administered "Unincorporated Association Register" (법인 아닌 사단 등록부).

## Legal Basis

The legal frameworks analyzed include:

- **Civil Act of the Republic of Korea (민법)**: Article 3 (Capacity of Enjoyment of Rights), Article 32 (Permission Principle for Non-Profit Incorporation), Article 275 (Collective Ownership / 총유), and Articles 276–277 (Management and Disposition of Collective Property).
- **Civil Procedure Act of the Republic of Korea (민사소송법)**: Articles 51 and 52 (Capacity to be a Party in Court).
- **Registration of Real Estate Act (부동산등기법)**: Article 26 (Registration Capacity), Article 48 (Registration Details), and Article 49 (Registration ID Number Procedures).
- **Supreme Court Registration Directive No. 1621**: Directive on Business Processing regarding Registration Applications by Unincorporated Associations.

## Background

In South Korea, hundreds of thousands of organizations — such as clan associations (종중), religious institutions (교회/사찰), and village associations (동/리/마을회) — do not register as incorporated bodies (법인), yet hold extensive rights over land (accounting for 8.3% of South Korea's total land area as of 2018). The absence of a public registration system (공시제도) has led to a high risk of representative fraud, invalidated land registrations, and overturned court judgments due to lack of legal party capacity.

## Objectives

1.  Establish an effective and efficient public registration system to verify the existence of associations and the authenticity of their legal representatives.
2.  Improve legal certainty in property transactions and reduce social costs and case burdens on the courts.

## Scope

- **Three-Country Comparative Study**:
  - **Germany**: Section 54 BGB (application of partnership rules) and Section 47 GBO (rejection of independent land registration capacity).
  - **France**: The 1901 Act on Non-Declared Associations (*associations non déclarées*).
  - **Japan**: The 2001 Intermediate Corporation Act (중간법인법) and the 2008 Non-Profit Corporation Reform (일반법인법).

## Key Provisions

- **Enactment of Article 26-2 of the Real Estate Registration Act**: Requires court registration officers (등기관) to maintain an "Unincorporated Association Register."
- **Notary Authentication (참석인증)**: Requires notary attendance and certification of general membership meetings and articles of association to guarantee representative authenticity.
- **Registration Certificate (법인 아닌 사단 등록사항증명서)**: Contains the association's name, office address, registration number, representative's name/address, and a clause on general assembly consent for disposal of collectively owned property (Civil Act Article 276(1)).
- **Protection of Bona Fide Third Parties (선의의 제3자 보호)**: Guarantees that registered representative information cannot be challenged by conflicting internal claims.

## Implementation

Transfers registration authority from local government heads (시장/군수/구청장) to district courts/court registry offices (등기소) so that verification is conducted rigorously by legal professionals and integrated with the land registration system.

## Policy Issues

- The failure of Japan's model (중간법인법) shows that relaxing incorporation requirements (준칙주의) alone is insufficient if associations lack tax or operational incentives to submit to state oversight.
- Uncertainty in representative verification during non-contentious procedures such as court deposits (공탁) and compulsory execution (강제집행).

## Reform / Amendments

- Establishment of a court registration system for unincorporated associations.
- Mandatory notary authentication for general meetings and articles of association.
- Issuance of official registration certificates for legal and court transactions.
