# JPW-POLICY-000028

The **Lawyer's System in China Framework** (Stable ID: `JPW-POLICY-000028`) is a national statutory, regulatory, and professional policy framework governing the legal definition, business scope, law firm structure, dual management, and professional ethics of lawyers in the People's Republic of China. Codified under the *Law of the People's Republic of China on Lawyers* (adopted May 15, 1996, effective January 1, 1997) and guided by the Ministry of Justice (MOJ) and the All-China Lawyers Association (ACLA), the framework redefined Chinese lawyers from "state legal workers" under the 1980 Interim Regulations into independent legal practitioners providing services to the public. By 1997, the framework regulated 8,441 law firms (including 2,957 non-state-run firms) and 98,902 lawyers across China. Note: Named entities *Cecep Mustafa* and *Reihan Abdan Syakur* are *Not found in the available sources*.

## Summary

The framework traces its historical lineage from late Qing drafts (1902–1906) and Republican codes to the post-1949 PRC system. Abolished during the 1957 Anti-Rightist Campaign as "bourgeois", the system was restored in 1979–1980 and reformed under the State Council's 1993 Plan. It establishes six core structural pillars: (1) Statutory Practitioner Definition, defining lawyers as licensed practitioners providing public legal services; (2) Expanded Business Scope, authorizing representation in civil, criminal, administrative, IP, trade, banking, and real estate matters; (3) Dual Management Architecture, combining MOJ administrative supervision with ACLA self-discipline; (4) Four-Tier ACLA Structure, establishing the National Congress (every 4 years), Council, Executive Council, and Special Committees; (5) Mandatory Association Membership, linking local membership directly to national ACLA membership; and (6) Code of Ethics & Discipline, enforcing strict practice rules against kickbacks, fraud, and improper judicial contacts.

## Legal Basis

Authority and execution derive from primary national statutes, administrative reform plans, and professional codes:

- **Primary National Enactments:** Law of the People's Republic of China on Lawyers (adopted May 15, 1996, effective Jan 1, 1997); Interim Regulations of the PRC on Lawyers (adopted Aug 26, 1980).
- **Administrative Reform Plans:** Plan for Further Reform of the Lawyer's System (approved by State Council, Dec 1993).
- **Professional Ethics Codes:** Code of Professional Ethics and Practice Discipline of Lawyers (adopted by ACLA, Oct 6, 1996); Ten Requirements and Ten Prohibitions for Lawyers (MOJ, 1990).

## Background & Policy Problems

### Background

Following legal reform efforts from 1902 through the 1940s, the PRC abolished old codes in 1949 and established public defender/adviser offices. The 1957 Anti-Rightist Campaign dismantled the profession. Restored in 1979 alongside the Criminal Procedure Law, the 1980 Interim Regulations designated lawyers "state legal workers". Rapid economic growth prompted the 1993 Reform Plan and the 1996 Law on Lawyers.

### Policy Problems

- Ideological Constraints on Legal Status: The 1980 Interim Regulations designated lawyers as "state legal workers," restricting independent practice and creating public suspicion.
- Market Transformation & Service Demands: Rapid economic opening created urgent needs for commercial legal expertise in foreign trade, banking, real estate, and intellectual property.
- Lack of Real Professional Autonomy: Lawyers Associations operated as administrative assistants to government judicial administration departments rather than fully autonomous self-disciplinary bodies.
- Widespread Ethics & Practice Discipline Breaches: Solicitations via kickbacks, middleman fees, squandering client funds, and illicit contacts with judges.

## Objectives

1\. Protect the lawful practice rights of lawyers against unlawful interference. 2. Transition the profession to independent practitioners serving the public. 3. Provide legal services across commercial, administrative, and criminal investigation fields. 4. Strengthen self-discipline through Lawyers Associations under State Council guidance.

## Scope & Implementation

### Scope

Applies across all law firms, full-time, part-time, and apprentice lawyers, Lawyers Associations, and judicial administration departments in the PRC.

### Key Provisions

- **Practitioner Definition (Art 2):** Independent licensed practitioner providing legal services to the public.
- **Mandatory Dual Membership:** Joining local associations automatically confers ACLA membership.
- **Prohibition of Private Fee Collection:** Strict ban on accepting private authorization, charging unrecorded fees, or paying middleman kickbacks.

### Implementation

- **Growth Statistics (1986–1997):** Law firms grew from 3,198 to 8,441; total lawyers grew from 21,546 to 98,902.
- **Non-State Law Firms:** Expanded to 2,957 non-state law firms (35.03% of total) by 1997.
- **ACLA Governance:** Operating National Congress, Council, Executive Council, and 5 Special Committees.

## Policy Problems

- Lack of Full Association Autonomy: Lawyers Associations acting as assistants to MOJ departments.
- Unfair Competition & Kickbacks: Soliciting clients via middleman fees and improper judicial influences.
- Practice Interference: Local obstruction or retaliation against defense lawyers.

## Reform

- Shifting management toward association self-regulation under MOJ guidance.
- Enforcing national qualification exams (182,000 candidates in 1999).
- Strengthening professional ethics enforcement.

## Related Key Figures & Institutions

### Key Figures

- **Shen Jiaben & Wu Tingfang:** Imperial Qing ministers who led early legal reform drafting (1902–1906).
- **Dr. Sun Yat-sen:** Head of the Nanking Interim Government (1911) that created a Draft Lawyer's Law.
- **Li Yunchang:** Legal author who clarified the political context of the 1980 "state legal worker" designation.
