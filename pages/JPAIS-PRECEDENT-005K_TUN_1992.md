## Overview

Supreme Court Decision No. 5 K/TUN/1992 dated January 21, 1993, establishes three fundamental legal principles in State Administrative Court Procedure: 1. **Lawsuit Time Limit (Article 55 of Law No. 5 of 1986):** The 90-day period is calculated from the time the Plaintiff realistically becomes aware of the issuance of the administrative decision that prejudices their interests. 2. **Status of the Defendant Party (Article 83 of Law No. 5 of 1986):** The joinder or designation of a third party as a "Co-Defendant" (\*Turut Tergugat\*) upon the Plaintiff's sole initiative is procedurally improper and impermissible within the State Administrative Court. 3. **Administrative Judges' Review Powers / Ultra Petita:** State Administrative Judges possess the authority to annul or review decisions or determinations of Administrative Bodies or Officials that contravene existing legal frameworks, even if not specifically pleaded in the Plaintiff's petitory relief (\*ultra petita\*), in order to uphold legal order and rectify administrative aberrations.

## Case Background

The Plaintiffs (Mrs. D binti A, Mrs. YR binti ZAS, Moh. R bin Moh. S) filed a lawsuit at the Jakarta State Administrative Court (PTUN) against the Head of the National Land Agency (BPN) as Defendant I and the Governor of DKI Jakarta as Defendant II, seeking the annulment of the Land Allocation Permit (SIPPT) and Building Use Rights (HGB) Certificates No. 116/KS and No. 138/KS covering a disputed 7,100 m² plot of land on Jalan HAS / KS, Central Jakarta (formerly Ownership Certificate No. 60/G belonging to the late ZAS). The Jakarta PTUN and Jakarta High State Administrative Court rulings involved the joinder of third parties as Co-Defendants and Intervening Defendants (PT JS and PT SGM). The parties subsequently filed cassation petitions to the Supreme Court.

## Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

- **Ratio Decidendi:**

1.  The time limit for filing a lawsuit under Article 55 of Law No. 5 of 1986 must be calculated from the moment the Plaintiff becomes aware of the prejudicial decision.
2.  Under Article 55 and Article 83 of Law No. 5 of 1986 alongside its official elucidation, it is impermissible for a Plaintiff on their own initiative to implead a private individual or legal entity as a Defendant (vide Article 1 point 6 of Law No. 5 of 1986) either as a Co-Defendant or an Intervening Defendant.
3.  Even if the original Plaintiffs did not explicitly request it in their petitum, the Supreme Court / Administrative Judge may consider and adjudicate upon any decisions or determinations that conflict with prevailing legal order. Restricting the judge's review power solely to the object of dispute raised by the parties is improper, as disputed objects frequently must be evaluated in relation to parts of administrative decisions that were not formally contested (\*ultra petita\*).

- **Ruling:** Granting the cassation petitions from Petitioner for Cassation I (Head of BPN), Petitioner for Cassation II (Governor of DKI Jakarta), Petitioner for Cassation III (PT JS and PT SGM), and Petitioner for Cassation IV (Mrs. D binti A et al.); Annulling the Jakarta High State Administrative Court Decision No. 13/B/1991/PT.TUN.JKT jo. Jakarta State Administrative Court Decision No. 010/G/1991/PTUN-Jkt; and Independently Adjudicating: Granting the Plaintiff's lawsuit in part, declaring invalid/annulling the sale-and-purchase deed, SIPPT, HGB Certificates No. 116/KS and No. 138/KS, and ordering the original Defendants to pay court costs.
