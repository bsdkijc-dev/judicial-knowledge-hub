## Policy Analysis

- **Background & Policy Problem**:
  - **FACT**: The 2022 Annual Report documents Japan's immigration management through FY2021. Due to COVID-19 border controls, foreign entries dropped by 91.8% in 2021 to 353,119 (including 151,726 new arrivals).
  - **FACT**: Total foreign resident stock at the end of 2021 stood at 2,760,635 (2.20% of total population). China was the primary origin country (716,606 / 26.0%), followed by Vietnam (432,934 / 15.7%) and South Korea (409.855 / 14.8%). Permanent Residents formed the largest status category (831,157), while Specified Skilled Workers (i) grew to 49,666.
- **Technical Intern Training Program (TITP) & Administrative Action**:
  - **FACT**: OTIT supervised 3,503 licensed supervising organizations in FY2021, approving 171,387 training plans. Due to legal violations, administrative dispositions revoked accreditation for 177 implementing organizations and licenses for 13 supervising organizations. Trainee absconding cases rose to 7,167 (4,772 from Vietnam). An April 2020 regulatory amendment mandated direct bank transfers for trainee wage payments to prevent exploitation.
- **Overstay Enforcement, Deportation, & Protection**:
  - **FACT**: Overstayers were estimated at 66,759 as of Jan 1, 2022 (down 19.4%), primarily ex-Temporary Visitors (64.8%). Deportation/departure procedures applied to 18,012 foreign nationals. Illegal workers totaled 13,255 (73.6% of immigration violators), primarily concentrated in construction (24.9%), agriculture (23.8%), and manufacturing (19.1%).
  - **FACT**: Special Permission to Stay (Kaitoku) was granted to 8,793 individuals. Out of 6,150 processed refugee applications (top origins: Myanmar, Turkey, Cambodia), 65 received refugee status and 580 were granted residence on humanitarian grounds. The ISA protected 11 Trafficking in Persons (TIP) victims and 178 Domestic Violence (DV) victims in 2021.
- **Interpretations & Structural Inferences**:
  - **INTERPRETATION**: The report reflects a dual policy model combining rigorous border enforcement with expanded humanitarian discretion (Kaitoku/humanitarian status) and stricter oversight of technical intern training infrastructure.
  - **INFERENCE**: High absconding rates among trainees and persistent illegal employment in construction and agriculture indicate structural labor market demands that require intensified on-site OTIT and ISA inspections.
