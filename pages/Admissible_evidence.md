# Admissible evidence

In the law of [evidence](Evidence.md), **admissible evidence** is any testimonial, documentary, or tangible evidence that may be introduced to a [trier of fact](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/trier_of_fact) (a judge or a jury) to establish or bolster a point put forth by a party to a legal proceeding.

For evidence to be deemed admissible, it must simultaneously satisfy strict legal criteria regarding relevance, reliability, and authenticity. Conversely, evidence that fails to meet these institutional standards is labeled **inadmissible**, meaning it cannot be presented in court or considered during judicial deliberations.

## Fundamental criteria for admissibility

The admissibility of evidence is governed by codifications such as the [Federal Rules of Evidence](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Federal_Rules_of_Evidence) (FRE) in the United States, or the [Police and Criminal Evidence Act 1984](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Police_and_Criminal_Evidence_Act_1984) (PACE) in the United Kingdom. Three core hurdles must be cleared:

### 1. Relevance

Evidence must be relevant to the case at hand. Under standard legal frameworks, evidence is considered relevant if:

- It has any tendency to make a fact more or less probable than it would be without the evidence.
- The fact is of consequence in determining the action.

### 2. Competence (Reliability)

Competent evidence matches traditional standards of reliability. For example, a witness must possess personal knowledge of the event they are describing. For scientific, technical, or specialized evidence, competence is evaluated using rigorous validation tests.

### 3. Authenticity

A party introducing physical or digital items must prove that the item is exactly what they claim it to be. In [digital forensics](Digital_forensics.md), this requires validating the data's integrity via [cryptographic hash functions](Cryptographic_hash_function.md) and documenting an unbroken [chain of custody](Chain_of_custody.md).

## Major rules of exclusion

Even if evidence is highly relevant, the court may exclude it if it violates specific constitutional, statutory, or common-law protective rules.

### The Hearsay Rule

[Hearsay](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Hearsay) is an out-of-court statement offered in court to prove the truth of the matter asserted. Because the person who made the original statement is not available under oath for [cross-examination](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/cross-examination), hearsay is generally inadmissible unless it falls under a defined legal exception (e.g., dying declarations, excited utterances, or business records).

### Unfair prejudice

Courts retain the authority to exclude relevant evidence if its [probative value](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/probative_value) is substantially outweighed by the danger of "unfair prejudice, confusing the issues, misleading the jury, undue delay, wasting time, or needlessly presenting cumulative evidence."

### The Exclusionary Rule

Derived from constitutional protections (such as the [Fourth Amendment](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Fourth_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution)), the [exclusionary rule](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/exclusionary_rule) mandates that evidence obtained through illegal search, seizure, or coercive interrogation is inadmissible. Under the **[fruit of the poisonous tree](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/fruit_of_the_poisonous_tree)** doctrine, any secondary evidence discovered as a direct result of an illegal search is likewise suppressed.

## Standards for scientific and technical evidence

When advanced forensic data—such as [DNA profiling](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/DNA_profiling), [digital forensics](Digital_forensics.md) images, or system analytics—is introduced, courts apply specialized frameworks to screen out "junk science."

| Standard                                            | Primary Jurisdictional Testing Mechanism | Core Requirement                                                                                                                                             |
|-----------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **[Frye standard](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Frye_standard)**       | General acceptance test                  | The scientific technique must be generally accepted as reliable within its relevant scientific community.                                                    |
| **[Daubert standard](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Daubert_standard)** | Judicial gatekeeping framework           | The judge acts as a gatekeeper evaluating whether the methodology has been tested, peer-reviewed, has a known error rate, and maintains technical standards. |
