# Judicial-policy-past-future-court-management-maan-2008

**The Past And Future For Management of Courts Framework** is a policy framework and operational analysis of court management formulated by Bert Maan (former President of a District Court in the Netherlands and Chair of the Judicial Budgeting Committee). It defines courts as structured organizations (*courts as organizations*), examines the relationship between judicial independence and proactive court leadership, the establishment of output-based budgeting with case weights, internal/external communication with the media, judicial specialization, the role of legal/judicial assistants, and quality management (*internal vs external quality*) \[1, 2, 5, 7, 9, 10, 14, 27, 31, 35, 37–43\].

## Summary

Published by Bert Maan in the *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA), this policy framework records the transformation of judicial management in the Netherlands and Europe. Overcoming the classic tension between judicial independence and organizational control, this study details the successful adoption of an output-based budgeting system with case weights managed by the Council for the Judiciary (*Raad voor de rechtspraak*) since 2002 \[5–8\]. The framework asserts that courts are complex organizations requiring authoritative leadership by Court Presidents, collaboration with professional court managers, transparent media relations, and the establishment of a legal assistants corps to maintain adjudicative quality \[10, 13, 22, 35, 39–43\].

## Legal Basis

- **Article 6 of the European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR):** Guarantees the right to a fair and public hearing as the foundation of judicial transparency.
- **Judiciary Organization Act 2002 (Wet op de rechterlijke organisatie):** The establishment of the *Raad voor de rechtspraak* as the independent body managing the budget and administration of the Dutch judiciary.
- **Dutch Code of Criminal and Civil Procedure:** The procedural foundation of the civil law system and inquisitorial procedures in criminal cases.
- **Council of Europe Committee of Ministers Recommendations:**
  - *Recommendation R(94)12:* Independence, efficiency, and role of judges.
  - *Recommendation R(86)12:* Measures to prevent and reduce the excessive workload in the courts.
  - *Recommendation R(2003):* The provision of information through the media in relation to criminal proceedings.

## Background

In the European civil law tradition, modern court management previously failed to develop due to the assumption that organizational structures could threaten judicial independence. Ministries of Finance often viewed the judiciary as a "bottomless pit" continuously requesting budgets without clear measures of accountability. To break this deadlock, the Netherlands established the *Council for the Judiciary* in 2002 and formulated budget allocations based on actual real-world workloads.

## Objectives

1\. Build a transparent judicial budgeting system based on outputs and case weight measurements. 2. Strengthen the leadership of Court Presidents in directing organizational change without sacrificing judicial independence. 3. Balance internal quality indicators (legal theory) and external quality indicators (public service). 4. Develop media communication strategies and encourage public openness (*open courthouse visits*).

## Scope

This policy applies to first-instance courts (*District Courts*) and appellate courts in the Netherlands, serving as an administrative reform reference for Eastern European countries under the auspices of the *Council of Europe*.

## Key Provisions

- **Output-Based Budgeting & Case Weights System:**
  - Budget allocation by the *Raad voor de rechtspraak* is calculated from the number of outgoing concluded cases multiplied by time weights based on a periodic time measurement survey.
  - *Weight Example:* A 3-judge panel in a District Court is allocated **1,052 minutes/judge/case**, whereas a simple single-judge case is allocated **90 minutes/judge/case**.
- **Court President Leadership Model:**
  - The Court President retains 95% of their judicial adjudicative functions and dedicates 5% to management duties during a 6-year term.
  - Leads through persuasion and authority, rather than authoritarian formal power.
- **Role of Legal Assistants:** The formation of a corps of legal assistants with law degrees to support research and assist judges in specializing in complex legal fields.
- **Peer Evaluation Technique (Intervision):** Observing trials conducted by peers using video recordings to provide constructive feedback on judicial conduct.

## Implementation

- **Weekly Open Courthouse Visits:** Hosting citizens to observe trials and discuss with judges/prosecutors to counter negative media framing \[20–22\].
- **Monthly Meeting of Court Presidents (Vergadering van Presidenten):** A national consensus forum of court presidents to align judicial governance policies.

## Policy Problems

- **Danger of Management Without Legal Specialization:** The risk of tension or contempt if non-judge court managers do not understand the independent nature of judicial work.
- **Challenges of Court Mergers:** The impact of creating larger courts, which forces citizens to travel longer distances to access justice.

## Reform / Change

- Assigning full responsibility for quantitative and qualitative outputs to judicial leadership.
- Institutionalizing the 13 future challenges and principles of court management according to Bert Maan.

## Multilingual Summaries (Swiss National Languages)

### Deutsch (Schweizer Hochdeutsch)

**Vergangenheit und Zukunft der Gerichtsverwaltung** Dieser von Bert Maan im *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA, 2008) veröffentlichte Rahmen dokumentiert die Transformation der Gerichtsverwaltung in den Niederlanden und Europa. Die Studie überwindet das klassische Spannungsfeld zwischen richterlicher Unabhängigkeit und organisatorischer Kontrolle und beschreibt die erfolgreiche Einführung eines leistungsbasierten Budgetierungssystems mit Fallgewichten (Output-Based Budgeting & Case Weights), das seit 2002 vom Rat für die Rechtsprechung verwaltet wird \[5–8\]. Der Rahmen betont, dass Gerichte komplexe Organisationen sind, die eine autoritative Führung durch Gerichtspräsidenten, die Zusammenarbeit mit professionellen Gerichtsmanagern, transparente Medienbeziehungen und den Aufbau eines Korps von juristischen Assistenten zur Sicherung der Rechtsprechungsqualität erfordern \[10, 13, 22, 35, 39–43\].

- **Rechtsgrundlage:** EMRK (Art. 6); Niederländisches Gesetz über die Gerichtsorganisation 2002; Empfehlungen des Europarates R(94)12, R(86)12, R(2003).

### 3 Most Important Improvements

1\. **Institutionalization of Output-Based Budgeting and Case Weights Systems:** Adopt periodic time measurement surveys to assign realistic weights to complex cases (e.g., 1,052 minutes for a 3-judge panel) to ensure the equitable allocation of the judicial budget. 2. **Implementation of Peer Evaluation Techniques (Intervision) and Trial Video Recordings:** Utilize these tools to observe and provide constructive feedback on judges' behavior and leadership during trials, thereby improving external service quality. 3. **Establishment of a Legal Assistants Supporting Staff Corps:** Develop units of highly qualified legal assistants with law degrees to support research and assist judges in specializing in complex areas of law (such as administrative and forensic law).
