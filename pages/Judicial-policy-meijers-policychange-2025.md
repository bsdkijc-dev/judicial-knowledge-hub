# Judicial-policy-meijers-policychange-2025

**Who accepts party policy change? The individual-level drivers of attitudes towards party repositioning** is a comparative empirical research paper authored by Maurits J. Meijers (Radboud University Nijmegen) and Ruth Dassonneville (Université de Montréal / KU Leuven), published in the *European Journal of Political Research* (EJPR) in 2025.

## Summary

This study examines the individual-level determinants of public acceptance or rejection of political party policy repositioning. Analyzing survey data from over 12,000 respondents across five European countries (Germany, the Netherlands, Poland, Spain, and the United Kingdom), the study finds that a majority of citizens support the principle that parties should be able to change their policy positions. However, populist attitudes and right-wing extremism are strongly associated with rejecting policy change, as these groups view repositioning as a form of political elite opportunism.

## Background

Political parties frequently change policy positions in response to public opinion, competing party strategy, or external crises. Prior research has produced contradictory results as to whether voters view policy repositioning as democratic, pragmatic flexibility or as dishonest, opportunistic "flip-flopping" \[2–4, 14, 20\].

## Objectives

1\. Measure the public's principled level of acceptance of party policy change directly, using a 1–7 scale. 2. Analyze the influence of political interest on acceptance of policy change (Hypothesis 1). 3. Test the impact of technocratic attitudes on acceptance of repositioning (Hypothesis 2). 4. Test the influence of ideological extremism on rejection of change (Hypothesis 3). 5. Test the impact of populist attitudes on rejection of party policy change (Hypothesis 4).

## Scope

This comparative study covers five European countries with varying electoral systems and government configurations: Germany, the Netherlands, Poland, Spain, and the United Kingdom.

## Key Provisions

- **Overall Level of Acceptance:** A majority of voters across the 5 countries tend to support party flexibility (median score of 4 in DE, PL, ES; score of 5 in NL, UK).
- **Effect of Populist Attitudes (Strongest Predictor):** Populist attitudes have a strongly statistically significant negative effect on acceptance of policy change across all 5 countries (b = -0.428, p \< 0.001). Populist voters view policy repositioning as evidence of power-seeking elite opportunism \[9, 23–25, 69, 76\].
- **Role of Political Interest:** High political interest increases acceptance of policy change (b = 0.131, p \< 0.001), as informed voters better understand shifting context and political dynamics \[9, 15–16, 45, 47\].
- **Dynamics of Ideological Extremism:** The negative effect of ideological extremism is driven specifically by right-wing extremist groups; left-wing extremism shows no significant negative effect.
- **Open-Text Analysis Findings:** Pro-change respondents use words such as "circumstances" and "new insights," while anti-change respondents use words like "power," "interests," and "benefit."

## Implementation

The methodology was implemented through weighted quota surveys by YouGov and ElectionCompass, OLS regression analysis (pooled and country-specific), and automated text analysis (Quanteda and the DeepL API) applied to open-ended responses. Replication data is publicly deposited on Harvard Dataverse.

## Policy Problems

1\. The challenge political parties face in making governing-coalition compromises without triggering perceptions of elite betrayal among populist voters. 2. The risk of reputational and credibility erosion arising from accusations of opportunism and pure power-seeking.

## Reform / Change

A shift from assessing the aggregate electoral impact of repositioning toward understanding the psychological determinants and individual-level voter attitudes underlying acceptance of policy flexibility \[4, 5, 8, 72–74\].
