# JPW-REPORT-015

**Twenty-Second Report of the Prosecutor of the International Criminal Court to the United Nations Security Council on the Situation in Libya** is the 22nd periodic report submitted by the Office of the Prosecutor (OTP) to the UN Security Council on 23 November 2021, pursuant to UNSC Resolution 1970 (2011), covering investigative progress in Libya, the Prosecutor's voluntary recusal from the Saif Al-Islam Gaddafi case, and the Office's complementarity and international-cooperation strategy.

## Summary

This is the first report issued under Prosecutor Karim A.A. Khan QC, who took office on 16 June 2021. It covers the reporting period 18 May to 17 November 2021, including the Prosecutor's voluntary recusal in the Saif Al-Islam Gaddafi case, investigations into crimes in Tarhuna, crimes against migrants, and cooperation with an international Joint Team.

## Legal Basis

- [UN Security Council Resolution 1970 (2011)](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/UN_Security_Council_Resolution_1970_%282011%29): Referral of the Libya situation.
- [Rome Statute](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Rome_Statute):
  - **Article 42(6)**: Voluntary recusal of the Prosecutor due to a conflict of interest.
  - **Article 93(10)**: Legal assistance and cooperation with States Parties (positive complementarity).

## Background

Since the UNSC referral in 2011, the OTP has investigated international crimes in Libya. This 22nd report was prepared following Karim A.A. Khan QC's appointment as Prosecutor on 16 June 2021.

## Objectives

- Ensure transparency of proceedings and safeguard institutional integrity against conflicts of interest.
- Encourage genuine national investigations by the Government of National Unity (GNU).
- Investigate crimes against migrants and detainees in official and unofficial detention facilities.

## Key Provisions

- **Voluntary Recusal of Karim A.A. Khan QC**: Recusal from the Saif Al-Islam Gaddafi case under Article 42(6), with functions delegated to the Deputy Prosecutor.
- **Verification of Suspects' Deaths**: Ongoing verification of reported deaths of Mahmoud Mustafa Busayf Al-Werfalli and Al-Tuhamy Mohamed Khaled.
- **Crimes in Tarhuna and Tripoli**: Investigation into the discovery of mass graves in Tarhuna, allegedly linked to forces affiliated with the Libyan Arab Armed Forces (LAAF).
- **Crimes Against Migrants**: Joint Team cooperation (Europol, Italy, UK, Netherlands) leading to a Dutch arrest warrant issued in October 2021.

## Implementation

The OTP processed requests for assistance (RFAs), met with victims' groups and NGOs, and facilitated complementarity-based investigative coordination under Article 93(10).

## Evidence

- Credible reports of mass graves in Tarhuna.
- IOM data showing 22,045 migrants and refugees intercepted and returned to Libya as of 14 August 2021.
- The October 2021 report of the UN Independent Fact-Finding Mission on Libya, documenting systematic and widespread abuses at detention facilities.
- A national arrest warrant issued by the Netherlands in October 2021, resulting from Joint Team cooperation.

## Policy Issues

- The practice of granting amnesties for serious crimes, which the OTP found incompatible with international human rights law.
- Limited field/in situ investigative access due to Libya's security situation.
