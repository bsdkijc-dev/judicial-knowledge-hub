# Judicial-policy-irish-courts-service-1998

**The Irish Courts Service Model** (*Management of the Courts: The Irish Courts Service Model*) is a policy and court administration governance framework in Ireland based on an *independent agency model*. Established via the *Courts Service Act, 1998*, it transferred the authority of court management from the executive branch (*Department of Justice, Equality and Law Reform*) to an autonomous body (the *Courts Service*). This body is led by a Board with a judicial majority (9 out of 17 members) and managed by a Chief Executive Officer (CEO) who is accountable to the Parliament (*Oireachtas*) \[FACT\].

## Summary

Published by P.J. Fitzpatrick (the first CEO of the Irish Courts Service) in the *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA), this policy framework analyzes the fundamental transformation of judicial administration in Ireland. Ending the "Ministry of Justice" governance system that had been in place since 1924, the 1996 report of the *Working Group on a Courts Commission*, chaired by Justice Denham, spurred the enactment of the *Courts Service Act, 1998*. This model delegates court operations, building management, ICT, and budget allocation to the *Courts Service*, an autonomous statutory body that combines the leadership of judges, legal professionals, staff, and public representatives.

## Legal Basis

- **Courts Service Act, 1998:** The primary statute establishing the *Courts Service*, regulating its organizational functions (Section 5), reform proposal authority (Section 6F), Board governance (Sections 13-15), CEO accountability, and judicial independence safeguards (Sections 9, 20, 21).
- **Courts of Justice Act 1924 & Courts (Establishment and Constitution) Act 1961:** Early foundational statutes structuring the Irish judicial organization post-independence.
- **Courts (Supplemental Provisions) Act 1961 (Eighth Schedule):** Regulated the administrative authority of the Minister of Justice prior to 1998.

## Background

For over 70 years following independence, the administration of the Irish judiciary was under the full control of the Department of Justice, with no formal input from judges regarding resource allocation. This lack of autonomy prompted the establishment of the *Working Group on a Courts Commission* in October 1995. The Working Group recommended creating an independent agency to shift the judicial philosophy from merely a "court system" to a public-oriented "court service."

## Objectives

1\. Independently manage and administer all levels of courts in Ireland. 2. Provide high-quality support facilities for judges to perform their judicial duties. 3. Provide adequate information and courthouse facilities for the public and court users. 4. Ensure that judicial administration does not interfere with the adjudicative independence of judges.

## Scope

This policy applies nationally, encompassing all levels of courts in Ireland: the *Supreme Court*, *High Court*, *Circuit Court*, and *District Court*, as well as 1,100 judicial staff employees.

## Key Provisions

- **Courts Service Board Composition:** The Board consists of 17 members, with a judicial majority of 9 (Chief Justice, 3 Court Presidents, 1 Expert Judge, and 4 elected Judges), plus 8 non-judicial members (CEO, barrister, solicitor, staff representative, ministry official, consumer representative, trade union representative, and a business expert).
- **CEO Accountability to Parliament:** The CEO acts as the *Accounting Officer* before the *Public Accounts Committee* of the Parliament (*Oireachtas*), but is legally exempted from answering questions related to the exercise of judicial adjudicative functions (*Section 21*).
- **Protection of Judicial Independence (Section 9):** Explicitly states that no authority of the *Courts Service* may be used to interfere with court proceedings or erode judicial independence.

## Implementation

- **Drafting the 3-Year Strategic Plan:** The formulation of a 3-Year Strategic Plan, which must be approved by the Minister and laid before Parliament, detailing budget utilization targets and Irish language bilingualism policies.
- **Management of Beneficial Funds (€1.062 Billion):** The professional management of trust funds for litigants and minors by the Board.
- **ICT & Building Infrastructure Construction:** The construction of the *Criminal Courts Complex* in Dublin and the modernization of trial facilities.

## Policy Problems

- **Limits of Parliamentary Examination Authority:** The risk of breaching judicial independence when the CEO is examined by Parliamentary Committees, addressed via a dispute testing clause to the High Court (*Section 21*).
- **Annual Budget Dependency:** The persistent need for the budget to be dependent on the annual appropriation vote by *Dáil Éireann*.

## Reform / Change

- Granting official statutory advisory rights (*Section 6F*) to the *Courts Service* to propose changes to procedural law and the redistribution of court jurisdiction.
- Establishment of the *Judicial Support Unit* and structuring support units for the *Courts Rules Committees*.

## Relationships with Other Entities

- **Department of Justice, Equality and Law Reform:** The executive partner responsible for the annual budget allocation funding.
- **Oireachtas (Irish Parliament):** The legislative body receiving the annual reports and the 3-year strategic plans.
