## Core Definition

The \*\*Center for Legal and Judicial Policy Strategy\*\* (\*Pusat Strategi Kebijakan Hukum dan Peradilan\* / \*\*Pustrajak\*\*) is the specialized research center and policy think-tank operating within the Policy Strategy, Education, Training, and Research Agency of the Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia (\*Badan Strategi Kebijakan dan Diklat Hukum dan Peradilan MA RI\*), responsible for executing empirical legal studies, regulatory impact assessments (RIA), and formulating strategic policy papers to drive evidence-based court governance.

- **Simple Definition:** The official research and think-tank arm of the Supreme Court of Indonesia that conducts legal studies, analyzes case data, and drafts academic policy papers to guide court regulations and judicial modernization.
- **Legal / Technical Definition:** The institutional echelon-II strategic research body within the Supreme Court tasked with analyzing statutory issues, examining caseflow jurimetrics (via SIPP and the Direktori Putusan), drafting Academic Drafts (\*Naskah Akademik\*), preparing policy briefs, and evaluating the socioeconomic and administrative impacts of proposed Supreme Court Regulations (\*PERMA\*) and Circular Letters (\*SEMA\*).
- **Plain-Language Explanation:** It acts as the "intellectual engine" of the Supreme Court, ensuring that whenever court leaders create new procedural rules, adopt digital court systems, or propose legal reforms, those decisions are backed by thorough empirical research, data analysis, and comparative legal studies.

## Why It Matters

- **Anchors Judicial Modernization in Verified Data:** Replaces subjective or ad-hoc administrative rule-making with quantitative jurimetrics and empirical case data from across the country.
- **Formulates Enforceable Academic Drafts (\*Naskah Akademik\*):** Provides the required theoretical, constitutional, and empirical justification for major Supreme Court Regulations and national statutory proposals.
- **Enhances Regulatory Quality through RIA:** Conducts Regulatory Impact Assessments to identify unintended procedural bottlenecks or financial costs before court regulations are formally promulgated.
- **Fosters Comparative Judicial Expertise:** Conducts deep comparative studies (e.g., analyzing civil dispute mediation, case management systems, and technology integration in global jurisdictions) to adapt international best practices to Indonesia’s legal framework.

## Key Principles

Principle of Evidence-Driven Regulatory Formulation (Asas Perumusan Regulasi Berbasis Bukti)  

- **Meaning:** Every strategic policy proposal, procedural draft, and administrative recommendation must be grounded in verified empirical evidence, statistical case analysis, and field research.
- **Why It Matters:** Ensures court regulations effectively solve operational problems rather than adding administrative complexity.
- **Legal Basis / Source:** Law No. 11/2019 (Sisnas Iptek); Pustrajak Strategic Guidelines.
- **Related Concepts:** Regulatory Impact Analysis (RIA), jurimetrics, evidence-based policy.

Principle of Normative-Empirical Synthesis (Asas Sintesis Normatif-Empiris)  

- **Meaning:** Research conducted by Pustrajak must harmonize empirical sociological findings with formal statutory hierarchies (\*Stufenbau der Rechtsordnung\*) and constitutional principles.
- **Why It Matters:** Prevents policy papers from recommending technocratic solutions that conflict with due process or statutory law.
- **Legal Basis / Source:** Law No. 12/2011 jo. Law No. 13/2022; Master Legislative Framework Standard.
- **Related Concepts:** Normative legal research, statutory construction, IRAC method.

Principle of Epistemic Integrity and Research Autonomy (Asas Integritas Epistemik dan Otonomi Riset)  

- **Meaning:** Policy research, statistical findings, and academic analyses must adhere to strict scientific rigor and truth-seeking standards, free from political, administrative, or partisan interference.
- **Why It Matters:** Protects the intellectual independence of the judiciary and the credibility of judicial policy recommendations.
- **Legal Basis / Source:** Bangalore Principles Value 6; Master Philosophical & Methodological Standard.
- **Related Concepts:** Epistemic labeling, scholarly integrity, peer appraisal.

## Indonesia Section

- **Organizational Placement & Structure:** Pustrajak operates under the Supreme Court's Policy Strategy, Education, Training, and Research Agency (\*Badan Strategi Kebijakan dan Diklat Hukum dan Peradilan\*). It is staffed by judicial judges (\*Hakim Yustisial\*), senior policy analysts (\*Analis Kebijakan\*), legal researchers, and data engineers.
- **Core Functional Operational Pipeline:**

1\. \*Empirical Problem Identification:\* Monitoring case delays, procedural bottlenecks, and clearance rates across the four judicial branches using SIPP data. 2. \*Scholarly Submissions & Deep Research:\* Conducting qualitative field studies, comparative international surveys, and stakeholder focus group discussions (FGD). 3. \*Policy Formulation & RIA:\* Drafting structured Policy Papers and Regulatory Impact Assessments compliant with National Institute of Public Administration (\*LAN\*) standards. 4. \*Legislative & Regulatory Recommendation:\* Delivering finalized \*Naskah Akademik\* to the Chief Justice, Supreme Court Leadership, and Chamber Plenary (\*Pleno Kamar\*) to guide the enactment of PERMA, SEMA, or legislative inputs to Parliament.

- **Nomenclature:** Pusat Strategi Kebijakan Hukum dan Peradilan (Pustrajak) — \*Center for Legal and Judicial Policy Strategy\*; Badan Strategi Kebijakan — \*Policy Strategy Agency\*.

## Important Decisions & Policy Initiatives

- Policy Initiative: Standardized Policy Paper and Academic Drafting Framework

` * *Issuing Body:* Mahkamah Agung RI (Pustrajak)`  
` * *Instrument Reference:* Pustrajak Research Guidelines & Policy Paper Standards (Pustrajak 2026 / LAN 2026)`  
` * *Legal Principle:* Mandatory requirement that all institutional reforms, procedural amendments, and court regulatory drafts must be supported by an empirical Policy Paper and Regulatory Impact Assessment.`

## Debates & Controversies

- Debate: Pure Academic Legal Research vs. Operational Court Urgency

` * *Position A (Rapid Operational Focus):* Pustrajak should concentrate exclusively on short-term practical solutions, quick policy briefs, and immediate procedural drafting to solve current court backlogs.`  
` * *Position B (Long-Term Academic Rigor):* Pustrajak must produce comprehensive, theoretical, and empirical monographs with multi-year scope to systematically reform the underlying legal architecture.`  
` * *Evidence:* Balance between fast-track policy briefs and long-term academic drafts produced for Supreme Court leadership.`  
` * *Current Status:* Resolved via a dual-track strategy: short-term Policy Briefs provide immediate guidance for urgent SEMA/PERMA matters, while comprehensive *Naskah Akademik* address long-term statutory and procedural reforms.`

## Rule-of-Law Connection

- \*Legal Predictability:\* Ensuring court rules resolve existing ambiguities without introducing conflicting administrative layers.
- \*Transparency & Public Consultation:\* Engaging legal practitioners, civil society, and academic experts in the formulation of \*Naskah Akademik\*.
- \*Evidence-Based Justice:\* Ensuring court modernization directly reduces litigation costs, trial durations, and geographic barriers for citizens.

## AI & Future of Law

- \*Jurimetric AI Modeling:\* Deploying machine learning models to analyze millions of decisions in the Direktori Putusan to detect regional disparities in statutory interpretation.
- \*RAG Integration in Policy Research:\* Connecting Pustrajak’s research repository directly with the Judicial Knowledge Hub, allowing policy analysts to perform fast, citation-safe comparative syntheses.
- \*Automated RIA Simulations:\* Testing the projected caseload impacts of proposed small claims threshold expansions or e-Litigation procedural changes prior to nationwide enactment.

## Common Misconceptions

- \*Myth:\* Pustrajak determines how judges must rule on individual trials (\*teknis yudisial\*).

` * *Reality:* Pustrajak conducts research on *administrative rules, procedural frameworks, and institutional policies*; individual judicial decisions are strictly protected by judicial independence.`  
` * *Source:* Law No. 48/2009; Supreme Court Strategic Architecture.`

- \*Myth:\* Pustrajak only handles internal administrative matters and does not participate in national lawmaking.

` * *Reality:* Pustrajak regularly prepares *Naskah Akademik* and substantive feedback on national draft laws (such as Private International Law, Criminal Procedure, and judicial status bills) on behalf of the judiciary.`  
` * *Source:* Pustrajak Annual Policy Agendas.`

## Practical Example

- \*Scenario:\* The Supreme Court plans to modernize civil dispute resolution by enhancing court-annexed mediation timelines and expanding electronic mediation hearings.
- \*Question:\* How does Pustrajak execute the policy research to support this reform?
- \*Applicable Principle:\* Evidence-Driven Regulatory Formulation & RIA (Pustrajak Guidelines).
- \*Analysis:\*

` 1. Pustrajak analysts extract mediation success rates and average dispute durations from SIPP databases across pilot district courts.`  
` 2. Researchers review comparative mediation frameworks (such as Japanese *Chotei* mediation or Singapore SIMC practices).`  
` 3. Pustrajak conducts Regulatory Impact Assessments to calculate cost savings for small business litigants.`  
` 4. The center drafts an official Policy Paper and Academic Draft (*Naskah Akademik*) proposing specific amendments to PERMA No. 1 of 2016, presenting it to the Civil Chamber Plenary for formal adoption.`

- \*Limitation:\* Policy recommendations require formal deliberation and approval by the Supreme Court Leadership and Chamber Plenary before gaining binding regulatory force.
