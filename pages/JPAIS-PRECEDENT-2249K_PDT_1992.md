# JPAIS:PRECEDENT:2249K PDT 1992

**Supreme Court Decision Reg. No. 2249 K/Pdt/1992** (dated June 22, 1994 / deliberation on May 23, 1994) is a fundamental Indonesian family and divorce law jurisprudence precedent establishing vital rules regarding the prohibition against husbands engaged in extramarital affairs or cohabiting with other women from filing for divorce on the grounds of continuous disputes (the guilty party doctrine / *Damni Culpa dati reparation* / Supreme Court Circular Letter No. 3 of 1981), alongside the custody rights (*hadhanah*) of minor children vested in the biological mother.

## Executive Summary

The Supreme Court established two primary legal principles:

1.  **Incompatibility of Spousal Infidelity as a Divorce Ground (The Guilty Party Doctrine):** Marital disputes between a Plaintiff (husband) and Defendant (wife) caused by the Plaintiff's proven involvement and cohabitation with another woman as his mistress cannot serve as grounds for divorce under Article 19 letter f of Government Regulation No. 9 of 1975. The law does not grant the right to seek a divorce to a husband who serves as the primary cause or guilty party (*misconduct*) responsible for marital friction.
2.  **Principle of the Best Interests of the Child & Maternal Custody (*Hadhanah*):** Toddlers and minor children are, by operation of law, entitled to be raised and cared for by their biological mother (affirming Supreme Court Jurisprudence Nos. 239 K/Sip/1968, 102 K/Sip/1973, and 27 K/AG/1982), rather than being surrendered to a husband cohabiting with a mistress.

The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition of the Petitioner for Cassation (\[Wife\]), annulled Semarang High Court Decision No. 792/Pdt/1991/PT.Smg dated April 3, 1992, and independently adjudicated (*mengadili sendiri*) by affirming Semarang District Court Decision No. 12/Pdt/G/1991/PN.Smg dated July 30, 1991, which **rejected the Plaintiff's (Drs. \[Husband\]) divorce lawsuit in its entirety**.

## Legal / Procedural Issues

### 1. Application of the Guilty Party Doctrine (*Damni Culpa dati reparation* / SEMA No. 3 of 1981) in Divorce Actions under Article 19(f) of Gov. Reg. No. 9 of 1975

*Is a husband who commits infidelity and cohabits with a mistress entitled to file a divorce lawsuit on grounds of continuous disputes provoked by his own misconduct?*

**Supreme Court Rule: Not Entitled (Lawsuit Must Be Rejected).** Pursuant to the general legal maxim *Nemo auditur propriam turpitudinem allegans* and *Damni Culpa dati reparation* (in conjunction with Supreme Court Circular Letter No. 3 of 1981), the right to file a divorce lawsuit is granted exclusively to innocent parties. A husband engaging in infidelity constitutes the guilty party responsible for marital breakdown, and is thus legally denied the right to seek a divorce based on disputes he provoked.

### 2. Protection of Minor Child Custody Rights (*Hadhanah* under the Best Interests of the Child Principle)

*To whom should custody (*hadhanah*) of a toddler (approx. 3–\[age removed\]) be awarded when parents separate?*

**Supreme Court Rule: Awarded to the Biological Mother.** Pursuant to Supreme Court Jurisprudence (Nos. 239 K/Sip/1968, 102 K/Sip/1973, and 27 K/AG/1982), in the interest of emotional and moral development, toddlers and minor children must be placed in the custody of their biological mother, rather than surrendered to a father cohabiting with another woman.

### 3. Joinder of Child Custody Claims in Divorce Proceedings (Article 24 of Gov. Reg. No. 9 of 1975)

Requests for child custody determinations represent direct legal consequences inseparable from divorce proceedings, permitting joinder and joint adjudication within a single divorce action.

## Question of Law vs Question of Fact

### Question of Law (*Judex Juris* / Supreme Court)

Did the *Judex Facti* Semarang High Court (Decision No. 792/Pdt/1991/PT.Smg) err in applying family law (*foutieve toepassing van het recht*) by granting a divorce lawsuit filed by a proven adulterous husband and awarding toddler custody to the guilty husband?

**Supreme Court Legal Answer:** Yes. Semarang High Court misapplied the law. The Supreme Court granted the wife's cassation petition (\[Wife\]), annulled Semarang High Court Decision No. 792/Pdt/1991/PT.Smg dated April 3, 1992, and independently adjudicated by affirming Semarang District Court Decision No. 12/Pdt/G/1991/PN.Smg dated July 30, 1991, which **rejected the Husband's divorce lawsuit in its entirety**.

### Question of Fact (*Judex Facti* / Semarang District Court & Semarang High Court)

## Jurisprudence and Related Knowledge

### Statutory Regulations

- **Law No. 1 of 1974 on Marriage:** Article 1 (Purpose of Marriage), Article 39 (Grounds for Divorce).
- **Government Regulation No. 9 of 1975:** Article 19 letter f (Continuous Dispute Grounds) & Article 24 (Interim Child Custody Measures).
- **Supreme Court Circular Letter (SEMA):** SEMA No. 3 of 1981 (Guilty Party Doctrine Barring Divorce Claims).
- **Law No. 14 of 1970 in conjunction with Law No. 14 of 1985 on the Supreme Court:** Independent Adjudication Powers at Cassation Level.

### Established Supreme Court Jurisprudence

- **Clean Hands Doctrine (Guilty Party Doctrine):** Supreme Court Decision No. 1604 K/Pdt/1984 dated September 26, 1985, in conjunction with SEMA No. 3/1981 — A husband/wife serving as the primary catalyst for marital breakdown due to reprehensible acts or infidelity has no right to file for divorce.
- **Toddler Custody Jurisprudence:** Supreme Court Decisions Nos. 239 K/Sip/1968, 102 K/Sip/1973, and 27 K/AG/1982 — Minor children must be raised by their biological mothers.

## Uncertainties and Gaps

**Integrity of Marriage Institutions Against Dispute Manipulation (*Unclean Hands*):** This landmark ruling establishes an exceptionally robust legal precedent in Indonesia preventing the manipulation of divorce law: spouses engaging in infidelity cannot deliberately instigate quarrels or abandon the marital home only to exploit self-created friction as grounds to seize divorce judgments in court. The law protects spouses who persevere in preserving family unity (*clean hands*).

## Frequently Asked Questions (GEO)

Can a husband who commits adultery and cohabits with another woman file for divorce on grounds of continuous disputes, and who is entitled to toddler custody?  
**Infidelity husbands cannot sue for divorce, and toddler custody vests in the biological mother.** Pursuant to Supreme Court Jurisprudence No. 2249 K/Pdt/1992:

- A husband proven to have committed adultery and cohabited with another woman is classified as the guilty party (*faulty party* / *Damni Culpa dati reparation* / SEMA No. 3/1981), and is thus **denied the right** to sue for divorce based on disputes he provoked under Article 19 letter f of Gov. Reg. No. 9 of 1975.
- Pursuant to established Supreme Court Jurisprudence, minor children/toddlers must by operation of law be raised and cared for by their biological mothers, rather than surrendered to a father cohabiting with a mistress.
- The Supreme Court annulled the Semarang High Court verdict and rejected the husband's divorce lawsuit in its entirety.
