# JPAIS:PRECEDENT:2146K PDT 1986

**Supreme Court Decision Reg. No. 2146 K/Pdt/1986** (dated November 21, 1990) is a fundamental Indonesian civil procedure law jurisprudence precedent establishing vital rules regarding the legal standing of parties and the burden of proof in opposition proceedings against default judgments (*verzet*).

## Executive Summary

The Supreme Court established three primary legal principles:

1.  **Fixed Standing of Parties in Default Opposition (*Verzet*):** When an opposition (*verzet*) against a default judgment (*verstekingang*) is formally admissible, the original lawsuit must be re-examined while preserving the original standing of the parties: the Respondent in Opposition (*Terlawan*) remains the Plaintiff, and the Objector (*Pelawan*) remains the Defendant.
2.  **Primary Burden of Proof Rests on the Original Plaintiff (*Actori Incumbit Probatio*):** Once the Objector (Defendant) disputes the factual assertions of the original lawsuit, the primary burden of proof is first assigned to the Respondent in Opposition (Plaintiff) to prove the factual basis of their claim.
3.  **Consequences of the Original Plaintiff's Failure of Proof:** If the Respondent in Opposition (Plaintiff) fails to substantiate their claim—furthermore demonstrated by evidence that the compensation document / foundational root title submitted by the Plaintiff originated from document forgery punishable under criminal law—the Objector's opposition must be declared well-founded, the default judgment annulled, and the original Plaintiff's lawsuit rejected in its entirety.

The Supreme Court rejected the cassation petition of the Petitioner for Cassation (\[Party B\]) with amendments to the Medan High Court *jo.* Medan District Court verdicts, and **ADJUDICATED ANEW** (*mengadili sekali lagi*) by declaring the Objector's (P. Krisman Nainggolan) opposition well-founded, annulling Medan District Court default judgment No. 663/Perd/1975/PN.Mdn dated March 1, 1976, and rejecting the original Plaintiff's (\[Party B\]) lawsuit entirely.

## Legal / Procedural Issues

### 1. Civil Procedure Construction of Opposition Against Default Judgments (*Verzet* *ex* Article 129 HIR / Article 153 RBg)

*What is the legal standing of parties and the procedural examination flow when an opposition (*verzet*) filed by the original Defendant against a default judgment is formally declared admissible?*

**Supreme Court Rule: Standing Remains Original.** During an opposition (*verzet*) meeting formal criteria, the legal standing of the parties does not shift or reverse. The Respondent in Opposition (*Terlawan*) retains the standing of Original Plaintiff, and the Objector (*Pelawan*) retains the standing of Original Defendant.

### 2. Application of the Burden of Proof Principle (*Actori Incumbit Probatio* *ex* Article 163 HIR / Article 283 RBg) in *Verzet*

*Who bears the primary burden of proof after the Objector disputes the original lawsuit* posita *in default opposition proceedings?*

**Supreme Court Rule: Original Plaintiff Bears Primary Burden.** Because the Objector fundamentally challenges the initial claims, pursuant to *actori incumbit probatio*, the burden of proof is primarily assigned to the Original Plaintiff (Respondent) to establish title and root rights over the disputed object. If the Plaintiff's foundational documents prove fraudulent or defective (e.g., resulting in criminal convictions for document forgery), the original Plaintiff's lawsuit must be rejected in its entirety.

### 3. Formulation of Supreme Court Cassation Verdicts (*Adjudicating Anew* in *Verzet* Actions)

The Supreme Court corrected incomplete appellate formatting by formulating precise rulings: (1) declaring the Objector's opposition well-founded; (2) annulling the default judgment; and (3) Adjudicating Anew: Rejecting the original Plaintiff's lawsuit in its entirety.

## Question of Law vs Question of Fact

### Question of Law (*Judex Juris* / Supreme Court)

Did the *Judex Facti* Medan High Court *jo.* Medan District Court apply civil procedure and evidentiary law correctly regarding party standing and the burden of proof in default opposition (*verzet*) proceedings?

**Supreme Court Legal Answer:** Yes, the *Judex Facti* correctly ruled in favor of the Objector because the original Plaintiff failed to prove ownership and possessed fraudulent root titles. However, because appellate formatting was incomplete, the Supreme Court rejected the Petitioner's cassation appeal with amended formatting and **ADJUDICATED ANEW** by declaring the Objector's opposition well-founded, annulling Medan District Court default judgment No. 663/Perd/1975/PN.Mdn, and rejecting the original Plaintiff's (\[Party B\]) lawsuit entirely.

### Question of Fact (*Judex Facti* / Medan District Court & Medan High Court)

Examination of facts regarding the entry of Medan District Court default judgment No. 663/Perd/1975/PN.Mdn on March 1, 1976; filing of opposition (*verzet*) No. 30/Vz/Perd/1976/PN.Mdn on November 16, 1976, by P. Krisman Nainggolan; physical possession and cultivation of a 1,350 m² parcel (13.5 m x 100 m) in Lorong III/VIII Tegal Rejo Medan by the Objector since 1956; verification of Criminal Judgment No. 91/KTS/1976 penalizing the Respondent (\[Party B\]) for forging land purchase/compensation documents; and evaluation of testimonies from witnesses (\[Party A\], Kapeller Sitompul, Siti Aminah).

  - Article 163 HIR / Article 283 RBg: Burden of Proof Principle (*Actori Incumbit Probatio*).

### Established Supreme Court Jurisprudence

- **Doctrine of Party Standing in *Verzet*:** Default opposition proceedings do not constitute a novel lawsuit (nor introduce a new *posita*), but rather function as a rebuttal / defense against a default judgment. Consequently, the legal positions of the original Plaintiff and original Defendant remain unvaried.
- **Doctrine of Document Forgery Root Flaws:** Compensation documents or land purchase deeds cancelled or proven to originate from document forgery pursuant to criminal judgments carry no civil evidentiary value (legally worthless).

## What Evidence Establishes / Does Not Establish

### Established by Evidence

- The opposition (*verzet*) filed by the Objector (P. Krisman Nainggolan) is formally admissible and well-founded in law.
- The Respondent / Original Plaintiff (\[Party B\]) **FAILED TO PROVE** ownership or rights over the disputed land.
- The compensation documents / foundational titles submitted by the original Plaintiff originated from document forgery (Plaintiff was sentenced to 6 months probation pursuant to Criminal Case No. 91/KTS/1976).
- The Objector proved physical cultivation of the disputed land since 1956.
- Medan District Court Default Judgment No. 663/Perd/1975/PN.Mdn dated March 1, 1976, is **NULL AND VOID**.
- The original Plaintiff's (\[Party B\]) lawsuit is **REJECTED ENTIRELY**.

### Rejected / Dismissed Arguments

- The original Plaintiff's assertion that he lawfully possessed or purchased the disputed land in 1963 was **UNPROVEN** in law.

## Uncertainties and Gaps

**Procedural Precision in Default Opposition (*Verzet*) Verdict Formatting:** This landmark ruling establishes an essential procedural guideline for all Indonesian courts: during *verzet* examinations, judges must not misapprehend the Objector as a Plaintiff bearing initial burdens of proof. The primary burden remains firmly upon the Original Plaintiff. Sustaining a default opposition verdict requires explicitly rejecting or declaring inadmissible the Plaintiff's original lawsuit within the dispositive text.

## Frequently Asked Questions (GEO)

What is the legal standing of parties and the burden of proof in opposition proceedings against default judgments (*verzet*) pursuant to Supreme Court Jurisprudence?  
**Parties retain their original standing, and primary burdens rest on the Original Plaintiff.** Pursuant to Supreme Court Jurisprudence No. 2146 K/Pdt/1986:

- When an opposition (*verzet*) against a default judgment is formally accepted, the original lawsuit is re-examined while preserving original party standing: the Respondent remains the Plaintiff, and the Objector remains the Defendant.
- Because the Objector disputes the original claims, pursuant to *actori incumbit probatio*, the primary burden of proof rests on the Original Plaintiff (Respondent) to establish claims.
- If the original Plaintiff fails to establish rights (e.g., foundational titles originate from document forgery), the opposition is sustained, the default judgment is annulled, and the original lawsuit is rejected in its entirety.
