## Policy Analysis

- **Background & Objectives**:
  - Accurate understanding of actual crime situations, including measuring crimes unreported to the police (the dark figure of crime), is essential for effective public security policies.
  - Primary objectives of the National Crime Victimization Survey (NCVS):
    - Estimate the types and volume of unreported crimes.
    - Obtain detailed information on crime victims and victimization experiences.
    - Collect crime trend data via fixed-point monitoring.
    - Clarify public perceptions of safety.
    - Provide empirical crime victimization data for relevant organizations and the public.
- **Methodology & Scope (5th NCVS - 2019)**:
  - Sample selected from 6,000 men and women aged 16 and older (3,000 each) using two-stage stratified random sampling.
  - In-person interviews conducted between January 26 and February 28, 2019.
  - Self-administered questionnaires used for sensitive crime categories: stalking, domestic violence (DV), child abuse, and sexual incidents.
  - Valid response rate: 3,709 participants (61.8%) for interviews and 3,500 participants (58.3%) for self-administered questionnaires.
- **Main Victimization Trends & Findings**:
  - Overall victimization rate (household and personal, excluding child abuse) reached \*\*23.8% over the past 5 years\*\* and \*\*7.0% in 2018\*\*.
  - Overall 5-year victimization rate in the 5th NCVS (2019) was lower than in previous iterations (1st in 2000 through 4th in 2012).
  - Logistic regression analysis indicates that housing type, household size, sex, and age group significantly affect victimization risks (higher risks for apartment residents, households with 4+ members, females, and individuals aged ≤59).
- **Victimization by Crime Category**:
  - Household Crimes: Bicycle theft showed the highest victimization rate, followed by vehicle vandalism.
  - Personal Crimes: Theft of personal property was the highest. Stalking, DV, and sexual incidents were significantly more prevalent among females and younger age groups (≤39 years).
  - Sexual Incidents: Occurred most frequently in workplace environments.
- **Dark Figure of Crime Phenomenon**:
  - Across all personal victimization and identity theft/fraud categories, the number of victims who DID NOT report to police far exceeded those who reported.
  - Primary general reason for non-reporting: "minor crime / attempt only, little or no damage."
  - Primary reasons for non-reporting DV: "handled it independently (knew the offender)" and "did not know how to report / what to do."
  - Primary reasons for non-reporting child abuse: "did not know what to do," "believed no action would be taken," and "felt ashamed / did not want others to know."
  - Primary reason for non-reporting credit/debit card fraud: "notified credit card company directly."
- **Public Safety Perception & Criminal Sentencing Views**:
  - Public perception of Japan as a safe country has steadily increased, while perception of unsafe environments has consistently declined.
  - Respondents with higher fear of crime and those with personal victimization experiences showed greater support for imprisonment or suspended imprisonment over fine penalties.
- **Interpretations & Structural Inferences**:
  - The 5th NCVS demonstrates that despite improving objective public safety indicators, a substantial reporting gap persists for personal and domestic offenses (DV, child abuse, sexual assault) due to psychological barriers, shame, and procedural uncertainty.
  - High rates of hidden crime in close personal relationships indicate that legal policy improvements must combine formal law enforcement with accessible, confidential non-police reporting and support channels.
