# Judicial-policy-vandewardt-mindthegap-2021

**Mind the gap: How party–voter incongruence fuels the entry and support of new parties** is an empirical comparative political science and public policy research paper authored by Marc van de Wardt (Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam, Ghent University, & ULB) and Simon Otjes (Leiden University & University of Groningen), published in the *European Journal of Political Research* (EJPR) in 2021 (print edition Vol. 61, pages 194–213, 2022).

## Summary

This study analyzes how ideological incongruence — the gap between voters' policy positions and the positions of established parties along the left–right spectrum — drives the emergence of new political parties in parliamentary elections and fuels voter support for those new parties. Testing a core assumption of spatial voting theory, the study consists of two parts: Study 1 examines new party entry rates across 47 elections in 17 Western European countries (2000–2016), and Study 2 examines individual-level voting decisions among 18,323–23,338 voters across 11 countries (1996–2015). The findings demonstrate that **representational gaps significantly increase the number of new parties entering elections and drive voters toward the new party that is ideologically closer to them**.

## Background

In representative democracy, voters control public policy by electing the party closest to their own views. When established parties shift toward the center or converge, representational gaps emerge. Political figures such as Pablo Iglesias (Podemos, Spain), Thierry Baudet (Forum voor Democratie, Netherlands), and Emmanuel Macron (En Marche, France) have openly stated that they founded new parties to fill ideological space abandoned by established parties. Spatial theory (Downs 1957) assumes this dynamic, but a comprehensive, large-scale cross-national empirical test had not previously been carried out.

## Objectives

1\. Test Hypothesis 1 (New Party Entry): the rate of new party entry in elections will be higher when party–voter incongruence is higher. 2. Test Hypothesis 2 (New Party Voting): voters will be more likely to vote for a new party when that party is perceived as ideologically closer than the nearest established party. 3. Compare the effect of policy proximity against democratic dissatisfaction (protest voting).

## Scope

The research covers a sample of 17 consolidated Western European democracies between 1996 and 2016, drawing on data from the Comparative Study of Electoral Systems (CSES), ParlGov, and national election records.

## Key Provisions / Findings

- **Hypothesis 1 Supported (New Party Entry):** Party–voter incongruence has a statistically significant positive effect on the number of new parties (p \< 0.05). An increase in incongruence from the 25th to the 75th percentile raises the number of new parties from **6.22 to 11.54**.
- **Hypothesis 2 Supported (New Party Voting Behavior):** The relative proximity of a new party significantly increases a voter's probability of voting for it (p \< 0.01). Moving relative proximity from the 25th to the 75th percentile increases the predicted probability of a new-party vote from **0.01 to 0.07**.
- **Valence Advantage of Established Parties:** When a new party and an established party are equally close ideologically to a voter, the probability of voting for the new party is only **0.15**, while the probability of voting for the established party is **0.85**. This shows that established parties hold a strong reputational and track-record advantage.
- **Policy Motivation vs. Protest Voting:** Ideological proximity has a much stronger effect than dissatisfaction with democracy, demonstrating that new-party voters are primarily driven by policy considerations ("policy voters") rather than simply casting protest votes.

## Implementation

The methodology was implemented through Poisson regression for event counts in Study 1, using the Earth Mover's Distance (EMD) incongruence measure, and logistic regression with clustered standard errors in Study 2. The research was funded by the Research Foundation – Flanders (FWO Grant FWO16/PDO/198) and F.R.S.-FNRS (Grant 28091302).

## Policy Issues

1\. Representational gaps arising from established parties shifting away from voter preferences. 2. The valence disadvantage that makes it difficult for new parties to compete unless they offer a clearly distinct ideological position.

## Reform / Implications

1\. Integrating short-term party–voter incongruence variables into theoretical models of strategic party entry. 2. Recognizing that voters possess sufficient rationality and political information to assess parties' ideological proximity.
