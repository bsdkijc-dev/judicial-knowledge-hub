# JPW-PUBLICATION-HORIZON-FINCRIME-2025

**Horizon Scanner – Financial Crime** is a global regulatory horizon-scanning report and intelligence publication released by [Eversheds Sutherland](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Eversheds_Sutherland) on August 14, 2025 \[1–4\]. The report synthesizes legal, policy, and enforcement developments surrounding financial crime across the United Kingdom, European Union, and United States over a two-year forward-looking horizon \[1–4\].

## Executive Summary

The publication provides a comprehensive risk-navigation framework for international business organizations and financial institutions to anticipate shifts in the global financial crime regulatory landscape \[1–4\]. It details upcoming corporate criminal enforcement, anti-money laundering (AML) reforms, sanctions compliance priorities, and legal frameworks governing digital assets and stablecoins \[1–4\].

## Legal Basis & Legal Disclaimer

- **Formal Legal Basis**: Economic Crime and Corporate Transparency Act 2023 (ECCTA) (UK), Bank Secrecy Act (BSA) (US), GENIUS Act (US), alongside regulatory enforcement guidance issued by the FCA, NCA, FinCEN, DOJ, and OFAC \[1–4\].
- **Informational Disclaimer**: The content presented in the report is provided strictly for general informational purposes and does not constitute formal legal advice \[1–4\].

## Background

Escalating regulatory scrutiny regarding money laundering, corporate fraud, terrorism financing, and digital currency exploitation demands that commercial entities maintain deep operational awareness of legal reforms across multiple jurisdictions \[1–4\]. This report is designed to assist market participants in identifying legal exposure and upgrading internal compliance frameworks \[1–4\].

## Objectives

1.  Provide a consolidated overview of financial crime regulatory developments across the UK, EU, and US over the next two years \[1–4\].
2.  Outline the operational implications of the new corporate offense of "failure to prevent fraud" under the UK ECCTA \[1–4\].
3.  Detail US statutory changes, including the GENIUS Act regulating stablecoins and extended AML compliance timelines for investment advisers under the Bank Secrecy Act \[1–4\].
4.  Clarify beneficial ownership reporting requirements mandated by FinCEN for non-US entities registered to conduct business in the United States \[1–4\].

## Key Provisions & Recommendations

- **UK Corporate Offense (ECCTA)**: The new corporate criminal offense of "failure to prevent fraud" under the Economic Crime and Corporate Transparency Act 2023 officially takes effect on September 1, 2025 \[1–4\].
- **UK Economic Crime Priorities**: In July 2025, the National Crime Agency (NCA) and Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) published joint economic crime priorities aligned with the 2025 National Risk Assessment on money laundering and terrorist financing \[1–4\].
- **FCA PEP Guidance**: The FCA issued updated guidance regarding Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs) for anti-money laundering compliance \[1–4\].
- **US GENIUS Act**: Enactment of the GENIUS Act by President Trump, establishing the first comprehensive federal regulatory framework governing stablecoins \[1–4\].
- **US Investment Adviser AML Program**: The effective date requiring investment advisers to implement formal AML programs under the Bank Secrecy Act (BSA) has been extended to January 2028 \[1–4\].
- **US FinCEN Beneficial Ownership Reporting**: Mandatory beneficial ownership information reporting rules apply to non-US companies registered to operate in the United States \[1–4\].
- **US Sanctions & Enforcement Directives**: OFAC issued updated sanctions relief regarding Syria, designated specific cartel members as terrorists, and the DOJ released new enforcement priorities \[1–4\].

## Implementation & Deployment

- **Publication Release**: Officially released globally on August 14, 2025 \[1–4\].
- **Lead Practice Team**: Managed by Eversheds Sutherland’s Corporate Crime and Financial Services Regulation group, featuring lead partners Anupreet Amole, Rebecca Copley, Andrea L. Gordon, Steve Smith, Victoria Turner, and Zia Ullah \[1–4\].

## Policy & Operational Challenges

- **Cross-Border Compliance Friction**: Multinational corporations face operational challenges in aligning internal controls simultaneously with strict UK corporate criminal offenses and evolving US AML timelines \[1–4\].
- **FinCEN Reporting Burden for Non-US Entities**: Foreign entities operating in the US face expanded compliance and disclosure obligations under FinCEN beneficial ownership rules \[1–4\].
