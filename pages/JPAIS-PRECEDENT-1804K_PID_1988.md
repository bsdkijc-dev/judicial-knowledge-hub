# JPAIS:PRECEDENT:1804K PID 1988

**Supreme Court Decision Reg. No. 1804 K/Pid/1988** (dated October 23, 1991 / pronounced February 12, 1992) is an Indonesian substantive criminal law precedent establishing a fundamental rule regarding the juridical qualification of complicity (*deelneming*) between "Ordering an Act" (*Doen Plegen* *ex* Article 55 paragraph (1) 2nd of the Criminal Code / KUHP) and "Direct Perpetrator / Co-perpetration" (*Dader* / *Mededader* *ex* Article 170 paragraph (1) of the Criminal Code).

## Executive Summary

The Supreme Court established two primary legal principles:

1.  **Doctrine and Requirements of *Doen Plegen* ("Ordering an Act"):** In criminal law theory, the form of complicity known as "Ordering an Act" (*Doen Plegen*) implies that the direct perpetrator (*manus ministra* / tool) cannot be held criminally liable (for example, due to a lack of mental capacity, being under duress or irresistible force, or lacking intent/culpability).
2.  **Requalification of Community Leaders' Actions in Mass Riots:** If trial facts prove that Defendants (the Village Head and the LKMD Chairman) did not merely shout instructions, but were physically present and personally or jointly carried out acts of violence and property destruction at the scene, their actions constitute direct execution (*Dader* / *Mededader*). Consequently, the proper legal qualification is Direct Perpetrator of Public Assault / Property Destruction *ex* Article 170 paragraph (1) of the Criminal Code, and **not** "Ordering an Act" (*Doen Pleger*).

The Supreme Court granted the Defendants' (I, II, IV, V) cassation petition, annulled Maluku High Court in Ambon Decision No. 15/PID.B/1987/PT.MAL dated May 2, 1988 *jo.* Labuha District Court Decision No. 01/PTS/Pid/B/1986/PN.LBH dated March 21, 1987, and independently adjudicated (*mengadili sendiri*) by declaring Defendants I, II, and V legally and convincingly guilty of committing the crime of "Publicly and jointly using violence against property" *ex* Article 170 paragraph (1) of the Criminal Code (as direct perpetrators) and sentencing them to imprisonment of 4 (four) months (Defendant I), 5 (five) months (Defendant II), and 3 (three) months (Defendant V), respectively. Defendant IV (\[Party E\]) was found guilty of violating Article 406 paragraph (1) of the Criminal Code (2 months imprisonment), while Defendants VI, VII, and VIII were acquitted of all charges with their reputations restored.

## Legal / Procedural Issues

### 1. Construction of Complicity (*Deelneming* *ex* Article 55 KUHP): Delineation Between *Doen Plegen* ("Ordering an Act") and *Medeplegen* / *Dader* ("Co-perpetration / Direct Perpetrator")

*Is a village official / community leader present at a mass riot who shouts instructions for destruction while physically participating in structural demolition appropriately qualified as a person who "ordered an act" (*doen pleger*)?*

**Supreme Court Rule: Inappropriate (Misapplication of Law).** The form of complicity known as *Doen Plegen* (Article 55 paragraph 1 2nd of the Criminal Code) requires the presence of an instrument (*manus ministra*) who cannot be penalized due to a lack of intent or culpability. If community members present at the scene are penally liable and the Defendant directly participates in the destruction physically, the Defendant's legal qualification is Direct Perpetrator / Co-perpetrator (*Dader* / *Mededader* *ex* Article 170 paragraph 1 of the Criminal Code).

### 2. Forfeiture of Public Prosecutor's Cassation Right Due to Procedural Default (Article 248 of KUHAP)

A Public Prosecutor who files a cassation petition but fails to submit a cassation memorandum/brief within the 14-day statutory deadline stipulated under Article 248 paragraphs (1) and (4) of KUHAP causes their cassation right to be forfeited by operation of law, resulting in the petition being declared inadmissible (*Niet Ontvankelijk Verklaard* / NO).

### 3. Proving Public Assault / Property Destruction (Article 170(1) KUHP vs. Article 406(1) KUHP)

Joint actions in public places destroying houses and furnishings belonging victims fulfill all elements of Article 170 paragraph (1) of the Criminal Code. Meanwhile, individual actions destroying kitchen utensils (pots/pans) outside the main mass riot are more appropriately qualified as individual property destruction offenses *ex* Article 406 paragraph (1) of the Criminal Code.

## Question of Law vs Question of Fact

### Question of Law (Judex Juris / Supreme Court)

Did the *Judex Facti* (Ambon High Court Decision No. 15/PID.B/1987/PT.MAL *jo.* Labuha District Court Decision No. 01/PTS/Pid/B/1986/PN.LBH) err in applying substantive criminal law (*foutieve toepassing van het recht*) by sentencing Defendants I, II, and V as persons who "ordered an act" (*doen pleger*), despite facts proving they directly executed violent/destructive acts jointly with residents in the field?

**Supreme Court Legal Answer:** Yes. The *Judex Facti* misapplied the law. The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition of Defendants I, II, IV, and V, annulled the *Judex Facti* decisions, and independently adjudicated by requalifying the actions of Defendants I, II, and V as Direct Perpetrators of Public Assault / Property Destruction offenses *ex* Article 170 paragraph (1) of the Criminal Code.

### Question of Fact (Judex Facti / Labuha District Court & Ambon High Court)

Examination of facts regarding mass riot reactions on Tuesday night, May 21, 1985, through Wednesday early morning, May 22, 1985, in Amasing Kota Village, Labuha, Bacan, following a slashing incident perpetrated by victim witness \[Party D\]; shouting by Defendant I (Village Head) and Defendant II (LKMD Chairman) in front of the Koramil commander's house and market complex ("just dismantle that house, do not be afraid, I take responsibility"); physical destruction of 1 wooden/thatched-roof house and kitchen items (plates, glasses, stoves, thermoses, mirrors) belonging to Ny. Sarifah Sumampoh and \[Party D\]; and evaluation of individual roles of Defendants I through VIII.

## Jurisprudence and Related Knowledge

### Statutory Regulations

- **Criminal Code (KUHP):**
  - Article 55 paragraph (1) 1st & 2nd: Forms of Complicity (Perpetrator, Ordering an Act, Co-perpetration).
  - Article 170 paragraph (1): Public and Joint Violence Against Persons or Property.
  - Article 406 paragraph (1): Property Destruction Crimes.
- **Law No. 8 of 1981 (Criminal Procedure Code / KUHAP):**
  - Article 248 paragraphs (1) & (4): Obligation to Submit Cassation Memorandums and Forfeiture of Cassation Rights.
  - Article 253 paragraph (1) letter a: Cassation Grounds Due to Errors in Applying Law.
- **Law No. 14 of 1970 *jo.* Law No. 14 of 1985 on the Supreme Court:** Authority to independently adjudicate at the cassation level.

### Doctrines & Established Supreme Court Jurisprudence

- Doctrine of Complicity (*Deelneming* / *Doen Plegen* vs. *Medeplegen*): *Doen Plegen* requires a blameless tool (*manus ministra* / *niet strafbaar*). If field perpetrators are penally liable and the instigator joins in executing acts in the field, the legal qualification is *medeplegen* / direct perpetrator (*dader*).

- Defendant IV (\[Party E\]) is legally and convincingly proven guilty of committing the crime of "Intentionally destroying property" *ex* Article 406 paragraph (1) of the Criminal Code.
- Defendant I sentenced to 4 months imprisonment; Defendant II to 5 months imprisonment; Defendant IV to 2 months imprisonment; Defendant V to 3 months imprisonment.
