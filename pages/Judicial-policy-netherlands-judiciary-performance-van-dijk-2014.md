# Judicial-policy-netherlands-judiciary-performance-van-dijk-2014

**The Policy Framework for the Performance and Economic Impact of the Netherlands Judiciary** (*Performance of the Netherlands Judiciary & Economic Impact Policy Framework*) is a judicial performance evaluation and macro/microeconomic impact policy framework formulated by Dr. Frans van Dijk (Director of the Council for the Judiciary, Netherlands). It evaluates a 15-year transformation of the Dutch judiciary (1998–2013) following the Leemhuis Commission Report and the 2002 Reorganization Act (the establishment of the \*Raad voor de rechtspraak\* and \*gerechtsbesturen\*), measures court delay reductions, user satisfaction, and calculates realized societal economic gains of €795M–€970M per year (0.13%–0.16% of GDP).

## Summary

Published by Dr. Frans van Dijk in the \*International Journal for Court Administration\* (IJCA), this empirical policy framework provides a retrospective evaluation of Dutch judicial reforms between 1998 and 2013. Initiated by the \*Leemhuis Commission Report\* (1998) and the establishment of the \*Council for the Judiciary\* (\*Raad voor de rechtspraak\*) in 2002, the Dutch judiciary implemented output-based management, quality and lead-time standards, and a judicial map restructuring (\*Herziening Rechterlijke Kaart 2013\*). Van Dijk's evaluation demonstrates that civil trial duration reductions (190 days faster) and administrative streamlining successfully generated tangible societal economic gains ranging from €795M to €970M annually.

## Legal Basis

- **Wet op de rechterlijke organisatie / 2002 Judiciary Governance Reform Act:** Established the Council for the Judiciary and transferred operational court management responsibilities to integrally responsible court boards (\*gerechtsbesturen\*).
- **Crisis- en herstelwet (2010 Crisis and Recovery Law):** Instituted expedited procedures and strict statutory deadlines for administrative litigation involving infrastructure projects.
- **Wet op de herziening van de rechterlijke kaart (2013 New Judicial Map Law):** Restructured judicial jurisdictions by consolidating 21 first-instance courts into 11, and 5 appeal courts into 4.
- **2011 Small Claims Jurisdiction Regulations:** Increased the monetary threshold for small claims courts (\*kantonrechter\*) from €5,000 to €25,000.
- **Not found in the available sources:** Other specific formal statutory regulations are not mentioned in the source text.

## Background

In 1998, the Dutch Ministry of Justice examined the economic impacts of court inefficiencies. Three primary categories of societal losses were identified: transaction costs, investment postponement costs, and capital uncertainty costs.

## Objectives

1\. Reduce court delays for major civil disputes by up to 8 months and administrative cases by up to 6 months. 2. Improve judicial reasoning clarity and the uniform application of the law. 3. Lower courtroom waiting times from 1–2 hours to less than 30 minutes. 4. Realize potential societal economic gains of up to 0.24% of GDP.

## Scope

This policy covers the evaluation of Civil Law and Administrative Law litigation across First-Instance Courts and Courts of Appeal in the Netherlands.

## Key Provisions

- **Three Categories of Economic Losses from Court Delays:**

1.  \*Transaction Costs:\* Litigation fees, attorney remuneration, and productive time losses.

<!-- -->

1.  \*Costs of Postponing Activities:\* Freezing investment projects and business permits due to ongoing disputes.

<!-- -->

1.  \*Costs of Uncertainty:\* Reserving liquidity capital buffers to hedge against judicial outcome unpredictability.

- **Efficiency Gains and Realized Economic Value (1998–2013):**

`*Major Civil Disputes:* Duration reduced by 190 days (from 626 to 436 days). Realized economic gain: **€530M per year**.`  
`*Administrative Cases:* Duration reduced by 2–4 months (down to 259 days) via the *Nieuwe zaaksbehandeling* method. Realized economic gain: **€175M–€350M per year**.`  
`*Courtroom Waiting Times:* Decreased from 1–2 hours to ~30 minutes. Realized economic gain: **€90M per year**.`  
`*Total Realized Societal Gains:* **€795M–€970M per year** (0.13%–0.16% of GDP).`

- **New Administrative Case-Handling Method (Nieuwe zaaksbehandeling 2011/12):**

`Mandatory initial hearings within 13 weeks where judges integrate mediation techniques to resolve disputes on the spot.`

## Implementation

- **Customer Satisfaction Surveys (Prisma & Synovate Regioplan 2011):** Satisfaction with legal reasoning rose from 59% to 83% for litigants and 52% to 67% for legal professionals; satisfaction regarding uniform law application climbed from 38% to 56%.
- **2013 New Judicial Map:** Jurisdictional consolidation to foster within-court specialization among judges.

## Policy Problems

- **Emerging Digital Access Bottleneck (KEI Program):** Growing public demand for e-filing, digital archives, and automated hearing schedules that remains partially unfulfilled.
- **Court Fee Barriers:** Government-mandated increases in court fees that risk restricting access to justice.

## Reform / Change

- Civil procedural simplification and full digital transformation (\*KEI Program 2013–2017\*).
- Governance adjustments anchored in public performance accountability.

## Relationships with Other Entities
