# Judicial-policy-gessler-regimedivide-2025

**A new regime divide? Democratic backsliding, attitudes towards democracy and affective polarization** is an empirical comparative political science research paper authored by Theresa Gessler (European University Viadrina) and Natasha Wunsch (University of Fribourg), published in the *European Journal of Political Research* (EJPR) in 2025.

## Summary

This study tests the reverse relationship between democratic backsliding and affective polarization \[1, 4, 15–16\]. Unlike conventional literature, which treats affective polarization purely as a driver of democratic backsliding, this study demonstrates that prolonged democratic erosion by a government can itself generate a new "regime divide" built around liberal democratic values \[1, 5, 19–20, 80\]. This divide unites ideologically diverse opposition voters and parties in joint mobilization to defend democracy \[1, 6, 67, 80–81\].

## Legal Basis

- **Not found in the available sources:** This document is an academic political science research study and is not based on any specific statutory or judicial legislative instrument \[1–119\].
- **2012 Hungarian Constitutional & Electoral Reform:** Changes to electoral rules enacted by the Fidesz government that strengthened the majoritarian system and reshaped party competition structures.

## Background

Earlier studies emphasized that affective polarization increases voters' tolerance for democratic violations committed by their own party's leaders. However, in contexts of prolonged democratic backsliding — such as under Fidesz rule in Hungary since 2010 — commitment to liberal democracy itself becomes a central axis dividing society between government supporters and opposition supporters \[1, 7, 19–20, 42\].

## Objectives

1\. Test for the existence of a government-versus-opposition divide in evaluations of party supporters (Hypothesis 1) \[23–24, 48, 52\]. 2. Analyze how commitment to liberal democracy shapes negative evaluations of ruling-party supporters and positive evaluations of opposition supporters (Hypotheses 2a/2b) \[27–28, 54, 57\]. 3. Test the moderating effect of vote intention on the relationship between democratic attitudes and affective polarization (Hypothesis 3) \[29–32, 58, 62\].

## Scope

The research's primary comparative analysis is conducted in Hungary using an original survey (N = 2,000) fielded by Inquiry/YouGov (December 2021 – January 2022), supplemented by secondary analysis in Poland \[1, 7, 33–34, 74\].

## Key Provisions / Findings

- **Hypothesis 1 Supported (Government–Opposition Divide):** Opposition supporters rate ruling-party (Fidesz) supporters 3 points lower than the population average on a 0–10 scale (over 50% select the extreme dislike option), while rating all opposition parties significantly more positively \[48, 50–52\].
- **Hypotheses 2a/2b Supported (Democracy Divide):** Liberal democratic commitment is significantly negatively associated with evaluations of Fidesz supporters (b = -0.27, p \< 0.001) and positively associated with evaluations of opposition-party supporters (Momentum b = 0.34; Dialogue b = 0.23; LMP b = 0.23; DK b = 0.16).
- **Exception — Jobbik:** The democracy-attitude effect is non-significant for the far-right Jobbik party, confirming that parties with questionable democratic commitment are excluded from the democratic in-group.
- **Hypothesis 3 Supported (Partisan Moderation):** The positive effect of liberal democratic attitudes on solidarity with opposition supporters is driven specifically by opposition voters; Fidesz voters' evaluations are unaffected by liberal democratic attitudes \[58–62\].
- **Role of Backsliding Duration:** The regime divide reaches full maturity in Hungary after 12+ years of Fidesz rule, producing a single joint 106-district opposition coalition in the 2022 election \[66–69, 77, 79\], whereas in Poland the process was still developing.

## Implementation

The methodology was implemented through OLS linear regression models with interaction terms, demographic and ideological controls, and questionnaire robustness testing \[36–38, 50, 53, 56\]. The pre-analysis plan is publicly pre-registered on OSF (\`https://osf.io/a8utq/\`).

## Policy Issues

1\. Systematic democratic backsliding through the erosion of judicial independence, media freedom, and electoral integrity. 2. Ideological fragmentation that frequently prevents opposition parties from consolidating against an autocratizing incumbent \[5, 21–22, 45\].

## Reform / Implications

The study offers a new, positive perspective on affective polarization, showing that polarization organized around democratic values can function as a bulwark defending democratic institutions \[1, 4, 6, 80–81\].
