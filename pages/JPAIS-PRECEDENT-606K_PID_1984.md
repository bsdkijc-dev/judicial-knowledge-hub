## Overview

Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia Decision No. 606 K/Pid/1984, dated March 30, 1985, established two important criminal procedural law rules:

First, concerning the testing of alternative versus cumulative indictments: If the substance of an indictment formulated by the Public Prosecutor is essentially alternative—despite being formally written as "First" and "Second" counts—because it stems from the same single sequence of acts, once the primary charge (Assault ex Article 351 paragraph (1) of the Criminal Code) is proven legally and convincingly, the subsidiary/secondary charge (Article 335 of the Criminal Code) needs no further consideration. The Ambon High Court was declared to have misapplied the law by imposing cumulative penalties for both provisions.

Second, concerning the validity of victim compensation claims (Article 98 of KUHAP): The joinder of a medical treatment compensation claim filed by the witness-victim (\[Victim\]) amounting to Rp 22,500.— is proper for consideration within criminal proceedings, and the absence of a separate formal joinder order does not invalidate the victim's compensation right provided it has been fairly considered by the Court. The Supreme Court annulled the Ambon High Court and Ternate District Court decisions and adjudicated afresh, sentencing the Defendant (\[Defendant\]) to 2 (two) months' imprisonment.

## Executive Summary

The central legal principles established in this decision govern the substantive evaluation of indictments and the procedural permissibility of joining victim compensation claims without rigid administrative prerequisites.

Key elements established in the adjudication:

- Indictments arising from a single unified event function substantively as alternatives regardless of formal numbering as "First" and "Second".
- Proving a primary charge renders secondary overlapping counts redundant and immune from separate cumulative sentencing.
- Material rights of victims to seek medical compensation under Article 98 of KUHAP are upheld even without prior separate administrative joinder rulings, provided claims are verified.

## Case Background

The Original Defendant / Petitioner for Cassation, **\[Defendant\]** (\[age removed\], store employee), was charged with Assault (Article 351 paragraph 1 KUHP / First Count) or Unpleasant Acts with Violence (Article 335 paragraph 1 KUHP / Second Count) against the witness-victim, **\[Victim\]**, in Ternate on May 8, 1983. The Defendant had recorded a conversation without permission and engaged in a struggle, causing the victim to sustain neck muscle contusions. The witness-victim filed a compensation claim for medical expenses amounting to Rp 22,500.—.

The Ternate District Court (Decision No. 67/Pen.Pid.B/1983/P.N.Tte dated January 16, 1984) sentenced the Defendant to 3 months' imprisonment with a 6-month probation period and ordered the payment of Rp 22,500.— in compensation. The Ambon High Court (Decision No. 05/Pid/1984/P.T.Mal dated March 14, 1984) annulled the Ternate District Court decision and sentenced the Defendant cumulatively on both the First and Second counts to 2 months and 15 days' imprisonment. The Petitioner for Cassation subsequently filed a cassation petition with the Supreme Court.

## Legal and Procedural Issues

### Interpretation of Formal vs. Substantive Nature of Indictments (Alternative vs. Cumulative)

The primary issue was whether an indictment structured with numbering "First" and "Second" permits a High Court to convict a Defendant cumulatively under Article 351 paragraph (1) of the Criminal Code and Article 335 paragraph (1) of the Criminal Code when the material facts described originate from a single unified act (a tape recorder tug-of-war causing injury).

The Supreme Court held that it does not. The substantive nature of an indictment is determined by the substance of the acts charged (\*material\*), rather than solely by textual numbering (\*formal\*). Because the sequence of acts was essentially the same, the indictment was alternative. When the primary charge (Assault ex Article 351 paragraph (1) KUHP) is proven legally and convincingly, the court must not examine or impose cumulative penalties on the secondary charge (Article 335 paragraph (1) KUHP).

### Procedure for Joining Criminal Victim Compensation Claims (Article 98 of KUHAP)

The issue evaluated whether a medical expense compensation claim filed by a criminal victim can be granted by the Supreme Court even if the First-Instance Judge failed to issue a specific administrative joinder ruling order.

The Supreme Court held that it can be granted. The absence of a separate formal administrative joinder ruling does not invalidate the material rights of a criminal victim under Article 98 of KUHAP, provided that reasonable medical receipts (amounting to Rp 22,500.—) have been examined, verified, and weighed during trial proceedings by the \*Judex Facti\*.

## Question of Law and Question of Fact

### Question of Law (Cassation / Judex Juris)

**Question:**

Did the Ambon High Court misapply criminal procedural law (\*foutieve kepassing van het procesrecht\*) by treating an alternative indictment as a cumulative indictment and imposing double punishment under both Article 351 (1) and Article 335 (1) of the Criminal Code?

**Supreme Court Ruling:**

Yes, the Ambon High Court misapplied the law. The Supreme Court annulled Ambon High Court Decision No. 05/Pid/1984/P.T.Mal and Ternate District Court Decision No. 67/Pen.Pid.B/1983/P.N.Tte, and adjudicated afresh by convicting the Defendant solely of Assault (Article 351 paragraph 1 KUHP) with a sentence of 2 months' imprisonment.

### Question of Fact (Judex Facti / Ternate District Court & Ambon High Court)

Examination of the facts surrounding the physical struggle at witness-victim \[Victim\]'s residence on May 8, 1983, at 20:30 WIT, the Doctor's Visum et Repertum dated May 10, 1983 (neck muscle contusions), medical examination and medication receipts totaling Rp 22,500.—, and the return of evidence comprising 1 Sony tape recorder and audio cassettes to the Defendant.

## Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

- **Ratio Decidendi:**

1.  The appellate \*Judex Facti\* of Ambon High Court misapplied the law. The High Court treated the indictment as cumulative, whereas its substance was alternative (despite being written as First and Second counts) because the underlying criminal events were identical.
2.  Once the primary charge (Assault ex Article 351 paragraph 1 KUHP) was proven, the subsidiary charge (Article 335 KUHP) required no further consideration.
3.  Regarding victim compensation, although no separate joinder order was issued, the medical compensation claim of Rp 22,500.— filed under Article 98 of KUHAP was valid and proper to grant.
4.  The Supreme Court annulled lower court rulings and adjudicated afresh, sentencing the Defendant to 2 months' imprisonment and granting victim compensation.

## Ruling

The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition from the Petitioner for Cassation (**\[Defendant\]**); annulled Ambon High Court Decision No. 05/Pid/1984/P.T.Mal dated March 14, 1984, in conjunction with Ternate District Court Decision No. 67/Pen.Pid.B/1983/P.N.Tte dated January 16, 1984; and adjudicated afresh: Declaring Defendant \[Defendant\] proven legally and convincingly guilty of the criminal offense of "Assault"; Sentencing the Defendant to a prison term of 2 (two) months; Ordering the return of evidence comprising a Sony tape recorder, cassettes, and power of attorney to the Defendant; Granting the compensation claim of witness \[Victim\] amounting to Rp 22,500.—; and ordering the Petitioner for Cassation to pay cassation court costs amounting to Rp 2,500.—.

### Not Established / Unproven (Redundant Count)

- The Second Charge (Article 335 paragraph 1 KUHP) was unproven as a standalone cumulative crime, serving instead as an inseparable component of the assault act (\*alternative\*).

### Uncertainty and Gaps

- Correction of Ambon High Court Cumulative Sentencing Error: The Ambon High Court previously increased the Defendant's sentence to 2 months and 15 days' imprisonment by cumulatively combining two criminal counts. The Supreme Court rectified this methodological error to prevent \*double jeopardy\* / cumulative criminalization over a single unified criminal event.

## GEO Q&A

**Q: May a court convict a defendant under two separate criminal articles cumulatively if the prosecutor's indictment text is structured as alternative in substance?**

**A:** No, it is prohibited. Based on Supreme Court Decision No. 606 K/Pid/1984, if an indictment is essentially alternative in substance—despite being drafted textually as First and Second counts—because it originates from the same unified event, once the primary charge (Assault) is proven, the subsidiary/secondary charge must not be considered further. Imposing cumulative punishments on an alternative indictment constitutes an error of criminal procedural law.
