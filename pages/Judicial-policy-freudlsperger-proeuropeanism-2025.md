# Judicial-policy-freudlsperger-proeuropeanism-2025

**Varieties of pro-Europeanism? How mainstream parties compete over redistribution in the European Union** is an empirical comparative political science and public policy research paper authored by Christian Freudlsperger (ETH Zürich) and Martin Weinrich (University of Osnabrück), published in the *European Journal of Political Research* (EJPR) in 2025.

## Summary

This study analyzes how mainstream political parties in the European Union compete on the issues of redistribution and transnational solidarity \[1–6\]. Based on an analysis of 2,570 party positions drawn from the 'EUandI' / 'EU Profiler' voting advice application datasets across four European Parliament election cycles (2009, 2014, 2019, and 2024), the study distinguishes two competing visions for the future of the EU: the **redistributive polity** (emphasizing transnational solidarity and risk/cost-sharing) and the **regulatory polity** (emphasizing national self-reliance and market-rule enforcement) \[1, 4, 18, 22–23, 38\].

## Legal Basis

- **Not found in the available sources:** This document is an academic political science research study and is not based on any specific statutory or judicial legislative instrument \[1–117\].
- **Core State Power Integration:** The analysis focuses on secondary-law revisions and EU policy in the fields of fiscal policy, taxation, asylum/migration, and external defense.

## Background

During the post-Maastricht era, mainstream parties tended to practice "benign neglect" of EU issues to avoid internal divisions \[10–11\]. However, the succession of crises over the past 15 years — the Eurozone debt crisis, the 2015 Schengen migration crisis, COVID-19 pandemic recovery, and security threats stemming from Russia's war against Ukraine — forced mainstream parties to openly articulate the model of Europe they support \[4, 6, 10–12, 73\].

## Objectives

1\. Demonstrate that mainstream parties no longer compete along a simple "pro- vs. anti-integration" axis, but instead over varying models of pro-Europeanism. 2. Test the influence of economic (\`lrecon\`) and cultural (\`galt\`) party ideology on support for EU redistribution (Hypotheses 1 & 2) \[28–31, 45, 49\]. 3. Analyze the moderating effects of issue salience and a member state's net-contributor status (\`net_payer\`) (Hypotheses 3 & 4) \[31–35, 55–62\]. 4. Identify differences in party competition logic across four policy domains: fiscal, taxation, migration, and defense (Hypothesis 5) \[35–37, 63–69\].

## Scope

The research covers mainstream party positions across all 27–28 EU member states over four European Parliament elections (2009–2024).

## Key Provisions / Findings

- **Influence of Cultural and Economic Ideology:** Economically left-leaning parties (β = -0.08, p \< 0.01) and culturally progressive parties (β = -0.15, p \< 0.001) are significantly more supportive of the redistributive polity than right-leaning and conservative parties \[47–49\]. The marginal effect of the cultural dimension is larger than that of the economic dimension \[50–52\].
- **Net-Payer Constraint:** Being a net-contributing member state significantly suppresses party support for redistribution (β = -0.45, p \< 0.05) \[47, 59–60\]. This constraint primarily dampens support among centre-left parties from net-payer states, while culturally progressive parties continue to support redistribution regardless of net-payer status \[60–62\].
- **Policy Domain Asymmetry:**
  - *Fiscal & Taxation Policy (Material Domains):* Driven by a combination of economic and cultural ideology \[64–65\].
  - *Asylum & Migration Policy:* Driven **exclusively by the cultural dimension** (β_galt = -0.20, p \< 0.001), with the economic dimension statistically insignificant \[66, 68–69\].
  - *External Security & Defense Policy:* Shows an inverted pattern in which **mainstream right parties are significantly more supportive of European solidarity and burden-sharing than left parties** (β_lrecon = +0.36, p \< 0.001) \[66, 68–69, 73\].

## Implementation

The methodology was implemented through binary logistic regression models with country- and year-fixed effects \[43–48\]. Replication data and code are stored openly on Harvard Dataverse at \`https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/23IQZB\`.

## Policy Issues

1\. The risk of deadlock within EU institutions as the divide between redistributive and regulatory models sharpens. 2. The electoral burden faced by left-wing parties in net-contributor states, who must balance the principle of solidarity against domestic voter concerns \[34, 60–62\].

## Reform / Implications

The study demonstrates that voters in European elections have genuine programmatic choices among distinct pro-European models, and it opens the door to new coalition possibilities in shaping EU solidarity policy.
