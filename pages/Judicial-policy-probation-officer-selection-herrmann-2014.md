# Judicial-policy-probation-officer-selection-herrmann-2014

**The Selecting and Hiring Psychologically Fit Probation Officers Policy Framework** is a judicial human resources and psychological evaluation framework formulated by D. Scott Herrmann, Ph.D., ABPP (Superior Court of Arizona) and Scott Bedwell, Ph.D. (PDRI/CEB). Based on the predictive validity testing of the *PsychEval Personality Questionnaire / Protective Services Report Plus* (PEPQ / PSR Plus) instrument on 262 probation officer candidates (202 pre-employment candidates and 60 incumbents applying for firearms authorization), it aims to predict the risk of critical incidents, separate the probation function from pure policing, and strengthen accountability standards in court administration risk management.

## Summary

Published in the *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA) Vol. 6 No. 1 (June 2014), this research provides the first empirical predictive evidence regarding the reliability of specialized psychological testing tools for prospective court probation officers. Given the rapid growth of probation services in the US and Europe and the expanding authority for probation officers to carry firearms (operational in 83 of 94 U.S. federal judicial districts), the study emphasizes that police selection instruments cannot be directly equated with probation. Through a logistic regression model on 262 candidates in Arizona, the PEPQ / PSR Plus instrument proved significantly capable of predicting critical incidents and poor performance.

## Legal Basis

- **EEOC Enforcement Guidance on Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA 1995, 1997, 2000):** Regulates that psychological evaluations measuring pathological dimensions (such as Part II of the PEPQ) are only legally permissible after a candidate receives a conditional offer of employment.
- **Peace Officer Standards & Training Board (P.O.S.T.) Certification:** The standard provisions for certifying law enforcement officers in U.S. states.
- **Federal Judicial Districts Firearms Authorization Policy:** Provisions in 83 out of 94 federal judicial districts that grant firearms authorization to probation officers.
- **Other Specific Formal Statutory Regulations:** Not found in the available sources.

## Background

The expansion of probation services in the US and Europe has not been matched by the availability of scientific research on specialized psychological evaluations for probation officers. This gap poses a high risk because the failure to screen out psychologically unfit candidates can endanger the safety of the courts and the public.

## Objectives

1\. Empirically test the predictive validity of the PEPQ / PSR Plus instrument to screen out unfit probation officer candidates. 2. Identify the functional and philosophical orientation differences between police officers and probation officers. 3. Provide an evidence-based psychological screening standard for court HR managers.

## Scope

This policy applies to judicial HR administration units, adult/juvenile probation agencies in the Superior Court of Arizona, the U.S. federal judicial system, and European judiciaries.

## Key Provisions

- **Primary Predictive Indicators of Critical Incidents:**
  - *Alienation and Perceptual Distortion (AP Scale):* Each 1-unit increase on the AP scale increases the risk of a critical incident by **36%**.
  - *Interpersonal Relations (IR Composite):* Each 1-unit increase on the IR composite scale decreases the risk of a critical incident by **62%**.
  - *Intellectual Efficiency (IE Composite):* Each 1-unit increase on the IE composite scale decreases the risk of a critical incident by **50%**.
- **Intellectual Protective Effect:** A high Intellectual Efficiency (IE) score (Sten 9–10) has been proven to buffer and neutralize the negative impact of perceptual distortion risks.
- **Blind Evaluation Procedure:** The evaluation involves 325 PEPQ items and a 50-minute clinical interview that does not provide direct feedback to the candidates.

## Implementation

- **Arizona Sample Testing:** Operationalized on 262 subjects (202 pre-employment candidates and 60 incumbent applicants for firearms authorization) with a critical incident evaluation base rate of 14.9%.
- **Informed Consent & Ethics:** All candidates signed informed consent documents for the use of anonymized data in judicial administration research.

## Policy Problems

- **The Danger of Generalizing Police Selection Tools:** The unsuitability of directly adopting police tests for probation due to the differing functions of rehabilitation versus law enforcement.
- **Consequences of Selection Failure:** The risk of severe violations and firearms misuse by unstable probation officers.

## Reform / Change

- Mandatory adoption of psychological screening instruments specifically validated for the probation population (*evidence-based selection*).
- Integration of *fitness for duty* psychological assessments for incumbents applying for firearms authorization.

### 3 Most Important Improvements

1\. **Mandatory Application of Probation-Specific Validated Psychological Screening Tests:** Cease the use of generic police psychological tests and mandate instruments with proven empirical predictive validity, such as the PEPQ / PSR Plus, for all prospective probation officers. 2. **Strict Procedural Compliance with EEOC/ADA Guidelines:** Ensure that psychological instruments measuring pathology indicators are solely operationalized after the issuance of a conditional offer of employment. 3. **Integration of Psychological Screening with Firearms Authorization Policies:** Require a psychological fitness for duty assessment for incumbent probation officers applying for authorization to carry a firearm to mitigate the risk of critical incidents in the community.
