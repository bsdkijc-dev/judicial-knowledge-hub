#### 1. Regulatory Framework & Institutional Isomorphism

- **\[SOURCE EVIDENCE\]:** International arbitration derives its legal authority from party autonomy (*perjanjian para pihak*) and global treaties, notably the 1958 New York Convention recognized across 170+ states. In contrast, state court litigation is governed by rigid national procedural codes (*hukum acara perdata*).
- **\[THEORETICAL INFERENCE\]:** In Institutional Theory terms, state court systems experience path dependence and coercive isomorphism, adhering strictly to statutory procedural steps that often cause multi-year delays (1–3+ years). Meanwhile, international arbitration exerts normative isomorphism across global commercial law, establishing market standards for case management (Case Management Conferences, 2-round brief caps).
- **\[ANALYTICAL SYNTHESIS\]:** Indonesian court-annexed mediation under PERMA No. 1 of 2016 suffers from institutional decoupling—where mediation is treated as a routine checklist item rather than a managed, evaluative negotiation. Replicating arbitration case management within Indonesian court reform (RaPerMA) requires giving judges statutory authority to conduct early Case Management Conferences (CMC) that lock trial schedules and limit brief exchanges.

#### 2. Judicial / Institutional Structure & Organizational Capacity (5M)

Applying the 5M Framework to Attorney Tsuru Ayako's presentation highlights the structural mechanics that make managed dispute resolution efficient:

Man (Human Capital & Professional Roles)  

- **\[SOURCE EVIDENCE\]:** Parties in arbitration select specialized domain experts (*arbiter yang ahli*). In state courts, generalist judges decide cases based on general legal expertise.
- **\[THEORETICAL INFERENCE\]:** In Organizational Sociology, domain specialization reduces the cognitive load on adjudicators, speeding up factual understanding in complex corporate or technical disputes.

Method (Procedural Methodology)  

- **\[SOURCE EVIDENCE\]:** Arbitration enforces a strict 5-stage case management pipeline:
  1.  **Case Management Conference (CMC)** establishing a non-negotiable procedural schedule.
  2.  **Pleading Limits:** Maximum 2 rounds of brief exchanges (Statement of Claim/Defense, Reply/Rejoinder).
  3.  **Simultaneous Submission:** All documentary evidence and written witness statements must accompany the pleadings.
  4.  **Chess-Clock Time Management:** Strict minute-by-minute time caps allocated to each party for oral argument and cross-examination.
  5.  **Prioritized Cross-Examination:** Eliminating direct examination in favor of written witness statements, focusing oral hearings entirely on cross-examination.

Material (Case Files & Transcripts)  

- **\[SOURCE EVIDENCE\]:** Document production is strictly limited (no US-style broad discovery), preventing fishing expeditions.

Money (Trial Economy & Cost Structures)  

- **\[SOURCE EVIDENCE\]:** While arbitration incurs arbitrator and institutional fees, resolving claims within 6–18 months eliminates the long-term legal costs associated with multi-year court appeals.

Machine / Technology (Digital Infrastructure)  

- **\[SOURCE EVIDENCE\]:** Hearing efficiency is maximized through Live Notes (real-time screen sharing of spoken transcriptions) and remote witness examination via Zoom.

**Legal Culture & Social Structure:**

- **\[SOURCE EVIDENCE\]:** Commercial entities increasingly adopt Arb-Med-Arb protocols—initiating arbitration, pausing for evaluative mediation/caucusing, and either locking the settlement as a consent award or returning seamlessly to arbitration.
- **\[THEORETICAL INFERENCE\]:** This reflects a commercial legal culture that prioritizes relational preservation, confidentiality, and business continuity over public courtroom conflict.

**Street-Level Bureaucracy vs. Judicial/Arbitral Discretion:**

- **\[THEORETICAL INFERENCE\]:** Applying Lipsky's Street-Level Bureaucracy framework requires distinguishing frontline court staff from adjudicators:
  - **Frontline Administrative Staff (*Panitera*/Clerks):** Handle procedural intake, manage e-Court portals, and enforce filing fees. Their discretion is strictly administrative.
  - **Arbitrators / Judges:** Exercise curial discretion. In arbitration, tribunals exercise active case management discretion during CMCs to enforce strict pleading deadlines and chess-clock hearing caps. When facilitating settlement (*Wakai* / Arb-Med-Arb), adjudicators must maintain strict ethical boundaries, prohibiting ex parte communications to protect neutrality and prevent bias allegations.

#### 4. Implementation & Business Experience

- **\[SOURCE EVIDENCE\]:** Unmanaged litigation resulting in 1–3+ year court battles creates severe commercial uncertainty, high legal overheads, and enforcement hurdles across foreign jurisdictions.
- **\[THEORETICAL INFERENCE\]:** By establishing early procedural roadmaps, capping brief rounds, enforcing chess-clock hearing limits, and adopting Arb-Med-Arb settlement mechanisms, dispute systems deliver high regulatory predictability, reduced administrative burdens, and strong contract enforcement certainty for the business community.

### C. Synthesis of Evidence vs. Epistemological Uncertainty

**What is Firmly Supported by Source Evidence:**

- International arbitration resolves disputes faster (6–18 months) than cross-border state litigation (1–3+ years).
- The 1958 New York Convention guarantees cross-border enforcement across 170+ states.
- Efficient case management relies on early Case Management Conferences (CMCs) establishing fixed procedural schedules.
- Pleading rounds are capped (typically 2 rounds: Claim/Defense, Reply/Rejoinder) with simultaneous evidence submission.
- Hearing efficiency is achieved via chess-clock time limits, prioritized cross-examination, Live Notes transcription, and remote Zoom hearings.
- Arb-Med-Arb allows evaluative mediation during arbitration, provided ex parte communications are restricted to preserve adjudicator neutrality.
- Attorney Tsuru Ayako defines 5 core principles for case examination management.

**Explicit Uncertainty / Source Limits (\[UNCERTAIN\]):**

- **Specific Indonesian District Court Statistics:** Source *D3.AM_Manajemen pemeriksaan perkara (Attorney Tsuru).pdf* provides international arbitration standards and comparative litigation frameworks, but does not contain specific quantitative disposition statistics for Indonesian District Courts (*Pengadilan Negeri*).
- **Non-Judicial Administrative Licensing:** The source focuses exclusively on dispute resolution, arbitration, case management, and court-connected settlement; it contains no data on non-court business licensing software (e.g., OSS RBA) or regulatory agencies in Indonesia.

### D. Policy Recommendations for Indonesian Civil Procedure Reform (RaPerMA)

Based on Attorney Tsuru Ayako's analysis of international arbitration and case management best practices:

1.  **Mandate Pre-Trial Case Management Conferences (CMC):** Amend Supreme Court Regulations (RaPerMA) to require judges to conduct an early CMC with litigating counsel to establish a fixed, non-negotiable trial calendar.
2.  **Cap Pleading Rounds & Require Simultaneous Evidence:** Limit written exchanges to a maximum of two rounds (*Gugatan/Jawaban* and *Replik/Duplik*), requiring all documentary evidence and written witness statements to be submitted upfront.
3.  **Introduce Time-Capped Hearing Controls:** Adopt chess-clock time management for witness examinations to eliminate piecemeal court hearings and reduce trial duration.
4.  **Legitimize Digital Hearing Technologies:** Codify real-time transcription tools (Live Notes) and remote video witness examinations (e-Court / Zoom) as standard civil trial procedures.
5.  **Institutionalize Arb-Med-Arb Frameworks:** Establish clear statutory rules allowing courts to suspend litigation for structured, evaluative conciliation while protecting adjudicator neutrality.
