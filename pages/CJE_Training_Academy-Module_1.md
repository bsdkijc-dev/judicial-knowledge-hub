## Core Definition

**Module 1: Cybercrime & Digital Evidence Track** is the foundational training and knowledge framework designed for judges, judicial policy analysts, and court legal officers to master the elements of computer-related offenses, extraterritorial cyber jurisdiction, preliminary digital evidence handling, and substantive penal interpretations under modern statutory frameworks.

- **Simple Definition:** The core judicial course and reference guide explaining how to handle cybercrime cases—from hacking, online fraud, and illegal data interception to cross-border cyber jurisdiction and basic digital evidence analysis.
- **Legal / Technical Definition:** A structured curriculum module standardizing judicial adjudication of offenses against the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of computer data and systems (cyber-dependent crimes) and traditional offenses facilitated by computer networks (cyber-enabled crimes), integrating statutory procedural thresholds with international cybercrime norms.
- **Plain-Language Explanation:** It provides judges with the legal concepts, technological understanding, and procedural tools needed to identify cybercrimes, handle digital evidence properly, and resolve complex jurisdictional questions.

## Why It Matters

- **Addresses Rapid Technological Evolution:** Equips the judiciary to adjudicate complex offenses involving ransomware, identity theft, unauthorized access, and automated cyberattacks.
- **Harmonizes Substantive and Procedural Penal Codes:** Integrates cyber offenses defined in the UU ITE (Law No. 11/2008 jo. Law No. 1/2024) with the modernized general provisions of the New Criminal Code (Law No. 1/2023 / KUHP Nasional).
- **Resolves Borderless Cyber Jurisdiction:** Provides clear legal mechanisms for handling digital offenses where the perpetrator, infrastructure (cloud servers), and victims reside across multiple sovereign jurisdictions.
- **Protects Due Process in Digital Investigations:** Ensures that law enforcement seizures of digital devices and network traffic comply with human rights protections and judicial warrant requirements.

## Key Principles

Principle of Dual Classification (Cyber-Dependent vs. Cyber-Enabled Crimes)  

- **Meaning:** Distinguishing between crimes that can only be committed using computers and networks (e.g., malware injection, DDoS attacks) and traditional crimes augmented by digital technologies (e.g., online defamation, online fraud, illicit digital trade).
- **Why It Matters:** Determines the appropriate statutory basis, forensic complexity, and evidentiary requirements.
- **Legal Basis / Source:** Budapest Convention on Cybercrime; Law No. 11/2008 jo. Law No. 1/2024 Arts. 27–35.
- **Related Concepts:** System interference, data tampering, digital fraud.

Principle of Ubiquity and Extraterritorial Cyber Jurisdiction (Asas Ekstrateritorialitas & Asas Ubikuitas)  

- **Meaning:** The authority of domestic courts extends to any cybercrime committed outside national borders if the offense produces legal impacts within the jurisdiction or harms national interests/citizens.
- **Why It Matters:** Prevents cybercriminals from evading prosecution by routing malicious traffic through foreign proxies or cloud hosting providers.
- **Legal Basis / Source:** Law No. 11/2008 Art. 2; Law No. 1/2023 Arts. 4–8; KUHP Nasional Jurisdiction Principles.
- **Related Concepts:** Forum non conveniens, Mutual Legal Assistance (MLA), locus delicti.

Principle of Non-Alteration and Digital Volatility (Prinsip Keutuhan Data Volatil)  

- **Meaning:** Digital evidence is inherently fragile and volatile (e.g., RAM data, active connections); actions taken to secure digital crime scenes must minimize data destruction or modification.
- **Why It Matters:** Ensures the admissibility and reliability of digital evidence presented at trial.
- **Legal Basis / Source:** ISO/IEC 27037; SEMA Kamar Pidana; Law No. 1/2024 Art. 5.
- **Related Concepts:** Live forensics, write-blocking, cryptographic hash integrity.

Principle of Mens Rea and Intentionality in Automated Systems (Sikap Batin Pelaku Siber)  

- **Meaning:** Proving that unauthorized access, interception, or alteration was committed intentionally (*dengan sengaja*) and without lawful right (*tanpa hak / melawan hukum*), distinguishing malicious acts from inadvertent system glitches.
- **Why It Matters:** Protects legitimate cybersecurity researchers, IT administrators, and regular users from unwarranted criminal liability.
- **Legal Basis / Source:** Law No. 11/2008 Arts. 30–33; Law No. 1/2023 General Provisions on Culpability.
- **Related Concepts:** Unauthorized access, lawful authority, ethical hacking.

## Indonesia Section

- **Substantive Cybercrime Categories under UU ITE:**
  - *Illegal Content (Konten Ilegal - Arts. 27, 28, 29):* Distribution and transmission of content violating decency, gambling, defamation, extortion, hate speech (SARA), and cyber harassment.
  - *Unauthorized Access (Akses Ilegal - Art. 30):* Unlawfully accessing computer systems, breaching security perimeters (hacking/cracking).
  - *Illegal Interception (Penyadapan Ilegal - Art. 31):* Intercepting or listening to electronic transmissions or non-public communications without authorization.
  - *Data & System Interference (Gangguan Data dan Sistem - Arts. 32 & 33):* Altering, deleting, hiding, damaging computer data, or disrupting computer networks (e.g., malware, ransomware, DDoS).
  - *Facilitation & Misuse of Devices (Penyalahgunaan Perangkat - Art. 34):* Producing, selling, or distributing hardware/software designed for cybercrimes.
  - *Electronic Manipulation & Forgery (Manipulasi Dokumen Elektronik - Art. 35):* Creating or altering electronic documents to pass them off as authentic.
- **Interplay with Law No. 1/2023 (KUHP Nasional):**
  - KUHP Nasional integrates foundational cyber offenses into the general criminal code, establishing clear sentencing principles, alternative sanctions, and refined liability standards for legal entities/corporations committing cyber offenses.
- **Institutional Ecosystem:**
  - Adjudicated by District Court panels with specialized certification for cyber and electronic evidence, supported by the Directorate of Cyber Crime (Dittipidsiber Bareskrim Polri), BSSN (National Cyber and Crypto Agency), and accredited digital forensic centers (Puslabfor).

## Important Decisions & Jurisprudential Restatements

Decision  
Supreme Court Plenary Guidance on Online Defamation Restraints

- **Issuing Body:** Mahkamah Agung RI (Criminal Chamber Plenary)
- **Instrument / SEMA Ref:** SEMA No. 1/2021 jo. SKB Pedoman Implementasi UU ITE (2021) & SEMA No. 3/2023
- **Legal Principle:** Article 27(3) \[now amended under Law No. 1/2024\] applies strictly to natural persons (not institutions or legal entities); public interest criticism or performance evaluations of public institutions cannot be criminalized as cyber defamation.
- **Why It Matters:** Protects constitutional free speech and prevents arbitrary criminalization.
- **Official Source:** Supreme Court Chamber Reports.

Decision  
Precedential Ruling on Unauthorized System Access and Malware Deployment

- **Issuing Body:** Mahkamah Agung RI
- **Case Context:** Unauthorized penetration of banking payment gateways and ransomware deployment.
- **Legal Principle:** Deliberately bypassing multi-factor authentication or cryptographic security measures constitutes an intentional violation of Article 30 jo. Article 33 UU ITE, establishing direct culpability without requiring physical hardware damage.
- **Why It Matters:** Affirms that logical/software disruption satisfies the material element of damage (\*kerusakan data/sistem\*).
- **Official Source:** Jurisprudensi Mahkamah Agung RI.

## Debates & Controversies

Debate  
Rubber Articles (\*Pasal Karet\*) vs. Digital Public Order Enforcement

- **Position A (Civil Liberties & Overcriminalization Critique):** Broad wording in cyber-content articles (e.g., hate speech, online defamation, public disturbance) risks creating chilling effects on democratic participation and journalistic freedom.
- **Position B (Public Security & Cyber Defense Advocates):** Strong, flexible statutory provisions are essential to combat coordinated disinformation campaigns, financial fraud networks, and deepfake manipulations.
- **Evidence:** Case statistics showing frequent use of cyber defamation provisions prior to the 2021 Joint Ministerial Decree (SKB Pedoman ITE) and the 2024 Second Amendment.
- **Current Status:** Harmonized under Law No. 1/2024: Defamation provisions are strictly aligned with standard KUHP definitions, requiring direct personal complaint (\*delik aduan absolut\*).

## Rule-of-Law Connection

- **Principle of Legality (\*Nullum Delictum Sine Lege\*):** Cyber offenses must be explicitly defined; courts must avoid overbroad interpretations that penalize common technical usage.
- **Proportionality in Penalties:** Distinguishing minor online infractions from high-impact infrastructure attacks ensures balanced, reasoned sentencing.
- **Procedural Due Process:** Search and seizure of digital servers, phones, and computers must adhere strictly to statutory search warrant procedures to protect user privacy.

## AI & Future of Law

- **AI-Assisted Cyberattacks:** Module 1 addresses the emerging criminal liability for autonomous malware, AI-driven social engineering, and automated phishing operations.
- **Jurisdiction over Decentralized Infrastructure:** Standards for determining \*locus delicti\* and accountability when offenses are committed across decentralized finance (DeFi) platforms or unhosted blockchain smart contracts.
- **Algorithmic Attribution Challenges:** Safeguards against attributing cyber offenses solely based on IP address tracking without corroborating forensic user authentication.

## Common Misconceptions

Myth  
Tracing an IP address to a specific household is sufficient proof that the homeowner committed the cybercrime.

- **Reality:** An IP address identifies a network connection endpoint, not a specific human actor. The prosecution must provide corroborating device-level evidence (user accounts, session logs, biometric access) to establish individual guilt beyond reasonable doubt.
- **Source:** Digital Forensic Standards; Supreme Court Benchbook Chapter 3.

Myth  
Cybercrimes committed outside Indonesia cannot be prosecuted in Indonesian courts.

- **Reality:** Under Article 2 of UU ITE and Articles 4–8 of the KUHP Nasional, Indonesian courts have extraterritorial jurisdiction if the offense produces adverse legal impacts within Indonesia or affects Indonesian citizens/critical infrastructure.
- **Source:** Law No. 11/2008 Art. 2; Law No. 1/2023.

## Practical Example

- **Scenario:** A perpetrator located abroad deploys phishing software targeting an Indonesian banking server, obtaining unauthorized access to user accounts and transferring IDR 2,000,000,000 to an offshore digital wallet.
- **Legal Question:** How should the court determine jurisdiction and classify the charges?
- **Applicable Principle:** Extraterritorial Jurisdiction & Cyber-Dependent/Cyber-Enabled Concurrence (UU ITE Art. 2, 30, 33, 35; KUHP Nasional).
- **Possible Analysis:**

1.  The court exercises jurisdiction based on the Principle of Impact (Ubiquity), as the victim server and financial harm occurred within Indonesia.
2.  The indictment properly combines unauthorized access (Art. 30), system interference (Art. 33), and electronic manipulation/fraud (Art. 35 UU ITE jo. KUHP).
3.  Digital evidence gathered via Mutual Legal Assistance (server logs from foreign hosting providers) is evaluated under Module 2 chain-of-custody standards.

- **Limitation:** Enforcement of foreign arrest or asset recovery requires bilateral Mutual Legal Assistance (MLA) or INTERPOL coordination.

## Key Terms

| Term                      | Definition                                                                                                                                                                          | Related Term                       |
|---------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------|
| **Cyber-Dependent Crime** | Offenses that can only be committed through the use of information and communication technology devices, where the device is both the tool and the target (e.g., malware, hacking). | Akses Ilegal, System Interference  |
| **Cyber-Enabled Crime**   | Traditional crimes (e.g., theft, fraud, harassment, defamation) that are scaled or facilitated through the use of computers and the internet.                                       | Penipuan Online, Konten Ilegal     |
| **Locus Delicti Siber**   | The legal determination of the place where a cybercrime occurred, encompassing the location of the perpetrator, the server/network infrastructure, and the place of impact.         | Asas Ubikuitas, Cyber Jurisdiction |
| **Volatile Data**         | Digital data stored in temporary computer memory (RAM, active network connections, cache) that is lost immediately when the device is powered down.                                 | Live Forensics, ISO/IEC 27037      |
