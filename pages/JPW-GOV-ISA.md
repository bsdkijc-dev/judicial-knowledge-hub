## Policy Analysis

### Background & Institutional Reorganization

- Reorganized from the Bureau of Immigration into the Immigration Services Agency (ISA) under the Ministry of Justice in April 2019.
- Mandated with comprehensive responsibility for planning, designing, and coordinating residency management and social integration policies for foreign nationals in Japan.

### Border Control & Digital Integration

- Integrated KIOSKs combining customs and immigration procedures to roll out progressively at Kansai (Terminals 1 & 2), Haneda (Terminals 2 & 3), and Narita (Terminal 3) airports.
- Facial recognition automated gates deployed for Japanese nationals and departing foreign residents.
- Bio-carts deployed to capture fingerprints and facial images during queue management.
- Simplified landing permission procedures implemented for cruise ship tourists.

### Residency Management & Foreign Labor Reforms

- Certificate of Eligibility (COE) issuance digitalized via email.
- Online residency procedures expanded using My Number Card identity verification.
- The 2024 Immigration Control Act amendment establishes the Employment-for-Skill-Development Program to replace the Technical Intern Training Program and optimize the Specified Skilled Worker System, effective April 2027.
- Preferential pathways for highly skilled talent expanded via the Japan System for Special Highly-Skilled Professionals (J-Skip) and the Japan System for Future Creation Individual Visa (J-Find).

### Social Integration & Support Services

- Accepting Environmental Coordinators appointed across regional offices to manage one-stop consultation counters.
- "Guidebook on Living and Working" published in 19 languages alongside Plain Japanese guidelines and daily life orientation materials.
- Foreign Residents Support Center (FRESC) operated in Yotsuya, Tokyo, consolidating 8 organizations across 4 government ministries/agencies.

### Deportation Enforcement & Detention Reforms

- The June 2024 full implementation of the amended Immigration Control Act addresses long-term detention and deportation evasion.
- Exceptions to deportation suspension: Individuals applying for refugee status 3 or more times (without new supporting evidence), persons sentenced to 3+ years imprisonment, and designated terrorists may be deported while applications are pending.
- Introduction of a non-detainees supervision (sponsorship) system with mandatory 3-month periodic reviews.
- Mandatory quarterly medical examinations for detainees and voluntary return assistance programs operated in partnership with the IOM.

### Refugee Protection & Complementary Protection

- Complementary Protection System launched on December 1, 2023, providing legal status to conflict-displaced persons who do not meet the 1951 Convention refugee criteria.
- "Guide to Eligibility Determination for Refugee Status" published alongside an MOC signed with UNHCR.
- Settlement support programs providing Japanese language courses and daily life guidance sessions.

### Interpretations & Structural Inferences

- Japanese immigration policy demonstrates a balanced strategy combining high-tech border efficiency, attraction of global talent (J-Skip, J-Find), structural labor reform, strict enforcement against illegal stay, and expanded humanitarian protection frameworks.
- Transitioning from the Technical Intern Training Program to the Employment-for-Skill-Development Program by April 2027 reflects a shift from temporary skill transfer to long-term foreign workforce retention to alleviate labor shortages.
