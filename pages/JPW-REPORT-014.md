# JPW-REPORT-014

**Report on Preliminary Examination Activities (2019)** is the official annual report published by the Office of the Prosecutor (OTP) of the International Criminal Court on 5 December 2019, summarizing the OTP's analysis and preliminary-examination activity concerning situations of international crime worldwide during the period 1 December 2018 to 30 November 2019, applying the Article 53(1) standard of the Rome Statute.

## Summary

The report presents the OTP's independent evaluation of communications and situations potentially warranting investigation of international crimes during the reporting period. It explains the application of the four-phase filtering framework and reports on progress in preliminary examinations across multiple regions.

## Legal Basis

- [Rome Statute](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Rome_Statute):
  - **Article 53(1)**: Legal framework for determining a reasonable basis to open an investigation, based on jurisdiction, admissibility, and the interests of justice.
  - **Articles 12 and 13**: Jurisdictional preconditions and triggering mechanisms (state/UNSC referral, Article 12(3) declaration, proprio motu under Article 15).
  - **Article 17**: Admissibility assessment based on complementarity and gravity.
- [Rules of Procedure and Evidence](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Rules_of_Procedure_and_Evidence): Rules 48 and 49.

## Background

Preliminary examination is the OTP's principal filtering mechanism for distinguishing situations that meet Rome Statute criteria from those that do not, while also encouraging genuine national proceedings.

## Objectives

- Ensure transparency and public accountability for OTP decisions.
- Apply a holistic analysis through the four-phase framework.
- Contribute to ending impunity and preventing crime.

## Key Provisions

**Four-Phase Filtering Process**:

- **Phase 1**: Initial assessment of Article 15 communications.
- **Phase 2**: Subject-matter jurisdiction assessment.
- **Phase 3**: Admissibility assessment (complementarity and gravity).
- **Phase 4**: Interests of justice assessment.

**2019 Phase 1 Closure Decisions**:

- **North Korea (Dual Nationality)**: Rejected the claim that North Korean nationals held South Korean nationality from birth for purposes of personal jurisdiction.
- **North Korea (Overseas Labourers)**: Rejected the qualification of enslavement under Article 7(1)(c) for North Korean overseas laborers in States Parties.
- **Philippines (South China Sea)**: Rejected territorial jurisdiction over the EEZ/continental shelf, which does not form part of the coastal state's "territory."
- **Yemen (Mercenaries)**: Found no evidence of involvement by State Party nationals as mercenaries in war crimes.

**Phase 2 Situation**: Venezuela (analysis of alleged crimes against humanity since 2017).

**Phase 3 Situations**: Colombia, Guinea, Iraq/UK, Nigeria, Palestine, Philippines, and Ukraine.

**Completed Situation / Investigation Authorized**: Bangladesh/Myanmar (authorized by Pre-Trial Chamber III on 14 November 2019).

## Implementation

The OTP reviewed 795 Article 15 communications, conducted field missions to Conakry, Abuja, Kyiv, and Cox's Bazar, and participated in appellate proceedings before the ICC.

## Evidence

- Received 795 Article 15 communications (617 manifestly outside jurisdiction, 112 related to ongoing preliminary examinations, 25 related to active investigations/prosecutions, and 41 requiring further Phase 1 analysis).
- Completed the Bangladesh/Myanmar preliminary examination, with PTC III authorizing investigation on 14 November 2019 into alleged forced deportation of the Rohingya people (an estimated 600,000 to 1 million victims).
- Determined that the EEZ is not "territory" under the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS) and the \*Island of Palmas\* case.
- Collected tens of thousands of items of evidence and witness interviews across multiple countries.

## Policy Issues

- Limits on the Court's territorial jurisdiction in EEZ/international waters.
- Assessing the genuineness of domestic investigations under the complementarity framework.
- Constraints on evidence collection in active armed-conflict zones.
