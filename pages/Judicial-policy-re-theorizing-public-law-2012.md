# Judicial-policy-re-theorizing-public-law-2012

The **Re-Theorizing Public Law Framework** (officially known as the *Framework for Re-Theorizing Public Law and the Italian Public-Private Law Divide*) is a policy analysis and public law theory framework designed to evaluate the boundaries of the public-private law divide, the legitimacy of administrative justice, the return of state economic intervention following financial crises, and the integration of legal analysis with social and economic sciences [^1].

## Overview

Authored by [Giacinto della Cananea](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Giacinto_della_Cananea), Professor of Administrative Law at the [University of Rome "Tor Vergata"](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/University_of_Rome_%22Tor_Vergata%22), this editorial addresses the ongoing debate over the distinctive nature of public law in Italy, the European Union, and the United Kingdom [^2]. It highlights school-of-thought developments in Italy that deny the distinct character of state actions (such as grants and subsidies) and challenge the dualistic administrative justice system under the assertion that only a monistic judicial review system aligns with the Rule of Law [^3].

## Legal Basis

Rather than relying on specific statutory acts, the framework grounds itself in fundamental legal principles:

- **[Rule of Law Principles](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Rule_of_Law_Principles)**: Serves as the benchmark for debating the jurisdiction of administrative judges versus monistic judicial review systems [^4].
- **[Basic Standards of Public Life](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Basic_Standards_of_Public_Life)**: Encompasses the core principles of legality, fairness, rationality in decision-making, and the quality of public utilities [^5].

## Background

The global economic and financial crisis created new momentum for evaluating the erosion of the traditional boundary between public and private law [^6]. Contrary to predictions regarding the demise of public law, post-crisis developments revealed the "return of the State" as a major investor and owner in key economic sectors, evidenced by massive state financial support for the banking industry [^7].

## Objectives

- Evaluate whether Italian public law satisfies the basic standards of public life recognized across European jurisdictions [^8].
- Re-theorize 21st-century public law and examine its relationship with private law, economics, and social sciences [^9].
- Provide a platform for critical interdisciplinary dialogue via symposia hosted by the *Italian Journal of Public Law* [^10].

## Scope

The scope encompasses Italian public law, administrative jurisdiction, state intervention in the economy, and cross-disciplinary studies between law and economics across Italy, the European Union, and the United Kingdom [^11].

## Key Provisions & Doctrinal Debates

- **Denial of Authoritative State Actions**: Addressing legal trends in Italy that attempt to minimize or reject grants and subsidies as authoritative acts of public power [^12].
- **Challenges to Administrative Courts**: Critiquing arguments against the jurisdiction of administrative judges and the assertion that monistic judicial review is uniquely coherent with the Rule of Law [^13].
- **Return of State Intervention**: Demonstrating that financial crises have reinforced the role of the state as an economic actor rather than extinguishing public law [^14].

## Implementation

Operationalized through scientific symposia organized by the *Italian Journal of Public Law* (IJPL) to test the fitness of Italian public law for the 21st century, alongside the publication of interdisciplinary quantitative and economic studies on public accounting and regulation [^15].

## Policy Issues

- **Erosion of Public and Private Law Boundaries**: Categorization challenges regarding state intervention in modern financial markets [^16].
- **Alignment with European Standards**: Ensuring that public accountability, administrative procedures, and public accounts presentation in Italy remain aligned with European integration standards [^17].

## Proposed Reforms

Recommends gradual adjustments to public rules, administrative procedures, and oversight mechanisms to safeguard public trust in market operators and ensure transparency in public financial reporting [^18].
