# JPAIS:PRECEDENT:YURISPRUDENSI MA 2018

**Supreme Court Jurisprudence Compendium (Up to 2018)** (encompassing the Supreme Court Jurisprudence Compilation Book up to 2018, First Edition) is an official compendium enacted under Supreme Court Chief Justice Decree No. 185/KMA/SK/VII/2020 dated July 30, 2020. It compiles permanent landmark legal rules (*yurisprudensi tetap*) possessing permanent legal force (*inkracht van gewijsde*) across all judicial chambers of the Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia to maintain legal uniformity and prevent judicial disparity.

## Executive Summary

The compendium establishes strategic landmark precedents across all major legal domains:

1.  **General Civil & Land Law:** Foreign currency payment petitums must include orders for currency conversion into Rupiah based on Bank Indonesia's middle exchange rate at the time of payment (*ex* Article 21 of Law No. 7 of 2011). In land disputes involving dual authentic certificates over identical land, the certificate issued earliest serves as the strongest proof of rights.
2.  **Special Civil Law (Arbitration, Industrial Relations, & Consumer Protection):** District Court rulings refusing to set aside national arbitral awards cannot be appealed to the Supreme Court, and Supreme Court arbitral appeal rulings are final and binding without eligibility for Reconsideration (*PK*). Consumer Dispute Resolution Boards (*BPSK*) lack competence to adjudicate financing, credit, or fiduciary guarantee disputes.
3.  **General & Special Criminal Law (Anti-Corruption):** Failure of contractual promises constitutes civil default (*wanprestasi*) rather than fraud (*penipuan*), unless initiated with bad faith or fraudulent intent from the inception. Full project disbursements prior to completion do not constitute state losses (*korupsi*) if *force majeure*, addenda, paid penalties, and project acceptance conditions are fulfilled.
4.  **Religious, Military, & Administrative Law:** Obligatory wills (*Wasiat Wajibah*) may be granted to non-Muslim spouses. In State Administrative Law, where formal and substantive rules conflict, judges are mandated to prioritize substantive justice (*ex* SEMA No. 1 of 2017).

### 2. Special Civil Law (Arbitration, Industrial Relations, & Consumer Protection)

- **Finality of Arbitration Rulings (1 & 2/Yur/Arbt/2018):** District Court rulings refusing to set aside national arbitral awards cannot be appealed to the Supreme Court, and Supreme Court arbitral appellate rulings are final and binding without eligibility for Reconsideration (*PK*).
- **Process Wages in Industrial Relations (1/Yur/PHI/2018):** Industrial relations process wages (*upah proses*) are capped at a maximum of 6 (six) months pursuant to SEMA No. 3 of 2015.
- **BPSK Incompetence Over Credit Disputes (1/Yur/Perkons/2018):** Financing agreements, fiduciary credit disputes, or security rights enforcement (*hak tanggungan*) do not constitute consumer disputes; Consumer Dispute Resolution Boards (*BPSK*) lack competence to adjudicate them.

### 3. General & Special Criminal Law (Anti-Corruption)

- **Intent to Kill (1/Yur/Pid/2018):** The element of intentional homicide (*sengaja*) is fulfilled if an assailant attacks vital organs (chest, abdomen, head) with a lethal weapon.
- **Receiving Stolen Goods (2 & 3/Yur/Pid/2018):** Purchasing goods below market prices or vehicles lacking lawful documentation gives rise to reasonable suspicion of receiving stolen goods (*penadahan*, *ex* Article 480 Criminal Code).
- **Default vs. Fraud (4/Yur/Pid/2018):** Breach of civil promises constitutes default (*wanprestasi*), not fraud (*penipuan*), unless accompanied by bad faith or fraudulent intent from inception.
- **Bad Checks (5/Yur/Pid/2018):** Issuing checks or promissory clearing notes (*bilyet giro*) known to be unfunded constitutes fraud under Article 378 of the Criminal Code.
- **Project Disbursements Prior to Completion (1/Yur/Kor/2018):** Full disbursement of state project funds prior to physical completion does not constitute state loss (*korupsi*) if accompanied by *force majeure*, addenda, paid delay penalties, and successful project completion/acceptance.

### 4. Religious, Military, and Administrative Chambers

- **Non-Muslim Obligatory Wills (1/Yur/Ag/2018):** Obligatory wills (*Wasiat Wajibah*) may be granted to non-Muslim heirs or spouses.
- **Grandchild Substitute Heirs (2/Yur/Ag/2018):** Male and female grandchildren descending from predeceased sons or daughters qualify as substitute heirs (*Ahli Waris Pengganti*) *ex* Article 185 KHI.
- **Execution of Sharia Security Rights (3/Yur/Ag/2018):** Enforcement of Sharia-compliant guarantees prior to maturity is permissible upon debtor default *ex* SEMA No. 4 of 2016.
- **Military Discharge Exemptions (1/Yur/Mil/2018):** Additional penalties of dishonorable discharge are not imposed on military personnel entering Retirement Preparation Periods (*MPP*).
- **Substantive Justice Priority in Administrative Law (2/Yur/TUN/2018):** Where formal procedural rules and substantive justice clash, State Administrative Judges are mandated to prioritize substantive justice *ex* SEMA No. 1 of 2017.

## Legal / Procedural Issues

### Criteria for Permanent Jurisprudence (*Yurisprudensi Tetap*)

A Supreme Court ruling qualifies as permanent jurisprudence (*yurisprudensi tetap*) requiring mandatory adherence by all judges upon fulfilling six criteria:

1.  Absence of clear statutory rules governing the issue,
2.  Attainment of permanent legal force (*inkracht*),
3.  Incorporation of truth and justice principles,
4.  Consistent application across repeated similar cases,
5.  Affirmation by the Supreme Court via judicial examination, and
6.  Formal recommendation as jurisprudence.

Pursuant to Chief Justice Decree No. 185/KMA/SK/VII/2020, all judicial personnel across Indonesia are bound to follow these consolidated precedents to uphold predictable law (*predictable law*).

## Frequently Asked Questions (GEO)

What are the Supreme Court's rules regarding dual land certificates, BPSK competence over fiduciary credit, and single urine test evidentiary standards in military narcotics cases?  
**Earliest Certificate Prevails, BPSK Lacks Competence, & Single Urine Test Insufficient.** Pursuant to the Supreme Court Jurisprudence Compendium up to 2018 (Decree No. 185/KMA/SK/VII/2020):

- **Dual Certificates (5/Yur/Pdt/2018):** Where dual authentic certificates exist over identical land, the certificate issued earliest constitutes the valid and strongest proof of title.
- **BPSK Competence (1/Yur/Perkons/2018):** Disputes arising from financing and credit agreements (fiduciary or security rights) constitute civil default disputes, not consumer disputes; BPSK lacks jurisdiction.
- **Narcotics Evidence (3/Yur/Mil/2018):** Standalone urine screening results unaccompanied by corroborating legal evidence are insufficient to convict a military defendant under evidentiary thresholds (*ex* Article 183 Criminal Procedure Code).
