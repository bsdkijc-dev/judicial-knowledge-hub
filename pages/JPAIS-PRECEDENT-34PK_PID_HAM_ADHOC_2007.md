## Overview

Supreme Court Judicial Review (\*Peninjauan Kembali / PK\*) Decision No. 34 PK/PID.HAM.AD HOC/2007 dated March 14, 2008, acquits \[Defendant\] (Former Deputy Commander of the Pro-Integration Fighters / PPI) of all charges of gross human rights violations in the form of crimes against humanity. The Supreme Court affirmed that clashes or attacks at the residence of Manuel Viegas Carrascalao on April 17, 1999, constituted spontaneous riots devoid of widespread or systematic planning, and that the Petitioner for PK, as a civilian superior, lacked \*de jure\* or \*de facto\* effective authority (\*chain of command\*) to prevent or halt the actions of militia mobs belonging to other factions (such as Besi Merah Putih / BMP).

## Case Background

The Petitioner for PK / Convict (\[Defendant\]) was charged with omission (\*omissions\*) regarding the militia mob attack on the residence of Manuel Viegas Carrascalao in Dili on April 17, 1999, which resulted in the deaths of 12 civilians following a mass rally by PAM Swakarsa. The Ad Hoc Human Rights Court at the Central Jakarta District Court sentenced the Petitioner to 10 years of imprisonment (Decision No. 04/PID.HAM/AD.HOC/2002/PN.JKT.PST). The Ad Hoc High Human Rights Court of Jakarta reduced the sentence to 5 years of imprisonment (Decision No. 02/PID.HAM/AD.HOC/2004/PT.DKI). However, upon cassation, the Supreme Court annulled the appellate ruling and reinstated the 10-year prison sentence (Decision No. 06 K/PID.HAM AD HOC/2005). The Petitioner filed for Judicial Review (\*PK\*) incorporating new witness testimonies (\*novum\* from Eduardo De Yesus, Boa Ventura, Victor Pilipe, and Dominggus Bondia) alongside the acquittal ruling of former East Timor Governor Abilio Osorio Soares as a \*novum\*.

## Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

- **Ratio Decidendi:**

1.  Disputes, clashes, or riots occurring spontaneously without detailed planning do not fulfill the elements of an organizational policy to commit widespread or systematic crimes against humanity.
2.  Individuals or groups involved or participating in disputes, clashes, or riots cannot be categorized as "civilian populations."
3.  The criminal responsibility of a civilian superior for the acts of subordinates committing crimes against humanity (\*omission\*) must be grounded upon the existence of \*de jure\* or \*de facto\* authority possessing a genuinely effective leadership hierarchy (\*chain of command\*), akin to military hierarchies. The Petitioner for PK, acting as a civilian superior, did not hold effective \*de jure/de facto\* authority over members of opposing militia factions (BMP) carrying out the attack.

- **Ruling:** Granting the petition for judicial review from Petitioner for PK \[Defendant\]; Annulling the Supreme Court Decision No. 06 K/PID.HAM AD HOC/2005 jo. Ad Hoc High Human Rights Court Decision No. 02/PID.HAM/AD.HOC/2004/PT.DKI jo. Ad Hoc Human Rights Court at Central Jakarta District Court Decision No. 04/PID.HAM/AD.HOC/2002/PN.JKT.PST; and Re-Adjudicating: (1) Declaring that Petitioner for PK \[Defendant\] is not legally and convincingly proven guilty of committing the criminal offense charged by the Public Prosecutor; (2) Acquitting him of all charges; (3) Restoring the Convict's rights in capacity, position, honor, and dignity (rehabilitation); (4) Charging procedural costs across all levels of jurisdiction to the State.
