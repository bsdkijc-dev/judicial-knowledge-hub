# Judicial-policy-iraq-reconstruction-2003

**The Establishing Justice In Iraq & Post-Conflict Judicial Assessment Framework** is a policy framework and empirical analysis of the reconstruction of Iraq's criminal and civil justice systems following the 2003 coalition invasion. Compiled by a 13-member US legal assessment team (including judges, prosecutors, public defenders, and court administrator Luther D. "Dan" Thomas) under the *Coalition Provisional Authority* (CPA), it diagnoses the destruction of judicial infrastructure and the legacy of Ba'ath Party corruption and manipulation. It establishes emergency human rights safeguards and sets forth 14 judicial reform recommendations \[1–3, 15, 24–27, 34–40\].

## Summary

Published by Luther D. “Dan” Thomas in the *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA), this policy framework documents the journey of the US Department of Justice Judicial Assessment Mission to Iraq in May–June 2003. Operating under the *Coalition Provisional Authority* (CPA) led by Ambassador L. Paul Bremer, the team found total paralysis of court buildings due to mass looting, alongside a dominant practice of torture and corruption inherited from Saddam Hussein's regime \[1–4, 15, 24\]. The team successfully formulated four emergency human rights safeguards that were immediately enacted by the CPA, and submitted 14 structural reform recommendations covering judicial *vetting*, the reform of the inquisitorial system, legal aid for the poor, and an administrative land claims procedure \[25, 27, 34–40\].

## Legal Basis

- **Geneva Convention:** Authorized the Coalition Forces/CPA to restore public order and conduct the removal of Ba'ath Party officials for security purposes.
- **CPA Orders & Decrees (May 2003):** The De-Ba'athification Decree and the order establishing four *Emergency Human Rights Safeguards* by Amb. L. Paul Bremer.
- **1925 & 1970 Iraqi Constitutions:** The foundation of the Iraqi civil law system, the separation of powers, and the establishment of the Council of Judges.
- **Code of Hammurabi (1700 BC):** The historical reference for the oldest written law in the Mesopotamian region.

## Background

Following the 2003 coalition invasion, Iraq experienced a severe legal vacuum due to Saddam Hussein's release of thousands of violent criminals and the disbandment of the security apparatus. Courthouses were looted down to their electrical wiring. A US DOJ assessment team was dispatched to evaluate the condition of 12 Courts of Appeal and the Court of Cassation, and to design the restoration of the *Rule of Law* \[1–4, 9, 10\].

## Objectives

1\. Diagnose the physical and institutional damage to the Iraqi justice system across four main regions. 2. Halt human rights violations in criminal investigations (such as torture and the denial of the right to remain silent). 3. Cleanse the judicial system of Ba'ath Party politicization and corruption. 4. Draft a framework for infrastructure reconstruction, salary increases, and legal aid \[36–39\].

## Scope

This policy evaluates all levels of the judiciary in Iraq: the Court of Cassation, 12 Courts of Appeal (in Baghdad, Basrah, Mosul, Hillah, Kirkuk, Irbil, Najaf, Nasiriyah), Courts of First Instance, Criminal Courts, Special Courts (Personal Status, Labor, Juvenile), and the dissolution of the Revolutionary Courts \[8–13\].

## Key Provisions

- **Four Emergency Human Rights Safeguards (Bremer Safeguards):**

1.  *Absolute Ban on Torture Confessions:* The absolute prohibition on the use of confessions obtained through torture.
2.  *Advice of Rights:* The obligation to advise suspects of their rights according to international standards upon arrest.
3.  *Right to Counsel Since Investigation:* The right to be accompanied by a lawyer from the initial investigation stage by the investigating judge.
4.  *Right to Remain Silent:* The guarantee of the right to remain silent at all stages of the criminal justice process.

- **The "Baghdad is Silent" Phenomenon:** The passive attitude of judges who were reluctant to hold trials because they were accustomed to waiting for bureaucratic instructions from the Ministry of Justice.
- **Low Pay & Systemic Corruption:** Public defenders were only paid \$2–\$3 per serious criminal case, driving widespread bribery at the lower levels \[18–20\].

## Implementation

- **Distribution of Emergency Advocate Funds:** The distribution of over \$80,000 in coalition funds by Dan Thomas to Iraqi lawyers who were assisting in the search for victims of abduction/enforced disappearance.
- **Dissolution of Revolutionary Courts:** The abolition of Ba'ath political courts in the summer of 2003.
- **Reorganization of the Inquisitorial System:** The transfer of partial authority from the *Investigating Judge* to the *Public Prosecutor* to ensure an independent prosecution function.

## Policy Problems

- **Mass Land Disputes:** The forced displacement of ethnic Kurds and Shia by the Ba'ath regime created dual ownership claims that could not be resolved by ordinary civil courts.
- **Security Threats to Personnel:** The rampant terrorism attacks against court facilities and the assassination of local translators.

## Reform / Change

- Implementation of the 14 structural reform recommendations for the Iraqi judiciary \[34–40\].
- Increased salary incentives for prosecutors to equal those of judges to eradicate corruption.

### 3 Most Important Improvements

1\. **Establishment of a Special Administrative Property Claims Procedure:** Implement the team's recommendation to create an administrative tribunal/judicial body for property disputes to resolve dual ownership claims without overburdening ordinary civil courts. 2. **Alignment of Public Prosecutor and Investigating Judge Authority:** Restructure the Iraqi Code of Criminal Procedure to transfer partial investigative authority from the investigating judge to the public prosecutor to ensure independent and effective prosecution. 3. **Enhancement of Security Guarantees and Judicial Staff Salary Incentives:** Allocate permanent emergency funding for the arming of court facilities and raise the salaries of prosecutors and court staff to equal those of judges to eradicate corruption.
