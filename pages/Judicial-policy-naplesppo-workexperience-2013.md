# Judicial-policy-naplesppo-workexperience-2013

# 'Knowledge Transfer As A Leverage Of Change: The Case of the Public Prosecutor Office of Naples' (Piana, 2013)

### Wiki Entity Page

**Program "Work Experiences" (WEP) at the Public Prosecutor's Office of Naples** (*Procura della Repubblica di Napoli*) is an organizational innovation program based on knowledge transfer implemented in the first semester of 2013 through a partnership between the Public Prosecutor's Office of Naples and the University of Naples Federico II (via COINOR) with funding support from the *European Social Fund* (ESF) through the Campania Regional Government. This program was scientifically analyzed by Dr. Daniela Piana (University of Bologna) in the article titled *"Knowledge Transfer As A Leverage Of Change: The Case of the Public Prosecutor Office of Naples"* published in the *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA, Vol. 5, No. 2, October 2013).

## Summary

The Public Prosecutor's Office of Naples is the largest public prosecution institution in Italy, managing over 159,000 criminal cases per year. This office faced severe organizational challenges: an aging administrative staff (average age 59.1 years), a 30% personnel shortage, a lack of promotion opportunities, and the loss of implicit knowledge (*tacit knowledge*) when employees retire \[2, 16–18\]. The WEP program recruited 165 top graduates and students to be placed in 30+ working units to trigger micro-innovations, document implicit administrative procedures, and re-engineer work processes such as the payment of wiretapping costs \[1, 24, 26, 37–43\].

## Legal Basis

- **Italian Ministry of Justice Act No. 101/DG/37 (April 23, 2013):** Provided a national legal and financial framework to extend local training projects in judicial offices.
- **UNINA-PPO Naples Framework Agreement:** Inter-institutional Framework Agreement, Joint Agreement No. 1 (COINOR-PPO), Addendum No. 1 (IT Management of Seized Vehicles), and specialized agreements with the Departments of Chemistry, Biomedical Sciences, Electrical/IT Engineering, and Political Sciences \[19–22\].
- **CCPE Standards of the Council of Europe:** *Opinion No. 7 & No. 8* of the *Consultative Council of European Prosecutors* regarding management autonomy and public prosecutor accountability \[8–10, 17\].

## Background & Policy Problems

1\. **Structure & Workload:** The Naples PPO employs \>100 Senior Prosecutors (30 of whom are in the District Anti-Mafia Directorate / DDA) and 9 Vice-Chief Prosecutors. 2. **Organizational Bottlenecks:** Senior administrative employees had no incentive to learn new IT systems, while a hiring freeze prevented the entry of young personnel \[17–19\]. This caused practical knowledge (*informal organization*) to become isolated and threatened with extinction \[13–18, 41\].

## Three Pillars & Implementation Mechanisms

1\. **Intern Selection & Placement:** Screened 165 high-achieving participants with a €500/month stipend from ESF funds, placed across 6 sub-projects with a 92% preference match rate. 2. **Establishment of the Innovation Office (*Ufficio Innovazione*):** Led by Senior Staff Dr. Agostino D'Abelardo under the supervision of Vice-Chief Prosecutor Dr. Giovanni Melillo to act as a complexity-reduction mechanism and collective memory keeper. 3. **Knowledge Conversion (*Tacit to Explicit Knowledge*):** Applied Ikujiro Nonaka's theory (socialization, externalization, combination, internalization) where senior staff taught and reflected on their daily work routines alongside the interns \[13–15, 41–44\].

## Micro-Innovation Project: Wiretapping Cost Payments

A group of 15 interns across 4 units mapped the workflow of wiretapping cost payments, discovering that the average payment duration reached **17 months** \[37–39\]. The interns designed a digitalization solution that included IT-based invoice handling, shared inter-unit folders, and the use of digital signatures to cut bureaucracy \[40–43\].

## Problems & Sustainability Risks

1\. **Risk of Reversibility:** Old habits risk resurfacing if the recruitment of young personnel is not consistently continued. 2. **Reliance on Change Agents:** High dependence on the leadership of Dr. Giovanni Melillo and Dr. Agostino D'Abelardo demands the permanent institutionalization of procedures beyond individual figures \[35, 48–49, 51–52\].
