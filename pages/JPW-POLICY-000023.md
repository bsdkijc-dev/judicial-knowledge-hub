# JPW-POLICY-000023

The **IDE Asian Law Series No. 2 Judicial System and Reforms in Asian Countries (China)** (Stable ID: `JPW-POLICY-000023`) is a judicial reform and comparative legal system framework evaluating the constitutional status, court structure, procuratorial legal supervision, lawyers' system, and procedural law mechanisms in the People's Republic of China. Organized by Deputy Director Prof. Xin Chunying at the Institute of Law, Chinese Academy of Social Sciences (CASS) and published by the Institute of Developing Economies (IDE-JETRO) in March 2001, the policy framework documents civil, criminal, administrative, and maritime judicial procedures alongside legal supervision and lawyer ethics regulations. Note: Named entities *Cecep Mustafa* and *Reihan Abdan Syakur* are *Not found in the available sources*.

## Summary

The framework provides a structural overview of China's judicial branch and legal reform trajectory across five distinct sections: (1) Overview of Judicial Reform, analyzing chances and challenges; (2) The Court System of China, evaluating constitutional status, relationships with legislative, administrative, procuratorial, public security, and Communist Party organs, court organization, judicatory functions, and Special Administrative Region systems; (3) The System for People's Procuratorates, documenting constitutional status, procurator qualifications, and legal supervision scope across state organs, functionaries, investigations, criminal/civil/administrative trials, and execution enforcement; (4) The Lawyer's System in China, detailing the historical development of the profession, Lawyers Associations' powers and structure, and professional ethics; and (5) Judicial Procedure of China, codifying civil, administrative, and criminal procedural stages (filing, investigation, public prosecution, trial).

## Legal Basis

Authority and analytical structure derive from core primary legislative enactments in China:

- **Constitutional Charter:** The Constitution of 1982.
- **Organic Laws:** The Organic Law of People's Courts; The Organic Law of People's Procurators.
- **Procedural Codes:** The Civil Procedure Law of China; The Criminal Procedure Law of China.
- **Professional & Administrative Laws:** Lawyer's Law of China; Law for Judges of China; Law for Arbitration of China.

## Background & Policy Problems

### Background

Published in March 2001 as Part 2 of the IDE Asian Law Series, the report resulted from research collaboration between CASS Institute of Law in Beijing and IDE-JETRO in Japan to map legal institutions and judicial reform dynamics in China.

### Policy Problems

- Institutional Relationships: Defining and balancing the constitutional relationships between People's Courts and legislative bodies, administrative organs, procuratorates, public security organs, and Communist Party organs.
- Scope of Legal Supervision: Codifying the supervisory role of Procuratorates over state functionaries, citizen law violations, investigations, criminal/civil/administrative trials, and execution enforcement.
- Lawyer Profession Standardization: Transitioning the lawyer's system into an independent professional framework governed by Lawyers Associations and strict legal ethics under the Lawyer's Law of China.
- Specialized Adjudication & SAR Integration: Managing distinct judicial procedures across civil, criminal, administrative, and maritime litigation while accounting for particular court systems in Special Administrative Regions.

## Objectives

1\. Analyze the constitutional status and institutional relationships of People's Courts and Procuratorates. 2. Systematize the scope and content of procuratorial legal supervision. 3. Document the regulatory framework for lawyers and Lawyers Associations. 4. Detail civil, criminal, administrative, and maritime litigation procedures.

## Scope & Implementation

### Scope

Applies across all levels of People's Courts, People's Procuratorates, law firms, Lawyers Associations, and judicial administration organs in the People's Republic of China.

### Key Provisions

- **Constitutional Court Status:** Defining judicial powers and inter-agency relationships with legislative, administrative, procuratorial, public security, and Party organs.
- **Six Domains of Legal Supervision:** Authorizing procuratorial supervision over law discipline, police investigation, criminal trial, execution, civil trial, and administrative trial.
- **Four Criminal Procedural Stages:** Mandatory sequence comprising case filing, investigation, public prosecution initiation, and criminal trial.
- **Lawyers Association Governance:** Statutory regulation of lawyer qualifications, organizational structures, and professional ethics.

### Implementation

- **CASS Legal Research Output:** Published by IDE-JETRO under the leadership of Prof. Xin Chunying, Prof. Ye Ziqiang, Dr. Zheng Qiang, Mr. Xie Haiding, and Dr. Ge Mingzhen.
- **Statutory Codification:** Enforcing statutory requirements across courts, procuratorates, and legal practice under national procedural and organic laws.

## Policy Problems

- Balancing judicial independence with constitutional relationships across legislative, administrative, and political bodies.
- Ensuring effective procuratorial legal supervision over court trial procedures and execution enforcement.
- Standardizing professional ethics and self-regulation within expanding Lawyers Associations.

## Reform

- Advancing court trial independence and procedural transparency.
- Strengthening procuratorial supervision mechanisms across civil and administrative litigation.
- Elevating professional standards under the Law for Judges and Lawyer's Law.
