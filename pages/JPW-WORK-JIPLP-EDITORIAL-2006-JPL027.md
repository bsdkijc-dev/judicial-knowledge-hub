# JPW-WORK-JIPLP-EDITORIAL-2006-JPL027

**Editorial: Obviousness and IP: time to reconsider** is a legal editorial authored by [Jeremy Phillips](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Jeremy_Phillips) and published in the [Journal of Intellectual Property Law & Practice](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Journal_of_Intellectual_Property_Law_%26_Practice) (Vol. 1, No. 5) by [Oxford University Press](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Oxford_University_Press) on March 7, 2006 [Editorial Publication](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Editorial_Publication) \[1–9\].

## Summary

This editorial discusses the consultation paper launched by the [United Kingdom Patent Office](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_Patent_Office) on February 6, 2006, regarding the inventive step requirement in patent law [UK Patent Office Consultation](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/UK_Patent_Office_Consultation) \[1–9\]. The author critically assesses the subjective and costly nature of the *obviousness* standard and contrasts it with trade mark and copyright regimes [Inventive Step Critique](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Inventive_Step_Critique) \[1–9\].

## Legal Basis

- **Consultation on the Inventive Step Requirement**: The UK Patent Office consultation paper examining patent eligibility criteria and trivial patents [Consultation Document](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Consultation_Document) \[1–9\].
- **Burden of Patent Proof**: Novelty examination is objective, whereas obviousness testing is subjective, speculative, and time-consuming [Patent Criteria](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Patent_Criteria) \[1–9\].

## Background

On February 6, 2006, the UK Patent Office issued a discussion paper titled *Consultation on the Inventive Step Requirement in United Kingdom Patent Law and Practice* with a public response deadline of May 31, 2006 [Consultation Background](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Consultation_Background) \[1–9\].

## Objectives

1.  Investigate whether too many trivial patents are granted under the present patent system [Trivial Patents](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Trivial_Patents) \[1–9\].
2.  Test whether innovation and competitiveness are better served by easy, low-barrier patent systems [Competitiveness](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Competitiveness) \[1–9\].
3.  Question the continued necessity and relevance of the inventive step requirement in international patent law [Inventive Step Relevance](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Inventive_Step_Relevance) \[1–9\].

## Key Provisions

- **Two Consultation Questions**: Assessing the grant rate of trivial patents and the impact of low-threshold patents on market competition [Consultation Questions](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Consultation_Questions) \[1–9\].
- **Comparison with Other IP Rights**: The *obviousness* doctrine does not exist in trade mark law (which relies on distinctiveness) or copyright law [IP Comparison](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/IP_Comparison) \[1–9\].
- **Characteristics of Obviousness**: The *obviousness* test is speculative (conjecture), expensive, subjective, and uncertain when compared to the objective novelty test [Characteristics of Obviousness](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Characteristics_of_Obviousness) \[1–9\].

## Policy Issues

- **Subjectivity in Examination**: Determining obviousness in opposition proceedings (such as at the EPO) is costly and time-consuming without offering legal certainty [EPO Subjectivity](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/EPO_Subjectivity) \[1–9\].
- **Local Reform Barriers**: Patent law reform initiated by a single jurisdiction at the periphery is unlikely to affect global standards without central engagement from WIPO or the EPO [Global Reform Barriers](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Global_Reform_Barriers) \[1–9\].
