# 'Digital Acceleration During Covid-19 Pandemic: How the Indonesian Constitutional Court Brings the Citizens Justice' (Muhidin et al., 2023)

### Wiki Entity Page

The **Indonesian Constitutional Court Digital Acceleration Framework** (*Kerangka Akselerasi Digital Mahkamah Konstitusi Republik Indonesia*) is a digital judicial transformation and ICT governance policy implemented by the Constitutional Court of the Republic of Indonesia (MKRI) to guarantee the continuity of public access to constitutional justice during the COVID-19 pandemic. This framework combines online application registration through the Electronic Case Handling Information System (*Sistem Informasi Penanganan Perkara Elektronik* / SIMPEL), remote trials based on Zoom and mini courtrooms across 34 universities, digital decision signing with the National Cyber and Crypto Agency (BSSN), and live broadcasting via YouTube channels.

## Summary

Authored by Muhidin, Eman Suparman, Indra Perwira, and M. Guntur Hamzah in the *International Journal for Court Administration* (2023), this study outlines how the MKRI responded to the Large-Scale Social Restrictions (PSBB) policy and the closure of court buildings. Holding firmly to the principle "Justice delayed is justice denied," the MKRI launched PMK No. 1/2021 on Remote Trials, allowing parties to litigate from their homes, offices, or workspaces. During the 2020–2022 period, the MKRI's case resolution efficiency increased by 18% (resolving 132 out of 143 cases in 2022) and delivered five landmark decisions that restored citizens' constitutional rights \[65, 66, 67–77\].

## Legal Basis

- **1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia (UUD 1945):** Article 23(2), Article 24(3), Article 27(1), and Article 28D(1) (guaranteeing constitutional rights, the rule of law, and fair trials).
- **Health Emergency & SPBE Framework:** Presidential Decrees No. 11/2020 & No. 12/2020; Perppu No. 1/2020; and Presidential Regulation No. 95/2018 on Electronic-Based Government Systems (SPBE).
- **Constitutional Court Regulations (PMK):**
  - *PMK No. 18/2008 & PMK No. 18/2009:* Guidelines for Electronic Filing and Remote Trial Examination.
  - *PMK No. 9/2020 & PMK No. 2/2021:* Rules of Procedure for Judicial Review.
  - *PMK No. 1/2021:* Implementation of Remote Trials (permitting the use of Zoom from the applicant's domicile).

## Background & Policy Problems

The COVID-19 pandemic forced the MKRI to halt face-to-face hearings for nearly two months (March 17 – May 13, 2020). Postponing hearings risked neglecting human rights and allowing executive emergency measures to proceed without constitutional checks and balances. To overcome geographical constraints and internet disparities in Indonesia, the MKRI accelerated the transition from conventional methods to modern ICT-based justice.

## Main Components of the MKRI E-Justice Ecosystem

1\. **SIMPEL (Electronic Case Handling Information System):** A web portal for online application registration, file monitoring, receiving trial summons, and downloading decision copies for free. 2. **Remote Hearings (Virtual Trials):** The use of Zoom Meetings and the provision of smartboard mini courtrooms in collaboration with 34 universities across Indonesia. 3. **Judicial Administration System (JAS) & General Administration System (GAS):** Management of e-Minutasi, digital case tracking, portal management (\`www.mkri.id\`), and trial broadcasting via YouTube. 4. **BSSN Digital Signatures:** Implementation of electronic signature certification with the BSSN for judges and registrars, allowing decisions to be uploaded within hours of pronouncement.

## Application of Procedural Justice Theory (Tom R. Tyler)

- **Voice:** Fulfills the principle of *audi et alteram partem* by hearing statements from all parties virtually.
- **Neutrality:** Provides equal digital access for ordinary citizens and state institutions (President/DPR).
- **Respect:** Provides free e-court facilities as a form of respect for citizens' constitutional rights.
- **Trust:** Enhances public transparency through live broadcasts of hearings and open-access downloads.

## Five Landmark Decisions During the Pandemic

1\. **Decision No. 70/PUU-XVII/2019 (KPK Supervisory Board):** Affirmed that the KPK Supervisory Board is not authorized to grant *pro justitia* permits (wiretapping/searches) and set a two-year investigation time limit. 2. **Decision No. 37/PUU-XVIII/2020 (COVID-19 Finance Law):** Limited the validity period of Law No. 2/2020 to a maximum of 2 years and established good-faith standards in state budget usage. 3. **Decision No. 91/PUU-XVIII/2020 (Job Creation Law Formal Review):** Granted the formal review and declared Law No. 11/2020 *conditionally unconstitutional* due to procedural flaws in its formation \[71–73\]. 4. **Decision No. 72/PUU-XVII/2019 (Taspen/BPJS Pension Funds):** Canceled the mandatory transfer of civil servant pension fund management from PT Taspen to BPJS Employment. 5. **Decision No. 21/PUU-XIX/2021 (Child Sexual Abuse Reporting):** Expanded the reporting subjects of child sexual abuse under Article 293(2) of the Criminal Code so that parents or guardians can represent victims.
