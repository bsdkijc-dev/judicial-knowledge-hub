# Judicial-policy-comparative-mediation-li-verschoof-2017

\`\`\`mediawiki

**The Li & Verschoof Comparative Judicial Mediation Policy Framework** is a comparative judicial mediation policy and empirical socio-legal evaluation model concerning the practice of mediating judges (judges encouraging settlement) in the Netherlands and China. Formulated by Dr. Yedan Li and Prof. Rick Verschoof in the *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA), Vol. 9 No. 1 (December 2017), the framework compares the striking similarities in judicial mediation techniques (facilitating negotiation, narrow/evaluative intervention, and broad/therapeutic intervention) against the stark contrast in litigant perceptions. It reveals high satisfaction among Dutch litigants versus deep mistrust among Chinese litigants, attributing these divergent outcomes to differences in judicial independence, bureaucratic performance incentives, mediation frequency, and the unregulated use of *caucuses* \[1–21, 36–43, 62–68\].

## Summary

Published by Dr. Yedan Li and Prof. Rick Verschoof in the IJCA (December 2017), this policy framework examines the global convergence of "multi-tasking judges" who combine the roles of adjudicator and mediator \[1–5\]. Utilizing comparative empirical data from 100 civil cases in the Netherlands (involving 37 judges across 5 courts) and 49 labor cases in China (across 2 local courts), the study reveals that judges in both countries employ highly similar mediation techniques based on the Riskin Grid (facilitating, narrow/evaluative, and broad/therapeutic) \[17–35\]. However, the litigants' responses are diametrically opposed: Dutch litigants are highly satisfied, whereas Chinese litigants experience profound mistrust \[1, 7, 36–39\]. The authors demonstrate that this divergence is rooted in institutional disparities. In the Netherlands, judges are independent, mediation occurs during open, single-session court hearings adhering to *audi et alteram partem* (the right to be heard equally), and caucuses are strictly prohibited. In contrast, mediation in China is driven by bureaucratic case quality evaluation incentives, fears of *guanxi* (informal relational influence), repetitive mediation leading to inconsistent opinions, and the use of opaque, unregulated caucuses that trigger perceptions of judicial bias \[45–48, 50–57\].

## Legal Basis

- **Dutch Code of Civil Procedure (Wetboek van Burgerlijke Rechtsvordering - Rv) Articles 87 & 88:** The 2002 revised rules of Dutch civil procedure that obligate judges to explore opportunities for settlement during oral hearings.
- **Principle of Audi et Alteram Partem (Article 6 ECHR):** A fundamental principle of European procedural law ensuring the right of both parties to be present and heard equally during all court interventions.
- **PRC Civil Procedure Law Articles 9, 94, 133, 142, & 201:** The foundation of Chinese civil procedural law governing the mediation authority of judges and guaranteeing the principle of voluntariness.
- **SPC Opinions on Priority to Mediation (2010 Notice) & SPC Code of Conduct (2010 Art. 39):** Guidelines issued by the Supreme People's Court of China regarding the prioritization of mediation and the prohibition of forced settlements.
- **Not found in the available sources:** Other specific formal statutory regulations are not mentioned in the source text \[FACT\].

## Background

Global judicial reforms are pushing civil law judges to transition from rigid legal adjudicators into problem-solving judges. However, implementing this "multi-tasking" role triggers distinctly different neutrality challenges in developing nations compared to developed nations.

## Objectives

1\. Empirically compare the techniques, strategies, and interventions of mediating judges in the Netherlands and China based on the Riskin grid. 2. Identify the socio-legal factors causing the stark divergence in litigant satisfaction and trust toward judicial mediation. 3. Formulate ethical standards and procedural boundaries to ensure that the mediator-judge role does not undermine judicial independence and procedural fairness.

## Scope

This policy encompasses the empirical evaluation of civil judicial mediation across 5 first-instance courts in the Netherlands and labor dispute mediation in 2 local courts in China.

## Key Provisions

- **Three Categories of Judicial Mediation Intervention (Riskin Grid):**
  - **Facilitating Negotiation:** Providing a platform, facilitating communication, and encouraging independent negotiation (up to 25–30 interventions per hearing in the Netherlands).
  - **Narrow/Evaluative Intervention:** Proposing compromise figures, evaluating the strength/weakness of arguments, providing a *provisional judicial opinion*, or showing a draft judgment.
  - **Broad/Therapeutic Intervention:** Exploring underlying interests beyond formal legal claims (e.g., apologies, repairing neighborly relations).
- **Divergence in Institutional Practices:**
  - **Dutch Model:** Mediation is conducted once during an open court hearing, adheres strictly to *audi et alteram partem*, and absolutely forbids caucusing.
  - **Chinese Model:** Mediation is repetitive, tied to bureaucratic performance evaluations, and utilizes opaque, unregulated caucuses.

## Implementation

- **Execution of Empirical Observation & Interviews:** Data collection spanning 100 Dutch cases (257 interviews) and 49 Chinese cases (96 interviews) between 2011 and 2014.
- **Integration of the Provisional Opinion:** Dutch judges openly present a provisional legal opinion in front of both parties to anchor negotiations in the shadow of the law.

## Policy Problems

- **Breakdown of the Dispute Resolution Triad due to Caucusing:** One-sided caucusing in China makes litigants feel the judge is colluding with their opponent; if mediation fails, the subsequent judgment is perceived as fundamentally unfair (the triad breaks down into "two against one").
- **Distortion by Bureaucratic Performance Incentives:** Evaluating Chinese judges based on their mediation settlement rates pressures them to aggressively coerce litigants just to clear dockets or avoid writing difficult decisions.

## Reform / Change

- Eliminating rigid bureaucratic incentives that tie a judge's performance evaluation to pure mediation settlement percentages.
- Formulating procedural regulations and ethical codes that prohibit unregulated, non-transparent caucuses to strictly uphold the *audi et alteram partem* principle.

### Deutsch (Schweizer Hochdeutsch)

**Vergleichender politischer Rahmen zur richterlichen Mediation von Li & Verschoof** Dieser von Dr. Yedan Li und Prof. Rick Verschoof im *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA, 2017) veröffentlichte Rahmen vergleicht die Praxis der richterlichen Mediation in den Niederlanden und China. Die empirische Studie zeigt, dass Richter in beiden Ländern ähnliche Mediationstechniken (basierend auf dem Riskin-Grid) anwenden, die Reaktionen der Prozessparteien jedoch extrem unterschiedlich ausfallen: In den Niederlanden herrscht hohe Zufriedenheit, in China tiefes Misstrauen. Die Autoren führen diese Divergenz auf institutionelle Unterschiede zurück. Während in den Niederlanden das Prinzip des rechtlichen Gehörs (audi et alteram partem) in offenen Anhörungen ohne Einzelgespräche (Caucuses) gilt, wird die Mediation in China durch bürokratische Leistungsanreize, intransparente Caucuses und die Angst vor informellen Beziehungen ("Guanxi") verzerrt.

- **Rechtsgrundlage:** Niederländische Zivilprozessordnung Art. 87 & 88; EMRK Art. 6; Chinesisches Zivilprozessrecht; SPC-Richtlinien 2010.

### Français

**Cadre politique comparatif de la médiation judiciaire de Li & Verschoof** Publié par la Dre Yedan Li et le Prof. Rick Verschoof dans l' *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA, 2017), ce cadre compare la pratique de la médiation par les juges aux Pays-Bas et en Chine. L'étude empirique montre que les juges des deux pays utilisent des techniques de médiation très similaires (basées sur la grille de Riskin), mais que les réactions des plaideurs sont diamétralement opposées : grande satisfaction aux Pays-Bas, méfiance profonde en Chine. Les auteurs attribuent cette divergence à des différences institutionnelles. Aux Pays-Bas, le principe du contradictoire (audi et alteram partem) est respecté lors d'audiences publiques sans apartés (caucuses). En Chine, la médiation est faussée par des incitations bureaucratiques liées à l'évaluation des performances, des apartés opaques et la crainte des relations informelles ("Guanxi").

- **Base Légale:** Code de procédure civile néerlandais Art. 87 & 88 ; CEDH Art. 6 ; Loi de procédure civile chinoise ; Lignes directrices de la SPC (2010).

### Italiano

**Quadro politico comparativo della mediazione giudiziaria di Li & Verschoof** Pubblicato dalla Dott.ssa Yedan Li e dal Prof. Rick Verschoof nell' *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA, 2017), questo quadro confronta la pratica della mediazione da parte dei giudici nei Paesi Bassi e in Cina. Lo studio empirico mostra che i giudici in entrambi i paesi utilizzano tecniche di mediazione molto simili (basate sulla griglia di Riskin), ma le reazioni dei litiganti sono diametralmente opposte: grande soddisfazione nei Paesi Bassi, profonda sfiducia in Cina. In Cina, la mediazione è distorta da incentivi burocratici di valutazione delle prestazioni, caucus opachi e il timore di relazioni informali ("Guanxi").

- **Base Legale:** Codice di procedura civile olandese Art. 87 & 88; CEDU Art. 6; Legge di procedura civile cinese; Linee guida della SPC (2010).

### 3 Most Important Improvements

1\. **Elimination of Quantitative Bureaucratic Incentives (Case Quality Evaluation) in Judge Appraisals:** Remove the tie between a judge’s promotion or financial incentives and their pure mediation settlement percentage to ensure mediation is conducted without coercion or the judge’s self-interest in clearing dockets. 2. **Prohibition of Unregulated Caucuses and Strict Enforcement of Audi et Alteram Partem:** Adopt procedural law rules mandating that all judicial mediation must be conducted openly in the presence of both parties to preserve transparency and eliminate prejudices regarding *guanxi* or bias. 3. **Restriction of Judicial Mediation to Official Single-Session Court Hearings:** Limit judges' mediation interventions strictly to official hearing sessions to prevent the issuance of inconsistent provisional opinions and to eliminate the confusing overlap between the roles of adjudicator and mediator \[45–48, 53–57, 65–67\].\`\`\`
