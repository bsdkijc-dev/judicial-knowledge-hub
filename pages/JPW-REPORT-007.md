# JPW-REPORT-007

**Twenty-Third Report of the Prosecutor of the International Criminal Court to the United Nations Security Council on the Situation in Libya** is the 23rd periodic report submitted by the Office of the Prosecutor (OTP) of the International Criminal Court to the UN Security Council on 28 April 2022, pursuant to UNSC Resolution 1970 (2011), presenting a comprehensive review, updated strategy, and accountability roadmap for crimes committed in Libya.

## Summary

The report presents a comprehensive evaluation of investigative progress on crimes in Libya since the 2011 referral, alongside a new strategy focused on leveraging AI technology, strengthening complementarity with Libyan authorities, and setting concrete, time-bound targets.

## Legal Basis

- [UN Security Council Resolution 1970 (2011)](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/UN_Security_Council_Resolution_1970_%282011%29), adopted under Chapter VII of the UN Charter.
- [Rome Statute](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Rome_Statute): Article 42(6) (Prosecutor Recusal), Article 68 (Protection of Victims and Witnesses), and Article 93(10) (Mutual Legal Assistance).

## Background

Since receiving the UNSC referral in 2011, the OTP has investigated international crimes in Libya. The 23rd report reviews eleven years of investigative progress and sets out a new roadmap to address ongoing security and political obstacles.

## Objectives

- Prioritize the UNSC referral through adequate resource and staff allocation.
- Increase direct engagement with victims, witnesses, and affected communities on the ground.
- Advance complementarity by supporting Libyan domestic law enforcement and cooperation with third states/Europol.
- Develop a structured completion strategy for the Libya situation.

## Key Provisions

The OTP's work is organized around four main lines of inquiry:

- **2011 Crimes**: Investigation complete; focus now on executing outstanding arrest warrants.
- **Detention Facilities**: Investigation of torture and sexual violence in official and secret detention facilities, supported by 3D crime-scene modeling.
- **2014–2020 Military Operations**: Investigation of mass killings and enforced disappearances in Tarhuna and Tripoli.
- **Crimes Against Migrants**: Investigation of human trafficking, extortion, and torture, in cooperation with the Joint Team.

## Implementation

The OTP is deploying a big-data evidence-processing platform using artificial intelligence and machine learning for automated transcription and translation of Arabic-language audio and video material, alongside cloud-based eDiscovery tools. The Office has collected approximately 50,000 items of potential evidence, added 300+ new items during the reporting period, conducted 6 field missions, and held 35+ meetings with international partners and NGOs.

## Policy Issues

- Political deadlock and security instability limiting investigators' physical access to crime scenes in Libya.
- Budgetary and staffing constraints within the OTP.

## Relationships with Other Entities

- **[Office of the Prosecutor (ICC)](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Office_of_the_Prosecutor_%28ICC%29)**: Organ that published the report.
- **[United Nations Security Council](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council)**: UN body that received the report.
- **[Europol](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Europol)**: Operational cooperation partner within the Joint Team, alongside the Netherlands, Italy, the United Kingdom, and Ireland.
- **[Muammar Mohammed Abu Minyar Gaddafi](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Muammar_Mohammed_Abu_Minyar_Gaddafi)**: Former Libyan leader against whom an ICC arrest warrant was issued in connection with the 2011 violence; proceedings were terminated on 22 November 2011 following his death.
- **[Saif Al-Islam Gaddafi](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Saif_Al-Islam_Gaddafi)**: Defendant whose ICC arrest warrant remains unexecuted; his admissibility challenge was rejected by the Appeals Chamber on 5 April 2019.
- **[Abdullah Al-Senussi](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Abdullah_Al-Senussi)**: Former Libyan official whose ICC case was declared inadmissible by the Appeals Chamber on 24 July 2014, as it was being handled by Libya's domestic judicial process.
- **[Al-Tuhamy Mohamed Khaled](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Al-Tuhamy_Mohamed_Khaled)**: Subject of an ICC arrest warrant reportedly deceased; the OTP is confirming the reported death before withdrawing the warrant.
- **[Mahmoud Al-Werfalli](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Mahmoud_Al-Werfalli)**: Subject of two ICC arrest warrants (2017 and 2018) relating to crimes by groups affiliated with the Libyan Arab Armed Forces (LAAF), reportedly deceased.
- **[Stephanie Williams](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Stephanie_Williams)**: Special Adviser to the UN Secretary-General for Libya, who coordinates with the OTP on accountability efforts.
