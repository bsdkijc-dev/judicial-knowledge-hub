# Judicial-policy-austrian-avg-1925

The **Legal Framework and General Administrative Procedure Model of Austria 1925** (*Allgemeine Verwaltungsverfahrensgesetz - AVG 1925*) is the first codification of general administrative procedure law in Central Europe. It integrated fifty years of case law from the *Verwaltungsgerichtshof* (VwGH) into a package of positive statutes to guarantee citizen participation rights and administrative efficiency [^1].

## Summary

Authored and reviewed by [Angela Ferrari Zumbini](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Angela_Ferrari_Zumbini), Associate Professor of Administrative Law at the University of Naples Federico II, this study examines the formation, doctrinal characteristics, and transnational diffusion of AVG 1925 [^2]. AVG 1925 codified VwGH jurisprudence developed since 1875, constructing individuals as active subjects of rights (\*subjects of rights\*) and establishing due process principles (\*Rechtsverfahren\*) rooted in the nature of things (\*Natur der Sache\*) [^3].

## Legal Basis

This policy framework was enacted through a five-law package on July 21, 1925 (BGBl 274/1925, effective January 1, 1926):

- **[Allgemeine Verwaltungsverfahrensgesetz](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Allgemeine_Verwaltungsverfahrensgesetz) (AVG)**: General Administrative Procedure Act [^4].
- **[Einführungsgesetz zu den Verwaltungsverfahrensgesetzen](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Einf%C3%BChrungsgesetz_zu_den_Verwaltungsverfahrensgesetzen) (EGVG)**: Introductory Act defining application scope and exemptions [^5].
- **[Verwaltungsstrafgesetz](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Verwaltungsstrafgesetz) (VSG)**: Administrative Penal Act [^6].
- **[Verwaltungsvollstreckungsgesetz](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Verwaltungsvollstreckungsgesetz) (VVG)**: Administrative Execution Act [^7].
- **[Verwaltungsentlastungsgesetz](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Verwaltungsentlastungsgesetz) (VEG)**: Act on Administrative Relief and Bureaucratic Workload Reduction [^8].

## Background

When the VwGH was established under the Act of October 22, 1875, Austria possessed no general statutory law governing administrative acts [^9]. The VwGH utilized its power to annul decisions based on procedural defects to formulate individual protection standards rooted in the nature of things [^10]. These judicial principles were systematized by [Friedrich Tezner](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Friedrich_Tezner) and incorporated into AVG 1925 to fulfill League of Nations efficiency requirements (\*Genfer Reformbeschlüsse 1922\*) [^11].

## Objectives

- Codify VwGH case law into a uniform set of positive statutory rules [^12].
- Guarantee subjective individual rights protection within public administrative proceedings [^13].
- Achieve administrative efficiency, speed, simplification, and cost economy (\*Zweckmäßigkeit, Raschheit, Einfachheit und Kostenersparnis\*) [^14].

## Scope of Application

Pursuant to the EGVG, AVG applies to designated public administrative authorities, while excluding taxation, specific public service sectors, and private-law administrative actions (\*Privatwirtschaftsverwaltung\*) [^15].

## Key Provisions

- **Right to be Heard (Parteiengehör) & Material Truth**: Article 37 AVG defines investigations as tools to uncover material truth (\*materielle Wahrheit\*) while offering parties a forum to defend legal rights [^16].
- **Access to Files (Akteneinsicht)**: Article 17 establishes an absolute right for parties to inspect case documents without discretionary refusal [^17].
- **Decision Timeframes & Administrative Inaction**: Article 73(1) caps decision timelines at 6 months; administrative inertia grants parties an immediate right to hierarchical appeal (Article 73(2)) [^18].
- **Preference for Oral Proceedings & Speed**: Articles 18 and 40–44 encourage oral hearings and telephonic records to expedite administrative workflow [^19].

## Implementation & Transnational Diffusion

The AVG 1925 model was adopted across post-1918 Central European nations through the migration of former VwGH judges and scholars (\*personal factor\*):

- **[Liechtenstein](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Liechtenstein)**: Transposed AVG draft concepts into the \*Landesverwaltungspflegegesetz\* (1922) [^20].
- **[Poland](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Poland)**: Promulgated the Code of Administrative Procedure in 1928 under Polish Supreme Administrative Court President [Jan Sawicki](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Jan_Sawicki) [^21].
- **[Czechoslovakia](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia)**: Formed its Supreme Administrative Court and 1928 procedural code under former VwGH judges [František Pantůček](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Franti%C5%A1ek_Pant%C5%AF%C4%8Dek) and [Emil Hácha](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Emil_H%C3%A1cha) [^22].
- **[Yugoslavia](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Yugoslavia)**: Enacted the General Administrative Procedure Act of 1930 based on VwGH traditions [^23].
- **[Italy](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Italy)**: Influenced Chamber VI (Temporary) of the Council of State (1919–1923) presided over by [Guido de Bonfioli Cavalcabò](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Guido_de_Bonfioli_Cavalcab%C3%B2) [^24].

## Policy Issues & Limitations

- **Marginalization in Comparative Studies**: AVG's archetypal role is frequently neglected in modern Anglo-American comparative law due to the dominance of German doctrine centered on Otto Mayer's concept of administrative act (\*Verwaltungsakt\*) [^25].
- **Cassatory Control Limitations**: VwGH was restricted to annulment powers (\*cassatory power\*) without factual re-evaluation capabilities until the comprehensive judicial reform of 2012 [^26].
