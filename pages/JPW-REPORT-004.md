# JPW-REPORT-004

**Report of the Court on Key Performance Indicators 2020 (Revised Version)** is the official ICC report dated 11 December 2020, presenting qualitative and quantitative data measuring judicial and prosecutorial efficiency, international cooperation, complementarity, and organizational performance through 30 September 2020.

## Summary

The report measures the Court's achievements against the 2019–2021 Strategic Plan adopted by the three organs of the Court on 17 July 2019. For the first time, it incorporates indicators on Cooperation and Complementarity alongside the seven-phase judicial performance framework and organizational performance metrics.

## Legal Basis

- Mandate of the Assembly of States Parties: Resolution [ICC-ASP/13/Res.5](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/ICC-ASP/13/Res.5), Annex I, paragraph 7(b).
- [ICC Strategic Plan 2019–2021](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/ICC_Strategic_Plan_2019%E2%80%932021) (Presidency, OTP, Registry), dated 17 July 2019.
- [Rome Statute](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Rome_Statute): Article 74 (Trial Judgment), Article 75 (Reparations), Article 76 (Sentencing), Article 81 (Appeal), and Article 93 (International Cooperation).
- [Chambers' Practice Manual](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Chambers%27_Practice_Manual) (4th Edition, November 2019).

## Background

Development of the KPI framework began at the ASP's request in 2014. Following reports published in 2015, 2016, 2017, and 2019 (the 2018 report having been retroactively merged into the 2019 report), the 2020 report refines the measurement framework by incorporating cooperation and complementarity indicators for the first time on a systematic basis.

## Objectives

- Ensure judicial proceedings are fair and expeditious.
- Measure the effectiveness of requests for assistance and cooperation (RFA/RFC).
- Measure the impact of OTP support in strengthening national investigative and prosecutorial capacity (complementarity).
- Measure organizational performance in budget execution, geographical representation, gender balance, and IT security.

## Key Provisions

Judicial performance is measured across 7 phases:

- **Phase 1**: Confirmation of Charges.
- **Phase 2**: Trial Preparation.
- **Phase 3**: Trial.
- **Phase 4**: Trial Deliberations.
- **Phase 5**: Sentencing.
- **Phase 6**: Reparations.
- **Phase 7**: Final Appeals.

Judges also adopted decision deadlines in the 4th edition of the Chambers' Practice Manual (November 2019), including a 10-month deadline for Article 74 written judgments following the end of closing statements.

## Implementation

Data collection was led by the Registry, in coordination with the Presidency and the OTP. In 2020, the OTP sent 402 requests for assistance (RFAs), with 32.33% executed as of 15 September 2020 (average execution time 40.5 days), and 10 preliminary-examination requests for information (RFIs). On complementarity, the OTP received 34 RFAs from national authorities, responding substantively to 97% within less than three months (average 42 days). On IT security, the Court's systems repelled 40 million cyberattack attempts per month, with critical incident response time improving from 46 minutes (2019) to 29 minutes (2020). Staff performance appraisal completion reached 95–96%.

## Policy Issues

- 23 States Parties remain under-represented and 56 non-represented among Court staff as of 30 September 2020.
- Gender imbalance at the senior D-1 management level (only 11.11% women).
- Operational constraints from the COVID-19 pandemic and unilateral US sanctions.

- **[Germain Katanga](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Germain_Katanga)**: Convicted in Case ICC-01/04-01/06; implementation of individual and collective reparations orders totaling USD 1,000,000 continues to be monitored by Trial Chamber II.
