# JPAIS:PRECEDENT:3277K PDT 2000

**Supreme Court Decision Reg. No. 3277 K/Pdt/2000** (dated July 18, 2003) is a fundamental civil law and tort law (*Perbuatan Melawan Hukum* / PMH) jurisprudence precedent establishing vital rules concerning the legal qualification of a broken promise to marry, the legal consequences of reimbursing living and educational expenses, and damages for reputational restoration *ex* Article 1365 of the Civil Code.

## Executive Summary

The Supreme Court established three primary legal principles:

1.  **Broken Promise to Marry Constitutes a Tort (PMH):** "With the failure to fulfill a promise to marry, such action constitutes an unlawful act because it violates societal norms of decency and propriety."
2.  **Obligation to Reimburse Living and Postgraduate Studies Expenses:** Because the Original Defendant (Drs. \[Defendant\]) failed to fulfill his promise to marry the Original Plaintiff (\[Plaintiff\], B.A.) after initiating an intimate relationship and cohabiting as husband and wife without marriage, the Original Defendant is legally obligated to reimburse the Original Plaintiff for all expenses incurred to support the Defendant's living costs and graduate studies (Master's / S-2 program) amounting to Rp 7,500,000 (seven million five hundred thousand rupiahs).
3.  **Reputational Damages and *Ex Aequo et Bono* Determination:** A man who repudiates a marriage promise after damaging a woman's honor and reputation (a school teacher) before her family and community is ordered to pay Rp 10,000,000 (ten million rupiahs) in reputational restoration damages. The Supreme Court affirmed that damages do not need to be detailed meticulously in the *petitum* because, pursuant to Supreme Court Jurisprudence Reg. No. 2559 K/Pdt/1986, judges possess the authority to determine damages *ex aequo et bono*.

The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition of the Petitioner for Cassation (\[Plaintiff\], B.A.), annulled Surabaya High Court Decision No. 51/PDT/1999/PT.SBY dated April 13, 1999 (which had overturned Surabaya District Court Decision No. 142/Pdt.G/1997/PN.SBY dated October 28, 1997), and independently adjudicated (*mengadili sendiri*) by partially granting the Plaintiff's lawsuit in Convention, declaring that the Defendant committed a Tort, ordering the Defendant to reimburse living expenses of Rp 7,500,000 and pay reputational damages of Rp 10,000,000, while rejecting the Defendant's counterclaim in Reconvention.

## Legal / Procedural Issues

### 1. Legal Qualification of a Broken Promise to Marry (*Broken Promise to Marry* *ex* Article 1365 Civil Code) Following Intimate Cohabitation

*Does the action of a man (a university lecturer/graduate student) who persuades a woman to engage in sexual relations and cohabit regularly under a promise of marriage, but subsequently repudiates that promise unilaterally, qualify as an Unlawful Act (Tort)?*

**Supreme Court Rule: Constitutes an Unlawful Act (Tort).**

Pursuant to the analogy of Supreme Court Jurisprudence Reg. No. 3191 K/Pdt/1984 (February 8, 1986), repudiating a marriage promise after cohabiting and engaging in sexual relations constitutes a violation of societal norms of decency and propriety. Such conduct inflicts moral and material injury upon the woman, giving rise to a claim for damages *ex* Article 1365 of the Civil Code.

### 2. Right to Claim Reimbursement of Living Expenses and Postgraduate Studies Funding

A man who fails to fulfill his marriage promise is legally obligated to reimburse (make restitution for) all expenses incurred by a woman to support his daily living costs and educational pursuits (including Master's degree tuition) during their romantic relationship absent a valid matrimonial bond.

### 3. Judicial Discretion in Determining Damages *Ex Aequo et Bono* (Supreme Court Jurisprudence No. 2559 K/Pdt/1986)

Defendants or appellate courts cannot dismiss damage claims on the grounds that loss breakdowns are insufficiently specific or vague (*obscuur libel*). Pursuant to Supreme Court Jurisprudence Reg. No. 2559 K/Pdt/1986 (June 22, 1988), judges possess discretionary authority to determine fair and equitable damage amounts (*ex aequo et bono*) based on standards of fairness regarding the victim's suffering.

## Question of Law vs Question of Fact

### Question of Law (*Judex Juris* / Supreme Court)

Did the *Judex Facti* Surabaya High Court (Decision No. 51/PDT/1999/PT.SBY) err in applying the law (*foutieve toepassing van het recht*) by annulling the Surabaya District Court decision and declaring the Plaintiff's lawsuit inadmissible (NO) on the grounds that damage breakdowns were vague, whereas breaking a marriage promise is a tort *ex* Supreme Court Jurisprudence No. 3191 K/Pdt/1984 and judges possess authority to determine damages *ex aequo et bono* *ex* Supreme Court Jurisprudence No. 2559 K/Pdt/1986?

**Supreme Court Legal Answer:** Yes. Surabaya High Court misapplied the law. The Supreme Court granted \[Plaintiff\]'s cassation petition, annulled Surabaya High Court's decision, and independently adjudicated by declaring that Defendant Drs. \[Defendant\] committed a Tort, ordering him to reimburse living/S-2 expenses of Rp 7,500,000 and pay reputational damages of Rp 10,000,000.

### Question of Fact (*Judex Facti* / Surabaya District Court & Surabaya High Court)

Verification of facts regarding the relationship between \[Plaintiff\], B.A. and Drs. \[Defendant\] starting November 12, 1993; facts concerning the marriage promise stated before the Plaintiff's parents on November 24, 1995; facts regarding the Defendant's regular overnight stays from Saturday to Monday and sexual relations; funding of the Defendant's S-2 graduate studies and daily living expenses by the Plaintiff; the Defendant's evasion starting October 1996; and the Defendant's counterclaim in Reconvention concerning defamation across mass media (Jawa Pos, Memorandum, Wanita Indonesia).

### Established by Evidence

- The Defendant (Drs. \[Defendant\]) is **PROVEN TO HAVE PROMISED TO MARRY THE PLAINTIFF** before the Plaintiff's parents on November 24, 1995.
- The Defendant is **PROVEN TO HAVE REGULARLY STAYED OVERNIGHT AND ENGAGED IN SEXUAL RELATIONS** with the Plaintiff like husband and wife without a marriage bond.
- The Defendant is **PROVEN TO HAVE RECEIVED AND UTILIZED DAILY LIVING AND MASTER'S DEGREE STUDY FUNDING** provided by the Plaintiff.
- The Defendant is **PROVEN TO HAVE REPUDIATED HIS MARRIAGE PROMISE** and disappeared irresponsibly.
- The Defendant's conduct is **PROVEN TO CONSTITUTE AN UNLAWFUL ACT (TORT)** violating community decency and propriety.
- The Defendant is ordered to reimburse all expenses incurred by the Plaintiff amounting to Rp 7,500,000.
- The Defendant is ordered to pay reputational restoration damages to the Plaintiff amounting to Rp 10,000,000.
- Surabaya High Court Decision No. 51/PDT/1999/PT.SBY is **NULL AND VOID**.

### Dismissed Claims (Reconvention)

- The Defendant's counterclaim in Reconvention regarding mass media defamation was **REJECTED ENTIRELY BY THE SUPREME COURT**.

## Uncertainties and Gaps

**Legal Protection for Women Against Material and Moral Losses Caused by False Marriage Promises:** This landmark ruling reinforces Supreme Court Jurisprudence No. 3191 K/Pdt/1984 in Indonesian civil law: men who exploit women using marriage promises—including enjoying S-2 educational funding and living support—cannot evade civil liability. Courts affirm dual protections: the right to recover material investments made by victims (restitution) and the right to immaterial damages for the restoration of dignity and reputation.

## Frequently Asked Questions (GEO)

Can living expenses and graduate school tuition funded by a woman for her boyfriend be recovered if the man repudiates his marriage promise pursuant to Supreme Court Jurisprudence?  
**Yes, Mandatory Reimbursement.** Pursuant to Supreme Court Jurisprudence Reg. No. 3277 K/Pdt/2000:

- Repudiating a marriage promise after intimate romantic relations is qualified as an Unlawful Act (Tort) *ex* Article 1365 of the Civil Code because it violates community norms of decency and propriety.
- A man who repudiates a marriage promise must reimburse (make restitution for) all expenses incurred by the woman to support his living costs and graduate studies (S-2) during the relationship.
- In addition to material expense reimbursement, the man is ordered to pay reputational restoration damages (*in casu* Rp 10,000,000), the sum of which judges are authorized to determine *ex aequo et bono*.
