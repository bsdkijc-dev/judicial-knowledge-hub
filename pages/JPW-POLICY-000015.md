# JPW-POLICY-000015

The **Indonesian Dispute Resolution and Judicial System Framework** (Stable ID: `JPW-POLICY-000015`) is a national statutory, judicial, and alternative dispute resolution (ADR) policy framework evaluating court jurisdictions, judge appointments, commercial arbitration, labor dispute settlement, consumer protection, and environmental litigation in the Republic of Indonesia. Documented under IDE Asian Law Series No. 21 by Prof. Hikmahanto Juwana of the Faculty of Law, University of Indonesia in collaboration with IDE-JETRO, the policy analyzes the "One-Roof" judicial reform (Act No. 35 of 1999), commercial arbitration under Act No. 30 of 1999, Consumer Dispute Settlement Boards (BPSK), labor dispute committees (P4D/P4P), and NGO legal standing in environmental suits. Note: Named entities *Cecep Mustafa* and *Reihan Abdan Syakur* are *Not found in the available sources*.

## Summary

The framework examines judicial and ADR mechanisms during Indonesia's *Reformasi* transition following the end of the Soeharto administration. It outlines six core structural pillars: (1) Four-Jurisdiction Judicial Hierarchy, comprising General, Religious, Military, and Administrative Tribunals topped by a single Supreme Court (\*Mahkamah Agung\*); (2) One-Roof Judicial Independence, transferring organizational, administrative, and financial management of courts from executive departments to the Supreme Court under Act No. 35 of 1999; (3) Commercial Arbitration and ADR, establishing statutory rules for consultation, negotiation, mediation, conciliation, expert assessment, and arbitration under Act No. 30 of 1999; (4) Consumer Dispute Resolution, creating regency-level BPSK boards under Act No. 8 of 1999; (5) Tripartite Labour Adjudication, operating Regional and Central Labor Dispute Settlement Committees (P4D/P4P) under Act No. 22 of 1957; and (6) Environmental Tort Litigation, enforcing strict tort liability, class actions, and NGO legal standing (\*WALHI v. PT Inti Indorayon Utama\*) under Act No. 23 of 1997.

## Legal Basis

Authority and execution derive from constitutional provisions and statutory enactments:

- **Constitutional Charter:** 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia (Transitional Provisions Art. II; Art. 5(1), Art. 20(1)).
- **Judicial Power & Court Acts:** Judicial Power Act (Act No. 19 of 1964; Act No. 14 of 1970; as amended by Act No. 35 of 1999); Supreme Court Act (Act No. 14 of 1985); General Tribunal Act (Act No. 2 of 1986); Bankruptcy Act of 1998 (Act No. 4 of 1998); Human Rights Court Act (Act No. 26 of 2000); Tax Court Act (Act No. 14 of 2002).
- **Arbitration & Commercial Statutes:** Arbitration and Alternative Dispute Resolution Act (Act No. 30 of 1999); Antimonopoly Act (Act No. 5 of 1999); Presidential Decree No. 34 of 1981 (1958 New York Convention).
- **Consumer, Labour & Environmental Statutes:** Consumer Protection Act (Act No. 8 of 1999); Labor Dispute Act of 1957 (Act No. 22 of 1957); Termination of Employment Act of 1964 (Act No. 12 of 1964); Environmental Management Act of 1997 (Act No. 23 of 1997).
- **Colonial Procedural Codes:** HIR (\*Het Herziene Indonesisch Reglement\*); RBg (\*Rechtsreglement voor De Buitengewesten\*); Rv (\*Reglement op de Rechtsvordering\*).

## Background & Policy Problems

### Background

Following independence, Indonesia retained Dutch colonial court structures and procedural codes. During the New Order, executive departments controlled judicial administration. The post-1998 \*Reformasi\* era triggered comprehensive legal reforms to establish judicial independence, clear severe Supreme Court backlogs, and provide out-of-court commercial arbitration.

### Policy Problems

- Perennial Court Congestion & Backlogs: Unrestricted appeal rights accumulating thousands of pending cases at the Supreme Court (e.g., 23,915 total cases in 1999/2000).
- Protracted Civil Litigation: Civil lawsuits taking an average of 8 to 9 years to reach an enforceable judgment.
- Systemic Corruption & Distrust: Widespread bribery leading citizens to rely on extra-judicial debt collectors.
- Lack of ADR Finality: Statutory provisions allowing parties to unilaterally declare out-of-court settlements "unsuccessful" and litigate afresh in court.
- Foreign Award Enforcement Bottlenecks: Local courts frequently delaying or denying enforcement of foreign arbitral awards despite New York Convention accession.

## Objectives

1\. Guarantee judicial independence by consolidating court administration under the Supreme Court. 2. Declog court dockets through pre-trial court-administered mediation (S.C. Circular Letter 1/2002). 3. Provide statutory recognition and enforcement for commercial arbitration and ADR (Act 30/1999). 4. Establish accessible consumer dispute resolution via BPSK boards. 5. Adjudicate labor disputes through bipartite negotiations and LDSC committees. 6. Authorize class actions and NGO standing in environmental protection.

## Scope & Implementation

### Scope

Applies across all courts of first instance, appellate courts, the Supreme Court, specialized commercial/tax/human rights courts, arbitration centers (BANI, BAMUI, BAPMI), BPSK boards, and labor committees in Indonesia.

### Key Provisions

- **One-Roof Judicial Consolidation:** Full transfer of lower court administration, personnel, and finances to the Supreme Court by 2004.
- **Statutory Commercial ADR (Act 30/1999):** 14-day consultation/mediation targets, 30-day registration of settlement agreements at District Courts, and exclusive Central Jakarta District Court exequatur jurisdiction.
- **Consumer Board Jurisdiction:** BPSK authority to conciliate, mediate, or arbitrate consumer disputes and issue binding awards within 21 working days.
- **Environmental NGO Standing:** Statutory right of environmental NGOs to file lawsuits to demand environmental restoration.

### Implementation

- **Supreme Court Output:** Handled 23,915 total cases in 1999/2000, deciding 14,208 cases.
- **Foreign Arbitral Award Enforcement:** Central Jakarta District Court granted 9 out of 29 foreign award enforcement applications between 1990 and 2002.
- **Consumer Complaints:** YLKI processed 798 complaints in greater Jakarta during the first half of 2000.

## Policy Problems

- Severe Supreme Court Backlogs: Unrestricted cassation rights accumulating thousands of carryover cases.
- Lack of ADR Finality: Statutory provisions allowing parties to unilaterally declare out-of-court settlements "unsuccessful" and litigate in court.
- Systemic Corruption & Loss of Public Trust: Widespread bribery leading to extra-judicial debt collection by gangsters.

## Reform

- Appointment of non-career justices to the Supreme Court.
- One-Roof administrative consolidation.
- Enactment of the Industrial Relations Dispute Settlement Bill (PPHI).
- Supreme Court Regulation No. 1 of 2002 on Class Action Procedures.
