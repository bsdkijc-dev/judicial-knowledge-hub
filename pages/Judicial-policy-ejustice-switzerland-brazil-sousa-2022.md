# Judicial-policy-ejustice-switzerland-brazil-sousa-2022

**The Sousa, Kettiger & Lienhard Comparative E-Justice Policy Framework** is an empirical e-justice policy and cross-national comparative court innovation evaluation model formulated by Marcos Sousa, Dr. Daniel Kettiger, and Prof. Dr. Andreas Lienhard in the *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA), Vol. 13 No. 2 (August 2022). It analyzes the antecedents (drivers and hinderers at the environmental, organizational, innovation feature, and individual levels), the impacts, and the operational results of the integrated e-justice portal "Justitia 4.0" (*Justitia.Swiss*) in Switzerland compared with the Electronic Judicial Process system "PJe" (*Processo Judicial Eletrônico*) in Brazil. The framework is based on 47 in-depth interviews conducted across 7 cantons/7 states and the supreme/federal courts of both nations \[1–15, 37–48, 60–128\].

## Summary

Published by Marcos Sousa, Daniel Kettiger, and Andreas Lienhard in the IJCA (August 2022), this policy framework addresses the transnational research gap regarding ICT adoption strategies in judiciaries with distinct governance traditions \[1–13\]. Comparing two federal republics with civil law traditions, the study evaluates the "Justitia 4.0" single-portal project in Switzerland and the "PJe" system in Brazil \[2, 10–12, 59\]. Utilizing 47 qualitative interviews, the research maps innovation drivers and barriers across four levels of analysis \[41–48, 60\]. In Switzerland, the federalism of 26 cantons and 4 official languages necessitates a consensus-based approach that requires a lengthy implementation timeline (2018–2026) \[83–90\]. In Brazil, top-down centralization by the National Council of Justice (CNJ) rapidly digitized \>95% of case files but triggered complaints regarding poor usability and the loss of local customization autonomy \[55, 95–97, 102\]. Both countries report significant positive impacts on processing speed, the eradication of processual *dead time*, and the facilitation of judicial telework \[105–108, 118–121\].

## Legal Basis

- **Swiss Federal Constitution Article 191c:** The constitutional guarantee of Swiss judicial independence that restricts unilateral executive interference in court administration.
- **Swiss Civil and Criminal Procedure Codes Revision:** The foundational legal harmonization required to mandate electronic communication within the *Justitia 4.0* framework.
- **CNJ Resolutions No. 185/2013 & No. 335/2020 (Brazil):** The National Council of Justice regulations establishing PJe as the mandatory national standard for electronic justice systems.
- **CNJ Resolution No. 298/2019 (Brazil):** The regulatory framework governing the implementation of remote work (telework) within the Brazilian judiciary.
- **European Convention on Human Rights Article 6:** The international standard guaranteeing the right to a fair and public hearing within a reasonable time.
- **Not found in the available sources:** Other specific formal statutory regulations are not mentioned in the source text \[FACT\].

## Background

Modern judiciaries are compelled to adopt ICT to combat case backlogs and enhance access to justice, yet national governance structures (federal consensus vs. top-down centralization) heavily dictate how these technologies are adopted and received by users.

## Objectives

1\. Comparatively analyze the drivers and hinderers of national-scale e-justice adoption across environmental, organizational, innovation feature, and individual levels. 2. Measure the actual impacts of digitalization on workflow speed, operational costs, system usability, and procedural fairness. 3. Formulate evidence-based strategic recommendations for policymakers executing nationwide judicial ICT rollouts.

## Scope

This policy covers a comparative evaluation of e-justice implementations across 7 Swiss Cantons (including the *Bundesgericht*) and 7 Brazilian States (including the TRF1).

## Key Provisions

- **Four Levels of Innovation Antecedent Analysis:**
  - **Environmental Level:** The impact of Swiss cantonal federalism vs. Brazilian CNJ centralization, mandatory legislation, and ICT infrastructure.
  - **Organizational Level:** The timeline of adoption, the use of *sandboxes* (pilot testing), training programs, and integration with legacy IT systems.
  - **Innovation Feature Level:** The stringent data privacy/security demands in Switzerland vs. the persistent usability (user-friendliness) issues of Brazil's PJe.
  - **Individual Level:** Addressing resistance to change, the generation gap, and overall digital literacy.
- **Primary Impacts of E-Justice Adoption:**
  - **Speed & Dead Time Reduction:** The elimination of manual file transport and search times down to milliseconds.
  - **Simultaneous Access & Telework:** Enabling concurrent document access by multiple parties and sustaining court operations via telework during the COVID-19 pandemic.

## Implementation

- **Sandbox Methodology in Switzerland:** Conducting controlled pilot tests in specific cantons before executing the national rollout of Justitia 4.0.
- **Institutionalization of PJe in Brazil:** The aggressive integration of PJe across all state and federal courts under the strict oversight of the CNJ.

## Policy Problems

- **Complexities of Swiss Cantonal Consensus:** The slow pace of securing uniform agreements across 26 autonomous cantons and 4 language regions.
- **Rigid CNJ Centralization & PJe Usability Gaps:** The loss of regional autonomy to adapt software interfaces and the lack of court-provided physical infrastructure (e.g., dual monitors) for teleworking staff.

## Reform / Change

- Mainstreaming the *sandbox* methodology for large-scale judicial software development to ensure usability before national mandates.
- Improving judicial ICT interfaces to be highly user-friendly while providing adequate home-office infrastructure for judicial teleworkers.

## Multilingual Summaries (Swiss National Languages)

### Deutsch (Schweizer Hochdeutsch)

**Vergleichendes E-Justice-Rahmenwerk für die Schweiz und Brasilien von Sousa, Kettiger & Lienhard** Dieser von Marcos Sousa, Daniel Kettiger und Andreas Lienhard im *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA, 2022) veröffentlichte Rahmen vergleicht die Einführung von E-Justice in der Schweiz (Justitia 4.0) und Brasilien (PJe). Basierend auf 47 Interviews zeigt die Studie, wie der kantonale Föderalismus und die Viersprachigkeit in der Schweiz einen langsamen, konsensbasierten Ansatz (2018–2026) und Pilotprojekte (Sandboxes) erfordern. Im Gegensatz dazu führte die strikte "Top-Down"-Zentralisierung durch den Nationalen Justizrat (CNJ) in Brasilien zu einer schnellen Digitalisierung (\>95%), jedoch auf Kosten der regionalen Anpassungsfähigkeit und Benutzerfreundlichkeit (Usability). Beide Systeme verzeichnen enorme Gewinne bei der Geschwindigkeit, der Eliminierung von prozessualen Leerlaufzeiten (Dead Time) und der Ermöglichung von Telearbeit.

- **Rechtsgrundlage:** Schweizer Bundesverfassung Art. 191c; Schweizerische ZPO/StPO; CNJ-Resolutionen (Brasilien).

### Français

Les deux systèmes enregistrent des gains massifs en termes de vitesse, d'élimination des temps morts (dead time) procéduraux et de facilitation du télétravail.

### 3 Most Important Improvements

1\. **Application of Sandbox Pilot Methodologies Prior to National Rollouts:** Adopt the sandbox method (as seen in the Swiss Justitia 4.0 project) in specific cantons or courts to rigorously validate interface usability, technical feasibility, and legal compliance before mandating nationwide e-justice applications \[93–94\]. 2. **Enhancing Usability and Balancing Centralization with Regional Customization:** Overhaul judicial ICT interfaces (like Brazil's PJe) to be significantly more user-friendly, and grant measured flexibility for regional courts to customize application modules to meet specific local operational needs \[79–80, 96–97\]. 3. **Provision of Equitable Telework Infrastructure and Regulations:** Update judicial telework regulations to ensure that courts bear the responsibility for providing adequate physical working equipment (such as dual monitors) to prevent performance degradation and to relieve employees of self-funded infrastructure burdens \[120–121\].\`\`\`
