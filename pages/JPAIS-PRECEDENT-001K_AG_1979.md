## Overview

Supreme Court Decision No. 1K/Ag/1979 establishes a vital legal precedent regarding the cassation procedure for Religious Court cases as well as the limits of the absolute jurisdiction of Religious Courts. The Supreme Court affirmed that final judgments rendered by the Islamic High Court can be brought directly before the Supreme Court through a cassation petition. Furthermore, for divorces occurring prior to the enactment of Law No. 1 of 1974, Religious Courts only possess jurisdiction to rule on divorce matters and iddah maintenance, whereas claims regarding child maintenance costs and marital property (gono-gini) division fall under the authority of the District Court.

## Case Background

The Original Plaintiff (\[Party B\]) filed a lawsuit against the Original Defendant (\[Party A\]) at the Nganjuk Religious Court seeking iddah maintenance, name transfer and division of marital property, and child maintenance costs for four children. The divorce between the parties took place in 1967 (prior to the enactment of Law No. 1 of 1974). The Nganjuk Religious Court and the Surabaya Islamic High Court Branch granted parts of the Plaintiff's claims, including iddah and child maintenance. The Original Defendant subsequently filed a cassation petition to the Supreme Court.

## Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

- **Ratio Decidendi:** Against final judgments rendered by the Islamic High Court, a cassation petition may be requested directly from the Supreme Court. For divorces occurring prior to the enactment of Law No. 1 of 1974, the Islamic High Court / Religious Courts only have the authority to examine and decide upon iddah maintenance. Claims concerning child maintenance costs and marital property division constitute general civil property disputes that fall under the jurisdiction of the District Court.
- **Ruling:** Granting the cassation petition of the Petitioner (\[Party A\]) in part; annulling the Surabaya Islamic High Court Branch Decision No. 10/1978 and the Nganjuk Religious Court Decision No. 54/1978; ordering the Defendant to pay iddah maintenance in the amount of Rp 62,500; and declaring that the Religious Courts lack jurisdiction to adjudicate claims concerning child maintenance costs and marital property division.
