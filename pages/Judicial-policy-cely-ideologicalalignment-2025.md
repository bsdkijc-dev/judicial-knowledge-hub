# Judicial-policy-cely-ideologicalalignment-2025

**One more constrained than the other: Asymmetrical ideological alignment and its implications for polarization** is an empirical comparative political science and public policy research paper authored by Tadeas Cely of Masaryk University (Czech Republic), published in the *European Journal of Political Research* (EJPR) in 2025.

## Summary

This study tests the asymmetry of ideological alignment and belief constraint between left- and right-wing partisan camps across 15 European countries. Based on an analysis of 23,586 partisans from 131 political parties, using data from the European Social Survey (ESS 2008 & 2016), the Chapel Hill Expert Survey (CHES), and the Manifesto Project, the study demonstrates that left-wing partisans consistently show stronger belief alignment on economic issues than right-wing partisans. It further develops a theoretical model showing that the real extent of disagreement between two opposing partisan camps is bounded by the camp with weaker ideological alignment (the right).

## Background

Classic political science research assumed that ideological alignment makes voting decisions easier for citizens. However, recent scholarship shows that overly tight belief constraint fuels political stereotyping, the formation of echo chambers, and rising affective polarization. Prior literature generally assumed that ideological alignment is symmetric between left and right camps, without accounting for how asymmetry in belief structure affects the depth of political conflict.

## Objectives

1\. Test the Ideological Asymmetry Hypothesis: ideological alignment is weaker among supporters of right-pole parties than left-pole parties (Hypothesis 1). 2. Test the Position Blurring Hypothesis: ideological alignment is stronger among supporters of parties with clearer ideological positions (Hypothesis 2). 3. Test the Programmatic Nicheness Hypothesis: ideological alignment is stronger among supporters of niche parties (Hypothesis 3). 4. Identify the implications of ideological asymmetry for the expansion and depth of partisan conflict in society.

## Scope

The research covers an analysis of 23,586 partisan respondents from 131 political parties across 15 European countries, divided into 7 party families: Radical Left, Green, Socialist, Liberal, Christian Democratic, Conservative, and Radical Right.

## Key Provisions / Findings

- **Economic Dimension Asymmetry (Hypothesis 1 Supported):** On the economic dimension, a 1-point rightward shift on a 10-point ideology scale reduces individual ideological alignment by B = -0.32 (SE = 0.07, p \< 0.01), equivalent to disconnecting 1.5 policy attitudes from the party line.
- **2016 Sociocultural Realignment:** Sociocultural alignment showed strong left-wing asymmetry in 2008 (B = -0.43, SE = 0.09), but shifted to a symmetric, parabolic shape by 2016, in which extreme right-wing partisans reached a high alignment level (0.7) matching that of extreme left-wing partisans (0.6).
- **Belief Network Interconnectedness (GEI):** Left-wing party families show belief-network interconnectedness that is **10% to 47% higher** than right-wing families. The Radical Left recorded the highest network density (GEI = 7.69, 61% significant ties), followed by Socialists (GEI = 7.18) and Greens (GEI = 6.63). The Radical Right recorded the lowest density (GEI = 5.22, only 27% significant ties; p \< 0.001).
- **Limited Effects of Position Blurring and Nicheness:** Position blurring significantly reduced economic alignment in 2016 (B = -1.1, SE = 0.33) but had no effect on sociocultural alignment. Programmatic nicheness showed only a modest non-linear effect on economic alignment.
- **Model of Partisan Conflict Containment:** The overall breadth of substantive disagreement between two opposing camps is determined by the belief density of the **less-aligned camp** (the right). Political polarization can only expand to cover more issues if belief constraint deepens within right-wing partisan camps.

## Implementation

The methodology was implemented through three-level multilevel linear regression (individuals, parties, countries) and Gaussian graphical belief-network modeling using EBIC glasso regularization and NetworkComparisonTest. Replication code and data are published openly on GitHub at \`https://github.com/tadeascely/one_more_constrained\`.

## Policy Issues

1\. Rising intergroup intolerance driven by group homogeneity and echo-chamber formation within the highly aligned left camp. 2. The potential expansion of political conflict into sociocultural domains as ideological alignment increases within the radical right.

## Reform / Implications

The study shows that understanding mass polarization requires accounting for asymmetry in belief structure, in which the consolidation of a comprehensive narrative on the political right is the key determinant of escalating partisan conflict.
