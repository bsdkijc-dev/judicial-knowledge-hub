## Welcome

**Judicial Knowledge Wiki** is a structured knowledge platform for organizing, connecting, preserving and reusing knowledge about the judiciary, law, judicial processes, judicial reform, technology and access to justice.

The Wiki is designed as a **knowledge infrastructure**, not merely as a collection of articles.

Its core principles are:

- Evidence
- Traceability
- Structured knowledge
- Interconnection
- Versioning
- Reusability
- Human verification
- AI readiness

## Knowledge Architecture

The Wiki uses a structured knowledge model:

| Layer                | Function                                                                                                 |
|----------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Source**           | Original document, law, regulation, decision, dataset or research.                                       |
| **Evidence**         | Assessment of the reliability and verification status of a claim.                                        |
| **Knowledge Object** | Structured representation of a person, institution, law, case, concept, policy, dataset or other entity. |
| **Ontology**         | Defines entities, domains and relationships.                                                             |
| **Knowledge Graph**  | Connects related knowledge objects.                                                                      |
| **Wiki Page**        | Human-readable representation of the knowledge object.                                                   |
| **AI Layer**         | Enables structured retrieval, analysis, summarization and reuse.                                         |

## Knowledge Lifecycle

Knowledge should move through the following lifecycle:

**SOURCE** → **EVIDENCE** → **KNOWLEDGE OBJECT** → **ONTOLOGY** → **HUMAN REVIEW** → **MEDIAWIKI** → **VERSION** → **LINK** → **REUSE**

The Wiki does not treat AI-generated content as authoritative evidence.

## Comparative Judiciary

Judicial systems should be studied comparatively without assuming that foreign practices are automatically transferable.

Comparative analysis should distinguish:

**DESCRIPTIVE**

What does another jurisdiction actually do?

**NORMATIVE**

Should the practice be adopted?

**TRANSFERABILITY**

Under what conditions could it work in another jurisdiction?

## Knowledge Quality

Before publication, knowledge should be checked for:

- Accuracy
- Evidence
- Source provenance
- Legal classification
- Citation integrity
- Internal links
- Categories
- Version
- Verification status

AI may assist with extraction, classification, drafting, ontology construction and quality checking.

## Current Development

This Wiki is being developed progressively.

Priority:

1.  Build the ontology.
2.  Establish templates.
3.  Create anchor knowledge pages.
4.  Connect knowledge objects.
5.  Verify evidence.
6.  Develop the knowledge graph.
7.  Expand the knowledge base.
8.  Improve AI readiness.

The objective is not to maximize the number of pages.

The objective is to maximize:

**QUALITY × EVIDENCE × STRUCTURE × CONNECTIVITY × REUSABILITY**
