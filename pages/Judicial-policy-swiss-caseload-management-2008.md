# Judicial-policy-swiss-caseload-management-2008

**First Swiss Caseload Management Study** (\*Geschäftslastbewirtschaftung bei den Gerichten\*) is the first empirical caseload management research conducted for the highest administrative and social insurance courts in Switzerland. Carried out by Prof. Dr. iur. Andreas Lienhard and Mag. rer. publ. Daniel Kettiger of the University of Berne, commissioned by the Administrative Court of the Canton of Lucerne (\*Verwaltungsgericht des Kantons Luzern\*), the study evaluates time allocation, staff capacity, and case weighting across 19 standardized case categories across 26 cantons, in order to develop an objective caseload management system based on the percentage of a full-time staff member's annual working hours per case.

## Summary

Sponsored by the Lucerne Administrative Court's administrative committee (Dr. Andreas Korner, Dr. Patrick Müller, Dr. Heiner Eiholzer) and supported by a specialist advisory board (\*Fachbeirat\* — Prof. Arnold Marti, Dr. Hans-Jakob Mosimann, and Federal Supreme Court Judge Rudolf Ursprung), the research responds to rising case volume and complexity in Switzerland. The study combines cantonal case-filing statistics (Survey A) with time estimates from 198 judicial practitioners (Survey B). The findings show that, on average, a single administrative/social insurance case in Switzerland requires **38 to 42 working hours** (equivalent to 1.89% to 2.07% of the annual working hours of one full-time staff member).

## Legal Basis

- **Art. 188 para. 3 of the Swiss Federal Constitution:** Guarantees the right of judicial self-government (\*justizielle Selbstverwaltung\*) as a basis for court management.
- **Art. 126 para. 1 of the Swiss Federal Constitution:** The principle of efficiency and the economical use of public funds.
- **Art. 170 of the Swiss Federal Constitution:** A mandate to evaluate the effectiveness of the performance of state duties.
- **Art. 29 ff. of the Swiss Federal Constitution:** Guarantees procedural rights protections and timely case resolution.
- **Ordinance of the Federal Assembly on Judges' Positions (SR 173.110.1, Art. 2):** An obligation to implement controlling procedures and report workload to Parliament.

## Background & Policy Problems

Swiss courts face resource constraints amid rising case volumes. Former Chief Justice Arthur Aeschlimann emphasized the importance of setting workload targets for each court division. The main obstacles to caseload management in Switzerland include: 1. The absence of empirical reference data on average time allocation per case type. 2. Cantonal organizational heterogeneity resulting from cantonal autonomy over administrative procedure \[9, 32–34\]. 3. Data distortion from small/rural cantons that rely on part-time judges.

## Scope of the 19 Standardized Case Categories

The research established 19 case categories validated by the advisory board \[23, 24, 69–75\]:

- **Administrative Law:** (1) Planning, Land Use & Environment, (2) Compulsory Purchase, (3) Foreign Nationals Law, (4) Public Procurement, (5) Social Assistance & Crime Victim Support, (6) Road Traffic, (7) Guardianship/Child & Adult Protection, (8) Other Administrative Cases \[69–72\].
- **Tax Law:** (9) Direct Taxes, (10) Cantonal Special Taxes, (11) Charges for Specific Services, (12) Property Valuation/Building Insurance, (13) Other Tax Cases.
- **Social Insurance Law:** (14) Invalidity Insurance, (15) Health Insurance, (16) Occupational Pensions, (17) Accident/Military Insurance, (18) State Pensions (AHV/EO), (19) Unemployment/Other Social Insurance.

## Key Indicators & Methodology

- **Primary Recommended Metric:** Caseload is managed using the **Percentage of a Full-Time Staff Member's Annual Working Hours per Case** (% FTE per case). In the Canton of Lucerne, 1% FTE equals approximately 18.8 net working hours.
- **Points System Variant:** Grouping case categories into a 3-, 4-, or 5-point scale based on relative workload ratios.
- **Comparison Group Strategy:** Benchmarking data against a group of structurally comparable cantons (Aargau, Basel-Stadt, Bern, Lucerne, St. Gallen, and Zurich) to avoid distortion from small cantons.

## Key Empirical Findings

- **Total Annual Case Volume:** Approximately 21,800 cases filed annually across all highest cantonal administrative and social insurance courts in Switzerland.
- **Staffing Allocation:** Of approximately 415 full-time judicial and registry positions, **80% (345 FTEs)** are devoted directly to adjudication, while **20%** go toward management, training, and statistical functions.
- **Largest Case-Volume Shares:** Invalidity insurance accounts for **20.78%** of national caseload; unemployment insurance **11.61%**; foreign nationals/immigration law **9.55%**; accident insurance **8.80%**; planning/environment **7.90%**.
- **Workload per Case (Survey B, % FTE):** Planning/environmental law = **1.01%** (1.13% in comparison cantons); direct taxes = **0.73%**; foreign nationals law = **0.67%**; invalidity insurance = **0.59%**; occupational pensions = **0.53%**; other tax law = **0.26%** \[50–52\].
- **Appeal Reversal Rate (Quality Proxy):** The percentage of cantonal rulings overturned by the Federal Supreme Court varies by field: Administrative law (5.0%–12.9%), Tax law (0.0%–5.4%), Social insurance law (up to 42.6%, typically ~20%).
