## Policy Analysis

- **Background & Core Facts**:
  - The White Paper on Crime 2023 provides a comprehensive statistical review of criminal justice trends in Japan up to July 2023 (covering main statistics through CY 2022), drawing from National Police Agency, Public Prosecutors, Courts, Corrections, and Rehabilitation records.
  - **Key Crime Trends (2022)**: Reported Penal Code offenses reached 601,331 cases (thefts: 407,911 / 67.8%; non-theft: 193,420) with a clearance rate of 41.6%.
  - **Recidivism**: Repeat offenders accounted for 47.9% of all persons cleared for Penal Code offenses in 2022 (81,183 out of 169,409).
  - **Special Feature (Part 7 - Adverse Childhood Experiences)**: Survey of 865 juveniles (591 in training schools, 274 under probation) and 700 guardians revealed that 78.6% of female training school juveniles suffered psychological abuse, 73.2% physical abuse, and 50.0% witnessed domestic violence against mothers.
  - **Major Legislative Reforms**:
    - **Sexual Offense Reforms (Act No. 72 of 2017 & Act No. 66 of 2023)**: Expanded rape definitions, increased minimum penalties, created custodian sexual offenses, integrated offenses into indecent/penetrative assault, and raised the statutory age of consent to 16.
    - **Juvenile Act Revision (Act No. 47 of 2021)**: Enforced April 1, 2022; designated 18- and 19-year-olds as "Specified Juveniles" (特定少年), mandated prosecutor referrals for severe offenses, created 6/24-month probation terms, and instituted Class 5 Juvenile Training Schools.
    - **Visiting Foreign Nationals**: Public prosecutors received 13,892 visiting foreign nationals in 2022, led by China (35.8%), Vietnam (23.4%), South Korea (13.6%), and the Philippines (5.7%).
    - **Case Formulation in Probation/Parole (CFP)**: Full implementation since January 2021 for structured recidivism risk assessment and tailored intervention planning.
- **Interpretations & Structural Inferences**:
  - Demonstrates a transition toward evidence-based criminological policy, establishing causal links between childhood trauma (ACEs) and juvenile delinquency.
  - High repeat offender rates (47.9%) demonstrate that correctional success depends on integrated psychological interventions and family support networks rather than incarceration alone.
