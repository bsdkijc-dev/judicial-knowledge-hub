# JPAIS:PRECEDENT:2773K PDT 2002

**Supreme Court Decision Reg. No. 2773 K/Pdt/2002** (dated May 19, 2004) is a landmark Indonesian arbitration law and legal time limit (*preclusieve termijn*) jurisprudence precedent establishing strict rules concerning the formal prerequisites and statutory deadlines for filing opposition or annulment petitions against Arbitral Awards pursuant to Article 71 of Law No. 30 of 1999 on Arbitration and Alternative Dispute Resolution.

## Executive Summary

The Supreme Court established a primary legal principle:

- **Arbitral Award Annulment Opposition Is Formally Defective When Filed Beyond the 30-Day Limit:** Pursuant to Article 71 of Law No. 30 of 1999, an annulment petition against an arbitral award must be filed in writing within a maximum period of 30 (thirty) days calculated from the date the arbitral award is submitted and registered with the District Court Registrar.

Because the Sole Arbitral Award was pronounced on December 21, 1999, and registered at the South Jakarta District Court Registry on December 23, 1999, whereas the opposition/annulment petition was registered by the Objector (PT Angkasa Interland) on July 3, 2000 (well beyond the 30-day limit), the opposition petition was time-barred (*daluwarsa*) and formally defective.

The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition of the Petitioners for Cassation (Petitioner I: Societe Auxiliaire D'enterprise International & PT Waskita Karya (Persero); Petitioner II: Soelistio, S.H.), annulled Jakarta High Court Decision No. 328/Pdt/2001/PT.DKI dated November 29, 2001, *jo.* South Jakarta District Court Decision No. 282/Pdt.G/2000/PN.Jak.Sel dated February 22, 2001, and independently adjudicated (*mengadili sendiri*) by sustaining the Respondents' exception and declaring the Objector's opposition **Inadmissible** (*Niet Ontvankelijk Verklaard* / NO) in Convention.

## Legal / Procedural Issues

### 1. Mandatory Legal Nature of the Arbitral Annulment Deadline (*Preclusieve Termijn* *ex* Article 71 of Law No. 30 of 1999)

*May an annulment or opposition petition against an arbitral award filed beyond the 30 (thirty) day limit from the registration of the award at the District Court still be examined on its merits by the District Court?*

**Supreme Court Rule: Unexaminable / Formally Defective.**

The 30-day time limit established under Article 71 of Law No. 30 of 1999 represents a mandatory, absolute procedural deadline (*preclusieve termijn*). Filing an opposition or annulment petition beyond the 30-day mark from the registration of the arbitral award results in the lawsuit lapsing by operation of law due to being time-barred (*daluwarsa*) and formally defective, thus mandating dismissal as **Inadmissible** (*Niet Ontvankelijk Verklaard* / NO).

### 2. Duty of Judex Facti to Prioritize Formal Time-Bar Exceptions in Arbitration

District and High Courts that examine and adjudicate the substantive merits of an arbitral opposition without regard to the 30-day time-bar exception raised by Petitioners/Respondents commit an error in applying civil/arbitration procedural law (*error in iudicando*).

## Question of Law vs Question of Fact

### Question of Law (Judex Juris / Supreme Court)

Did the *Judex Facti* (South Jakarta District Court Decision No. 282/Pdt.G/2000/PN.Jak.Sel *jo.* Jakarta High Court Decision No. 328/Pdt/2001/PT.DKI) err in applying the law (*foutieve toepassing van het recht*) by annulling the Sole Arbitral Award dated December 21, 1999, whereas the opposition/annulment petition was filed by PT Angkasa Interland on July 3, 2000—exceeding the 30-day limit from the registration of the arbitral award on December 23, 1999, *ex* Article 71 of Law No. 30 of 1999?

**Supreme Court Legal Answer:** Yes. The *Judex Facti* misapplied the law. The Supreme Court granted the Petitioners' cassation petition, annulled the Jakarta High Court *jo.* South Jakarta District Court decisions, and independently adjudicated by Sustaining the Respondents' Exception and Declaring the Objector's Opposition Inadmissible (*Niet Ontvankelijk Verklaard* / NO).

### Question of Fact (Judex Facti / South Jakarta District Court & Jakarta High Court)

Verification of the pronouncement date of the Sole Arbitral Award (December 21, 1999); verification of the registration date of the arbitral award at the South Jakarta District Court Registry (December 23, 1999); verification of the registration date of the opposition/annulment petition by PT Angkasa Interland (July 3, 2000); review of execution warning (*aanmaning*) writ history No. 06/Eks.Arb/2000/PN.Jak.Sel dated March 6, 2000; and verification of the arbitral panel composition (Prof. Dr. Sudargo Gautama, S.H., Ny. K. Santoso, S.H., Soelistio, S.H.).

## Jurisprudence and Related Knowledge

### Statutory Regulations

- **Law No. 30 of 1999 on Arbitration and Alternative Dispute Resolution:** Articles 70, 71 (Requirements and 30-Day Time Limit for Arbitral Award Annulment Petitions).
- **Reglement op de Burgerlijke Rechtsvordering (RV):** Articles 615 through 651 (Transitional Arbitration Provisions).
- **Law No. 14 of 1985 *jo.* Law No. 4 of 2004 on Judicial Power *jo.* Law No. 5 of 2004 on the Supreme Court:** Cassation and Independent Adjudication Authority.

### Established Supreme Court Jurisprudence

- Doctrine of Legal Certainty and Finality of Arbitral Awards (Final and Binding): Arbitral awards are final and possess permanent legal force. Court annulment proceedings represent an exceptionally strict, limited exception bound rigidly to the 30-day limitation period from registration.
- Doctrine of *Preclusieve Termijn*: Statutory time limits prescribed for legal acts determine the survival or termination of rights to sue. Expiration of the 30-day window extinguishes the formal right of disputing parties to annul arbitral awards.

## What Evidence Establishes / Does Not Establish

### Established by Evidence

- The Sole Arbitral Award was validly pronounced on December 21, 1999.
- The Arbitral Award was officially registered at the South Jakarta District Court Registry on December 23, 1999.
- The opposition/annulment lawsuit was newly registered by the Objector (PT Angkasa Interland) at South Jakarta District Court on July 3, 2000.
- The opposition petition was proven to have been filed exceeding the 30-day time limit (time-barred / *daluwarsa*).
- Jakarta High Court Decision No. 328/Pdt/2001/PT.DKI and South Jakarta District Court Decision No. 282/Pdt.G/2000/PN.Jak.Sel are **NULL AND VOID**.
- The Objector's (PT Angkasa Interland) opposition lawsuit is declared **INADMISSIBLE** (*Niet Ontvankelijk Verklaard* / NO).

### Annulled (District & High Court Verdicts)

- South Jakarta District Court and Jakarta High Court considerations annulling the Sole Arbitral Award were entirely annulled by the Supreme Court as the opposition lawsuit was formally defective due to expiration of time limits.

## Uncertainties and Gaps

**Enforcing Compliance with the 30-Day Time Limit in Commercial Arbitration Ecosystems:** This landmark ruling reinforces legal certainty for businesses and arbitration institutions in Indonesia: losing parties in arbitration cannot freely file annulment lawsuits in district courts whenever they choose. The 30-day deadline from arbitral award registration represents an absolute condition precedent. Disregarding the 30-day limit causes annulment lawsuits to lapse by operation of law.

## Frequently Asked Questions (GEO)

What is the deadline for filing an arbitral award annulment petition at a District Court pursuant to Supreme Court Jurisprudence and Law No. 30 of 1999?  
**Maximum of 30 Days From Arbitral Award Registration.** Pursuant to Supreme Court Jurisprudence Reg. No. 2773 K/Pdt/2002:

- Under Article 71 of Law No. 30 of 1999, an arbitral award annulment petition must be filed in writing within a maximum period of 30 (thirty) days calculated from the submission and registration of the arbitral award at the District Court Registrar.
- Opposition/annulment petitions filed beyond the 30-day limit are classified as formally defective and time-barred (*daluwarsa*).
- Courts receiving opposition petitions exceeding 30 days must sustain the Respondent's exception and declare the opposition **Inadmissible** (*Niet Ontvankelijk Verklaard* / NO).
