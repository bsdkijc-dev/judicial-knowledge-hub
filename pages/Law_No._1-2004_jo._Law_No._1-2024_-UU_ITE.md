## Core Definition

- - Law No. 1 of 2024\*\* (formally enacting the second amendment to \*\*Law No. 11 of 2008 on Electronic Information and Transactions\*\* / \*\*UU ITE\*\*) is the primary national statutory framework governing electronic commerce, digital transactions, cybercrime definitions, online content moderation, and the legal admissibility of electronic evidence across Indonesian courts.

<!-- -->

- \*\*Simple Definition:\*\* The updated national cyber law that sets the rules for online behavior, regulates digital contracts, criminalizes cyber offenses like hacking and defamation, and ensures electronic files can be used as legal evidence in court.
- \*\*Legal / Technical Definition:\*\* The master statutory instrument establishing the legal validity of electronic records and digital signatures (matching UNCITRAL model laws), defining prohibited cyber acts (such as illegal access, data tampering, and restructured defamation provisions), and empowering state authorities with electronic system oversight and child protection mandates.
- \*\*Plain-Language Explanation: **It acts as the core legal foundation of Indonesia's digital space, balancing internet freedom and commercial growth with criminal accountability for cybercrime and the protection of electronic evidence.**

## Why It Matters

- \*\*Validates Electronic Evidence in Courts:\*\* Explicitly establishes that electronic information, electronic documents, and computer printouts constitute an extended, legally binding form of evidence (\*alat bukti\*) under Indonesian procedural law.
- \*\*Restructures Contentious Cybercrimes:\*\* Refines controversial provisions from the original 2008 statute—such as defamation (Article 27A) and hate speech—to reduce potential over-criminalization and protect free expression.
- \*\*Strengthens Child Online Protection:\*\* Introduces dedicated mandates requiring electronic system operators to enforce robust safety features and protections for minors utilizing digital platforms.
- \*\*Regulates Electronic Certification and Signatures:\*\* Provides clear legal recognition for digital certificates and electronic signatures, securing e-commerce and digital banking transactions nationwide.

## Key Principles

Principle of Legal Certainty in Digital Spaces (Asas Kepastian Hukum Ruang Digital)  

- \*\*Meaning:\*\* Electronic transactions, digital communications, and cyber operations must be governed by transparent, predictable legal rules to protect users and foster economic trust.
- \*\*Why It Matters:\*\* Essential for securing national e-commerce and preventing fraudulent online practices.
- \*\*Legal Basis / Source:\*\* Law No. 1/2024 Art. 3; UNCITRAL Model Law Framework.
- \*\*Related Concepts:\*\* E-commerce security, digital legal certainty, electronic contracts.

Principle of Electronic Evidence Admissibility (Asas Validitas Alat Bukti Elektronik)  

- \*\*Meaning:\*\* Computer records, logs, and digital files processed through compliant electronic systems possess equivalent legal weight to traditional physical documents in judicial proceedings.
- \*\*Why It Matters:\*\* Enables courts to prosecute modern cybercriminals using native digital artifacts.
- \*\*Legal Basis / Source:\*\* Law No. 1/2024 Art. 5; Criminal Procedure Code (KUHAP).
- \*\*Related Concepts:\*\* Electronic document, cryptographic integrity, digital forensics.

Principle of Proportionality in Cyber Enforcement (Asas Proporsionalitas Penegakan Hukum Siber)  

- \*\*Meaning:\*\* Restrictions on digital speech and enforcement of cyber offenses must be applied proportionally, safeguarding individual reputations while upholding public order.
- \*\*Why It Matters:\*\* Prevents the statute from being misused to stifle legitimate criticism or public debate.
- \*\*Legal Basis / Source:\*\* Law No. 1/2024 Explanatory Memorandum; Constitutional Court Jurisprudence.
- \*\*Related Concepts:\*\* Defamation balance, freedom of expression, state moderation.

## Indonesia Section

- \*\*Evolutionary Context:\*\* Enacted initially as \*\*Law No. 11 of 2008\*\*, amended by \*\*Law No. 19 of 2016\*\*, and comprehensively revised via \*\*Law No. 1 of 2024\*\*, the statute represents Indonesia’s primary legislative response to rapid digital transformation.
- \*\*Key Structural Changes Introduced in Law No. 1/2024:\*\*

1\. \*Refined Defamation Clauses:\* Separated and clarified defamation provisions (relocated to Article 27A), establishing them explicitly as complaint-based offenses (\*delik aduan\*) requiring direct victim reports. 2. \*Expanded Evidentiary Scope:\* Clarified that electronic documents, system printouts, and digital logs are valid extensions of evidence under Indonesian procedural codes. 3. \*Platform Accountability:\* Imposed clearer administrative and operational guidelines for Electronic System Operators (\*Penyelenggara Sistem Elektronik / PSE\*).

- \*\*Nomenclature:\*\* Undang-Undang Nomor 1 Tahun 2024 tentang Perubahan Kedua atas UU Nomor 11 Tahun 2008 tentang Informasi dan Transaksi Elektronik — \*Law No. 1 of 2024 (Second Amendment to UU ITE)\*.

## Debates & Controversies

- Debate: Protection of Individual Reputation vs. Freedom of Expression Online

` * *Position A (Pro-Strict Regulation):* Cyber defamation laws are vital to protect citizens from malicious online bullying, character assassination, and harassment on social media.`  
` * *Position B (Pro-Free Speech Reform):* Ambiguous cyber defamation clauses have historically been weaponized as "rubber articles" (*pasal karet*) to silence investigative journalists, activists, and political critics.`  
` * *Evidence:* High-profile criminal prosecutions under earlier versions of the UU ITE.`  
` * *Current Status:* Partially addressed in Law No. 1/2024 by introducing explicit exceptions for public interest disclosures and making defamation a complaint-based offense, though civil society groups continue monitoring implementation.`

## Rule-of-Law Connection

- \*Legal Certainty:\* Provides clear statutory definitions for cyber offenses, enabling prosecutors and judges to penalize digital fraud and hacking consistently.
- \*Evidentiary Modernization:\* Formally anchors digital forensic science into national procedural law, allowing courts to process modern electronic evidence.
- \*Balancing Rights:\* Seeks equilibrium between punishing malicious cyber actors and protecting citizens' constitutional right to communicate freely.

## AI & Future of Law

- \*AI-Generated Defamation and Deepfakes:\* Utilizing Law No. 1/2024 provisions to prosecute malicious actors who deploy generative AI to create and distribute deepfake pornography or fraudulent impersonations online.
- \*Automated Content Filtering Oversight:\* Balancing state electronic system oversight powers with judicial review to ensure AI content moderation remains transparent and proportionate.

## Common Misconceptions

- \*Myth:\* Law No. 1 of 2024 completely decriminalized online defamation in Indonesia.

` * *Reality:* Online defamation was not abolished; rather, it was restructured (relocated to Article 27A) with reduced maximum prison sentences and a requirement that it must be prosecuted as a personal complaint (*delik aduan*).`  
` * *Source:* Law No. 1 of 2024 Article 27A.`

- \*Myth:\* Digital screenshots of text messages are automatically accepted by courts as conclusive proof under UU ITE without verification.

` * *Reality:* While Article 5 recognizes electronic documents as valid evidence, courts still require expert forensic verification and chain-of-custody proof to ensure screenshots or files have not been fabricated.`  
` * *Source:* Cybercrime Adjudication Benchbooks; ISO/IEC 27037.`

## Practical Example

- \*Scenario:\* A business owner discovers a competitor is running an automated botnet attack that hacks into their e-commerce server database, stealing customer records and altering pricing files.
- \*Question:\* How do prosecutors apply Law No. 1 of 2024 to prosecute the offense?
- \*Applicable Principle:\* Illegal Access and Data Interference (Law No. 1/2024 Arts. 30 & 32).
- \*Analysis:\*

` 1. Law enforcement cyber units seize server logs and deploy ISO/IEC 27037 forensic write-blockers to extract digital evidence.`  
` 2. Prosecutors file charges under Article 30 (illegal system intrusion) and Article 32 (unauthorized alteration or transmission of electronic documents) of Law No. 1/2024.`  
` 3. The court admits the forensic server logs as valid electronic evidence under Article 5, resulting in a criminal conviction.`

- \*Limitation:\* Prosecution requires identifying the physical or digital perpetrators behind proxy servers, which often demands international mutual legal assistance (MLA).
