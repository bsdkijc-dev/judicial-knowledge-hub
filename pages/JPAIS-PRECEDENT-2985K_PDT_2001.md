# JPAIS:PRECEDENT:2985K PDT 2001

**Supreme Court Decision Reg. No. 2985 K/Pdt/2001** (dated January 29, 2004) is a fundamental civil procedure jurisprudence precedent establishing crucial rules concerning the formal validity of a Defendant's legal standing (*persona standi in judicio*) and formal procedural defects (*error in persona*) resulting from filing lawsuits against committees or institutions that have already been dissolved.

## Executive Summary

The Supreme Court established a primary legal principle:

- **Lawsuits Against Dissolved Entities Are Formally Defective (*Niet Ontvankelijk Verklaard* / NO):** "A lawsuit shall be declared inadmissible when, at the time the lawsuit is filed, the sued subject has already been dissolved beforehand."

Where plaintiffs file a civil lawsuit naming ad-hoc committees or bodies (such as the Regional Election Committee II / PPD II and the Election Supervisory Committee / PANWASLU of Bolaang Mongondow Regency) as Defendants, even though these committees were officially dissolved by law prior to the registration of the lawsuit at the District Court (dissolved on September 14, 1999, whereas the lawsuit was registered on September 27, 1999), the lawsuit suffers from a fatal defect regarding the legal subject and must be declared **Inadmissible** (*Niet Ontvankelijk Verklaard* / NO).

The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition of the Petitioners for Cassation (DPC PSII, DPD PAN, and DPD Partai Golkar of Bolaang Mongondow Regency), annulled Manado High Court Decision No. 173/Pdt/2000/PT.Mdo dated November 20, 2000, *jo.* Kotamobagu District Court Decision No. 42/Pdt.G/1999/PN.Ktg dated May 22, 2000, and independently adjudicated (*mengadili sendiri*) by declaring the Plaintiffs' lawsuit **Inadmissible** (*Niet Ontvankelijk Verklaard* / NO).

## Legal / Procedural Issues

### 1. Formal Validity Requirements for Defendant's Legal Standing (*Legitima Persona Standi in Judicio* *ex* *Error in Persona*)

*Can a civil lawsuit be entertained by a court when the legal subject or ad-hoc body arrayed as a Defendant has been officially dissolved by law prior to the registration of the statement of claim at the District Court Registry?*

**Supreme Court Rule: Inadmissible / Formally Defective.**

A Defendant legal subject must possess valid juridical existence (*persona standi in judicio*) at the exact time a lawsuit is registered. Arraying a defunct or non-existent entity—such as an ad-hoc committee already dissolved—inflicts the lawsuit with a formal procedural defect known as *error in persona* (wrong subject / fictitious defendant). Consequently, courts are mandated to declare the lawsuit **Inadmissible** (*Niet Ontvankelijk Verklaard* / NO) without examining the substantive merits.

### 2. Duty of Judex Facti to Verify Legal Subject Existence Prior to Substantive Adjudication

District and High Courts that ignore exceptions regarding the dissolution of Defendant institutions and proceed to adjudicate the substantive merits commit an error in applying civil procedure law (*error in iudicando*).

## Question of Law vs Question of Fact

### Question of Law (Judex Juris / Supreme Court)

Did the *Judex Facti* (Kotamobagu District Court Decision No. 42/Pdt.G/1999/PN.Ktg *jo.* Manado High Court Decision No. 173/Pdt/2000/PT.Mdo) err in applying civil procedure law (*foutieve toepassing van het procesrecht*) by granting the Plaintiffs' lawsuit, whereas Defendant V (KPU/PPD II Bolaang Mongondow) and Defendant VII (PANWASLU Bolaang Mongondow) had been officially dissolved on September 14, 1999—prior to the registration of the lawsuit on September 27, 1999?

**Supreme Court Legal Answer:** Yes. The *Judex Facti* misapplied the law. Lawsuits filed against dissolved legal subjects are formally defective. The Supreme Court granted the Petitioners' cassation petition, annulled the Manado High Court *jo.* Kotamobagu District Court decisions, and independently adjudicated by declaring the Plaintiffs' lawsuit Inadmissible (*Niet Ontvankelijk Verklaard* / NO).

### Question of Fact (Judex Facti / Kotamobagu District Court & Manado High Court)

Verification of the dissolution date of PPD II Bolaang Mongondow under North Sulawesi PPD I Decree No. 44 of 1999 dated September 14, 1999; verification of the registration date of the Plaintiffs' statement of claim at Kotamobagu District Court on September 27, 1999; examination of PPD II Bolaang Mongondow Plenary Session minutes dated July 19, 1999 No. 310/PPD II-BM/VII/1999; and the allocation of the remaining 7 DPRD II Bolaang Mongondow seats from the 1999 General Election.

## Jurisprudence and Related Knowledge

### Statutory Regulations

- **HIR / RBg:** Provisions concerning Formal Lawsuit Requirements & Legal Subject Exceptions (*Error in Persona* / Non-Existent Defendant).
- **Law No. 3 of 1999 on General Elections:** Provisions governing the structure and operational lifespan of election organizing bodies (KPU, PPI, PPD, Panwaslu).
- **Law No. 14 of 1985 *jo.* Law No. 5 of 2004 *jo.* Law No. 3 of 2009 on the Supreme Court:** Cassation Authority and Independent Adjudication Powers.

### Established Supreme Court Jurisprudence

- **Doctrine of *Persona Standi in Judicio*:** Judicial standing requires Defendants to retain active legal capacity at the time a lawsuit is filed. Ad-hoc bodies whose operational terms have expired and which have been officially dissolved lack *persona standi in judicio*.
- **Established Precedent on Dissolved Legal Subjects:** Supreme Court Decision No. 2985 K/Pdt/2001 represents a landmark precedent establishing that lawsuits filed against bodies, organizations, or committees dissolved prior to suit registration must be declared Inadmissible (NO).

## What Evidence Establishes / Does Not Establish

### Established by Evidence

- Defendant V (PPD II Bolaang Mongondow) and Defendant VII (PANWASLU Bolaang Mongondow) are **PROVEN TO HAVE BEEN OFFICIALLY DISSOLVED PRIOR TO LAWSUIT REGISTRATION** (dissolved on September 14, 1999; lawsuit registered on September 27, 1999).
- The Plaintiffs' lawsuit contains a **FORMAL LEGAL SUBJECT DEFECT** (*Error in Persona*).
- Manado High Court Decision No. 173/Pdt/2000/PT.Mdo and Kotamobagu District Court Decision No. 42/Pdt.G/1999/PN.Ktg are **NULL AND VOID**.
- The Plaintiffs' (DPC PNU & DPD PKB) lawsuit is declared **INADMISSIBLE** (*Niet Ontvankelijk Verklaard* / NO).

### Dismissed / Unconsidered Arguments

- The substantive validity of the local *Stembus Accord* and disputes concerning the allocation of the remaining 7 DPRD II seats **WERE NOT CONSIDERED BY THE SUPREME COURT**, as the lawsuit lapsed upon formal legal-subject testing.

## Uncertainties and Gaps

**Importance of Rigorous Defendant Designation Diligence Involving Ad-Hoc Bodies:** This landmark ruling establishes crucial procedural certainty in Indonesia: plaintiffs must meticulously verify the juridical existence status of entities designated as Defendants. Where disputes involve ad-hoc committees (such as election committees, procurement boards, or independent task forces) that have been dissolved, plaintiffs must not sue the defunct committee, but rather direct claims to the establishing legal entity or the definitive legal subject assuming its statutory responsibilities.

## Frequently Asked Questions (GEO)

Can a civil lawsuit be entertained if the legal subject or institution sued has been dissolved prior to the registration of the lawsuit at the District Court?  
**Inadmissible (NO).** Pursuant to Supreme Court Jurisprudence Reg. No. 2985 K/Pdt/2001:

- A lawsuit shall be declared inadmissible (*Niet Ontvankelijk Verklaard* / NO) if, at the time the lawsuit is filed, the legal subject or institution sued has already been dissolved beforehand.
- Arraying an ad-hoc body or committee (such as PPD II or Panwaslu) officially dissolved prior to the lawsuit registration date constitutes a formal *error in persona* defect.
- The Supreme Court annulled the High Court and District Court decisions and independently adjudicated by declaring the Plaintiffs' lawsuit Inadmissible (NO).
