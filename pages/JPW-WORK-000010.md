#### 4. POLICY ANALYSIS

**Background**: \[FACT\] Young researchers in Spain face working conditions marked by structural job precariousness, including temporary contracts, inadequate pay, and significant career uncertainty. This negatively affects motivation and scientific output, and contributes to a "brain drain" toward countries with more stable conditions.

**Policy Problem**: \[FACT\]

1.  *Historically Unequal Legal Treatment*: The use of scholarship schemes lacking full employment rights (the "grant holder" status under RD 1326/2003), or the assimilation of the predoctoral contract into either the training-contract category (RD 63/2006 & Art. 11 ET) or the project/service contract category (Art. 15 ET), both of which eliminated social protection and severance guarantees.
2.  *Low Economic Remuneration*: The minimum salary percentage scheme (56% in years 1–2, 60% in year 3, and 75% in year 4) is considered disproportionate to the level of qualification and cost of living in university regions.
3.  *Excessive Teaching Load*: Predoctoral researchers being used to cover structural teaching needs beyond the maximum limit of 180 total hours (60 hours per year).
4.  *Disputes over Severance Rights*: Historical legal uncertainty over whether severance pay (*indemnización por extinción*) applies at the end of a predoctoral contract term.

**Legal/Policy Gap**: \[FACT\] The absence of explicit severance-pay rules in the 2011 LCTI and the 2019 EPIPF led to conflicting rulings among Superior Courts of Justice (TSJ of the Basque Country rejecting it versus TSJ of Galicia accepting it), ultimately requiring resolution by the Supreme Court (STS 903/2020) and legislative reform through Law 17/2022. Additionally, a postdoctoral transition period (*orientación postdoctoral*) was previously not accommodated within the predoctoral contract's duration scheme.

**Objectives**: \[FACT\] Reduce job precariousness among young researchers; strengthen legal certainty; align Spanish regulation with European standards (the European Charter for Researchers); guarantee severance pay of 12 days' salary per year worked; and facilitate the transition to the postdoctoral stage.

**Implementation**: \[FACT\]

- *Law 17/2022 on Science & RD 1251/2024*: Explicit recognition of the right to severance pay of 12 days per year worked, under Article 49.1.c of the Workers' Statute, for contracts that end.
- *Royal Decree 1251/2024 (EPIPF)*: Addition of a postdoctoral transition period of up to 12 months (provided the overall 4-year maximum duration is not exceeded), adjustment of the minimum salary reference to Group M3 of the AGE Single Collective Agreement, contract extension for reduced working hours due to family reconciliation, adjustment of the social security contribution bonus (€115/month, RDL 1/2023), and a mandatory anti-harassment/violence protocol.
- *Application of the LOSU (Organic Law 2/2023)*: Expansion of predoctoral contract allocation into the industrial sector via the "industrial-mention doctorate" (Art. 9.7 LOSU).

**Evidence & Impact**: \[FACT\] Recent jurisprudence (STSJ Castilla y León, 6 May 2024 & 30 September 2024; STSJ Madrid, 21 March 2024) confirmed the non-retroactivity of severance pay: the right applies only to contracts still active as of 7 September 2022, or those signed thereafter.

**Reform & Unresolved Issues**: \[FACT\] The lack of an automatic continuity guarantee into the postdoctoral stage, which contributes to fragile scientific careers; limited university budgets to absorb managed researchers; and insufficient oversight of maximum teaching-hour limits.

6.  **Policy Implementation**: What key updates did Royal Decree 1251/2024 introduce to the EPIPF?
    - *Factual Answer*: \[FACT\] Recognition of a postdoctoral transition period (up to 12 months), integration with the LOSU (industrial doctorate), a Group M3 salary reference, severance pay upon contract termination, extension due to family reconciliation, a €115/month contribution bonus, and an anti-harassment protocol.
7.  **Policy Problem**: Why did conflicting jurisprudence arise among Superior Courts of Justice prior to the enactment of Law 17/2022?
    - *Factual Answer*: \[FACT\] Because the 2011 LCTI and the 2019 EPIPF did not contain explicit severance-pay rules, the TSJ of the Basque Country rejected severance pay (treating it as a special training contract), while the TSJ of Galicia accepted it (assimilating it to a project/service contract).
8.  **Legal Relationship**: What did the Supreme Court rule in STS 903/2020, of 13 October, regarding severance pay for predoctoral contracts?
    - *Factual Answer*: \[FACT\] The Supreme Court ruled that the predoctoral contract was a training contract equivalent to a practical-training contract (Art. 11 ET), and that at the time, the legislature had not established a severance-pay right upon contract termination.
9.  **Reform**: How did Law 17/2022, of 5 September, resolve the debate over predoctoral researchers' severance-pay rights?
    - *Factual Answer*: \[FACT\] Law 17/2022 formally recognized the right to severance pay of 12 days' salary per year worked, under Article 49.1.c of the Workers' Statute, for predoctoral researchers.
10. **Evidence/Case Study**: How was the non-retroactivity rule for severance pay applied under 2024 jurisprudence from the TSJ of Castilla y León and the TSJ of Madrid?
    - *Factual Answer*: \[FACT\] Severance pay applies only to contracts that were still active when Law 17/2022 took effect (7 September 2022), or those signed thereafter; contracts that ended before that date do not carry a right to severance pay.

#### 6. MEDIAWIKI OUTPUT

## Summary

**The Job Precariousness of Young Researchers: Regulatory, Doctrinal, and Jurisprudential Treatment of Predoctoral Research Trainees in Spain** is a labor law study written by Aída Cabello Roldán of Universidad de Málaga (UMA). This scholarly work provides an in-depth analysis of job precariousness among young researchers in Spain. The study evaluates the transformation of predoctoral contract regulation from a historical scholarship scheme into an independent employment relationship (Law 14/2011 LCTI, Law 17/2022, and Organic Law 2/2023 LOSU), the reform of the Statute of Predoctoral Research Trainees in Training (EPIPF, via Royal Decree 1251/2024), recognition of severance-pay rights (12 days per year), the postdoctoral transition period, and the evolving jurisprudence determining employment rights within the university sector.

## Legal Basis

The regulations and legal reforms analyzed in this publication are grounded in the following Spanish and EU legal framework:

- **Law 14/2011, of 1 June, on Science, Technology and Innovation (LCTI)**: Article 21 (initial regulation of the predoctoral contract as an independent modality).
- **Law 17/2022, of 5 September**: Reform of the LCTI explicitly recognizing severance-pay rights upon contract termination.
- **Royal Decree 103/2019, of 1 March (EPIPF)**: The initial framework for the Statute of Predoctoral Research Trainees in Training.
- **Royal Decree 1251/2024, of 10 December**: Reform of the EPIPF refining remuneration rights, the postdoctoral transition period, and social protections.
- **Royal Legislative Decree 2/2015 (Workers' Statute — ET)**: Article 11 (training contracts), Article 15, and Article 49.1.c (severance pay for fixed-term contracts).
- **Organic Law 2/2023, of 22 March (LOSU)**: Article 9.7 (integration of industrial doctorates).
- **Recommendation 2005/251/EC**: The European Charter for Researchers and Code of Conduct for the Recruitment of Researchers.

## Background

The scientific labor market for young researchers in Spain has historically been marked by high contractual uncertainty, salaries disproportionate to high qualifications, and a lack of career certainty following the doctorate. This has delayed financial independence and contributed to a "brain drain" phenomenon, with young talent moving abroad.

## Objectives

1\. Strengthen legal certainty and employment protection for predoctoral researchers. 2. Clearly distinguish the predoctoral contract from scholarship schemes or ordinary training contracts. 3. Guarantee fair remuneration and a severance-pay right of 12 days per year worked. 4. Facilitate the transition to a postdoctoral career through a postdoctoral orientation period. 5. Limit teaching loads so as not to interfere with completion of the doctoral thesis.

## Scope

The study covers in-depth analysis of four main pillars: 1. *Indicators of Precariousness*: Youth unemployment rates, delayed labor-market entry, and barriers to emancipation. 2. *Regulatory Evolution*: The transition from RD 1326/2003 (scholarship holder) through RD 63/2006 (training contract), the 2011 LCTI, Law 17/2022, to RD 1251/2024. 3. *Provisions of RD 1251/2024*: Alignment with the LOSU, the postdoctoral transition period (12 months), the Group M3 salary reference, extension due to family reconciliation, and the €115/month contribution bonus. 4. *Jurisprudential Analysis*: The severance-pay debate at the TSJ of the Basque Country, the TSJ of Galicia, STS 903/2020, and the enforcement of Law 17/2022's non-retroactivity by the TSJ of Castilla y León and the TSJ of Madrid.

## Key Provisions

- **Independent Nature of the Predoctoral Contract**: Configured as a special employment relationship with a maximum duration of 4 years, full-time.
- **Postdoctoral Transition Period**: RD 1251/2024 permits an extension of up to 12 months post-doctorate to prepare for career transition.
- **Teaching Limit**: Teaching duties capped at a maximum of 180 hours over the total contract period (maximum 60 hours/year).
- **Severance Pay (Indemnización)**: Set at 12 days' salary per year worked, under Article 49.1.c of the Workers' Statute, following Law 17/2022 and RD 1251/2024.
- **Principle of Severance-Pay Non-Retroactivity**: Severance pay applies only to contracts active as of 7 September 2022, or signed thereafter.

## Implementation

Implementation is carried out by universities and public/private research centers, with social-court oversight and adjustment of internal university regulations based on the LOSU.

## Policy Problems

- Still-limited schemes for absorbing postdoctoral talent into stable positions, contributing to ongoing career fragility.
- Insufficient university operating budgets to support the consolidation of permanent positions.
- Inadequate oversight of violations of maximum teaching-load limits at some faculties.

## Reform

- Enactment of Law 17/2022, of 5 September (reform of the Science Law).
- Enactment of Royal Decree 1251/2024, of 10 December (reform of the EPIPF).
- Enactment of Organic Law 2/2023 (LOSU).

## Relationships with Other Entities

- **Implements**: Law 14/2011 (LCTI, as amended by Law 17/2022) & Organic Law 2/2023 (LOSU).
- **Applies To**: Predoctoral research trainees in training at universities and research institutions.
- **Replaces**: The former "training contract" / "project-or-service contract" scheme for young researchers.
