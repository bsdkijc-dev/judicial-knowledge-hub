# Judicial-policy-transitional-justice-culture-ramirez-barat-2014

**The Transitional Justice, Culture, and Public Sphere Policy Framework** is a transitional justice policy framework and public sphere relational model formulated in a collective work edited by Clara Ramírez-Barat (ICTJ & SSRC, 2014) and critically reviewed by Elise Ketelaars (Utrecht School of Law, 2014) in the *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA). It establishes the doctrine that the success of transitional justice institutions (human rights courts, truth and reconciliation commissions/TRCs) depends not merely on completing formal juridical functions (delivering judgments, writing reports), but is heavily determined by interactions with culture, media, art, and the active involvement of society within the public sphere to foster civic trust and public legitimacy \[1–7, 17, 19\].

## Summary

Written by Elise Ketelaars in the *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA), this policy review evaluates the book \*Transitional Justice, Culture and Society: Beyond Outreach\*, edited by Clara Ramírez-Barat (ICTJ/SSRC). The policy critiques the "institutionalist bias" that focuses solely on formal courts and truth commissions. According to Paulo de Greiff and Ramírez-Barat, the success of transitional justice is highly dependent on cultural transformation and active public participation in the public sphere. Through case studies such as the South African TRC testimonies (Catherine M. Cole) and transitional justice photography in Argentina and Bosnia (González Cueva & Librizzi), this framework proves that independent artworks and journalism can prevent justice from being narrowed down into a dry set of standard rules (*juridicization and desiccation of best practices*) \[10, 12–17\].

## Legal Basis

- **UN Transitional Justice Framework & Mandate of the UN Special Rapporteur:** The UN framework regarding the right to truth, justice, reparation, and guarantees of non-recurrence.
- **South African Truth and Reconciliation Commission Act & International Tribunals Mandates:** The legal foundations for establishing truth commissions and special courts.
- **Advancing Transitional Justice Series (ICTJ & SSRC):** An international policy publication series on transitional justice.
- **Not found in the available sources:** Other specific formal statutory regulations are not mentioned in the source text \[FACT\].

## Background

Traditional transitional justice approaches have overly glorified official state institutions. Without the involvement of the public sphere and culture, courts and truth commissions fail to build enduring public trust.

## Objectives

1\. Expand the scope of transitional justice beyond the official outreach functions of institutions (*beyond outreach*). 2. Build civic trust through communication within the public sphere. 3. Avoid the dangers of juridicization and the dry homogenization (*juridicization and desiccation*) of transitional justice. 4. Utilize art, theater, and photography as rich, destabilizing instruments for revealing the truth \[13–17\].

## Scope

This policy covers international transitional justice case studies in South Africa, Bosnia, and Argentina, as well as the role of mass media, art, and post-conflict photography exhibitions.

## Key Provisions

- **Critique of Institutionalist Bias**:
  - The success of transitional justice is not sufficiently achieved by merely finalizing reports or delivering judgments; it requires transformation at the cultural and personal levels.
- **Two-Way Interaction (Communicating Vessels)**:
  - Official outreach programs and public sphere communication work interdependently to build trust in institutions and among citizens.
- **The Destabilizing Function of Art and Photography**:
  - Independent artworks and photography (such as the \*Absences\* series in Argentina) provide dimensions of interpretation that revive public memory and prevent the rigidity of formal legal norms \[16–17\].

## Implementation

- **Adaptation of South African TRC Testimonies:** Processing the testimony of Father Michael Lapsley into Antjie Krog’s literary memoir and Philip Miller’s musical cantata performance \[12–13\].
- **Use of Transitional Photography:** Utilizing fact-revealing photographs in Bosnia and visual exhibitions depicting the absence of victims in Argentina \[15–17\].

## Policy Problems

- **Risk of Juridicization**: The threat of transitional justice becoming a mere bureaucratic routine and a list of formal norms stripped of human sensitivity.
- **Manipulation of Images and Testimonies**: The risk of media editing or accusations of fabricated photographic evidence by specific political or ultra-nationalist groups.

## Reform / Change

- Formally integrating the public sphere and cultural projects into the research and policy planning agendas of transitional justice.
- Full protection of the independence of artists, journalists, and researchers in post-conflict nations \[13–14\].
