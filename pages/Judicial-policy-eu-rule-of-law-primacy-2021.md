# Judicial-policy-eu-rule-of-law-primacy-2021

The **Judicial Independence, Primacy of EU Law, and Rule of Law Mechanism Framework** is a European legal policy and constitutional doctrine framework that mandates all European Union (EU) Member States to ensure national judicial independence and uphold the primacy of EU law over conflicting domestic legislation and constitutional provisions.

## Overview

Authored by [Filippo Donati](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Filippo_Donati), Full Professor of Constitutional Law at the [University of Florence](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/University_of_Florence), this editorial analysis evaluates the escalating jurisdictional conflict between the Court of Justice of the European Union (CJEU) and the Constitutional Courts of several Member States (notably Poland and Romania) regarding judicial independence and the hierarchy of EU law [^1]. The CJEU affirmed that Article 19(1) TEU and Article 47 of the Charter of Fundamental Rights (CFR) possess direct effect, requiring national courts to disapply domestic rules—including national constitutional provisions—if they breach judicial independence [^2].

## Legal Basis

The framework relies on key European Union legal instruments:

- **[Article 19(1) TEU](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Article_19%281%29_TEU) & [Article 47 CFR](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Article_47_CFR)**: Mandates Member States to provide effective judicial protection and guarantees independent courts in fields covered by EU law [^3].
- **[Article 2 TEU](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Article_2_TEU) & [Article 4(3) TEU](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Article_4%283%29_TEU)**: Establishes the foundational values of the Rule of Law and the principle of sincere cooperation [^4].
- **[Regulation (EU, Euratom) 2020/2092](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/EU_Budget_Conditionality_Regulation)**: Establishes general rules for a conditionality regime to protect the EU budget from systemic Rule of Law breaches [^5].
- **[Decision 2006/928](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Romanian_CVM_Mechanism)**: Cooperation and Verification Mechanism for monitoring judicial reform and anticorruption progress in Romania [^6].

## Background

Landmark CJEU jurisprudence—starting with *Associação Sindical dos Juízes Portugueses* (C-64/16), *A.K. and Others* (Joined Cases C-585/18 et al.), and *A.B. and Others* (C-824/18)—triggered a severe constitutional backlash in Poland and Romania [^7]. The Polish Constitutional Tribunal (in Cases P 7/20 and K 3/21) declared an ECtHR judgment (\*Xero Flor\*) "non-existent" and held Articles 1, 2, 4(3), and 19(1) TEU unconstitutional to the extent they demanded primacy of EU law over the Polish Constitution [^8].

## Objectives

- Ensure courts across all EU Member States satisfy uniform standards of independence and impartiality [^9].
- Uphold the principle of primacy of EU law over domestic legislative and constitutional rules that infringe fundamental rights [^10].
- Leverage financial sanctions (\*financial leverage\*) to safeguard EU budget funds from democratic backsliding [^11].

## Scope

The scope of this framework covers European constitutional law, national judicial independence, infringement procedures, and financial conditionality enforcement across EU jurisdictions—including Poland, Romania, Hungary, and Portugal [^12].

## Key Provisions & Jurisprudential Interpretations

- **Direct Effect & Primacy of EU Law**: National courts are obligated to disapply any domestic legislative provision or constitutional ruling that undermines judicial independence [^13].
- **Prohibition of Disciplinary Sanctions against Judges**: EU law prohibits subjecting national judges to disciplinary proceedings or penalties for applying EU law or assessing national law compatibility with EU law (\*RS\* Case C-430/21) [^14].
- **Financial Conditionality Instrument**: Compliance with Rule of Law standards is a prerequisite for receiving EU budget disbursements (Upheld in CJEU Twin Judgments C-156/21 and C-157/21) [^15].

## Implementation

Operationalized through European Commission infringement actions, daily financial penalties imposed by the CJEU (such as the €1 million daily penalty against Poland regarding its Disciplinary Chamber), and the freeze of EU budget allocations under Conditionality Regulation (EU, Euratom) 2020/2092 [^16].

## Policy Issues

- **Article 7 TEU Unanimity Obstacle**: Sanction mechanisms under Article 7(2) TEU are politically paralyzed because targeted Member States (Poland, Hungary) use mutual vetoes to shield each other [^17].
- **Domestic Constitutional Defiance**: Direct refusal by national constitutional courts to abide by binding rulings of the CJEU and the ECtHR [^18].

## Proposed Reforms

The European Commission is urged to combine rigorous new infringement procedures with immediate financial leverage under the Guidelines of March 2, 2022 (C(2022) 1382 final) on the Conditionality Regulation [^19].
