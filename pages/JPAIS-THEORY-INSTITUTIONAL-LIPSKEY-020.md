#### 1. Regulatory Framework, Institutional Isomorphism, and Professional Diversity

\[SOURCE EVIDENCE\]  
Civil conciliation commissioners (Anggota Majelis Chōtei) at the Tokyo Summary Court are formally appointed by the Supreme Court of Japan (Mahkamah Agung Japan) through bi-annual appointment cycles every April and October<sup>5,6</sup>. Under the Civil Conciliation Act, each Conciliation Panel (Majelis Chōtei) consists of 3 members: 1 Presiding Judge and 2 Lay Commissioners<sup>2</sup>. Due to judicial workload constraints, parts of the conciliation sessions are frequently conducted directly by the 2 lay commissioners<sup>2</sup>.

\[EMPIRICAL EVIDENCE\]  
Empirical data from Tokyo Summary Court (Sumida Office, April 2026) reveals a highly specialized roster of 404 total commissioners (including 101 women, representing a 25.0% female ratio)<sup>2,3</sup>. The professional breakdown comprises:

- Practicing Advocates (Bengoshi): 182 persons (45.0%)<sup>3</sup>
- Real Estate Appraisers (Fudōsan Kanteishi): 47 persons (12.0%)<sup>3</sup>
- Tax Accountants (Zeirishi): 17 persons (4.2%)<sup>3</sup>
- Architects (Kenchikushi): 13 persons (3.2%)<sup>3</sup>
- Medical Doctors & Dentists: 11 persons (2.7%)<sup>3</sup>
- Labor Consultants (Sharōshi): 9 persons (2.2%)<sup>3</sup>
- Certified Public Accountants (Kōnin Kaikeishi): 7 persons (1.7%)<sup>3</sup>
- Surveyors & Judicial Scriveners (Shihōshoshi): 6 persons (1.5%)<sup>3,4</sup>
- Retired / Unemployed / Others: 110 persons (27.0%)<sup>3</sup>

\[THEORETICAL INFERENCE\]  
In Institutional Theory terms, this professional diversity creates normative isomorphism. Instead of relying solely on generalist career judges or pure legal formalists, the Japanese Summary Court injects domain-specific technical authority (e.g., real estate appraisers for rent disputes, architects for construction defects, tax accountants for corporate asset divisions) directly into pre-trial conciliation<sup>3,4</sup>.

\[ANALYTICAL SYNTHESIS\]  
In Indonesia, court-annexed mediation under PERMA No. 1 of 2016 suffers from severe institutional decoupling. Non-judge mediator rosters are dominated by generalist certified mediators who lack technical domain specialization, leading to high failure rates (~96% in civil lawsuits). Adapting the Tokyo Summary Court model into Indonesia's Supreme Court Regulation (RaPerMA) requires establishing specialized lay professional rosters (injecting certified real estate appraisers, accountants, and engineers) to build substantive credibility during court conciliation.

#### 2. Judicial / Institutional Structure & Organizational Capacity (5M Framework)

Applying the 5M Framework to the Tokyo Summary Court training presentation illuminates the organizational mechanics of commissioner capacity building:

**Man (Human Capital & Professional Mentorship)**

- \[SOURCE EVIDENCE\]: The system relies on a dual-layer mentorship culture: senior commissioners actively guide junior, less experienced commissioners during live cases<sup>7</sup>, while commissioners continuously absorb legal reasoning, judicial mindsets, and communication techniques directly from presiding judges<sup>7</sup>.
- \[THEORETICAL INFERENCE\]: In Organizational Sociology, structured senior-junior peer mentorship transfers tacit negotiation knowledge that formal classroom training cannot replicate, building institutional resilience across commissioner cohorts.

**Method (Procedural Methodology & Experiential Training Architecture)**

- \[SOURCE EVIDENCE\]: Conciliation operations follow a Standard Three-Session Pipeline (*Model Standar Mencegah Berlarut-larutnya Perkara*)<sup>4</sup>:
  - **Session 1 (Stage 1):** Information gathering, reviewing party statements/briefs, pre-session roadmap formulation, and instructing supplementary document submissions<sup>1,4</sup>.
  - **Session 2 (Stage 2):** Defining solution directions, constructing a framework of common ground, and aligning perception between parties<sup>4,8</sup>.
  - **Session 3 (Stage 3):** Active persuasion, explaining settlement benefits, and final coordination leading to agreement or formal impasse declaration<sup>2</sup>.
- \[SOURCE EVIDENCE\]: Commissioner capacity is continuously refined through a Continuous Experiential Learning Cycle combining OJT and OFF-JT<sup>4</sup>:
  - **On-the-Job Training (OJT):** Executed during live Chōtei sessions and pre/post-session judicial deliberations (*musyawarah awal & akhir*)<sup>1</sup>. Commissioners practice four core steps: ① Listening to party statements; ② Understanding case facts and structuring issues; ③ Formulating fair solution proposals; and ④ Explaining/persuading parties<sup>8</sup>.
  - **Off-the-Job Training (OFF-JT):** Classical seminars and specialized study groups (e.g., *Forum Studi Case Management*) featuring case study discussions (analyzing recurring procedural obstacles) and roleplay simulations (simulating party-mediator interviews to hone communication techniques)<sup>9</sup>.

**Material (Infrastructure & Onboarding Facilities)**

- \[SOURCE EVIDENCE\]: Newly appointed commissioners undergo structured onboarding prior to active service<sup>5</sup>:
  1.  Lectures by judges on civil procedure summaries and ethical codes of conduct (*pedoman perilaku*)<sup>5</sup>;
  2.  Lectures by senior commissioners on mediator professional stance (*sikap kerja*)<sup>5</sup>;
  3.  Mandatory live session observation (*menyaksikan hari sesi Chōtei*)<sup>5</sup>; and
  4.  Facility orientation (navigating Chōtei rooms, waiting rooms, member lounges, attendance verification, and time management)<sup>5</sup>.

**Money (State Budget & Systemic Cost Reduction)**

- \[THEORETICAL INFERENCE\]: Capping conciliation at three structured sessions prevents multi-year trial drag, delivering massive operational cost savings for the court system and commercial litigants.

**Machine / Technology (Digital Integration & Time Management)**

- \[SOURCE EVIDENCE\]: Onboarding explicitly trains commissioners in administrative time management and attendance tracking protocols<sup>5</sup>.

**Legal Culture & Social Structure**

- \[SOURCE EVIDENCE\]: Japanese summary court conciliation reflects a societal culture that highly values peer-led community problem-solving backed by professional expertise<sup>3,7</sup>. By incorporating non-lawyer professionals (27% retired/others, 12% appraisers, 4% tax consultants), the court bridges formal statutory law with everyday commercial and social reality<sup>3</sup>.

**Street-Level Bureaucracy vs. Judicial/Mediator Discretion**

- \[THEORETICAL INFERENCE\]: Lipsky's Street-Level Bureaucracy framework requires distinguishing frontline administrative staff from lay commissioners:
  - **Court Administrative Clerks (Panitera/Clerks):** Manage initial attendance verification, schedule room allocations, track time management logs, and handle orientation logistics<sup>5</sup>. Their discretion is purely administrative.
  - **Conciliation Commissioners (Anggota Majelis Chōtei):** Possess evaluative mediation discretion. Commissioners exercise discretion when caucusing with parties, questioning factual statements during Stage 1<sup>4</sup>, structuring issue frameworks in Stage 2<sup>4</sup>, and applying persuasion techniques during Stage 3<sup>4,8</sup>. Pre/post-session judicial deliberations (*musyawarah*) ensure that commissioner discretion remains anchored within statutory legal boundaries<sup>1</sup>.

#### 4. Implementation & Business Experience

- \[SOURCE EVIDENCE\]: Unmanaged, protracted litigation creates crippling financial uncertainty for commercial enterprises.
- \[THEORETICAL INFERENCE\]: Enforcing a standard 3-session conciliation limit, deploying multi-disciplinary expert panels (advocates, appraisers, accountants), and continuously training mediators through OJT/OFF-JT cycles provides commercial litigants with rapid, highly predictable, and technically accurate dispute resolution.

### C. Synthesis of Evidence vs. Epistemological Uncertainty

**What is Firmly Supported by Source Evidence:**

- Conciliation Commissioners are appointed bi-annually (April and October) by the Supreme Court of Japan<sup>5,6</sup>.
- Conciliation Panels consist of 3 members: 1 Presiding Judge and 2 Lay Commissioners; lay commissioners often lead sessions directly when judges are unavailable<sup>2</sup>.
- Tokyo Summary Court (April 2026) maintains a 404-commissioner roster with 101 women (25.0% female ratio)<sup>2,3</sup>.
- Roster composition: 45% Advocates, 12% Real Estate Appraisers, 6% CPAs/Tax Accountants, 3% Architects, 3% Medical Doctors, 2% Labor Consultants, 2.5% Surveyors/Scriveners, 27% Retired/Others<sup>3,4</sup>.
- Operations follow a Standard 3-Session Pipeline (Stage 1: Info Gathering; Stage 2: Solution Framework; Stage 3: Persuasion/Agreement)<sup>4</sup>.
- Mediator training utilizes a Dual Experiential Cycle: OJT (pre/post-session deliberations, live issue structuring, senior-junior mentorship) and OFF-JT (case study discussions, roleplay simulations, specialized Case Management forums)<sup>4</sup>.
- New Commissioner Onboarding includes judge lectures on procedure/ethics, senior commissioner lectures on stance, live session observation, and facility/time management orientation<sup>5</sup>.

### D. Policy Recommendations for Indonesian Civil Procedure Reform (RaPerMA)

Based on the Tokyo Summary Court case study:

1.  **Mandate Specialized Lay Professional Roster Accreditation:** Amend Supreme Court Regulations (RaPerMA) to require court-annexed mediator rosters to include certified non-lawyer technical professionals (real estate appraisers, public accountants, architectural engineers, labor experts) alongside advocates.
2.  **Institutionalize a Standard 3-Session Mediation Pipeline:** Codify a strict 3-session operational model for court mediation (Session 1: Info/Roadmap → Session 2: Common Ground → Session 3: Persuasion/Final Agreement) to prevent open-ended mediation drag.
3.  **Establish a Dual OJT/OFF-JT Mediator Training System:** Reform BSDK Kumdil mediator training by making live session observation, pre/post-session judicial deliberations, roleplay interview simulations, and senior-junior mentorship mandatory prerequisites for mediator certification.
4.  **Implement Structured New Mediator Onboarding:** Require all newly appointed mediators to complete a standardized orientation covering judicial ethics, code of conduct (*pedoman perilaku*), court facility navigation, and time management.
