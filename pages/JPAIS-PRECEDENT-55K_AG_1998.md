## Overview

Supreme Court Decision No. 55 K/AG/1998 dated July 29, 1999, establishes two fundamental legal principles in Islamic Civil Law and Islamic Civil Procedure Law:

- **Conditions for Proving a Grant (\*Hibah\*):** In a lawsuit concerning a grant, a grant may be declared void or invalid if the grantee fails to prove with concrete evidence that the disputed property or land was genuinely granted to them.
- **Exception of Defective Parties (\*Plurium Litis Consortium\*):** A lawsuit concerning a legal act of granting a single estate property claimed to have been granted to 8 (eight) grandchildren, where the Plaintiff sues only 1 (one) grandchild without joining the remaining 6 (six) grandchildren or other heirs as parties to the action, renders the lawsuit obscure and defective due to missing parties. Consequently, the Defendant's exception regarding \*plurium litis consortium\* must be sustained, and the Plaintiff's lawsuit must be declared inadmissible (\*niet ontvankelijke verklaard / NO\*).

## Case Background

The Original Plaintiff / Respondent for Cassation (\[Party C\]) sued her sibling, the Original Defendant / Petitioner for Cassation (\[Party B\]), at the Praya Religious Court. The Plaintiff argued that their late grandfather (\[Party A\], who passed away in 1980) during his lifetime, in the presence of the Head of Durian Village, granted a 0.500 ha (50 \*are\*) rice field plot located in Orong Pedape, Durian Village, Janaperia Subdistrict, Central Lombok, to 8 (eight) of his grandchildren. According to the Plaintiff's claim, the Defendant and Plaintiff each received a 0.2500 ha share, while the remainder was allocated to the 6 other grandchildren. The Plaintiff claimed her share of the land was controlled by the Defendant and transferred into the Defendant's name in 1984 without her consent.

The Defendant raised an exception arguing that the land was purchased through his own efforts and that the Plaintiff's lawsuit suffered from a defect of missing parties (\*plurium litis consortium\*) because it failed to join the other 6 grandchildren and heirs of the late \[Party A\]. The Praya Religious Court (Decision No. 302/Pdt.G/1995/PA.Pra dated January 11, 1996) and the Mataram High Religious Court (Decision No. 72/Pdt.G/1996/PTA.MTR dated January 6, 1997) rejected the Defendant's exception and granted the Plaintiff's lawsuit. Dissatisfied with these rulings, the Defendant filed a cassation petition to the Supreme Court.

## Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

- **Ratio Decidendi:**

1.  In a lawsuit concerning a Grant, a Grant may be declared void if the grantee fails to prove conclusively that the property was actually granted to them.
2.  The Mataram High Religious Court and the Praya Religious Court committed a legal flaw by issuing rulings lacking adequate legal reasoning (\*onvoldoende gemotiveerd\*) because they failed to consider the Defendant's exception regarding missing parties / failure to join all heirs and recipients of \[Party A\]'s grant.
3.  Because the Plaintiff sued over land alleged to have been granted to 8 (eight) grandchildren of \[Party A\], to ascertain whether a grant actually occurred, the 6 other grandchildren of \[Party A\] must necessarily be joined as parties. The failure to join all grant recipients and heirs renders the lawsuit defective due to missing parties (\*plurium litis consortium\*).
4.  On this basis, the Defendant's exception is admissible, and the Plaintiff's lawsuit must be declared inadmissible (\*niet ontvankelijke verklaard\*).

- **Ruling:** Granting the cassation petition from Petitioner for Cassation \[Party B\]; Annulling the Mataram High Religious Court Decision No. 72/Pdt.G/1996/PTA.MTR dated January 6, 1997, and the Praya Religious Court Decision No. 302/Pdt.G/1995/PA.Pra dated January 11, 1996; and Independently Adjudicating: (1) IN EXCEPTION: Declaring that the Defendant's exception is admissible; (2) IN THE MERITS: Declaring the Plaintiff's lawsuit inadmissible (\*niet ontvankelijke verklaard\*); Ordering the Plaintiff to pay procedural costs across all levels of jurisdiction (assessed at Rp 50,000 at the cassation level).
