**Supreme Court Decision No. 1295 K/Pid/1985** is an Indonesian criminal-law precedent concerning the proof of the element of intent (*opzet* / *dolus*) in the offence of murder under Article 338 of the Criminal Code (KUHP), and the distinction between murder and maltreatment resulting in death under Article 351(3) KUHP.

The decision establishes that intent to deprive another person of life can be proven from the type of instrument used and the location on the victim's body that was wounded, and that proof of intent does not require repeated stabs or the victim's instant death at the scene. It also confirms that an acquittal of a primary charge based on a mistaken interpretation of intent is a non-pure acquittal (*niet-zuivere vrijspraak*) against which the Public Prosecutor may file cassation.

## Executive Summary

Supreme Court Decision Reg. No. **1295 K/Pid/1985** (dated 2 January 1986) sets out a fundamental rule of substantive criminal law on **proof of the element of intent (*opzet* / *dolus*) in the offence of murder (Article 338 KUHP), based on the instrument of the crime and the vital organ of the victim that was attacked**.

Intent to deprive another person of life (*opzet* under Article 338 KUHP) can be proven legally and convincingly from the type of instrument used to commit the offence (a lethal kitchen knife) and the location on the victim's body wounded by that instrument (the left chest, penetrating the vital organs of the heart and lungs). The Supreme Court expressly rejected the *Judex Facti* reasoning that intent to kill must be proven by several stabs or by instant death at the scene: for an expert, a single stab accurately aimed at a vital organ is sufficient to kill, and the fact that the victim did not die instantly does not eliminate the intent to kill.

The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition of the Public Prosecutor at the West Jakarta District Prosecutor's Office, annulled DKI Jakarta High Court Decision No. 61/Pid/1985/PT.DKI and West Jakarta District Court Decision No. 046/JB./Pid./1984/PN.JKT.BAR, and, adjudicating the case itself, **declared the Defendant (\[Defendant\]) guilty of murder under Article 338 KUHP and sentenced him to 12 (twelve) years' imprisonment**.

## Case Background

The Defendant, \[Defendant\] (a vehicle assistant), was charged primarily under Article 338 KUHP (murder) and subsidiarily under Article 351(3) KUHP (maltreatment resulting in death). The charge arose because on 18 August 1984 in Gang Jamblang I, West Jakarta, he stabbed the victim \[Victim\] in the left chest with a kitchen knife, penetrating the heart and lungs to a depth of 15 cm and causing the victim's death.

The West Jakarta District Court acquitted the Defendant of the primary charge (Article 338) and sentenced him to five years' imprisonment under the subsidiary charge (Article 351(3)), reasoning that he stabbed only once and the victim did not die instantly.

The DKI Jakarta High Court affirmed the decision while increasing the sentence to seven years' imprisonment. The Public Prosecutor at the West Jakarta District Prosecutor's Office then filed a cassation petition with the Supreme Court.

## Procedural History

### First Instance: West Jakarta District Court

The West Jakarta District Court decided Case No. 046/JB./Pid./1984/PN.JKT.BAR on 5 February 1985. It acquitted the Defendant of the primary charge (Article 338 KUHP) and sentenced him to five years' imprisonment under the subsidiary charge (Article 351(3) KUHP).

### Appeal: DKI Jakarta High Court

The DKI Jakarta High Court decided Appeal No. 61/Pid/1985/PT.DKI on 17 June 1985. It affirmed the first-instance decision and increased the sentence to seven years' imprisonment.

### Cassation: Supreme Court

The Public Prosecutor filed a cassation petition. The Supreme Court accepted and granted the petition, annulled both lower-court decisions, adjudicated the case itself, and convicted the Defendant of murder under Article 338 KUHP.

## Legal Issues

The case raised the following principal legal issues:

1.  **Proof of the element of intent (*opzet* / *dolus*) in murder (Article 338 KUHP) through objective indicators (lethal instrument and vital organ of the victim):** whether stabbing a kitchen knife into the victim's left chest, penetrating the vital organs of the heart and lungs, satisfies the element of intent to kill under Article 338 KUHP, or amounts only to maltreatment resulting in death (Article 351(3) KUHP) because there was a single stab and the victim did not die instantly at the scene.
2.  **Admissibility of the Public Prosecutor's cassation petition against a non-pure acquittal (*niet-zuivere vrijspraak*, Article 244 KUHAP):** whether the Public Prosecutor may file cassation against a *Judex Facti* decision acquitting the Defendant of the primary charge (Article 338 KUHP) and convicting him only under the subsidiary charge (Article 351(3) KUHP).

## Supreme Court's Reasoning

### 1. Admissibility of Cassation against a Non-Pure Acquittal

The acquittal of the Defendant from the primary charge (Article 338 KUHP) by the *Judex Facti*, based on a mistaken interpretation of the element of intent, is in essence a non-pure acquittal (*niet-zuivere vrijspraak*). In accordance with the Supreme Court's jurisprudence on Article 244 KUHAP, the Supreme Court has full authority at the cassation level to assess and annul such an acquittal so as to enforce the law correctly. The Public Prosecutor's petition was therefore admissible.

### 2. Intent Can Be Proven from the Instrument and the Body Location

Intent to deprive another person of life can be proven by the instrument used to commit the offence and the place on the victim's body that was wounded by that instrument.

### 3. The Kitchen Knife and the Vital Organs Struck

A kitchen knife is a lethal instrument (a *notoir feit*, i.e. a matter of common knowledge). The stab to the victim's left chest penetrated the fifth rib, the left lung, the pericardium and the right lung to a depth of 15 cm, which proves the intent to kill.

### 4. Rejection of the Repeated-Stab and Instant-Death Requirement

The Supreme Court did not agree with the *Judex Facti* view that intent to kill must be proven by several stabs. For an expert, a single stab accurately aimed at a vital organ is sufficient to kill. Nor does the fact that the victim did not die instantly mean that the Defendant lacked intent.

### 5. The Lower Courts Misapplied the Law

The *Judex Facti* (the DKI Jakarta High Court and the West Jakarta District Court) erred in applying the law (*foutieve toepassing van het recht*) in qualifying the element of intent to kill.

### 6. Consequence: The Primary Charge Was Proven

All elements of Article 338 KUHP were proven legally and convincingly. The Supreme Court therefore annulled the lower-court decisions and, adjudicating the case itself, convicted the Defendant of murder and sentenced him to 12 (twelve) years' imprisonment.

## Ratio Decidendi

The core *ratio decidendi* may be stated as follows:

> **Intent to deprive another person of life (*opzet* under Article 338 KUHP) can be proven legally and convincingly from the type of instrument used (a lethal kitchen knife) and the location on the victim's body that was wounded (the left chest, penetrating the vital organs of the heart and lungs). Proof of intent does not require several stabs or the victim's instant death: for an expert, a single stab accurately aimed at a vital organ suffices, and the victim's not dying at once does not eliminate the intent to kill.**

The Supreme Court's considerations (p. 30), as recorded in the source, read in the original Indonesian:

> "Kesengajaan untuk menghilangkan nyawa orang lain dapat dibuktikan dengan alat yang dipergunakan untuk melakukan tindak pidana tersebut dan tempat pada badan korban yang dilukai alat itu."

In English: *Intent to deprive another person of life can be proven by the instrument used to commit the offence and the place on the victim's body wounded by that instrument.*

And, on the rejection of the repeated-stab requirement:

> "Mahkamah Agung tidak menyetujui pertimbangan judex facti yang berpendapat untuk membuktikan niat/kesengajaan untuk menghilangkan nyawa orang lain harus terbukti dengan beberapa tusukan... Begitu pula korban tidak meninggal seketika, tidak berarti bahwa terdakwa tidak ada niat/kesengajaan..."

In English: *The Supreme Court does not agree with the judex facti's view that, to prove the intent to deprive another person of life, this must be proven by several stabs... Likewise, the fact that the victim did not die instantly does not mean that the defendant lacked intent...*

## Holding

The Supreme Court:

1.  **Accepted and granted** the cassation petition of the Cassation Petitioner, the Public Prosecutor at the West Jakarta District Prosecutor's Office.
2.  **Annulled** DKI Jakarta High Court Decision No. 61/Pid/1985/PT.DKI dated 17 June 1985.
3.  **Annulled** West Jakarta District Court Decision No. 046/JB./Pid./1984/PN.JKT.BAR dated 5 February 1985.
4.  **Adjudicated the case itself** (*mengadili sendiri*):
    1.  Declared the Defendant \[Defendant\] **guilty** of the offence charged in the primary charge, namely "murder" as described in Article 338 KUHP.
    2.  Sentenced him accordingly to **12 (twelve) years' imprisonment**.

## Question of Law vs Question of Fact

### Question of Law (*Judex Juris* / Supreme Court)

Whether the *Judex Facti* (the DKI Jakarta High Court and the West Jakarta District Court) erred in applying the law (*foutieve toepassing van het recht*) by acquitting the Defendant of the primary charge under Article 338 KUHP and re-qualifying the offence under Article 351(3) KUHP on the assumption that intent to kill requires several stabs and instant death at the scene.

**Supreme Court's answer:** Yes, the *Judex Facti* erred in applying the law. The Supreme Court granted the Public Prosecutor's cassation petition, annulled DKI Jakarta High Court Decision No. 61/Pid/1985/PT.DKI and West Jakarta District Court Decision No. 046/JB./Pid./1984/PN.JKT.BAR, and, adjudicating the case itself, **declared the Defendant \[Defendant\] guilty of murder under Article 338 KUHP and sentenced him to 12 (twelve) years' imprisonment**.

### Question of Fact (*Judex Facti* / West Jakarta District Court and DKI Jakarta High Court)

The facts examined by the *Judex Facti* were:

- The stabbing occurred on Saturday, 18 August 1984, at about 20:30 WIB, in Gang Jamblang I, West Jakarta.
- The Defendant took a kitchen knife from his parents' house before stopping to eat fried noodles.
- A quarrel arose after the Defendant bumped into the victim, \[Victim\].
- The victim was dragged into Gang Jamblang III.
- The Defendant stabbed the victim once, in the lower left chest (below the nipple).
- The Defendant threw the knife from the Jeling bridge.
- *Visum et Repertum* No. 2140 SK-II/1386/2-84 dated 28 August 1984, by Dr. Wibisono Widiatmaka, recorded a stab wound channel 15 cm deep penetrating the left lung, the pericardium and the right lung.
- The victim died en route to, or on arrival at, Sumber Waras Hospital.
- The Defendant was a recidivist.

## What the Evidence Establishes / Does Not Establish

### What the Evidence Establishes (Legally Proven)

- The Defendant \[Defendant\] was proven legally and convincingly guilty of **murder under Article 338 KUHP**.
- The Defendant deliberately used a lethal instrument (a kitchen knife) and stabbed it directly into a vital area of the victim's body (the left chest, penetrating the heart and lungs to a depth of 15 cm).
- The Defendant was sentenced to **12 (twelve) years' imprisonment**.

### What the Evidence Does Not Establish (*Judex Facti* Reasoning Annulled)

- The reasoning of the West Jakarta District Court and the DKI Jakarta High Court that the Defendant lacked intent to kill because he stabbed only once and the victim did not die instantly at the scene was **annulled in its entirety by the Supreme Court** as contrary to the law on proof of intent.

## Uncertainty and Gaps

**Commentary in the source record (not a holding of the Court):** the decision closes a gap often invoked in cases of stabbing to vital organs, where the defence seeks to move the charge from Article 338 KUHP (maximum 15 years) to Article 351(3) KUHP (maximum 7 years) by pleading that there was "only one stab" or that the "victim was taken to hospital before dying". In the record's reading, the Supreme Court affirmed strong protection of the right to life: an attack on a vital organ with a lethal instrument is murder.

**Scope note:** the Court's own holding is framed as intent *can be proven* from the instrument used and the body location wounded, applied to the facts of this case; it is not stated as an automatic rule for every attack on a vital organ.

## Legal Significance

This precedent is significant for Indonesian criminal law because it sets out objective indicators for proving intent to kill and confirms the reviewability of non-pure acquittals in cassation.

The decision illustrates several principles:

- Intent to kill under Article 338 KUHP can be proven from the type of instrument used and the location on the victim's body that was wounded.
- Neither several stabs nor instant death is required to prove intent to kill; a single accurate stab to a vital organ can suffice, and the victim's failure to die at once does not eliminate intent.
- An acquittal from the primary charge based on a mistaken interpretation of intent is a non-pure acquittal, and the Public Prosecutor's cassation petition against it is admissible (Article 244 KUHAP jurisprudence).
- The Supreme Court may annul the *Judex Facti* decisions, adjudicate the case itself, and convict the defendant under the primary charge.
