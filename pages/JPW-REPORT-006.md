# JPW-REPORT-006

**Situation in the Plurinational State of Bolivia: Final Report** is the official final report published by the Office of the Prosecutor (OTP) of the International Criminal Court on 14 February 2022, formally concluding the closure of the preliminary examination into the situation in Bolivia after finding no reasonable basis to believe that crimes against humanity occurred during the August 2020 road blockades.

## Summary

The report presents an independent factual and legal analysis of the Bolivian government's referral concerning nationwide road blockades from 3 to 14 August 2020. The Prosecutor concluded that there was no reasonable basis to believe that crimes against humanity within the Court's jurisdiction had been committed, and the preliminary examination was formally closed.

## Legal Basis

- [Rome Statute](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Rome_Statute): Article 5 (Jurisdiction of the Court), Article 7(1)(a) (Murder), Article 7(1)(k) (Other Inhumane Acts), Article 7(2)(a) (Organizational Policy/Attack Element), Article 14(1) (State Party Referral), Article 30 (Mental Element), Article 53(1) (Reasonable Basis Standard), Article 53(3)(a) (Pre-Trial Chamber Review), and Article 53(4) (Reconsideration on New Facts).
- Bolivia ratified the Rome Statute on 27 June 2002 (jurisdiction effective from 1 September 2002).

## Background

Following the resignation of President [Evo Morales](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Evo_Morales) in November 2019, Interim President [Jeanine Áñez](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Jeanine_%C3%81%C3%B1ez) scheduled new elections. Repeated postponements of the election date due to the COVID-19 pandemic triggered nationwide road-blockade protests organized by the \*Central Obrera Boliviana\* (COB) and the \*Pacto de Unidad\* (PdU) in August 2020.

## Objectives

- Assess whether allegations of medical oxygen shortages caused by the road blockades meet the subject-matter jurisdiction threshold for crimes against humanity under Article 7 of the Rome Statute.
- Determine whether a legally sufficient basis exists to seek authorization to open a formal investigation under Article 53(1).

## Key Provisions

- **No Structured Attack Policy**: The road blockades constituted a limited (12-day) form of political protest not designed to attack the civilian population.
- **Safe-Passage Directives**: Protest leadership (COB and PdU) issued official directives permitting medical and oxygen convoys to pass.
- **Mens Rea Analysis**: The murder qualification under Article 7(1)(a) was rejected because protesters were not found to have acted with direct intent (\*dolus directus\*) as required by Article 30 of the Rome Statute.

## Implementation

The government activated "Operation Salvando Vidas," using the Bolivian Air Force to transport 158 tonnes of medical oxygen. The OTP examined the referral together with 250 supporting annexes and cross-institutional data, including from OHCHR, the OAS, and the IACHR.

## Evidence

- Leadership of the main organizations (COB and PdU) repeatedly issued written and verbal instructions for participants to allow safe passage to ambulances and medical oxygen convoys.
- Medical vehicles and convoys successfully passed through various blockade points following on-site negotiation.
- The government's air corridor operation transported 158 tonnes of medical oxygen across 41 Bolivian Air Force flights.
- For nine deaths potentially linked to supply delays, no evidence was found that protesters acted with direct intent (\*dolus directus\*) to kill under Article 30; at most, awareness of risk (\*dolus eventualis\*) was found, which does not meet the threshold for murder under Article 7(1)(a).

## Policy Issues

- Assessing the threshold distinguishing incidental consequences of political protest from a structured attack under Article 7 of the Rome Statute.
- The OTP's lack of investigative powers at the preliminary examination stage.

## Relationships with Other Entities

- **[Office of the Prosecutor (ICC)](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Office_of_the_Prosecutor_%28ICC%29)**: Organ that published the report.
- **[Pre-Trial Chamber III](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Pre-Trial_Chamber_III)**: Chamber assigned to the situation.
- **[Government of Bolivia](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Government_of_Bolivia)**: Party that submitted the Article 14(1) referral.
- **[Central Obrera Boliviana](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Central_Obrera_Boliviana)** (COB): Organization that led the road blockades.
- **[Pacto de Unidad](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Pacto_de_Unidad)** (PdU): Alliance of social organizations supporting the protests.
- **[Jeanine Áñez Chávez](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Jeanine_%C3%81%C3%B1ez_Ch%C3%A1vez)**: Interim President of Bolivia who signed the accreditation letter and submitted the official referral to the ICC on 4 September 2020; later arrested on 12 March 2021 on charges of terrorism and sedition related to the November 2019 transfer of power.
- **[Evo Morales](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Evo_Morales)**: Former President of Bolivia and leader of the \*Movimiento al Socialismo\* (MAS) party, who resigned in November 2019 amid the disputed October 2019 election, and voiced support for the August 2020 protests.
- **[Luis Arce](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Luis_Arce)**: MAS presidential candidate who won Bolivia's presidential election on 18 October 2020 with 55% of the vote.
- **[Juan Carlos Huarachi](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Juan_Carlos_Huarachi)**: Executive Secretary of the \*Central Obrera Boliviana\* (COB), who led the organization of the nationwide road blockades and signed instructions to secure medical supply routes.
- **[Leonardo Loza](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Leonardo_Loza)**: Leader of the \*Pacto de Unidad\* (PdU) and a coca-growers' federation, involved in organizing the blockades and signing their suspension.
- **[Fatou Bensouda](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Fatou_Bensouda)**: ICC Prosecutor who received Bolivia's official referral on 4 September 2020 and announced the opening of the preliminary examination on 9 September 2020.
