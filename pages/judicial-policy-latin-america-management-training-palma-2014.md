# `judicial-policy-latin-america-management-training-palma-2014`

**The Latin American Judicial Management Training and Administrative-Jurisdictional Separation Policy Framework** is a judicial administration reform policy and case management training model in Latin America. Formulated by Dr. Luis María Palma (Vice President IACA for Latin & South America / University of Belgrano, 2014) in the *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA), it is based on 16 years of experience organizing over 160 practical training workshops in Argentina, Brazil, Colombia, El Salvador, and Mexico. The framework directs a shift from artisanal/obsolete work methods and the confusion between judicial and administrative activities toward the professional separation of court administration, workshop-based problem-solving legal education, and the creation of modern court models oriented towards efficiency, team leadership, and the use of information technology \[1–3\].

## Summary

Published by Dr. Luis María Palma in the *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA), this policy framework evaluates systemic issues in Latin American judicial units. Most courts, prosecutor's offices, and public defender's offices in the region suffer from a fundamental confusion between jurisdictional and administrative activities. Judges and prosecutors are burdened with bureaucratic tasks due to the absence of professional court administrators. Through his experience conducting over 160 case management training courses across various Latin American countries, Palma demonstrates that practical, workshop-based training can foster critical thinking, work process reengineering, and improvements in the quality of judicial services.

## Legal Basis

- **Doctrine of Separation of Judicial and Administrative Activities:** The principle of modern judicial governance that separates the handling of case merits from office administration management.
- **Judicial Service Efficiency and Quality Assurance Principles:** The public's right to rapid and high-quality conflict resolution.
- **Not found in the available sources:** Other specific formal statutory regulations are not mentioned in the source text \[FACT\].

## Background

Latin American judicial systems share a similar historical heritage and political dynamics, triggering bureaucratic problems and obsolete work methods. Passive, theoretical law school curricula fail to equip prospective judges with practical management skills.

## Objectives

1\. Overcome the confusion between jurisdictional and administrative functions in Latin American judicial units. 2. Transform legal teaching methods into practical, workshop-based problem-solving learning. 3. Equip judges and judicial officials with leadership, strategic planning, and process reengineering skills. 4. Build consensus for the creation of new court models that embrace the professional court administrator position.

## Scope

This policy encompasses training models and court administrative reforms in Argentina, Brazil, Colombia, El Salvador, Mexico, and the broader Latin American region.

## Key Provisions

- **Two Main Problems in Latin American Judicial Governance**:

1.  \*Functional Confusion\*: The merging of administrative and jurisdictional tasks directly executed by judges and prosecutors.
2.  \*Absence of Professional Administrators\*: The lack of recognized professional court managers to run office operations.

- **Practical Workshop-Based Training Approach**:
  - Participants work in groups to identify operational problems in their own workplaces and design immediate practical solutions.
  - Materials focus on case management, process reengineering, IT, performance indicators, leadership, and drafting operational manuals.

## Implementation

- **Hosting 160+ Regional Courses**: Execution of postgraduate training programs and judicial academies over 16 years across 5 Latin American countries.
- **Applying Operational Solutions**: Participants successfully implemented governance changes in their court units to improve judicial outcomes and user services.

## Policy Problems

- **Passive Legal Education**: Academic law school curricula that only teach doctrinal theory without instilling operational and management understanding.
- **Delayed Structural Reforms**: The unevenness of national laws mandating the separation of administrative and jurisdictional functions across the region.

## Reform / Change

- Institutionalization of the professional \*court administrator\* position across all Latin American courts.
- A cultural shift from traditional (\*artisanal\*) work methods to ICT automation and process reengineering.
