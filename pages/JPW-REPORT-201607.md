## 4. Policy Analysis

Background  
Law enforcement search and seizure in South Korea faced long-standing public criticism due to overbroad, indiscriminate bulk search practices. Investigators routinely seized whole paper file collections or physical computer hardware and transferred them to police or prosecutorial offices, conducting covert, unconstrained data exploration over extended periods.

<!-- -->

Policy Problem  
Bulk hardware and file seizures inflict massive operational disruption on corporations, violate fundamental constitutional rights to privacy and property, and risk converting specific crime warrants into unconstitutional general warrants ("fishing expeditions").

<!-- -->

Legal/Policy Gap  
Traditional Criminal Procedure Code provisions were constructed around tangible physical objects. Although the 2011 CPC amendment introduced Article 106(3) — establishing the output or copying of specific digital data as the mandatory default method — investigators frequently resorted to statutory exceptions, seizing physical storage media or entire disk images without proper ex-ante judicial limitations or ex-post controls.

<!-- -->

Objectives  
Harmonize the dual imperatives of criminal procedure: maintaining effective truth-finding and societal security while strictly enforcing constitutional due process, proportionality, and the warrant requirement.

<!-- -->

Implementation  
Formulated a comprehensive four-stage reform framework:

1.  *Warrant Issuance Stage*: Implement substantial ex-ante hearings (examining affiants/investigators under oath), establish an Electronic Warrant (e-Warrant) system, introduce separate or phased search vs. seizure warrants, and impose explicit ex-ante method restrictions on warrant attachments.
2.  *Execution Stage*: Enforce mandatory attendance and participation rights for suspects, defendants, and defense counsel during all off-site digital parsing, filtering, and copying phases.
3.  *Post-Execution Stage*: Require mandatory return of warrants, detailed inventory lists, and non-relevant data deletion records to the issuing court; actively enforce and notify statutory return/temporary return rights under CPC Article 218-2.
4.  *Trial Stage*: Apply the Exclusionary Rule (위법수집증거 배제법칙) rigorously under the Relevance Rule (관련성 원칙), rejecting attempts to import foreign "plain view" exceptions into digital search contexts.

<!-- -->

Evidence  
Empirical analysis of 2014 ROK judicial statistics reveals that out of 181,067 search/seizure/inspection warrant applications (which saw a 91.7% issuance rate), only 380 court applications for the return or temporary return of seized items were filed nationwide. This stark disparity demonstrated that post-seizure remedy mechanisms were severely underutilized due to lack of explicit procedural notices.

<!-- -->

Impact  
Directly shaped the Seoul Central District Court's revised warrant practice (effective August 1, 2015), which explicitly restricted digital seizure scope in warrant text, mandated detailed electronic inventory delivery, enforced the destruction of non-relevant data, and guaranteed full participation rights across all imaging and parsing phases.

<!-- -->

Evaluation  
Serves as a foundational policy roadmap for digital evidence jurisprudence in Korea, reinforcing Supreme Court En Banc Decision 2011Mo1839 and entrenching the Relevance Rule as a non-negotiable constitutional check on state coercive power.

<!-- -->

Reform Recommendations  
Institutionalize e-Warrant platforms, enact statutory obligations requiring immediate deletion of non-relevant digital data, mandate post-execution court return filings, and insert clear procedural remedy rights directly onto search warrant forms.

<!-- -->

Unresolved Issues  
Resolving cross-border data access (trans-border remote searches) under cloud computing frameworks, establishing legal mechanisms for encryption key disclosure without infringing the privilege against self-incrimination, and managing judicial administrative workloads resulting from increased ex-ante and ex-post hearings.

## 6. MediaWiki Output

**Research on the Improvement of the Search and Seizure Procedure** is an official judicial policy research report published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of South Korea's Supreme Court.

## Summary

This document is an official judicial policy research report published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of South Korea's Supreme Court. It comparatively analyzes search and seizure law in Japan, the United States, and Germany, and formulates comprehensive reforms to prevent bulk seizures and unrestricted seizure of electronic information.

## Legal Basis

The legal frameworks analyzed include:

- **Constitution of the Republic of Korea**: Article 12 (Personal Liberty & Due Process), Article 16 (Warrant Doctrine & Inviolability of Residence), Article 17 (Privacy), Article 18 (Secrecy of Communications), Article 21 (Freedom of Expression), and Article 37(2) (Proportionality Principle).
- **Criminal Procedure Code of the Republic of Korea (형사소송법)**: Article 106 (Digital Data Output/Copy Principle), Article 109, Article 199(1) (Proportionality), Article 215 (Crime Relevance), Articles 216–218 (Warrant Exceptions), and Article 218-2 (Right to Return of Seized Items).
- **Protection of Communications Secrets Act (통신비밀보호법)**: Articles 3, 5, 6, 9-2, 9-3, and 13.

## Background

Law enforcement practice in South Korea has historically involved bulk seizure of entire document collections or computer hardware without filtering for crime relevance. Such devices were brought to investigators' offices and analyzed covertly over extended periods, violating constitutional rights and disrupting business operations.

## Objectives

1.  Balance the discovery of substantive truth (실체적 진실규명) with the protection of human rights and due process of law (적법절차).
2.  Formulate judicial controls at the warrant issuance, execution, and post-execution stages of search and seizure.

## Scope

- **Comparative Study**: Japanese law (2011 CPC Amendment & Wiretapping Act), US law (4th Amendment, Fed. R. Crim. P. 41/4.1, SCA, Wiretap Act, CalECPA), and German law (Grundgesetz & StPO).
- **Main Focus**: Seizure of digital evidence (디지털 증거), the right of the examined party to be present (참여권), and ex-ante restrictions (사전적 제한).

## Key Provisions

- **Output/Copy Principle**: Seizure of digital data must be carried out by printing or copying the relevant files on-site (Article 106(3) of the Criminal Procedure Code).
- **Relevance Principle (관련성 원칙)**: Seizure is only valid for data with objective, subjective, and temporal relevance to the alleged crime.
- **Plain View Exception**: The report rejects extending the "plain view" doctrine to exploratory digital data searches.

## Implementation

The report evaluates and supports the integrated warrant practice adopted by the Seoul Central District Court (effective August 1, 2015), which requires submission of detailed digital file inventories, deletion of irrelevant data, and guarantees the examined party's right to be present throughout all analysis stages.

## Policy Issues

- The high warrant issuance rate (91.7% in 2014) stands in stark contrast to the low number of applications for return of seized items (only 380 cases nationwide).
- Risk of digital examination being misused as a general warrant / fishing expedition.

## Reform / Amendments

1.  Establishment of an Electronic Warrant (e-Warrant) System.
2.  Substantive Pre-Issuance Review (examination of witnesses/investigators under oath).
3.  Separate/Phased Warrant Issuance (분리/단계적 발부).
4.  Statutory Obligation to Return and Delete Irrelevant Data Post-Execution.

## Relationship to Other Entities

- **Issuing Institution**: Judicial Policy Research Institute & Supreme Court of Korea.
- **Research Team**: Son Ji-young, Kim Joo-seok.
- **Related Judicial Practice**: Seoul Central District Court (서울중앙지방법원).
