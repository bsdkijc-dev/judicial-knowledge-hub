# JPW-WORK-JIPLP-ED-2007-JPL194

**"Double-dipping"** is a legal editorial written by [Jeremy Phillips](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Jeremy_Phillips), editor of the *[Journal of Intellectual Property Law & Practice](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Journal_of_Intellectual_Property_Law_%26_Practice)*, published by [Oxford University Press](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Oxford_University_Press) in Vol. 2, No. 1, p. 1, DOI: 10.1093/jiplp/jpl194, released in Advance Access on 8 December 2006.

## Summary

The editorial discusses the decision of the [United States Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/United_States_Court_of_Appeals_for_the_Federal_Circuit) of 2 October 2006 in *Aero Products International, Inc. v Intex Recreation Corp.*, in which separate patent and trade mark damages had been awarded on the same infringing sales of an inflatable air mattress. The appellate court treated the trade mark award as an impermissible double recovery. Phillips uses the outcome to make a pleading point: a claimant may recover under two intellectual property regimes on the same conduct, but only by pleading and proving two distinct heads of loss.

## The Decision

*Aero Products International, Inc. and Chaffee v Intex Recreation Corp., Quality Trading Inc. and Wal-Mart Stores Inc.*, 466 F.3d 1000 (Fed. Cir. 2006), decided 2 October 2006. *ID:* \`JPW-CASE-AERO-V-INTEX-2006\`

- Patent damages of approximately \$5.8 million were sustained, derived from an actual damages

` figure of roughly $2.9 million enhanced on a finding of wilful infringement.`

- Trade mark damages of approximately \$1 million were set aside as a double recovery.
- The reason was evidential rather than doctrinal: the loss had been quantified purely by

` reference to the same number of unlawfully sold mattress units, without distinguishing`  
` which sales were attributable to the patent, to the mark, or to both.`

## Background

Both awards arose from the same sales of the "ONE TOUCH" air mattress. Because the same unit count drove both calculations, the second award compensated an injury already paid for. *(The source's summary described the case as involving copyright as well; it did not. Copyright was not in issue.)* — **CORRECTED**

## Why the Regimes Differ

A patent operates as a barrier around a product or process: it excludes competitors from the protected technology outright. A trade mark protects the link between a sign and the goodwill it carries in the purchaser's mind. Because the two rights secure different commercial advantages, they are capable of generating different losses — but only if those losses are separately identified.

## Conditions for Two Awards

Separate recovery under both regimes on the same conduct remains available where the claimant pleads and proves distinct injuries: for example, lost sales attributable to the patent infringement, together with damage to goodwill, dilution, or lost licensing revenue attributable to the trade mark infringement.

## Policy Issues

- **Pleading discipline** — particulars of loss must be drafted to match the economic

` function of each right relied on, or the second award will not survive appeal.`

- **Open scenarios** — co-branded goods; conduct straddling patent expiry into continuing

` goodwill protection; patent infringement accompanied by misuse of confidential know-how.`  
` Each presents a harder double-recovery question than `*`Aero Products`*`.`

## Interpretation and Inference

- **Interpretation** — patent and trade mark rights confer economically distinct advantages,

` and taking two payments for one legal wrong is not justified.`

- **Inference** — claimants can still obtain separate awards on the same conduct if they

` specifically claim and prove two different kinds of loss. This is drawn from the editorial's`  
` reasoning, not from the holding, which addressed only the record before it.`
