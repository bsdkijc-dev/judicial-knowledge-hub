## Policy Analysis

- **Background & Policy Problem**:
  - **FACT**: The enactment of the Act on Promotion of Prevention of Recidivism (Act No. 104 of 2016) and the approval of the 2017 Re-offending Prevention Promotion Plan established Japan's national framework for correctional rehabilitation.
  - **FACT**: Statistical data indicates that the 2-year re-incarceration rate for full-term releasees (満期釈放者) reached 23.3% in 2019—more than double the 10.2% rate for parolees (仮釈放者).
  - **FACT**: Unlike parolees who receive probation supervision, full-term releasees have no statutory supervision obligations, making them vulnerable to social isolation.
  - **FACT**: In 2020, wardens' primary reasons for not submitting parole applications included: inadequate housing/environment (62.5%), poor conduct in prison (33.2%), and lack of intent to disassociate from organized crime/gangs (7.7%). Overall, 43.2% of full-term releasees in 2020 lacked a fixed abode upon release.
- **Policy Objectives & Quantitative Targets**:
  - **FACT**: The Ministerial Council on Crime Measures established the Acceleration Plan in December 2019.
  - **FACT**: Numerical Target: Reduce the 2-year re-incarceration count of full-term releasees by 20% or more to 2,000 persons or fewer by 2022 (from a baseline average of 2,726 persons in 2013–2017).
  - **FACT**: In 2019, the 2-year re-incarceration count for full-term releasees dropped to 1,936 persons, meeting the target ahead of schedule.
- **Key Provisions & Interventions**:
  - **FACT**: **Guidelines for Full-Term Releasee Measures (満期釈放者対策ガイドライン)**: Formulated by the MOJ in FY2020 to strengthen early coordination during incarceration and post-release Urgent Aftercare Services (更生緊急保護).
  - **FACT**: **Housing Support Expansion (自立準備ホーム & 更生保護施設)**: Utilizes Independent Preparation Homes run by NPOs/private entities alongside rehabilitation hostels to offer emergency housing and living support.
  - **FACT**: **Special Social Welfare Adjustments (特別調整 & 被疑者等支援業務)**: Deploys welfare specialists and social workers within correctional facilities to arrange welfare services for elderly and disabled inmates prior to release.
  - **FACT**: **International Initiatives (Kyoto Congress & UN Standards)**: Japan hosted the 14th UN Crime Congress in March 2021, adopting the Kyoto Declaration and leading the formulation of the UN Standards for the Prevention of Re-offending (Kyoto Model Strategy).
  - **FACT**: **Innovative Financing (PFS / SIB)**: Deploys Pay for Success (PFS) and Social Impact Bond (SIB) models to fund private-sector learning support and rehabilitation programs for juvenile and adult offenders.
- **Interpretations & Structural Inferences**:
  - **INTERPRETATION**: The Acceleration Plan marks a paradigm shift in Japanese penal strategy—moving from isolated punitive containment to a framework of continuous social integration (seamless support / 息の長い支援). Reintegration failure is formally recognized as a structural welfare defect rather than purely an individual failure.
  - **INFERENCE**: Although the absolute count dropped below 2,000 in 2019 (1,936 persons), the persistent gap in 2-year re-incarceration percentages (23.3% vs. 10.2%) shows that the absolute reduction was partially driven by an overall decline in prison populations. Long-term efficacy remains dependent on scaling Independent Preparation Homes (自立準備ホーム) and local government capacity.
