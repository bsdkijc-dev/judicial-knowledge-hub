## Overview

Supreme Court Decision Reg. No. 124 K/AG/1991 dated January 23, 1993, establishes fundamental Islamic Civil Procedure jurisprudence regarding the relative jurisdiction of courts: An exception concerning the relative jurisdiction of a court (domicile or territorial jurisdiction of the Religious Court) must be raised by the Respondent / Defendant at the first hearing. If the Defendant fails to attend the first hearing and does not raise an exception of relative competence, the Religious Court where the Plaintiff filed the lawsuit has full authority to try the case, and the High Religious Court is not justified in annulling the Religious Court's judgment solely on grounds of relative competence.

## Case Background

The Original Plaintiff / Petitioner for Cassation (\[Plaintiff\], residing in \[address removed\]) filed a divorce suit against her husband, the Original Defendant / Respondent for Cassation (\[Defendant\], residing in \[address removed\]), at the Klaten Religious Court. Both were married on December 10, 1977, at the Office of Religious Affairs (\*KUA\*) of Ketandan Sub-District, Klaten, and were blessed with 2 children. The marriage deteriorated due to continuous quarrels over financial issues, physical abuse by the Defendant, expelling the Plaintiff back to Klaten, and failing to provide maintenance for more than 6 consecutive months.

The Klaten Religious Court (Decision No. 614/1990 dated August 27, 1990 AD / 6 Shafar 1411 AH) granted the Plaintiff's lawsuit by default (\*verstek\*) because the Defendant failed to appear despite being summoned properly, and declared a single \*Khul'i\* divorce pronounced by the Defendant against the Plaintiff with a compensation (\*iwadl\*) of Rp 50. However, the Semarang High Religious Court (Decision No. 24/G/1991 dated April 29, 1991 AD) annulled the Klaten Religious Court judgment and declared the decision null and void on the grounds that the Klaten Religious Court had exceeded its territorial relative jurisdiction (deeming the Plaintiff to be domiciled in \[address removed\]). Dissatisfied with the appellate ruling, the Plaintiff (\[Plaintiff\]) filed a cassation petition to the Supreme Court.

## Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

- **Ratio Decidendi:**

1.  The Semarang High Religious Court committed an error in applying the law by failing to provide proper legal considerations when annulling the Klaten Religious Court judgment.
2.  The Original Plaintiff was in fact domiciled in \[address removed\]; concerning the relative competence of the court, such an exception should have been raised by the Respondent for Cassation / Original Defendant at the first hearing.
3.  Because the Respondent for Cassation / Original Defendant failed to raise an exception of relative competence at the first hearing, sufficient grounds exist for the Supreme Court to grant the Original Plaintiff's cassation petition, annul the Semarang High Religious Court decision No. 24/G/1991, and uphold the Klaten Religious Court decision No. 614/1990 dated August 27, 1990 AD, which was accurate and correct.

- **Ruling:** Granting the cassation petition from Petitioner for Cassation \[Plaintiff\]; Annulling the Semarang High Religious Court decision dated April 29, 1991 AD / 14 Shawwal 1411 AH, No. 24/G/1991; and Independently Adjudicating: (1) Declaring that the Defendant was properly summoned to appear at trial but failed to attend; (2) Granting the Plaintiff's lawsuit by default (\*verstek\*); (3) Declaring a single \*Khul'i\* divorce of Defendant (\[Defendant\]) over Plaintiff (\[Plaintiff\]) with an \*iwadl\* amounting to Rp 50 (fifty rupiahs); (4) Ordering the Plaintiff to pay first-instance court costs amounting to Rp 26,500 (twenty-six thousand five hundred rupiahs); (5) Ordering the Appellant to pay appellate-level court costs amounting to Rp 10,500 (ten thousand five hundred rupiahs); (6) Ordering the Petitioner for Cassation to pay cassation-level court costs amounting to Rp 20,000 (twenty thousand rupiahs). \*(Decided in the deliberation meeting of the Supreme Court Cassation Panel of Justices on Monday, December 28, 1992, and pronounced on Saturday, January 23, 1993 AD, by Prof. H. Busthanul Arifin, SH. \[Junior Chairman\], H. Masrani Basran, SH., and H. Amiroeddin Noer, SH. \[Associate Justices\]).\*
