# Judicial-policy-uae-e-litigation-civil-courts-2022

**The Electronic Judicial Litigation Procedures Before the UAE Civil Courts Framework** is an e-Litigation and remote trial policy and procedural framework in the United Arab Emirates (UAE) that regulates the electronic execution of civil, commercial, and personal status case workflows based on modern telecommunications \[1, 2, 8–10, 15, 63–65\].

## Summary

Published in the *International Journal for Court Administration* (2025) by Prof. Moustafa Elmetwaly Kandeel, this framework analyzes the full legislative adoption of e-Litigation based on *Federal Decree-Law No. 42 of 2022* (Civil Procedures Act) and the *Minister of Justice’s Decree No. 260 of 2019*. The policy covers the entire electronic trial workflow: online claim registration, electronic legal notifications, witness/evidence examination via teleconference, secret online judicial deliberation, and the signing of judgments using electronic signatures \[2, 10, 15, 34, 60–62, 63–65\].

## Legal Basis

- **Federal Decree-Law No. 42 of 2022 (Civil Procedures Act):** Articles 6, 9, 10, 11, 44, 47, 48, 54, 128, 129, 328, and 330.
- **Minister of Justice’s Decree No. 260 of 2019:** *Procedural Guide for Legal Regulation of Litigation via Electronic Means & Telecommunication Technology*.
- **Federal Decree-Law No. 32 of 2022 (Judicial Authority Law):** Article 25 (Arabic Language and the Role of Translators).
- **Federal Decree-Law No. 10 of 2019:** Regulation of judicial relations between federal and local authorities.
- **Dubai Court of Cassation Jurisprudence:** Judgments such as *Commercial Cassation No. 709 of 2015* and *Civil Cassation No. 19 of 2017* (Certainty of electronic filing date).

## Background

The evolution of electronic justice in the UAE progressed in four stages: Stage I (Pre-2017: limited fax/email notifications), Stage II (2017–2020: Decree-Law No. 10/2017 & Decree No. 260/2019), Stage III (2021: Decree-Law No. 15/2021), and Stage IV (2022–Present: Decree-Law No. 42/2022 which fully adopted e-Litigation) \[3–5, 63–65\].

## Objectives

1\. Modernize civil justice towards a simple, fast, and low-cost trial system. 2. Provide absolute legal validity for all electronic trial documents and procedures. 3. Strictly distinguish e-Litigation (ICT-based trials by human judges) from the use of AI/robots that risk violating human rights. 4. Guarantee the right to data privacy and the protection of litigation guarantees.

## Scope

This policy applies nationally across all UAE Civil Courts for:

- Civil Claims.
- Commercial Claims.
- Personal Affairs Claims.
- **Exclusions:** Labor claims and Administrative claims are excluded to protect workers and account for the specific involvement of government institutions.

## Key Provisions

- **Determination of the Official Filing Date (Article 47/1):** The date of electronic file submission is recognized as the legally binding official registration date, provided court fees are paid within 3 working days from the billing notification.
- **Electronic Legal Notice (Article 9/1/a & 6/4):** Notifications can be made via recorded voice/video calls, SMS, smart applications, email, or fax, and are valid throughout the UAE without spatial jurisdiction limitations \[34–35\].
- **Role of the Case Management Office:** Responsible for verifying files, issuing encrypted passwords for litigants/lawyers, and overseeing verified translations \[41–44\].
- **Electronic Deliberation & Judgment (Articles 128 & 129):** Online judicial deliberation must be kept secret via an encrypted portal. Judgments are issued and signed using the judge's electronic signature certificate \[60–62\].

## Implementation

- **Remote Trial:** Physical presence is replaced by remote attendance via secure teleconference channels with clear audio-visuals.
- **Witness Examination & Translators:** Witnesses and litigants are examined online; a translator must be appointed if the litigant does not speak Arabic, connecting via a high-speed system.

## Policy Problems

- **Textual Error in Article 44/2/b:** The law's text requires the plaintiff to include the defendant's fax/email number without the clause "if any," and erroneously copies the plaintiff's chosen domicile clause into the defendant's section \[30–31, 67–68\].
- **Cyber & Privacy Risks:** The need for technical security guarantees to prevent the leakage of trial data.

## Reform / Change

- **Addition of a New Article 339:** Explicitly codifying the obligation to enforce all litigation guarantees (privacy, public transparency, and the right of defense) in the e-Litigation environment.
- **Correction to the Text of Article 44/2/b:** Adding the phrase "if any" to the defendant's communication details and replacing the defendant's chosen domicile clause with "last known domicile or workplace" \[67–68\].
- **Mandatory Judicial Delegation:** Amending Ministerial Decree No. 260/2019 to mandate judicial delegation coordination between federal and local courts.
