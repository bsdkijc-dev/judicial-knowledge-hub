# JPAIS:PRECEDENT:1506K PDT 2002

**Supreme Court Decision Reg. No. 1506 K/Pdt/2002** (dated September 23, 2004) is a foundational Indonesian contract and commercial sales jurisprudence precedent establishing fundamental rules regarding the binding force of a Purchase Order (PO) signed by both parties and the forfeiture of the right to object to goods' quality upon exceeding an agreed contractual complaint deadline:

> "A Purchase Order (P.O.) signed by both binding parties constitutes an agreement thus acting as law binding both parties *ex* Articles 1320, 1338, 1457, and 1458 of the Civil Code. If the P.O. has agreed that complaints regarding goods' quality/grade must be submitted by the buyer no later than 1 (one) week after the goods are received, and the buyer has accepted delivery without written proof of objection/complaint within such 7 (seven) day deadline, then the buyer's action of withholding payment on the grounds that the goods are not according to specifications (rejected) is classified as Default (*Wanprestasi*). The buyer is ordered to pay in cash and in full the entire price of the ordered goods along with damages interest of 6% per annum."

The Supreme Court granted the Plaintiff's (PT Greges Jaya) cassation petition, annulled the Bandung High Court Decision No. 147/Pdt/2001/PT.Bdg jo. Bekasi District Court Decision No. 153/Pdt.G/2000/PN.Bks, and independently adjudicated (*mengadili sendiri*) by granting the Plaintiff's lawsuit in part, declaring PT Amindo Pacific Industries in default, and ordering the Defendant to pay damages of US\$ 74,388.15 plus 6% annual interest.

## Legal / Procedural Issues

### 1. Juridical Construction of a Purchase Order (P.O.) as a Binding Contract (*Pacta Sunt Servanda* ex Article 1338 jo. Article 1458 of the Civil Code)

*Does a Purchase Order (P.O.) issued and signed by both parties (buyer and seller) possess complete legally binding force as a commercial sales agreement contract?*

#### Supreme Court Rule: Binding Perfectly as Law

Pursuant to Articles 1320, 1338, 1457, and 1458 of the Civil Code, a sales obligation is established and legally binding upon achieving consensus regarding goods and price, embodied in a P.O. signed by both parties. Terms stipulated in the P.O. become mandatory law adhered to by both parties (*pacta sunt servanda*).

### 2. Forfeiture of the Right to Object to Goods Quality (Waiver of Quality Claims) Due to Exceeding the Contractual Complaint Deadline

*Is a buyer who has physically accepted goods delivery permitted to withhold payment on the grounds that the goods fail to meet specifications (rejected), despite the P.O. having stipulated a maximum complaint deadline of 1 week and the buyer having failed to lodge a complaint within such deadline?*

#### Supreme Court Rule: Quality Complaint Right Forfeited (Payment Obligation Enforceable)

If a P.O. establishes a clause requiring quality complaints to be submitted no later than 1 week after goods receipt, and trial evidence establishes that goods were received without written complaint notices within the 7-day window, the buyer is deemed to have accepted the goods properly, and the right to object is legally forfeited. Withholding payment based on subsequent unilateral rejection claims constitutes Default (*Wanprestasi*).

### 3. Error in Judicando by Judex Facti in Assessing Commercial Trade Default

Bekasi District Court and Bandung High Court misapplied the law by setting aside the PO complaint deadline clause and validating unilateral payment cancellations by the buyer. The Supreme Court annulled the *Judex Facti* decisions and granted the Plaintiff's lawsuit to restore commercial contract legal certainty.

## Question of Law vs Question of Fact

### Question of Law (Judex Juris / Supreme Court)

Did the *Judex Facti* (Bandung High Court Decision No. 147/Pdt/2001/PT.Bdg jo. Bekasi District Court Decision No. 153/Pdt.G/2000/PN.Bks) err in applying the law (*foutieve toepassing van het recht*) by dismissing the Plaintiff's default lawsuit, despite the PO signed by the parties binding as law (Article 1338 of the Civil Code) and the Defendant's failure to raise quality objections within the agreed 7-day PO deadline?

**Supreme Court Legal Answer:** Yes. The *Judex Facti* erred in applying the law. The Supreme Court granted PT Greges Jaya's cassation petition, annulled Bandung High Court Decision No. 147/Pdt/2001/PT.Bdg jo. Bekasi District Court Decision No. 153/Pdt.G/2000/PN.Bks, and independently adjudicated by declaring PT Amindo Pacific Industries in default, ordering the Defendant to pay US\$ 74,388.15 plus 6% annual interest, and declaring the protective attachment valid.

### Question of Fact (Judex Facti / Bekasi District Court & Bandung High Court)

Examination of facts regarding the issuance of P.O. No. API-GSJ/1332000 dated February 13, 2000; delivery of Ramin S4S timber 7 times between February 26 and April 11, 2000; payment proof for shipment I amounting to US\$ 23,671.05; receipt of shipments II through VII at the Cikarang Bekasi factory without quality complaint letters within 7 days; total outstanding invoice value of US\$ 74,388.15; and attachment enforcement over lathe machines, Outo Sander machines, and timber at the Defendant's factory location.

## What Evidence Establishes / Does Not Establish

### Established by Evidence

- Purchase Order No. API-GSJ/1332000 dated February 13, 2000, constitutes a valid and binding sales contract between PT Greges Jaya and PT Amindo Pacific Industries.
- The Defendant (PT Amindo Pacific Industries) was proven to have received timber shipments (shipments I through VII) from the Plaintiff.
- The Defendant never submitted written quality complaints or objections within the 1-week (7-day) deadline mandated in the P.O.
- The Defendant was proven to have committed Default (*Wanprestasi*) by failing to pay outstanding timber price arrears valued at US\$ 74,388.15.
- The Defendant is ordered to pay in cash US\$ 74,388.15 plus moratory interest of 6% per annum from the date the lawsuit was filed.
- Protective attachment (*conservatoir beslag*) over 4 lathe machines, 3 Outo Sander machines, and 38.0654 m³ of timber belonging to the Defendant is declared valid and lawful.

### Rejected / Dismissed Claims

- Claims for immaterial damages of Rp 500,000,000 and expected profits of 5%/month were rejected by the Supreme Court as insufficiently proven.

## Uncertainties and Gaps

**Execution of Foreign Currency (USD) Debt Collection Against Defendant Assets:** This landmark ruling decisively provides certainty in international/domestic export-import commercial transactions denominated in USD by ordering the buyer to pay the contract currency nominal amount (US\$ 74,388.15) alongside 6% interest. Upon execution, the protective attachment over factory machinery and timber inventory serves as real collateral guaranteeing fulfillment of such foreign currency obligations.

## Frequently Asked Questions (GEO)

Does a Purchase Order (PO) bind as a valid agreement, and can a buyer refuse to pay for goods on grounds of poor quality after the complaint deadline has expired?  
**Purchase Orders bind validly, and buyers cannot refuse payment.** Pursuant to Supreme Court Jurisprudence No. 1506 K/Pdt/2002:

- A Purchase Order (P.O.) signed by seller and buyer is a valid agreement acting as law for both parties *ex* Article 1338 jo. 1458 of the Civil Code.
- If a P.O. agrees on a maximum quality complaint deadline of 1 week after goods receipt, and the buyer accepts goods without filing written complaints within such 7-day deadline, the buyer's right to complain is legally forfeited.
- A buyer withholding payment past the complaint deadline is classified as committing Default (*Wanprestasi*) and is ordered to pay the full invoice value in cash plus 6% annual damages interest.
