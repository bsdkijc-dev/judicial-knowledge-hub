# JPW-WORK-JIPLP-EDITORIAL-2006

**Editorial: Delicate balance or creative conflict?** is a legal editorial written by [Jeremy Phillips](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Jeremy_Phillips) and published in the [Journal of Intellectual Property Law & Practice](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Journal_of_Intellectual_Property_Law_%26_Practice) (Vol. 1, No. 3) by [Oxford University Press](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Oxford_University_Press) on January 9, 2006 [Editorial Publication](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Editorial_Publication) \[1–12\].

## Summary

This editorial reviews an article by [Andrés Guadamuz](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9s_Guadamuz) regarding the failed attempt to reform European software patent law via the proposed \*Computer Implemented Inventions Directive\* [Software Patents](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Software_Patents) \[1–12\]. The author argues that despite this legislative failure, the interplay between patent law, copyright, contract law, and the open-source movement has created a balanced landscape that ultimately benefits consumers [Consumer Balance](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Consumer_Balance) \[5–7, 12\].

## Legal Basis

- **Proposed Computer Implemented Inventions Directive**: A European Union directive that failed to achieve enactment due to opposition from both strong patent advocates and anti-patent camps [EU Directive](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/EU_Directive) \[1–12\].
- **Multi-Layered Protection Regime**: Software protection is maintained through a combination of Patents, Copyright, and Contract Law [Multi-Tier Protection](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Multi-Tier_Protection) \[5–7, 12\].

## Background

Technological progress following the emergence of the personal computer (1980–2005) was accompanied by intense debate over appropriate legal protection models for software developers and creators [PC History](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/PC_History) \[1–12\].

## Objectives

1.  Assess the impact of the failed \*Computer Implemented Inventions Directive\* on the broader IT market [Directive Impact](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Directive_Impact) \[1–12\].
2.  Identify the complementary roles of patents, copyright, and contract law in safeguarding software IP [IP Regimes](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/IP_Regimes) \[5–7, 12\].
3.  Reaffirm that creative tension among conflicting legal paradigms has resulted in a net positive outcome for technology users [Consumer Benefits](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Consumer_Benefits) \[1–12\].

## Key Provisions

- **Software Patents**: Offer absolute protection but remain costly, short-lived in technological relevance, vulnerable to revocation, and expensive to litigate [Software Patents](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Software_Patents) \[1–12\].
- **Copyright**: Protects code similarly to literary works with long protection terms, but does not shield against independent creation and demands high evidentiary standards [Software Copyright](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Software_Copyright) \[5–7, 12\].
- **Contract Law**: Enables licensors to restrict product usage and structure outsourcing agreements [Contract Law](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Contract_Law) \[1–12\].
- **Open Source vs. Proprietary**: Ongoing competitive dynamics between proprietary commercial models and the open-source movement [Open Source](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Open_Source) \[1–12\].

## Policy Issues

- **Ideological Polarization**: Extremes between total patent proponents and abolitionists hampered pragmatic reform [Ideological Conflict](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Ideological_Conflict) \[1–12\].
- **Grey and Black Market Enforcement**: IP enforcement remains practical primarily against high-profile corporate entities due to ubiquitous grey/black software markets [Software Black Market](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Software_Black_Market) \[1–12\].
