# JPAIS:PRECEDENT:1498K PDT 2006

**Supreme Court Decision Reg. No. 1498 K/Pdt/2006** (dated January 23, 2008) is a landmark Indonesian civil procedure and land law jurisprudence precedent establishing three vital legal rules regarding the admissibility of photocopies of photocopies under specific circumstances, the burden of proof for land sale validity based on the principle of fairness (*billijkheid beginsel*), and the nullity of land transfer deeds executed under power of attorney originating from a deceased person.

## Executive Summary

The Supreme Court established three primary legal principles:

1.  **Admissibility of Photocopies of Photocopies (Article 1888 of the Civil Code):** Under specific circumstances, documentary evidence in the form of a photocopy of a photocopy is admissible and assessable as valid evidence if used to support and reinforce the opposing party's admission (i.e., Respondent for Cassation / Defendant III / PT Pertamina Tbk.), which acknowledged that the disputed land originally belonged to the Petitioner for Cassation's / Plaintiff's parent (the late \[Plaintiff's parent\]).
2.  **Burden of Proof for Land Transfer Validity (Billijkheid Beginsel):** To prove whether the transfer or sale of the disputed land occurred correctly and validly under the law, pursuant to the principle of equity and fairness (*billijkheid beginsel*), the burden of proof rests upon the buyer (i.e., Respondent for Cassation / Defendant III), as the buyer is in a better position to prove the validity of the legal title and the chain of transfer transactions.
3.  **Nullity by Operation of Law of Power of Attorney from a Deceased Person and Subsequent Transfers:** A land transfer power of attorney dated June 13, 1970, from \[Plaintiff's parent\] to Defendant I is null and void and without legal force because the principal (\[Plaintiff's parent\]) was proven to have passed away in 1963. Consequently, Land Release Deed No. 25 dated June 20, 1970, and Agreement Deed No. 11 dated May 22, 1970, contain an absolute legal defect (*nemo plus iuris*), ordering Defendant III to return the 1,500 m² disputed land (Girik C No. 721) to the Plaintiff.

The Supreme Court granted the Plaintiff's (\[Party A\]) cassation petition, annulled the Jakarta High Court Decision No. 234/Pdt/2005/PT.DKI jo. Central Jakarta District Court Decision No. 164/Pdt.G/2004/PN.Jkt.Pst, and independently adjudicated (*mengadili sendiri*) by granting the Plaintiff's lawsuit in part.

## Legal / Procedural Issues

### 1. Evidentiary Weight of Documentary Evidence in the Form of a Photocopy of a Photocopy (Article 1888 of the Civil Code jo. Opposing Party Admission)

*Is the High Court justified in rejecting photocopy-of-photocopy evidence absolutely based on Supreme Court Decision No. 701 K/Sip/1976, even though such photocopies were used to support Defendant III's admission that the disputed land originally belonged to the Plaintiff's parents?*

**Supreme Court Rule:** Admissible (Exception to Photocopy Rules). The Jakarta High Court erred in applying the law by dismissing the Central Jakarta District Court's judgment without adequately evaluating the evidentiary context (*onvoldoende gemotiveerd*). The doctrine of Article 1888 of the Civil Code restricting photocopies is not absolute when a photocopy of a photocopy is presented to support the express admission of the opposing party (Defendant III). Because Defendant III acknowledged that the land originally belonged to \[Plaintiff's parent\], the existence of such photocopy evidence is valid and legally binding.

### 2. Allocation of Burden of Proof Based on Equity (*Billijkheid Beginsel* / Fairness Principle in Burden of Proof)

When disputed land is proven to have originally belonged to the Plaintiff's parents and the Plaintiff asserts that the land has never been validly compensated or sold, pursuant to the principle of fairness (*billijkheid beginsel*), the burden of proving that the land transfer occurred lawfully and in good faith rests upon the buyer (Defendant III), as the buyer is in a better position to command transaction documents.

### 3. Unlawful Acts (Tort ex Article 1365 of the Civil Code) and Absolute Legal Defect of Land Transfers Originating from False Powers of Attorney (*Nemo Plus Iuris*)

The execution of a Power of Attorney dated June 13, 1970, in the name of \[Plaintiff's parent\]—who had already passed away in 1963—constitutes a tort embodying an absolute legal defect. Pursuant to the principle *nemo plus iuris ad alium transferre potest quam ipse habet* (no one can transfer greater rights than they possess), all derivative deeds originating from such fraudulent power of attorney (Land Release Deed No. 25 jo. Agreement Deed No. 11 jo. Amendment Deed No. 2) are null and void and without binding legal force.

## Question of Law vs Question of Fact

### Question of Law (Judex Juris / Supreme Court)

Did the Jakarta High Court err in applying the law of evidence (Article 1888 of the Civil Code) and issue an inadequately reasoned decision (*onvoldoende gemotiveerd*) by rejecting photocopy-of-photocopy evidence supported by Defendant III's admission while disregarding the absolute legal defect of a power of attorney executed by a deceased person?

**Supreme Court Legal Answer:** Yes. The Jakarta High Court erred in applying the law. The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition of Plaintiff \[Party A\], annulled Jakarta High Court Decision No. 234/Pdt/2005/PT.DKI dated September 29, 2005, and independently adjudicated by adopting the reasoning of Central Jakarta District Court Decision No. 164/Pdt.G/2004/PN.Jkt.Pst: granting the Plaintiff's lawsuit in part, declaring the Plaintiff the lawful owner of Girik C No. 721 covering 1,500 m², and ordering Defendant III to return the disputed land.

### Question of Fact (Judex Facti / Central Jakarta District Court & Jakarta High Court)

Examination of ownership facts regarding the inheritance land of the late \[Plaintiff's parent\] covering 2,280 m² (Parcel 55 a, Girik C No. 718) in Kampung Guji, Duri Kepa, Kebon Jeruk, West Jakarta; the 12-hectare land acquisition by Defendant III (Pertamina) in 1970; the issuance of subdivided Girik C No. 721 covering 1,500 m² in the name of \[Plaintiff's parent\]; facts concerning the death of \[Plaintiff's parent\] in 1963 based on Inheritance Distribution Deed No. 296/APW/1992/PA.JB and Certificate of Heirs dated May 8, 2003, corroborated by witness testimonies of \[Party B\] and Amsir bin H. Musa; the forgery of the Power of Attorney dated June 13, 1970, to Defendant I; the issuance of Land Release Deed No. 25 dated June 20, 1970, before Deputy Notary Ambrosius Sitompul, S.H.; Agreement Deed No. 11 dated May 22, 1970, jo. Amendment Deed No. 2 dated October 10, 1970, before Notary Januar Hamid, S.H.; and facts of land occupation by Defendant III since 1971.

## Jurisprudence and Related Knowledge

### Statutory Regulations

- **Civil Code (KUHPerdata):**
  - Article 1888: Evidentiary weight of documentary evidence / photocopies.
  - Articles 1328 & 1335: Nullity of agreements arising from false / prohibited causes (*causa*).
  - Article 1365: Unlawful Acts (*Perbuatan Melawan Hukum* / Tort).
  - Article 834: Inheritance Rights (*Hereditatis Petitio*).
- **Law No. 4 of 2004 jo. Law No. 14 of 1985 jo. Law No. 5 of 2004 on the Supreme Court:** Cassation authority & independent adjudication powers.

### Doctrines & Supreme Court Jurisprudence

- *Billijkheid Beginsel* in Evidence: Placing the burden of proof upon the party best positioned to prove it (fairness in burden of proof).
- Principle of *Nemo Plus Iuris*: Legal defects in foundational root titles vitiate the validity of all derivative rights.

## What Evidence Establishes / Does Not Establish

### Established by Evidence

- \[Plaintiff's parent\] alias Amit bin Kibi was legally proven to have passed away in 1963.
- The Power of Attorney dated June 13, 1970, in the name of the late \[Plaintiff's parent\] to Defendant I is forged and null and void.
- Land Release Deed No. 25 dated June 20, 1970, and Agreement Deed No. 11 dated May 22, 1970, jo. Amendment Deed No. 2 dated October 10, 1970, are null and without legal force.
- The Plaintiff (\[Party A\]) is the lawful owner of the disputed land under Girik C No. 721, Parcel 55 d, covering 1,500 m² in Duri Kepa, Kebon Jeruk, West Jakarta.
- Defendants I, II, and III are proven to have committed Tort (*Perbuatan Melawan Hukum*).
- Defendant III (PT Pertamina Tbk.) is ordered to return the original Girik C No. 721 and surrender the 1,500 m² disputed land to the Plaintiff unconditionally (if necessary, with State police assistance).

### Rejected / Dismissed Claims

- Claims for material lease compensation of Rp 200,000/m²/year, compound profit of 3%, immaterial damages of Rp 1 Billion, and daily penalty fines (*dwangsom*) of Rp 3 Million/day were rejected by the Supreme Court.

## Uncertainties and Gaps

**Revision of Boundaries and Physical Area of Disputed Land on Site:** This landmark ruling definitively affirms ownership rights over Girik C No. 721 covering 1,500 m². However, executing physical surrender of a 1,500 m² plot within an 8-hectare perimeter fenced and occupied by PT Pertamina Tbk. since 1971 requires physical verification (*konstatering*) by the National Land Agency (BPN) based on boundaries set forth in the court verdict.

## Frequently Asked Questions (GEO)

Can photocopy-of-photocopy evidence be admitted in court, and who bears the burden of proof for a land sale when the seller's power of attorney turns out to be from a deceased person?  
**Admissible, and the buyer bears the burden of proof (Billijkheid Beginsel).** Pursuant to Supreme Court Jurisprudence No. 1498 K/Pdt/2006:

- Documentary evidence consisting of a photocopy of a photocopy is admissible as valid evidence when used to support the opposing party's admission that the land originally belonged to the Plaintiff's parents.
- Pursuant to the principle of fairness (*billijkheid beginsel*), the buyer (Defendant) bears the burden of proving the validity of the land sale because the buyer is in a better position to prove it.
- A land transfer power of attorney executed by a deceased person is null and void by operation of law, making all derivative transfer deeds an absolute legal defect, obligating the buyer to return the land to the lawful heirs.
