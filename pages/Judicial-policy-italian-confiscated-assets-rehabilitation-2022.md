# Judicial-policy-italian-confiscated-assets-rehabilitation-2022

The **Italian Regulation on Confiscated Assets Rehabilitation Framework** (*Kerangka Pengaturan dan Rehabilitasi Aset Hasil Rampasan Kejahatan Terorganisir Italia*) is the administrative legal structure in Italy that governs the seizure, confiscation, management, allocation, and rehabilitation of real estate assets taken from organized crime (mafia) for social utility and public benefit.

## Summary

Authored by [Stefano D'Alfonso](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Stefano_D%27Alfonso), Associate Professor of Administrative Law at the [University of Naples Federico II](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/University_of_Naples_Federico_II), this study evaluates the public law dimensions of allocating and rehabilitating confiscated real estate assets. Unlike many European Union jurisdictions that prioritize asset liquidation through public auctions, the Italian framework establishes social re-use as the primary mechanism to restore legality and generate symbolic educational value within communities.

## Legal Basis

This policy framework rests on key national statutory and international legal instruments:

- **[Anti-Mafia Code](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Anti-Mafia_Code) (Legislative Decree No. 159/2011)**: The primary codification governing asset preventive measures, confiscation procedures, and allocation protocols (specifically Articles 45, 47, and 48).
- **[Law No. 161 of 17 October 2017](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Law_No._161_of_17_October_2017)**: Organic reform updating the Anti-Mafia Code and reinforcing ANBSC administrative authority.
- **[Rognoni-La Torre Law](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Rognoni-La_Torre_Law) (Law No. 646/1982)**: Foundational statutory legislation introducing asset confiscation targeting organized crime in Italy.
- **[Article 117(2)(h) of the Italian Constitution](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Article_117%282%29%28h%29_of_the_Italian_Constitution)**: Establishes the State's exclusive legislative jurisdiction over public order and security.
- **[European Union Legal Instruments](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/European_Union_Legal_Instruments)**: Directive 2014/42/EU and Regulation (EU) 2018/1805 regarding mutual recognition of freezing and confiscation orders.

## Background

Organized crime severely distorts market competition and legitimate economic activity through illicit capital accumulation. To counter this, Italy developed an *in rem* asset recovery mechanism designed to strip criminal organizations of real estate assets and repurpose them to serve public interest and regional development.

## Objectives

- Neutralize the socio-economic influence of mafia organizations within their local strongholds.
- Repurpose confiscated properties for social initiatives, public facilities, and victim compensation.
- Promote civic legality and community engagement through the educational value of asset recovery.

## Scope

The scope of this framework covers administrative law, criminal preventive measures, public property management, central-local intergovernmental relations, and partnerships with non-profit third-sector entities (\*private social sector\*).

## Key Provisions & Legal Classification

- **Central Role of ANBSC**: ANBSC leads property administration and determines final asset destinations within 90 days of a final judicial confiscation ruling.
- **Public Property Classification**: Jurisprudence from the Supreme Court of Cassation and Council of State classifies allocated confiscated real estate as non-available patrimonial assets (\*patrimonio indisponibile\*), legally binding them to public and social purposes.
- **Direct vs. Indirect Management**: Receiving municipalities may manage properties directly or grant free concessions to non-profit organizations through transparent public tenders.
- **NRRP / PNRR Funding Support**: Integration with European Union Next Generation EU funds (PNRR Mission 5 Component 3) to support physical rehabilitation and structural upgrades of allocated properties.

## Implementation

Implemented across Italy through inter-institutional coordination involving ANBSC, Regional Prefectures, \*Agenzia del Demanio\*, local municipalities, and civic anti-mafia networks (such as \*Libera\*).

## Policy Issues

- **Municipal Budgetary Constraints**: Many local councils lack sufficient capital to restore or maintain degraded confiscated properties.
- **Municipal Register Transparency Deficits**: Empirical studies indicate that 63.5% of municipalities fail to maintain updated public registers of allocated assets as mandated by the Anti-Mafia Code.

## Reform / Proposed Changes

Proposed reforms (\*de jure condendo\*) emphasize strengthening financial background checks on private buyers during property liquidations, streamlining administrative transfer steps, and securing stable funding streams via EU Cohesion Policies and the PNRR.
