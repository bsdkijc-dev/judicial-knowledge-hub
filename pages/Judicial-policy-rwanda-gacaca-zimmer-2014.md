# Judicial-policy-rwanda-gacaca-zimmer-2014

**Rwanda’s Gacaca Courts Transitional Justice Policy Framework** is a transitional justice policy framework and Rwandan indigenous community court model reviewed by Markus Zimmer (Executive Editor of the IJCA, 2014) in the *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA). It is based on a 10-year experiment (2002–2012) to try mid- and lower-level perpetrators following the 1994 genocide through a panel of 9 lay judges (respected community elders) without the presence of lawyers. The framework prioritized reconciliation, healing, and community service without violating the necessity of enforcing justice \[2, 6–8\].

## Summary

Published by Markus Zimmer in the *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA), this policy framework reviews the successes and challenges of the Gacaca Courts in Rwanda. Following the 1994 genocide, the *International Criminal Tribunal for Rwanda* (ICTR) in Arusha only prosecuted top-level ringleaders. The mass detention of tens of thousands of lower-level perpetrators paralyzed Rwanda's formal justice system. As a solution, the Rwandan Government revived the indigenous Gacaca courts—a justice system without lawyers, led by panels of 9 lay judges chosen from respected community elders. Operating in open-air settings, these proceedings prioritized reconciliation and community service sentences. Despite possessing flaws when compared to 21st-century Western judicial standards, the Gacaca Courts, which officially closed on June 17–18, 2012, are globally recognized as a bold and innovative transitional justice experiment.

## Background

The failure of the international justice system to try lower-level perpetrators and the operational paralysis of Rwanda's formal prisons prompted the birth of this community-based judicial innovation \[4–6\].

## Objectives

1\. Independently and inclusively try tens of thousands of mid- and lower-level post-genocide perpetrators. 2. Realize social reconciliation and community healing over the spirit of vengeance. 3. Alleviate the severe burden on the national prison system through community service sentences.

## Scope

This policy encompasses the prosecution of mid- and lower-level genocide crimes across villages and towns throughout the Republic of Rwanda.

## Key Provisions

- **Panel of 9 Lay Judges:** Trials were presided over by nine respected community elders without the involvement or representation of lawyers.
- **Open Community Proceedings:** Hearings were conducted outdoors in public spaces, attended by local residents and survivors to confirm or refute the defendants' confessional testimonies.
- **Community Service Sentences:** A strong preference for community service penalties to restore the community and reintegrate the perpetrators into their original environments.

## Implementation

- **10-Year Operationalization (2002–2012):** The processing of tens of thousands of genocide suspects through thousands of localized Gacaca panels.
- **Official Closing Ceremony:** The formal termination of the Gacaca Courts by the Government of Rwanda on June 17–18, 2012.

## Policy Problems

- **Absence of Legal Counsel:** International criticism regarding the lack of defense lawyers for the accused during Gacaca proceedings (*justice without lawyers*).
- **Overextended Prison Capacity:** An operational crisis resulting from the mass detention of suspects following the civil war.

## Reform / Change

- Global recognition of indigenous/community-based courts as a viable transitional justice model in post-conflict environments.

### Deutsch (Schweizer Hochdeutsch)

**Ruandas Gacaca-Gerichte und Rahmenwerk für Übergangsjustiz** Dieser von Markus Zimmer im *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA, 2014) veröffentlichte Rahmen bewertet das zehnjährige Experiment (2002–2012) der Gacaca-Gerichte in Ruanda. Nach dem Völkermord von 1994 waren die formellen Gefängnisse überlastet, und das UN-Tribunal (ICTR) verfolgte nur die Haupttäter. Ruanda reaktivierte traditionelle Gemeindegerichte, in denen Gremien aus 9 Laienrichtern (respektierten Ältesten) ohne Anwälte über Täter der mittleren und unteren Ebene urteilten. Das System priorisierte Versöhnung, Heilung und gemeinnützige Arbeit und wird als mutiges Modell der Übergangsjustiz anerkannt.

- **Rechtsgrundlage:** Ruandische Richtlinien zur Übergangsjustiz; UN-Mandat und ICTR.

### Français

**Tribunaux Gacaca du Rwanda et cadre de justice de transition** Publié par Markus Zimmer dans l' *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA, 2014), ce cadre évalue l'expérience de 10 ans (2002-2012) des tribunaux Gacaca au Rwanda. Après le génocide de 1994, les prisons formelles étaient saturées et le tribunal de l'ONU (TPIR) ne jugeait que les principaux responsables. Le Rwanda a réactivé des tribunaux communautaires traditionnels où des collèges de 9 juges non professionnels (anciens respectés) jugeaient les auteurs de niveau intermédiaire et inférieur, sans avocats. Le système, qui privilégiait la réconciliation, la guérison et les travaux d'intérêt général, est reconnu comme un modèle audacieux de justice de transition.

- **Base Légale:** Politique rwandaise de justice de transition ; Mandat de l'ONU et TPIR.

### Italiano

Dopo il genocidio del 1994, le carceri formali erano sovraffollate e il tribunale dell'ONU (ICTR) processava solo i leader principali. Il Ruanda ha riattivato i tribunali comunitari tradizionali in cui collegi di 9 giudici laici (anziani rispettati) giudicavano i colpevoli di livello medio e inferiore senza l'ausilio di avvocati. Il sistema, che dava priorità alla riconciliazione, alla guarigione e ai servizi sociali, è riconosciuto come un coraggioso modello di giustizia di transizione.
