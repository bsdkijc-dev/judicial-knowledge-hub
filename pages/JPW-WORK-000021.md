## 4. POLICY ANALYSIS

- **Background**: \[FACT\] The 2024 report was drafted following a structured dialogue facilitated by the European Commission between Spain's two main political parties starting in January 2024. This dialogue yielded a historic agreement on June 25, 2024, to renew the General Council of the Judiciary (CGPJ), ending a political deadlock that had persisted for over five years since December 2018.
- **Policy Problem**: \[FACT\]

1.  **CGPJ Deadlock & Operational Impact**: The five-year stalemate resulted in 99 unfulfilled judicial vacancies across high courts in Spain, including 27 vacancies at the Supreme Court, severely undermining judicial efficiency.
2.  **Independence of the Prosecutor General**: Public and stakeholder concern over the alignment of the State Attorney General's term with the executive government's term of office.
3.  **Delayed Criminal Procedure Reform**: Continued delay in adopting the reform of the Criminal Procedure Act (LECrim) to accelerate high-level corruption investigations.
4.  **Conflicts of Interest & Lobbying**: Limited sanctioning powers of the Conflict of Interest Office (OCI) and the lack of mandatory lobbying regulation and registries.
5.  **Institutional Advertising & Media Transparency**: Lack of detailed breakdown in institutional advertising distribution to specific media outlets and high risks of media ownership concentration.
6.  **Amnesty Law & Emergency Procedures**: Rapid enactment of the Organic Amnesty Law on May 30, 2024, and frequent use of emergency legislative procedures restricting public consultation.

- **Legal/Policy Gap**: \[FACT\] Absence of an automatic anti-deadlock mechanism in the LOPJ for CGPJ renewals; lack of statutory cooling-off periods for judges and prosecutors returning from political roles; absence of a mandatory centralized register for lobbyists; and delays in establishing the Independent Authority for Whistleblower Protection under Law 2/2023.
- **Objectives**: \[FACT\] Resolve the CGPJ deadlock and reform its appointment process according to European standards; strengthen the independence of the Attorney General; advance the LECrim reform and national anti-corruption strategies; enhance transparency in institutional advertising allocations and media ownership; and ensure legislative procedures adhere to strict legal certainty standards.
- **Implementation**: \[FACT\]
- **CGPJ Agreement of June 25, 2024**: Appointment of 20 new CGPJ members in July 2024 and a mandate for the new council to draft a proposal reforming the CGPJ selection process within six months.
- **Judicial Digitalisation**: Execution of Royal Decree-Law 6/2023 establishing an integrated catalog of digital services and mandatory e-litigation mechanisms.
- **Audiovisual Information Protection**: Launch of the State Register of Audiovisual Media Services Providers in May 2024 under RD 1138/2023 and mandatory registration for influencers under RD 444/2024.
- **Amnesty Law Enactment**: Approval of Organic Law 1/2024 on Amnesty on May 30, 2024, and its entry into force on June 11, 2024.
- **Evidence & Impact**: \[FACT\] Public perception of judicial independence in Spain remains low (37% of the general public and 32% of businesses rated it as good in 2024); the addition of 23 technical attorneys to the Supreme Court's Technical Cabinet enabled an extra 1,000 rulings per year; and the disposition time for civil/commercial cases at the Supreme Court decreased from 826 days (2021) to 691 days (2022).
- **Reform & Unresolved Issues**: \[FACT\] Finalizing the LOPJ Reform Bill to mandate a 2-year cooling-off period for judges returning from politics; adopting the National Anti-Corruption Strategy; passing statutory lobbying legislation; revising the 1968 Official Secrets Act; and enacting the Public Justice Service Efficiency Bill.

## 6. MEDIAWIKI OUTPUT

## Summary

**2024 Rule of Law Report: Country Chapter on Spain** (SWD(2024) 809 final) (Stable ID: **JPW-WORK-000021**) is an official evaluation report issued by the European Commission on July 24, 2024, in Brussels. The document provides a comprehensive evaluation of the Rule of Law in Spain. A central highlight of the report is the historic agreement reached on June 25, 2024, through structured dialogue facilitated by the European Commission, ending a five-year political deadlock over the renewal of the General Council of the Judiciary (CGPJ). Additionally, the report examines progress in judicial digitalisation (RD-Law 6/2023), challenges regarding the independence of the State Attorney General, pending reforms to the Criminal Procedure Act (LECrim), new registry requirements for media ownership and influencers, and the institutional impact of the Organic Amnesty Law for Catalonia.

## Legal Basis

This evaluation report was prepared under the annual European Union Rule of Law Mechanism and references the following constitutional and statutory frameworks in Spain:

- **Spanish Constitution (CE)**: Article 117 (judicial independence), Article 122 (structure and appointment of the CGPJ), Article 124 (Statute of the Public Prosecutor's Office), and Article 159 (Constitutional Court).
- **Judicial & Procedural Statutes**: Organic Law 6/1985 on the Judiciary (LOPJ Art. 568), Law 50/1981 on the Organic Statute of the Public Prosecutor's Office, Royal Decree-Law 6/2023 (judicial digitalisation), and RD-Law 5/2023.
- **Anti-Corruption & Transparency Regulations**: Law 19/2013 on Transparency, Law 2/2023 on Whistleblower Protection, and Law 3/2015 on Senior Officials.
- **Media & Fundamental Rights Statutes**: General Audiovisual Communication Law 13/2022, Royal Decree 1138/2023, RD 444/2024, Organic Law 4/2015 on Citizen Security, and Organic Law 1/2024 on Amnesty.

## Background

From December 2018 until mid-2024, the CGPJ operated under an ad interim status due to the lack of a three-fifths parliamentary majority required for its renewal. This stalemate accumulated 99 high-court judicial vacancies across Spain, severely impairing Supreme Court operations. In January 2024, the European Commission initiated a structured dialogue between Spain's governing and opposition parties, culminating in the signing of the CGPJ Renewal Agreement on June 25, 2024.

## Objectives

1\. Facilitate the immediate appointment of 20 new CGPJ members and initiate structural reforms to its appointment system in line with Council of Europe standards. 2. Reinforce the operational and statutory autonomy of the State Attorney General's Office from the executive branch. 3. Accelerate the adoption of the Criminal Procedure Act (LECrim) reform to expedite complex corruption inquiries. 4. Promote statutory regulation for lobbying transparency and mandatory lobbyist registration. 5. Increase transparency in state advertising allocations and media ownership structures. 6. Ensure the implementation of Organic Law 1/2024 on Amnesty complies with EU law and Venice Commission recommendations.

## Scope

The assessment is divided into four main pillars: 1. *Justice System*: CGPJ renewal, Supreme Court vacancy backlogs, Attorney General statute, digitalisation under RD-Law 6/2023, and the Defense Rights Bill. 2. *Anti-Corruption Framework*: High-level corruption prosecution, LECrim reform, Conflict of Interest Office (OCI) enforcement, lobbying regulation, and Law 2/2023 execution. 3. *Media Freedom and Pluralism*: CNMC autonomy, State Media Registers (RD 1138/2023 & RD 444/2024 for influencers), institutional advertising, and Official Secrets Act revision. 4. *Institutional Checks and Balances*: Evaluation of the Catalan Amnesty Law, emergency legislative procedures, and Citizen Security Law reforms.

## Key Provisions

- **CGPJ Agreement of June 25, 2024**: Mandates the appointment of 20 new CGPJ members and requires the council to submit a proposal within 6 months to reform future judicial selection rules.
- **Incompatibilities & Cooling-Off Periods**: Establishes a mandatory 2-year cooling-off period for judges and prosecutors returning to judicial duties after serving in political or executive office.
- **Attorney General Statute**: Recommends prohibiting individuals who have held political office in preceding years from being appointed State Attorney General.
- **Judicial Digitalisation (RD-Law 6/2023)**: Establishes a mandatory catalog of digital judicial services and standardized e-litigation platforms.
- **Audiovisual Media Register (RD 1138/2023)**: Requires public disclosure of media ownership details and influencer commercial activity.

## Implementation

Implementation is driven through the execution of the political CGPJ agreement, enforcement of RD-Law 6/2023 by the Ministry of Justice, and oversight by the European Commission, GRECO, and the Venice Commission.

## Policy Problems

- Lack of statutory decoupling between the State Attorney General's term of office and the executive government's mandate.
- Prolonged investigation timelines in complex corruption cases due to delayed LECrim reforms.
- Absence of independent sanctioning authority within the Conflict of Interest Office (OCI).
- Opaque distribution criteria and data regarding state institutional advertising at national and regional levels.
- Frequent use of fast-track emergency legislative procedures restricting public stakeholder consultation.

## Reform

- Execution of the CGPJ Renewal Agreement on June 25, 2024.
- Enactment of Royal Decree-Law 6/2023 on the Digitalisation of Justice.
- Issuance of Royal Decrees 1138/2023 and 444/2024 governing Audiovisual Media Services and Influencers.
- Passage of Organic Law 1/2024 on Amnesty for Catalonia.

## Relationships with Other Entities

- **Implements**: EU Rule of Law Mechanism & Spain's Recovery, Transformation, and Resilience Plan (RRP).
- **Applies to**: CGPJ, Supreme Court, State Attorney General's Office, Conflict of Interest Office, CNMC, RTVE, and public institutions across Spain.
- **Conflicts with**: Political deadlocks, unfulfilled judicial vacancies, and opaque public fund allocations.
