# JPW-REPORT-001

**2019 Report on Preliminary Examination Activities (ICC OTP)** is the official annual report published by the Office of the Prosecutor (OTP) of the International Criminal Court on 5 December 2019 \[\].

## Summary

This report summarizes the analysis and preliminary examination activities conducted by the OTP between 1 December 2018 and 30 November 2019 \[\]. The document covers examinations of various international situations worldwide grouped according to analytical phases \[\].

## Legal Basis

The execution of preliminary examinations is grounded in Article 53(1) of the [Rome Statute](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Rome_Statute), which stipulates that the Prosecutor must consider criteria of jurisdiction (temporal, territorial/personal, and subject-matter), admissibility (complementarity and gravity), and the interests of justice \[\].

## Background

The preliminary examination process can be triggered through three pathways: (i) information from individuals, groups, States, or NGOs via article 15 communications; (ii) referrals from States Parties or the UN Security Council; or (iii) declarations accepting jurisdiction under Article 12(3) of the Rome Statute \[\].

## Objectives

The primary objective of this report is to promote transparency in the preliminary examination process, provide rationale for decisions to proceed or not proceed with investigations, and encourage genuine national proceedings \[\].

## Key Provisions

The OTP applies a four-phase filtering process:

- \*\*Phase 1\*\*: Initial review of all incoming Article 15 communications \[\].
- \*\*Phase 2\*\*: Assessment of jurisdictional boundaries (territorial/personal) and subject-matter jurisdiction \[\].
- \*\*Phase 3\*\*: Assessment of admissibility, focusing on national complementarity and crime gravity \[\].
- \*\*Phase 4\*\*: Assessment of the interests of justice \[\].

## Implementation

During the reporting period, the OTP received 795 Article 15 communications \[\]. Of those, 617 communications were manifestly outside the Court's jurisdiction, 112 were linked to ongoing situations under examination, 25 related to active investigations/prosecutions, and 41 required in-depth factual and legal analysis \[\].

## Policy Issues

Major challenges faced include the absence of investigative powers for the OTP during the preliminary examination stage, the compromising of evidence at domestic judicial levels in certain states, and the lack of a strict statutory time limit to complete examinations \[\].
