## Overview

Supreme Court Decision Reg. No. 266 K/Ag/1993 dated June 25, 1994, establishes fundamental Islamic civil jurisprudence in divorce proceedings: The provisions of Article 19 letter f of Government Regulation No. 9 of 1975 are fulfilled if the \*judex facti\* considers that the grounds for divorce are proven without questioning who is at fault. If the court is convinced that the marriage has broken down (\*broken marriage\*), meaning the hearts of both parties are estranged, continuous disputes and quarrels without hope of reconciliation are established. Furthermore, the determination of \*mut'ah\* for the wife in the form of the entire shared matrimonial house and land is proper and just, taking into account the wife's standing and the husband's financial capacity.

## Case Background

The Petitioner for Cassation / formerly Petitioner / Appellee (\[Husband\]) filed a petition for permission to pronounce \*talak\* against the Respondent for Cassation / formerly Respondent / Appellant (\[Wife\]) at the South Jakarta Religious Court. The Petitioner and Respondent were married on December 25, 1985, at the Office of Religious Affairs (\*KUA\*) of Luhak 50 Kota Sub-District (Payakumbuh) and were blessed with no children. Since early 1991, disputes and estrangement arose due to differing life values, and both had lived separately for more than 1.5 years.

The South Jakarta Religious Court (Decision No. 473/Pdt.G/1992/PA.JS dated October 8, 1992) granted the Petitioner's request and granted permission to the Petitioner to divorce the Respondent. The Jakarta High Religious Court (Decision No. 03/1993/PTA.JK dated July 14, 1993) annulled the South Jakarta Religious Court judgment and rejected the Petitioner's request in its entirety on the grounds that the dispute originated from the Petitioner's involvement with another woman. Dissatisfied with the Jakarta High Religious Court ruling, the Petitioner filed a cassation petition to the Supreme Court.

## Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

- **Ratio Decidendi:**

1.  The Jakarta High Religious Court erred in applying the law by failing to consider the actual facts occurring between the litigant spouses, where the Petitioner and Respondent had lived separately for more than 1 (one) year, and disharmony existed in their household.
2.  The grounds for divorce under Article 19 letter f of Government Regulation No. 9 of 1975 are solely directed at the marriage itself without questioning who is at fault. If the court is convinced that the marriage has broken down (\*broken marriage\*), it means the hearts of both parties are estranged, thereby fulfilling the requirements of Article 19 letter f of Government Regulation No. 9 of 1975.
3.  Regarding \*mut'ah\*, considering the wife's standing, the husband's financial capacity, and the Petitioner's offer submitted in the cassation memorandum, it is proper and just that \*mut'ah\* in the form of the entire house and land constituting the shared matrimonial residence located at \[address removed\], South Jakarta, be surrendered to the Respondent.

- **Ruling:** Granting the cassation petition from Petitioner for Cassation: \[Husband\]; Annulling the Jakarta High Religious Court decision dated July 14, 1993, No. 03/1993/PTA.JK; and Independently Adjudicating: Affirming the South Jakarta Religious Court decision dated October 8, 1992, No. 473/Pdt.G/1992/PA.JS with amendments to the dispositive order to read as follows: (1) Granting the Petitioner's request; (2) Granting permission to the Petitioner (\[Husband\]) to pronounce \*talak\* against the Respondent (\[Wife\]) before the session of the South Jakarta Religious Court; (3) Ordering the Petitioner to surrender \*mut'ah\* in the form of the house and land at \[address removed\], Lebak Lestari Indah, Lebak Bulus Village, Cilandak Sub-District, South Jakarta, to the Respondent; (4) Ordering the Petitioner to pay court costs. \*(Decided in the deliberation session of the Supreme Court on Saturday, June 25, 1994).\*
