# JPW-POLICY-000008

The **Chinese Dispute Resolution and Judicial Reform Framework** (Stable ID: `JPW-POLICY-000008`) is a national judicial policy and research framework established to evaluate and modernize court litigation and out-of-court alternative dispute resolution (ADR) in the People's Republic of China. Documented under IDE Asian Law Series No. 15 by Professor Xin Chunying, Professor Liu Junhai, and Professor Ye Ziqiang of the CASS Institute of Law, the policy outlines structural measures for open trials, anti-judicial corruption, judge professionalization, execution of court decisions, and specialized consumer, labor, and environmental dispute resolution. Note: Named entities *Cecep Mustafa* and *Reihan Abdan Syakur* are *Not found in the available sources*.

## Summary

The framework addresses the exploding litigation caseloads (increasing from 1.7 million cases in 1986 to over 6.2 million in 2000) generated by China's transition to a market economy. It outlines five core structural pillars: (1) Judicial Transparency, mandating open trials and pioneering live television court broadcasts across 58 intermediate courts; (2) Judicial Anti-Corruption, implementing the Supreme People's Court (SPC) 1998 Punishment Regulation for Trial Malpractice and a 3-year ban on retired judges practicing before former courts; (3) Judge Professionalization, establishing unified nationwide legal examinations starting in 2002; (4) Enforcement Reform, publicizing debt repudiators' names in local media and restricting luxury consumption; and (5) Multi-Tiered Dispute Pyramid, integrating traditional Confucian "no-litigation" (*Wu Song*) mediation with modern commercial arbitration (CIETAC) and specialized statutory procedures.

## Legal Basis

Authority and execution derive from constitutional provisions and statutory enactments:

- **Constitutional Basis:** Constitution of the People's Republic of China (1982, Arts. 123–128).
- **Procedural Codes:** Civil Procedure Law of the PRC (1991, Arts. 135, 146, 159, 177–186); Administrative Procedure Law (1989); Criminal Procedure Law (1996).
- **Specialized & ADR Statutes:** Arbitration Law of the PRC (1994, Art. 16); Labor Law of the PRC (1994, Arts. 79–84); Law on the Protection of Consumer Rights and Interests (1993, Arts. 32, 34, 50); Environmental Protection Law (1989, Art. 41).
- **Judicial Governance:** Organic Law of the People's Courts; Judges Law of the PRC.

## Background & Policy Problems

### Background

Economic reforms and the breakdown of the urban workplace unit (*Danwei*) system created a "litigious society" in China. As private property and contractual relations expanded, citizens increasingly sought social justice through courts. In 1999, the SPC formulated the *Outline of Five-Year Reform Plan for the People's Courts* to modernize judicial administration.

### Policy Problems

- Judicial Corruption & Misjudged Cases: Bribes, power abuse, and retired judges working as counsel within 3 years to influence former colleagues.
- Lack of Judicial Independence & Local Subordination: Financial and administrative reliance on local governments, allowing local authorities to control judge salaries, facilities, and appointments.
- Rampant Regional Protectionism: Protection of local state-owned enterprises against outside creditors, resulting in massive backlogs of unexecuted court decisions (~1 million unexecuted cases worth 190 billion yuan in 1998).
- Unqualified Judicial Personnel: Historical recruitment of retired military officers lacking formal legal education.
- Excessive Supervision Interference: Individual case supervision by political bodies and prosecutors overriding judicial finality.

## Objectives

1\. Secure judicial impartiality by eliminating bribes, power abuse, and judge corruption. 2. Enhance transparency through open trials and televised court hearings. 3. Eradicate regional protectionism and enforce pending court judgments. 4. Professionalize judicial personnel through rigorous national examination standards. 5. Provide accessible, low-cost dispute resolution via negotiation, grass-roots people's mediation, and specialized arbitration.

## Scope & Implementation

### Scope

Applies across all levels of People's Courts (Basic, Intermediate, Higher, Supreme People's Court), People's Procuratorates, arbitration commissions, consumer associations, and grass-roots mediation committees in the PRC.

### Key Provisions

- **Open Trial Mandate:** Mandatory public access for citizens aged 18+ to court hearings and live TV trial coverage.
- **Anti-Corruption Reporting:** SPC hotlines, public reporting centers, and a 3-year post-retirement litigation ban for judges.
- **Labor Dispute Sequence:** Mandatory 3-stage process (Employing Unit Mediation → Labor Dispute Arbitration Committee within 60 days → People's Court Lawsuit within 15 days).
- **Environmental Mediation & Coercion:** Environmental administrative departments empowered to mediate damage claims and issue plant suspension/closure orders.
- **Enforcement Measures:** Media exposure of debt repudiators, detention of recalcitrant debtors, and daily consumption caps.

### Implementation

- **SPC Five-Year Reform Execution:** Strengthening collegial benches and limiting Adjudication Committees (*Shenpan Weiyuanhui*) to complex cases.
- **Media Telecasts:** Live TV trial broadcasts across 58 intermediate courts and CCTV.
- **Arbitration Expansion:** Operationalization of CIETAC and Beijing Arbitration Commission (BAC) handling hundreds of commercial cases annually.

## Policy Problems

- Local Fiscal Dependency: Local governments controlling court budgets, fostering regional protectionism.
- Individual Case Interference: People's Congresses and political committees issuing case instructions that override judicial finality.
- Judgment Execution Backlog: Over 1 million unexecuted cases valued at 190 billion yuan pending in 1998.

## Reform

- Proposed creation of a National Judicial Reform Committee under the NPC Standing Committee.
- Establishing small claims courts and centralizing judicial financing.

### Key Figures

- **Professor Xin Chunying:** Deputy Director, Institute of Law, Chinese Academy of Social Sciences (CASS); Team Leader and principal author.
- **Professor Liu Junhai:** Professor at the Institute of Law, CASS; author of sections on court systems, ADR, and consumer protection.
- **Professor Ye Ziqiang:** Professor at the Institute of Law, CASS; contributor.
- **Xiao Yang:** President of the Supreme People's Court of China (appointed 1998); champion of open trials, live TV broadcasts, and court reform.

- **Luo Ji & Huang Lizhi:** Former leadership of the Anti-Corruption Bureau under the Supreme People's Procuratorate.
- **Confucius (Kong Zi):** Ancient philosopher whose "no-litigation" preference (*Wu Song*) shaped traditional legal culture.
