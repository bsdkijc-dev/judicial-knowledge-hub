## Overview

Supreme Court Decision No. 004K/AG/1979 establishes an essential legal rule regarding divorce procedures under national marriage law. The Supreme Court outlined that since the enforcement of Law No. 1 of 1974 jo. Government Regulation No. 9 of 1975, a divorce initiated by a husband (talak) must be performed in front of a session of the local Religious Court / Mahkamah Syar'iyah. Unilateral pronouncements of talak outside the court session without valid legal grounds are considered "wild talak" (\*talak liar\*) that fail to comply with statutory provisions.

## Case Background

The Defendant for Cassation / Original Petitioner (\[Party B\]) unilaterally pronounced talak outside the court against his wife, the Petitioner for Cassation / Original Respondent (\[Party A\]), on December 4, 1977, accusing her of using witchcraft. \[Party B\] subsequently petitioned the Padang Panjang Religious Court to legalize the divorce. The Padang Panjang Religious Court / Mahkamah Syar'iyah (Decision No. 03/1978) rejected the divorce petition and declared \[Party B\]'s talak pronouncement as a "wild talak" due to non-compliance with legal procedures. However, the Provincial Religious Court / Mahkamah Syar'iyah in Padang (Decision No. 1/1978) annulled the first-instance decision and legalized the pronouncement of the first talak. Dissatisfied with the appellate ruling, \[Party A\] filed a cassation petition to the Supreme Court.

## Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

- **Ratio Decidendi:** Since the enactment of Law No. 1 of 1974 jo. Government Regulation No. 9 of 1975, a divorce executed by a husband (talak) must be conducted before a session of the local Religious Court / Mahkamah Syar'iyah. A husband's act of pronouncing talak outside the court session fails to fulfill the grounds and requirements for divorce under applicable laws and regulations, thus categorizing such a talak pronouncement as a wild talak (\*talak liar\*).
- **Ruling:** Granting the cassation petition filed by Plaintiff for Cassation \[Party A\]; annulling the Provincial Religious Court / Mahkamah Syar'iyah Decision in Padang No. 1/1978 dated March 9, 1978; and Independently Adjudicating: (1) Rejecting the divorce petition from \[Party B\] against his wife \[Party A\] due to a lack of legal grounds and requirements for divorce; (2) Establishing and classifying \[Party B\]'s talak pronouncement toward his wife \[Party A\] as a wild talak (\*talak liar\*) for failing to conform to prevailing statutory provisions; (3) Ordering the Petitioner / Defendant for Cassation to bear all procedural costs.
