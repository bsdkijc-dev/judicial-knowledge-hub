# JPW-POLICY-TJPP2022

**Civil Liability of Property Business Actors for Delayed Construction** () is an Indonesian legal framework and civil liability mechanism. It holds property developers and construction business actors accountable for failing to complete or delaying building construction within agreed timelines. Under Indonesian civil law, such delays are categorized as a breach of contract (*wanprestasi*) unless explicitly caused by *force majeure*.

## Policy Analysis

### Background & Context

Housing is a primary human necessity and a strategic economic sector generating multi-industry multiplier effects, particularly across raw material and steel production. In the first nine months of 2021, the Indonesian real estate and construction sectors experienced constrained year-on-year (yoy) growth of 2.40% and 2.43% respectively, largely driven by subdued household consumption (1.50% yoy). Despite economic fluctuations, housing demand remains constant, making legal certainty in real estate transactions critical.

### Core Legal & Policy Problems

1.  **Construction Delays:** Developers and property business actors frequently fail to complete construction within contractually agreed timelines (e.g., promising completion within 6 months but exceeding deadlines without legal justification).
2.  **Misleading Marketing:** Developers engage in misleading promotional practices prior to consumers signing binding sale and purchase agreements (*PPJB*) or entering mortgage loan agreements with financial institutions.
3.  **Standardized Contracts (*Klausula Baku*):** Imbalance of bargaining power in pre-formulated standard agreements limits consumer negotiation, raising doubts regarding authentic mutual consent (*kata sepakat*) under Article 1320 of the Indonesian Civil Code (KUHPerdata).

### Legal Basis & Mandatory Provisions

Under **Article 47 of Law No. 2 of 2017**, every written Construction Work Contract (*Kontrak Kerja Konstruksi*) executed between a contractor (*penyedia jasa*) and a consumer client (*pengguna jasa*) within Indonesia must contain at least 15 mandatory clauses:

1.  Clear identification of all contracting parties
2.  Scope of work, contract value, unit prices, lump sums, and execution deadlines
3.  Maintenance and warranty periods under developer responsibility
4.  Equal rights and obligations between parties
5.  Requirement to employ certified construction personnel
6.  Payment terms and financial guarantees
7.  Default provisions and liability assignment
8.  Dispute resolution procedures
9.  Grounds and mechanisms for contract termination
10. Liabilities regarding Building Failure and *force majeure*
11. Occupational health, safety, and social security protections
12. Third-party protection against loss, injury, or death
13. Environmental compliance obligations
14. Risk insurance and legal liability coverage
15. Selection of the dispute settlement forum

### Impact & Legal Remedies

When a developer fails to complete construction on schedule, the delay constitutes a breach of contract (*wanprestasi*). Under \*\*Article 1243 of the KUHPerdata\*\*, default triggers civil liability, granting consumers the right to demand:

- Specific performance of the contract
- Full contract cancellation
- Compensation for incurred costs, actual damages, and interest accrued
- A combination of cancellation and financial compensation

How does Article 1243 KUHPerdata connect developer default to civil liability?  
Article 1243 establishes that failure to fulfill a contractual obligation after formal notice of default obligates the defaulting party to compensate the injured party for costs, damages, and interest.
