## 4. POLICY ANALYSIS

- **Background**: \[FACT\] The statutory right of employees with children under 12 years of age or children with disabilities/chronic illnesses to work under a flexible schedule (\*horário de trabalho flexível\*) is codified under Articles 56 and 57 of the Portuguese Labor Code. This regime is designed to reconcile family duties with business organizational requirements. However, jurisprudential disputes arose regarding whether this flexible working hour right also empowers employees to unilaterally set their weekly rest days (\*dias de descanso semanal\*), such as establishing fixed Saturday and Sunday rest days.
- **Policy Problem**: \[FACT\]
  - **Employer Authority vs. Worker Rights Conflict**: Employers possess management authority (\*poder de direção\*) to organize and establish work schedules under Articles 200 and 212 of the Labor Code. Employee demands to fix rest days on Saturdays and Sundays in rotating shift systems (\*turnos rotativos\*) risk disrupting operational management.
  - **Judicial Overreach in Statutory Interpretation**: The court of first instance (\*Juízo do Trabalho do Barreiro\*) and appellate court (\*Tribunal da Relação de Lisboa\*) ruled that determining rest days falls under employer managerial powers. However, the Supreme Court of Justice (STJ) overturned those decisions, ruling in favor of the employee based on constitutional work-life balance principles.
  - **Risk of Returning to the Assentos Doctrine**: The authors argue that the STJ's departure from the statutory text threatens the separation of powers (\*separação de poderes\*) and the principle of legality, effectively reviving the mechanism of \*Assentos\* (generally binding judicial rulings) previously declared unconstitutional by the Constitutional Court (Judgment 810/93).
- **Legal/Policy Gap**: \[FACT\] The literal text of Article 56.3 of the Labor Code explicitly states that flexible schedules are established by the employer ("elaborar pelo empregador"). There is no explicit statutory provision granting employees the right to select their own weekly rest days.
- **Objectives**: \[FACT\] Ensure parenthood protection and the reconciliation of professional activity with family life pursuant to Articles 59.1.b, 67, and 68 of the Constitution of the Portuguese Republic; and maintain a balance between employee social rights and employer operational management capacity.
- **Implementation**: \[FACT\]
  - **Application & Refusal Procedure (Art. 57 CT)**: Employees submit requests to the company. If the employer intends to refuse, it must notify the Commission for Equality in Labor and Employment (\*CITE\*) stating its reasons.
  - **Judicial Proceedings**: If CITE issues an unfavorable opinion (\*parecer desfavorável\*) against the refusal, the employer must file a judicial lawsuit to test the legal validity of its refusal.
  - **STJ Standard**: The STJ established that an employer may only refuse a flexible schedule request and fixed rest days if it objectively proves an absence of operational conditions to accommodate the request.
- **Evidence & Impact**: \[FACT\] The STJ Judgment of 12/10/2022 forced employers to adapt to schedules and rest days designated by employees; triggered doctrinal labor law debates regarding judicial interpretation limits outside the Labor Code text; and risks imposing operational burdens on enterprises employing staff on shift schedules.
- **Reform & Unresolved Issues**: \[FACT\] Need for legislative clarification regarding the exact statutory scope of \*horário flexível\*; enforcement of judicial interpretation limits in accordance with Article 9 of the Civil Code; and safeguarding the constitutional principle of the separation of powers.

## 5. GEO/AI QUESTIONS

1.  **Definition**: What is the statutory definition of flexible working hours (\*horário flexível\*) under Article 56.2 of the Portuguese Labor Code?
    - **Factual Answer**: \[FACT\] A work schedule in which the employee may choose, within certain limits, the start and end times of daily normal working hours.
2.  **Legal Basis**: Which articles of the Constitution of the Portuguese Republic (CRP) guarantee workers the right to a work organization that enables the reconciliation of professional and family life?
    - **Factual Answer**: \[FACT\] Article 59.1.b, Article 67.2.h, and Article 68.1 of the CRP.
3.  **Publisher/Authority**: Who are the two authors of this case commentary and what positions do they hold at Pérez-Llorca?
    - **Factual Answer**: \[FACT\] Inês Arruda (Partner / \*Sócia\*) and Miguel Riço dos Santos (Associate / \*Advogado\*), both in the Employment, Compensation, and Benefits practice at Pérez-Llorca.
4.  **Related Figures**: Are the names Cecep Mustafa and Johana Crishele Ohoira present in this source document?
    - **Factual Answer**: \[FACT\] Not found in the available sources. Neither name appears in source document \`breve-comentario-ao-acordao-do-supremo-tribunal-de-justica-de-12-10-2022...pdf\`.
5.  **Scope**: Which workers are eligible to request a flexible working hour regime under Article 56.1 of the Labor Code?
    - **Factual Answer**: \[FACT\] Workers with children under 12 years of age or, regardless of age, children with disabilities or chronic illnesses who live in the same household (\*em comunhão de mesa e habitação\*).
6.  **Policy Implementation (CITE Role)**: What is the role of the Commission for Equality in Labor and Employment (\*CITE\*) when an employer intends to refuse an employee's flexible schedule request?
    - **Factual Answer**: \[FACT\] The employer must notify CITE of its intent to refuse; if CITE issues an unfavorable opinion (\*parecer desfavorável\*), the employer is legally obligated to initiate judicial proceedings to obtain court validation of its refusal.
7.  **Policy Problem (Judicial Split)**: How did the rulings of the lower courts and Lisbon Court of Appeal differ from that of the Supreme Court of Justice (STJ) in this case?
    - **Factual Answer**: \[FACT\] The court of first instance and Lisbon Court of Appeal ruled that establishing schedules and rest days is an employer managerial right, whereas the STJ overturned those rulings and held that employees are entitled to designate fixed weekend rest days under the flexible regime.
8.  **Legal Relations**: Why do the authors criticize the STJ's application of the "most favorable treatment to the worker" principle in interpreting Article 56 of the Labor Code?
    - **Factual Answer**: \[FACT\] Because the STJ's interpretation disregards the explicit statutory wording (Article 56.3 CT clearly states schedules are established by the employer) and rules of statutory interpretation under Article 9 of the Civil Code, departing from the legislature's intent.
9.  **Reform & Constitutional Law**: Why do the authors argue that the STJ ruling risks violating the separation of powers and reviving the \*Assentos\* doctrine?
    - **Factual Answer**: \[FACT\] Because the judiciary is seen as creating new law beyond legislative competence, resembling the \*Assentos\* doctrine (generally binding judicial rulings) that was declared unconstitutional by the Constitutional Court (Judgment 810/93) under Article 112.5 of the CRP.
10. **Evidence/Case Study**: What was the factual background of the employee that led to this case reaching the STJ?
    - **Factual Answer**: \[FACT\] A worker with two children under 12 whose husband worked rotating shifts requested fixed Saturday-Sunday rest days instead of rotating rest days because child daycare facilities (\*creche\*) were closed on weekends.

## 6. MEDIAWIKI OUTPUT

## Summary

**Brief Commentary on the Supreme Court of Justice Judgment of 12/10/2022** (*Breve comentário ao Acórdão do Supremo Tribunal de Justiça de 12/10/2022*) (Stable ID: **JPW-WORK-00031**) is a doctrinal legal analysis and labor case commentary authored by Inês Arruda and Miguel Riço dos Santos of Pérez-Llorca Abogados. Published in *Revista Jurídica Pérez-Llorca* (May 2024), the study critically analyzes the Judgment of the Supreme Court of Justice of Portugal (\*Supremo Tribunal de Justiça - STJ\*) of October 12, 2022 (Proc. 423/20.9T8BRR.L1.S1). The work addresses the conflict between employer managerial authority (\*poder de direção\*) in schedule setting and the right of workers with children under 12 to obtain flexible working hours (\*horário flexível\*) and designate fixed weekend rest days (Saturday-Sunday) for family reconciliation. The authors argue that the STJ exceeded statutory interpretation limits under Article 56 of the \*Código do Trabalho\*, risking a breach of the constitutional separation of powers.

## Legal Basis

The policy and statutory frameworks evaluated in this publication rely on Portuguese domestic law:

- **Constitution of the Portuguese Republic (CRP)**: Article 59.1.b (work organization and family reconciliation), Article 67.2.h (family protection), Article 68.1 (parental protection), and Article 112.5 (hierarchy of norms and separation of powers).
- **Labor Code (Código do Trabalho - CT)**: Articles 56 and 57 (flexible working hours regime), Article 59 (overtime), Article 60 (night work), Article 200 (concept of work schedule), and Article 212 (schedule drafting).
- **Related Legislation**: Law No. 99/2003, Law No. 35/2004, and Articles 2 and 9 of the Civil Code (\*Código Civil\* - rules of statutory interpretation).

## Background

Article 56 of the Labor Code grants flexible working schedule rights to workers with children under 12. In practice, disputes emerged over whether this right allows employees to self-designate fixed weekly rest days on weekends, overriding company rotating shift systems.

## Objectives

1\. Analyze legal limits governing the \*horário flexível\* regime for workers with family responsibilities. 2. Evaluate the division of authority between employer schedule drafting (\*elaborar o horário\*) and employee subjective rights. 3. Assess the consistency of judicial interpretation with statutory interpretation rules under Article 9 of the Civil Code. 4. Safeguard the principle of legality and separation of powers against judicial activism akin to \*Assentos\*.

## Scope

The study covers five primary operational areas: 1. *Factual Case Review*: A rotating shift worker's request for fixed Saturday-Sunday rest days for childcare purposes. 2. *CITE Procedure*: Employer refusal notices, unfavorable opinions (\*parecer desfavorável\*) by CITE, and judicial validation lawsuits. 3. *Judicial Split*: Lower Court and Lisbon Court of Appeal rulings (in favor of employer) vs. Supreme Court of Justice ruling (in favor of employee). 4. *Interpretation Criticism*: Departure from the literal wording (\*elemento literal\*) of Article 56.3 CT and misapplication of the "most favorable treatment" principle. 5. *Constitutional Doctrine*: Warning against reviving the \*Assentos\* doctrine, previously declared unconstitutional (TC Judgment 810/93).

## Key Provisions

- **Flexible Working Hours Definition (Art. 56.2 CT)**: Schedules allowing employees to choose start and end times within set limits.
- **Employer Authority (Art. 56.3 CT)**: Flexible schedules must be established by the employer, specifying mandatory core hours (\*presença obrigatória\*) and rest intervals.
- **STJ Ruling Position (Proc. 423/20.9T8BRR.L1.S1)**: Employees possess the right to fix weekly rest days, and employers may only refuse if they prove an absence of operational conditions.
- **Principle of Legality**: Judicial interpretation cannot disregard the clear intent of the legislature expressed in statutory text.

## Implementation

Implementation is executed through CITE oversight procedures, refusal validation proceedings in Labor Courts (\*Juízo do Trabalho\*), and judicial enforcement by the Supreme Court of Justice.

## Policy Problems

- Legal uncertainty for employers in managing shift operations and team scheduling.
- Risk of double standards between employer managerial rights and family reconciliation rights.
- Judicial tendency toward corrective interpretation (\*interpretação corretiva\*) exceeding judicial power.

## Reform

- Statutory clarification required from the legislature (\*Assembleia da República\*) regarding the scope of \*horário flexível\*.
- Strict enforcement of judicial interpretation boundaries pursuant to Article 9 of the Civil Code.
