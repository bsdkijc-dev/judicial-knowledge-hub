# JPAIS:PRECEDENT:YURISPRUDENSI 2011 PART3

**Supreme Court Landmark Decisions Compendium (2011 Part 3)** (encompassing Supreme Court Decisions Reg. Nos. 1542 K/Pid.Sus/2008 and 2399 K/Pid.Sus/2010) represents a pivotal compilation of Supreme Court jurisprudence established under the Supreme Court Jurisprudence framework. It consolidates strategic landmark rulings across Special Environmental Criminal Law and Special Anti-Corruption Criminal Law.

## Executive Summary

The Supreme Court established two primary legal principles:

1.  **Prohibition of Foreign Hazardous Waste Dumping & Review of Acquittals (*Vrijspraak*):** "Every person is prohibited from dumping waste originating from outside Indonesia into the Indonesian environmental media (*ex* Article 20 of Law No. 23 of 1997 on Environmental Management)." The Supreme Court possesses the authority to examine and annul pure acquittals (*vrijspraak*) in the interest of law and justice whenever the *Judex Facti* is proven to have erred in applying the law or misinterpreting evidentiary rules. An operational manager (Acting Resident Manager) of a hotel who approves contracts and payment for transporting and dumping hazardous B3 waste (used oil, paint, B3 sludge) from a foreign cruise ship ("Leisure World") into Indonesian terrestrial territory bears criminal liability.
2.  **Proportional Justice in Anti-Corruption Sentencing:** "The Supreme Court in its adjudicative function may apply the law to concrete cases in a manner whose actualization is not entirely aligned with the exact spirit and intent of the legislature, but is harmonized with the amount of state losses arising from the Defendant's actions and the demands of public justice." The application of mandatory minimum special penalties *ex* Article 2 paragraph (1) of the Anti-Corruption Law (minimum 4 years' imprisonment) may be departed from or harmonized by the Cassation Judge when personal gains obtained by the defendant are extremely minor (e.g., an intermediary fee of Rp 2,900,000), resulting in a sentence of 1 (one) year's imprisonment to prevent disproportionate sentencing that injures public a sense of justice.

The Supreme Court granted the Public Prosecutor's cassation petitions in both cases, annulled the lower court rulings (*judex facti*) that improperly acquitted or disproportionately mitigated sentences, and independently adjudicated (*mengadili sendiri*) by imposing prison sentences and fines harmonized with law and justice.

## Legal / Procedural Issues

### 1. Managerial Criminal Liability (*Corporate Operational Responsibility*) for Environmental Crimes (Articles 20, 42, 43, 45 of Law No. 23 of 1997 *jo.* Gov. Reg. No. 85 of 1999)

*Can a daily operational manager / person in charge (Acting Resident Manager) be sentenced for dumping foreign B3 waste into Indonesian environmental media, even if they are not the primary corporate Board of Directors?*

**Supreme Court Rule: Prosecutable / Fully Responsible.** A daily operational manager/controller of a company possessing authority to approve cooperation contracts, sign invoice payment claims, and stop B3 waste collection who allows such activities to continue is legally proven to commit Environmental Pollution *ex* Article 20 *jo.* Articles 42, 43, and 45 of Law No. 23 of 1997.

### 2. Supreme Court Authority to Review Pure Acquittals (*Vrijspraak*) at Cassation Level (Article 244 Criminal Procedure Code *jo.* Supreme Court Jurisprudence)

*Does the Supreme Court possess authority to examine and annul an acquittal (*vrijspraak*) rendered by a District Court?*

**Supreme Court Rule: Authorized to Examine & Annul.** Where a defendant's acquittal by the *Judex Facti* relies on erroneous interpretation of delict elements or misapplication of evidentiary law (impure acquittal / *onzuivere vrijspraak*), the Supreme Court in the interest of law and social justice holds the authority to annul such acquittal and independently adjudicate the criminal case.

### 3. Judicial Discretion to Depart from Mandatory Minimum Special Penalties for Substantive Justice (Disproportionality Mitigation *ex* Article 2 Anti-Corruption Law)

Where a defendant's conduct fulfills the formal delict elements of Article 2 paragraph (1) of the Anti-Corruption Law, but state losses or personal gains obtained by the perpetrator are extremely minor (Rp 2,900,000), the Cassation Judge may harmonize penalties (*strafmaat harmonisation*) by imposing a sentence below the 4-year statutory minimum (i.e., 1 year's imprisonment) so that law enforcement does not injure public a sense of justice.

## Question of Law vs Question of Fact

### Question of Law (Judex Juris / Supreme Court)

- *Environmental Case:* Did the *Judex Facti* Batam District Court (Decision No. 846/PID.B/2007/PN.BTM) err in applying evidentiary law by acquitting the Defendant on the pretext that the Defendant was not a Director of PT Horizon Bandiar Bahru?
    
  **Supreme Court Answer:** Yes. Batam District Court misapplied the law. An Acting Resident Manager controlling operational activities bears full responsibility for B3 waste. The Supreme Court granted the Prosecutor's cassation, annulled the acquittal, and sentenced the Defendant to 3 years' imprisonment and a Rp 30 million fine.
- *Anti-Corruption Case:* Did West Kalimantan High Court in Pontianak (Decision No. 195/Pid/2010/PT.PTK) exceed its authority and err in imposing a lenient 1-year sentence without adequate legal reasoning?
    
  **Supreme Court Answer:** Yes. Pontianak High Court provided insufficient reasoning (*onvoldoende gemotiveerd*). However, in the interest of proportional justice regarding the Rp 2.9 million loss, the Supreme Court independently adjudicated by sentencing the Defendant to 1 year's imprisonment and a Rp 200 million fine.

### Question of Fact (Judex Facti / Batam District Court, Singkawang District Court, Pontianak High Court)

Verification of facts regarding waste transport from the "Leisure World" cruise ship in OPL Malacca Strait by wooden pompong boats belonging to Andi Abdullah alias Te Lai to the Nongsa Batam temporary dumping site; verification of National Police Bareskrim Labfor Report No. LAB.623/KTF/2007 dated March 12, 2007, concerning B3 contents; verification of temporary disposal site permits; and verification of the Singkawang DPRD uniform procurement project for FY 2007 and fee withdrawals.

## Jurisprudence and Related Knowledge

### Statutory Regulations

- **Law No. 23 of 1997 on Environmental Management:** Articles 20, 42, 43, 45 (B3 Waste & Waste Imports).
- **Government Regulation No. 85 of 1999 on Hazardous and Toxic Waste Management (B3):** Attachments I & II (TCPL Quality Standards List).
- **Law No. 31 of 1999 *jo.* Law No. 20 of 2001 on Anti-Corruption:** Articles 2 paragraph (1), 18 (Corruption Penalties & Replacement Money).
- **Criminal Procedure Code (KUHAP):** Articles 244 (Cassation of Acquittals) & 253 (Cassation Grounds).

### Established Supreme Court Jurisprudence

- **Supreme Court Decisions No. 275 K/Pid/1983, No. 892 K/Pid/1983, and No. 532 K/Pid/1984:** Landmark precedents confirming Supreme Court authority to review cassation appeals against pure acquittals (*vrijspraak*) whenever District Courts misinterpret delict elements.
- **Substantive Justice Doctrine in Sentencing (Substantive Justice vs Strict Legality):** Criminal law enforcement must not be executed in a rigid-formalistic manner (mechanical jurisprudence) if mandatory minimum penalties injure public justice due to extremely minor impact scales.

## Uncertainties and Gaps

**Protecting Indonesia's Environmental Sovereignty from Foreign Waste & Proportional Corruption Justice:** Landmark rulings in this compendium establish two absolute legal principles in Indonesia: (1) Indonesia shall not serve as a dumping ground for toxic B3 waste from foreign vessels in international waters; corporate managers facilitating it face imprisonment and fines; and (2) Anti-corruption enforcement must prioritize proportional justice to avoid rigid formalism when adjudicating small-scale perpetrators.
