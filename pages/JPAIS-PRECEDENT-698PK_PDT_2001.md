## Overview

Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia Review Decision No. 698 PK/Pdt/2001, dated February 27, 2003, established a foundational rule in Marine Insurance regarding the duty of disclosing material facts (\*utmost good faith\* / \*non-disclosure\*) pursuant to Article 251 of the Commercial Code (\*KUHD\*):

Legally, the insured has a legal obligation to disclose the true condition (\*the whole truth\*) of the vessel to be insured. If the insured intentionally conceals the true condition or provides incorrect disclosures during the conclusion of the insurance policy (such as hiding the fact that M.V. Lucky Fortune was formerly M.V. Bethesda 2, which had caught fire on December 16, 1990, and lacked valid classification from Bureau Veritas), the insurance contract is rendered void (\*batal demi hukum\* ex Article 251 KUHD), thereby resulting in the total rejection of the claim for Total Loss / Constructive Total Loss.

## Executive Summary

The central principle established in this decision is that failure to disclose vital historical and safety data regarding a vessel prior to policy issuance voids the marine insurance contract under statutory commercial law.

Key elements established in the adjudication:

- Insured parties are bound by an absolute duty of disclosure (\*utmost good faith\*) regarding vessel history and classification.
- Concealment of past structural damage or lack of valid classification renders insurance policies void \*ab initio\* under Article 251 KUHD.
- Erroneous cassation judgments ignoring statutory disclosure duties are subject to reversal upon judicial review (\*peninjauan kembali\*).

## Case Background

The Original Plaintiffs / Respondents for Review (1. **Intercontinental Maritime Pte Ltd**, 2. **PT Layar Sentosa Shipping Corporation**) sued the Original Defendant / Petitioner for Review, **PT Wataka General Insurance**, at the South Jakarta District Court over the rejection of an insurance claim for M.V. Lucky Fortune Policy No. 01.81.92.00004 (Hull & Machinery US\$ 1,000,000.— & Removal of Wreck US\$ 250,000.—), which capsized and sank due to a typhoon at Tanjung Priok Port on January 28, 1993. The Defendant rejected the claim because the Plaintiffs failed to disclose material facts (\*non-disclosure of material facts\*) that M.V. Lucky Fortune was formerly M.V. Bethesda 2, which had suffered severe fire damage on December 16, 1990, and lacked Bureau Veritas classification.

The South Jakarta District Court (Decision No. 88/Pdt.G/1995/PN.Jkt.Sel dated September 1, 1995) granted the Plaintiff's lawsuit in part. The Jakarta High Court (Decision No. 240/Pdt/1996/PT.DKI dated August 14, 1996) annulled the district court decision and dismissed the lawsuit entirely. However, the Supreme Court Cassation Panel (Decision No. 916 K/Pdt/1997 dated October 14, 1999) annulled the Jakarta High Court decision and affirmed the South Jakarta District Court judgment. PT Wataka General Insurance subsequently filed a Review petition (\*Peninjauan Kembali\*) with the Supreme Court.

## Legal and Procedural Issues

### Duty of Disclosing Material Facts (Utmost Good Faith / Non-Disclosure) in Commercial Insurance (Article 251 KUHD)

The primary issue was whether a marine insurance contract is valid when an insured covers a vessel without disclosing material facts establishing that the vessel suffered a prior constructive total loss by fire and lost its official classification status from a rating agency (Bureau Veritas).

The Supreme Court held that the insurance contract is void (\*nietig\* / void \*ab initio\*) pursuant to Article 251 of the Commercial Code (\*KUHD\*). The insured is bound by an absolute duty to disclose the whole truth (\*the whole truth\*). Non-disclosure of material facts during policy conclusion violates the principle of utmost good faith, entitling the insurer to reject claims and rendering the contract void.

### Review Panel Authority to Annul Cassation Rulings Due to Manifest Judicial Error (Article 67 letter f Law No. 14 of 1985)

The issue evaluated how the Supreme Court responds on Review when a prior Cassation Panel misinterprets evidence T.3 (survey/classification reports) and ignores the concealment of vessel classification status.

The Supreme Court on Review possesses authority to annul Cassation Decision No. 916 K/Pdt/1997 dated October 14, 1999, and adjudicate afresh by rejecting the Plaintiffs' lawsuit entirely due to manifest judicial errors committed in applying Article 251 KUHD.

## Question of Law and Question of Fact

### Question of Law (Cassation / Judicial Review)

**Question:**

Did the Supreme Court Cassation Panel in Decision No. 916 K/Pdt/1997 misapply the law (\*foutieve kepassing van het recht\*) by ignoring Article 251 of the Commercial Code, which mandates contract nullification for insurance policies concluded while intentionally concealing material facts regarding vessel condition and classification?

**Supreme Court Ruling:**

Yes, the Cassation Panel committed a manifest error. The Supreme Court granted PT Wataka General Insurance's Review petition, annulled Cassation Decision No. 916 K/Pdt/1997, and adjudicated afresh by rejecting the Plaintiffs' lawsuit entirely.

### Question of Fact (Judex Facti / South Jakarta District Court, Jakarta High Court, & Review Panel)

Examination of facts concerning Marine Insurance Policy No. 01.81.92.00004 dated January 31, 1992, over M.V. Lucky Fortune (Hull & Machinery US\$ 1,000,000.— & Removal of Wreck US\$ 250,000.—); the capsizing and sinking of the vessel due to a typhoon at Tanjung Priok Port on January 28, 1993; verification of evidence T.3 proving M.V. Lucky Fortune was formerly M.V. Bethesda 2, which burned on December 16, 1990; Bureau Veritas suspension/revocation reports dated February 24–25, 1992; and adjuster reports by Zoreale, PT Suisindo, and PT Carsurin.

## Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

- **Ratio Decidendi:**

1.  Review grounds were justified due to manifest judicial error at the cassation level.
2.  Evidence T.3 established that M.V. Lucky Fortune was formerly M.V. Bethesda 2, which burned on December 16, 1990, and lacked valid Bureau Veritas classification upon insurance coverage.
3.  Under Article 251 KUHD, the insured has a strict duty to disclose the true condition of the vessel (\*the whole truth\*). Because the Plaintiffs intentionally concealed true conditions during policy conclusion, the insurance contract is void (\*batal demi hukum\*).
4.  The Supreme Court granted the Review petition, annulled Cassation Decision No. 916 K/Pdt/1997, and adjudicated afresh by rejecting the Plaintiffs' lawsuit entirely.

## Ruling

The Supreme Court ruled: Granting the Review petition from Petitioner for Review (**P.T. WATAKA GENERAL INSURANCE**); Annulling the Supreme Court decision dated October 14, 1999, No. 916 K/Pdt/1997; and ADJUDICATING AFRESH: Under Provision: Rejecting the Plaintiffs' provisional claims; Under the Merits: Rejecting the Plaintiffs' lawsuit entirely; and ordering the Respondent for Review to pay Review court costs amounting to Rp 500,000.—.

## Evidence Status and Gaps

### Established by Evidence

- M.V. Lucky Fortune insured on January 31, 1992, was actually former M.V. Bethesda 2, which suffered total destruction by fire (\*Constructive Total Loss\*) on December 16, 1990.
- At policy conclusion on January 31, 1992, the vessel possessed no valid classification status from Bureau Veritas.
- Plaintiffs / Respondents for Review intentionally concealed vessel conditions and classification status from Defendant / Petitioner for Review during policy issuance.

### Not Established / Void Contract

- Plaintiffs were unproven entitled to insurance compensation claims of US\$ 1,000,000.— (Hull & Machinery) and US\$ 250,000.— (Removal of Wreck) because the insurance contract was void \*ab initio\*.

### Uncertainty and Gaps

- Application of Utmost Good Faith in Marine Insurance: This landmark Review ruling establishes strict legal certainty for the national insurance industry: the duty of disclosure rests entirely on prospective insureds. Concealment of fatal vessel accident histories is intolerable even after policy issuance.

## GEO Q&A

**Q: Must an insurer pay a claim if the insured intentionally conceals severe prior damage and lack of vessel classification when concluding a marine insurance policy?**

**A:** No, the insurer is not obligated. Based on Supreme Court Review Decision No. 698 PK/Pdt/2001, pursuant to Article 251 of the Commercial Code (\*KUHD\*), the insured has a legal duty to disclose the true condition of the vessel (\*the whole truth\*). If the insured intentionally conceals material facts (such as past fire history and absence of valid classification certificates) during policy conclusion, the insurance contract is void (\*void ab initio\*), and the insurance company is entitled to reject all compensation claims.
