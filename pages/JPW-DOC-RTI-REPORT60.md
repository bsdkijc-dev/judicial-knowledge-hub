## Policy Analysis

- **Background & Trends (Chapter 2)**:
  - Reported cases of bodily injury and assault/intimidation spiked sharply in 2000. Injury cases peaked in 2003, subsequently declining and stabilizing at ~20,000 cases annually, while assault cases remained elevated above 30,000 cases per year since 2006.
  - Homicide and robbery cases steadily declined following their 2003 peak.
  - Across all violent crime categories, the proportion of elderly offenders (aged 65 and older) continuously increased.
  - Re-imprisonment rates within 5 years post-release reached ~30% for injury cases and ~40% for assault cases.
- **Specialized Inmate Survey (Chapter 3.1)**:
  - Conducted June 2016 questionnaire survey of 176 violent inmates (159 males, 17 females) across Japanese correctional facilities.
  - Compared to theft and drug offenders, violent offenders were on average younger, with higher proportions of first-time offenders, organized crime affiliations, and histories of juvenile protective measures.
  - Male violent offenders exhibited lower agreeableness, higher aggressiveness, and elevated risk of Alcohol Use Disorder (AUD).
  - Correspondence and cluster analyses identified 4 clusters along 2 primary axes: "non-domestic/contingent violence vs. domestic/frequent violence" and "behavioral problems in early life/adverse childhood experiences."
  - Decision tree analysis categorized offenders into 6 distinct support-need groups (e.g., employment support for organized crime exit, correctional guidance for alcoholism/substance abuse).
- **Specialized Probationer Survey (Chapter 3.2)**:
  - Studied 439 probationers (400 males, 39 females) with full suspension of execution of sentence whose supervision ended in 2013.
  - Young offenders (≤29 years) accounted for ~40% of the sample. Primary offense types were injury for males and homicide for females.
  - Probation revocation/redisposition rate reached 28.9% (127 individuals), strongly correlating with substance abuse histories, alcohol issues, and domestic violence (DV) background.
- **Treatment Status & Policy Recommendations (Chapters 4 & 5)**:
  - Evaluated implementation of Violence Prevention Programs (VPPs) across penal institutions and probation offices.
  - Three Primary Policy Recommendations:
    - Expansion of assessment content and correctional guidance within VPPs to flexibly address diverse offender needs.
    - Strengthening organic inter-agency coordination and information sharing for employment support, AUD treatment, and drug dependency interventions.
    - Promotion of further research on managing domestic violence offenders and other specialized violent crime categories.
- **Interpretations & Structural Inferences**:
  - Japanese violent offenders are not a homogeneous group; effective rehabilitation depends on precise mapping of psychological traits, childhood trauma, organized crime links, and substance/alcohol use disorders.
  - High probation revocation rates (28.9%) correlating with alcohol problems and DV history confirm that community supervision lacking addiction management and domestic interaction controls risks triggering post-release failure.
