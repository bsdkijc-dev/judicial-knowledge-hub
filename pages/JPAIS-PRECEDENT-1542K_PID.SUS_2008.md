# JPAIS:PRECEDENT:1542K PID.SUS 2008

**Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia Decision No. 1542 K/Pid.Sus/2008** (28 January 2009) is a landmark [Indonesian](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Indonesia) [environmental](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/environmental_law) and [criminal procedure law](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/criminal_procedure) ruling of the [Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Indonesia) (*Mahkamah Agung RI*). The decision established three important legal principles: (1) the admissibility of cassation (*kasasi*) against a "non-pure acquittal" (*niet-zuivere vrijspraak*) under Article 244 of the Indonesian Code of Criminal Procedure (KUHAP); (2) the criminal liability of an operational manager (an "Acting Resident Manager") for corporate environmental pollution; and (3) an absolute prohibition on the disposal of foreign-origin hazardous and toxic waste (*limbah B3*) into Indonesia's environment.

The Court granted the cassation petition filed by the Public Prosecutor of the Batam District Attorney's Office, overturned the acquittal issued by the Batam District Court, and, ruling on the merits itself, convicted the defendant, **\[Defendant\]**, of the crime of "environmental pollution," sentencing him to three years' imprisonment and a fine of Rp 30,000,000 (thirty million rupiah), with six months' imprisonment in lieu of payment.

## Background

The defendant, \[Defendant\] (\[age removed\], born \[details removed\]), served as Acting Resident Manager and head of the Hotel Batam View Beach Resort, operated by PT. Horizon Bandiar Bahru. Beginning in April 2003, the hotel had a waste-disposal contract with \[Contractor\] (of CV. Andi Bangun Wahana) to collect waste originating from the gambling/cruise vessel *Leisure World*, which operated in international waters in the Strait of Malacca, and transport it by wooden *pompong* boat (12 tons, Yanmark 16 HP engine) to a waste collection site at Kampung Trai Nongsa, Batam, without an official permit.

The defendant, who took over operational leadership of the hotel from predecessors Svein Moldskred and Gerald Lim, approved continuation of the contract and approved an increase in the transport fee from SGD 100 to SGD 110 per trip in January 2006. Monthly payments of approximately USD 39,710 were made through the hotel's cashier, I Nyoman Suta Aryana.

On 27 January 2007, the Riau Islands Regional Police (Polda Kepri) raided the site. Forensic laboratory analysis by the Indonesian National Police Criminal Investigation Agency (Puslabfor Bareskrim Polri), recorded in Laboratory Report No. LAB.623/KTF/2007 (12 March 2007), found that solid waste samples (oil sludge and paint residue) contained zinc (Zn) at 58.50 mg/l and lead (Pb) at 9.42 mg/l, both exceeding the Toxicity Characteristic Leaching Procedure (TCLP) quality standards under Government Regulation No. 85 of 1999.

The defendant was charged, cumulatively/alternatively, under Article 41(1), Article 43(1), and Article 42(1) in conjunction with Article 45 and Article 20(2) of Law No. 23 of 1997 on Environmental Management, in conjunction with Government Regulation No. 85 of 1999, in conjunction with Article 55(1)(1) and Article 64(1) of the Indonesian Penal Code (KUHP).

## Trial court decision

The Batam District Court, in Decision No. 846/PID.B/2007/PN.BTM (4 June 2008), acquitted the defendant of all charges (*vrijspraak*), reasoning that he was not a member of the board of directors (*Direksi*) of PT. Horizon Bandiar Bahru and therefore could not be held criminally liable — an *error in persona* defense based on an interpretation of Article 103 of Law No. 40 of 2007 on Limited Liability Companies.

The Public Prosecutor of the Batam District Attorney's Office filed a petition for cassation with the Supreme Court.

## Issues

### Admissibility of cassation against an acquittal

Whether the Public Prosecutor could file a cassation petition against a district court decision acquitting the defendant of all charges, and whether the Supreme Court had authority to overturn that acquittal.

### Criminal liability of an operational manager

Whether an Acting Resident Manager/hotel manager who is not a member of the board of directors of the corporation can be held criminally liable for environmental pollution arising from the corporation's hazardous-waste transport contract.

### Legal classification of the waste disposal

Whether the unlicensed transport of waste from a foreign-flagged vessel in international waters to a site on Indonesian soil constitutes unlawful "environmental pollution" under Indonesian law.

## Holding

### Admissibility of cassation against a "non-pure" acquittal

The Supreme Court held that it has authority to review, examine, and overturn a district court's acquittal in the interest of law and justice where the acquittal was based on a mistaken interpretation of law (a "non-pure acquittal," or *niet-zuivere vrijspraak*). The Court found that the Batam District Court had erred in treating the case as one of mistaken identity of the responsible party (*error in persona*) on the ground that the defendant was not a director of PT. Horizon Bandiar Bahru, when in fact the defendant, as Acting Resident Manager and head of the Hotel Batam View, held full operational control and actively approved the extension and fee increase of the hazardous-waste transport arrangement.

This holding relied on established Supreme Court jurisprudence, including Decisions No. 275 K/Pid/1983, No. 892 K/Pid/1983, No. 532 K/Pid/1984, and No. 449 K/Pid/1985, as well as Supreme Court Letter No. MA/Pemb/2651/83 and Ministry of Justice Decree No. M.14-PW.07.03 of 1983, on the admissibility of prosecutorial cassation against non-pure acquittals under Article 244 of the KUHAP.

### Criminal liability of the operational manager

The Court held that a company's operational leader or manager who possesses full authority over operational activities, approves payments, and continues an unlicensed waste-transport contract from a foreign vessel to Indonesian soil is criminally liable, as the party responsible for the activity, for the resulting environmental pollution, under Article 20 in conjunction with Articles 41, 42, and 43, in conjunction with Article 45, of Law No. 23 of 1997 on Environmental Management.

The Court reasoned that criminal liability for corporate environmental offenses cannot be confined to a company's formally registered board of directors; any officer or manager who holds factual operational control over business activities and approves conduct that pollutes the environment may be held individually criminally liable as the party responsible for the activity.

### Prohibition on disposal of foreign-origin waste

The Court held that Article 20 of Law No. 23 of 1997 absolutely prohibits any person or business entity from bringing in and disposing of waste — including hazardous and toxic waste such as oil sludge, used oil, and paint residue containing zinc (Zn) and lead (Pb) in excess of TCLP quality standards — originating from outside Indonesian territory (here, from the vessel *Leisure World* in the Strait of Malacca) into Indonesia's environment (here, the waste site at Kampung Trai Nongsa, Batam).

## Disposition

The Supreme Court:

1.  Granted the cassation petition of the Public Prosecutor of the Batam District Attorney's Office;
2.  Overturned Batam District Court Decision No. 846/PID.B/2007/PN.BTM of 4 June 2008;
3.  Ruling on the merits itself (*mengadili sendiri*):
    - Declared the defendant, \[Defendant\], legally and convincingly guilty of the crime of "Environmental Pollution";
    - Sentenced the defendant to three years' imprisonment and a fine of Rp 30,000,000 (thirty million rupiah), with six months' imprisonment in lieu of payment of the fine;
    - Ordered forfeiture and destruction of evidentiary items (used work equipment, hardened paint cans, used oil jerry cans, etc.) and forfeiture to the State of one 12-ton wooden *pompong* boat (Yanmark 16 HP engine); ordered return of receipts, invoices, and permit/contract documents to their rightful owners;
    - Ordered the defendant to bear the costs of proceedings at all levels (cassation costs: Rp 2,500).

## Significance

The decision is frequently cited in Indonesian environmental and corporate criminal law for two propositions: first, that a prosecutor may seek cassation against an acquittal that rests on an erroneous legal interpretation (a "non-pure acquittal"), and second, that criminal liability for corporate environmental pollution extends beyond a company's formal board of directors to any manager or officer with factual operational control over the polluting activity.
