# JPAIS:PRECEDENT:3180K PDT 1985

**Supreme Court Decision Reg. No. 3180/Pdt/1985** (deliberated on December 24, 1986 / pronounced on January 28, 1987) is a fundamental family and divorce law jurisprudence precedent establishing vital rules concerning the judicial interpretation of continuous disputes and irreconcilable differences (*onheelbare tweespalt*) pursuant to Article 19 letter f of Government Regulation No. 9 of 1975 *jo.* Article 1 of Law No. 1 of 1974.

## Executive Summary

The Supreme Court established a primary legal principle:

- **Focus on the Factual Reality of Continuous Disputes Rather Than Their Causes:** "The concept of continuous disputes and irreconcilable differences (*onheelbare tweespalt*) is not focused on proving the underlying causes of the dispute, but rather on observing the factual reality that continuous disputes are indeed proven to exist to the extent that reconciliation is no longer possible."

Where the Palu High Court ruled that a husband's frequent drunkenness and returning home late "did not yet fall within the definition of continuous disputes and irreconcilable differences," the Supreme Court held that such reasoning was erroneous and misapplied the law. Under the *broken marriage* doctrine, once witness testimony proves that a couple is in constant discord, that the husband has abandoned the shared domicile, and that all hope of reconciliation is lost, the statutory requirements for divorce under *onheelbare tweespalt* are fully satisfied.

The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition of the Petitioner for Cassation (\[Party A\]), annulled Palu High Court Decision No. 48/Pdt/1984/PT. Palu dated October 17, 1984 (which had overturned Palu District Court Decision No. 7/1984/Pdt.G dated May 19, 1984), and independently adjudicated (*mengadili sendiri*) by partially granting the Plaintiff's lawsuit, declaring the marriage dissolved due to divorce, granting custody of the four children to the Plaintiff, and ordering both parties to share living and educational expenses according to their respective financial capabilities.

## Legal / Procedural Issues

### 1. Judicial Interpretation of Continuous Disputes and Irreconcilable Differences (*Onheelbare Tweespalt* *ex* Article 19 letter f of Government Regulation No. 9 of 1975 *jo.* Article 1 of Law No. 1 of 1974)

*In a divorce lawsuit based on Article 19 letter f of Government Regulation No. 9 of 1975, must the court examine and prove the degree or severity of the underlying causes of a dispute (such as a husband's habit of drunkenness), or is it sufficient to prove the factual reality that continuous disputes have occurred with no hope of reconciliation?*

**Supreme Court Rule: Focus on the Factual Reality of Disputes.** The concept of continuous disputes and irreconcilable differences (*onheelbare tweespalt*) does not focus on proving the underlying causes of conflict, but rather on observing the factual reality that continuous disputes are proven to exist such that reconciliation is impossible. Judicial reasoning that questions whether an underlying cause meets a threshold of severity is erroneous.

### 2. Evaluation of Witness Testimony in Divorce Disputes (Article 1908 BW / Articles 171–172 HIR / Article 22 paragraph 2 of Government Regulation No. 9 of 1975)

The testimonies of three witnesses under oath confirming that the parties' domestic life has broken down, frequent quarrels have occurred, and the Defendant has abandoned the shared home satisfy the minimum evidentiary threshold required to grant a divorce.

### 3. Prohibition Against Raising New Legal Grounds (*Novum*) During Cassation Proceedings

Cassation objections introducing new facts or legal events never raised during first-instance or appellate examinations are classified as an inadmissible *novum* before the Supreme Court.

## Question of Law vs Question of Fact

### Question of Law (*Judex Juris* / Supreme Court)

Did the *Judex Facti* Palu High Court (Decision No. 48/Pdt/1984/PT. Palu) err in applying the law (*foutieve toepassing van het recht*) by dismissing the divorce lawsuit on the grounds that "frequent quarrels caused by the Defendant frequently returning home drunk do not fall within the definition of *onheelbare tweespalt*"?

**Supreme Court Legal Answer:** Yes. Palu High Court misapplied the law. The Supreme Court granted the Petitioner's cassation petition, annulled Palu High Court's decision, and independently adjudicated by declaring the marriage between the Plaintiff and Defendant dissolved due to divorce.

### Question of Fact (*Judex Facti* / Palu District Court & Palu High Court)

Verification of marriage in Manado on March 4, 1968; birth of four children; facts regarding the Defendant's frequent drunkenness and late returns; the Defendant's sale of shared residential property without the Plaintiff's consent; and testimonies of witnesses \[Party C\], Nyoman Putu, and Petrus Wongkar.

## Jurisprudence and Related Knowledge

### Statutory Regulations

- **Law No. 1 of 1974 on Marriage:** Article 1 (Purpose of Marriage) & Article 39 paragraph (2) (Grounds for Divorce).
- **Government Regulation No. 9 of 1975 on the Implementation of Law No. 1 of 1974:** Article 19 letter f (Grounds for Divorce: Continuous Disputes / *Onheelbare Tweespalt*) & Article 22 paragraph (2) (Examination of Family / Close Relative Witnesses).
- **Law No. 14 of 1970 *jo.* Law No. 1 of 1950 *jo.* Law No. 14 of 1985 on the Supreme Court:** Cassation and Independent Adjudication Authority.

### Established Supreme Court Jurisprudence

- **Doctrine of Broken Marriage:** Where a marital relationship has factually broken down, affection has vanished, physical separation has occurred, and reconciliation efforts fail, the marital bond must not be forcibly maintained as it contradicts the essence and purpose of marriage under Article 1 of Law No. 1 of 1974.
- **Established Precedent on *Onheelbare Tweespalt*:** Supreme Court Decision Reg. No. 3180/Pdt/1985 serves as a landmark decision utilized as a primary authority by general civil courts and religious courts across Indonesia in assessing the validity of continuous-dispute divorce lawsuits.

### Established by Evidence

- The Plaintiff and Defendant are proven to be lawful husband and wife married in Manado on March 4, 1968, and blessed with 4 children.
- Continuous disputes and irreconcilable differences (*onheelbare tweespalt*) causing domestic breakdown and irreconcilability are **PROVEN AS A FACTUAL REALITY**.
- The Defendant is proven to have abandoned the shared marital home and only occasionally visits the children.
- Palu High Court Decision No. 48/Pdt/1984/PT. Palu is **NULL AND VOID**.
- The marriage between the Plaintiff and Defendant is **OFFICIALLY DISSOLVED DUE TO DIVORCE**.
- Custody, care, and guidance of the four minor children remain with the Plaintiff.
