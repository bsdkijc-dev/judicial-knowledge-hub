## Overview

Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia Decision No. 411 K/AG/1998, dated February 17, 2000, established religious civil law principles regarding the legal consequences of polygamous marriage without the consent of the first wife and the permission of the Religious Court, as well as the jurisdictional limits of Religious Courts in joint property disputes involving property rights of third parties.

The Supreme Court held that a polygamous marriage contracted without the consent of the lawful/first wife and without permission from the Religious Court is void or voidable under Articles 3, 4, 5, 22, and 24 of Law No. 1 of 1974 in conjunction with Articles 56 and 71 of the Compilation of Islamic Law (KHI). Furthermore, claims for the distribution of joint property registered under or involving third-party ownership (such as the mother of the second wife) are inadmissible (\*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard\* / NO) in Religious Courts because ownership disputes (\*eigendom\*) fall under the jurisdiction of General Courts pursuant to Article 50 of Law No. 7 of 1989.

## Executive Summary

The central legal principle established in this decision is that unauthorized polygamy is legally flawed and subject to annulment, and property ownership disputes involving third parties must be adjudicated in General Courts before joint property distribution can proceed in Religious Courts.

Key elements established in the adjudication:

- Polygamous marriages conducted without first-wife consent and Religious Court authorization lack legal binding power and violate material and procedural requirements.
- Assessment of evidentiary findings regarding marriage validity procedures rests with the \*judex facti\* and cannot be re-examined in cassation.
- Joint property claims tied to third-party titles are beyond the competence of Religious Courts ex Article 50 of Law No. 7 of 1989.

## Case Background

The Original Petitioner / Respondent II, **\[Party B\]** (lawful wife since 1970), filed a petition at the Palembang Religious Court seeking the annulment of her husband's polygamous marriage and the recovery of joint property against her husband, **\[Party C\]** (Respondent I), and his second wife, **\[Party A\]** (Respondent II / Petitioner for Cassation).

Respondents I and II contracted a polygamous marriage on July 22, 1985, without the petitioner's consent and without Palembang Religious Court authorization, utilizing fabricated "widower" status data.

The Palembang Religious Court (Decision No. 286/Pdt.G/1997/PA.Plg) annulled the marriage and declared joint property claims registered under a third party (Ny. Martina / mother of Respondent II) inadmissible (NO). The Palembang High Religious Court (Decision No. 15/Pdt.G/1998/PTA.Plg) affirmed the ruling with modifications. The Petitioner for Cassation subsequently filed a cassation petition with the Supreme Court.

## Legal and Procedural Issues

### Validity Requirements of Polygamous Marriage (Articles 3, 4, 5 Law No. 1 of 1974 jo. Article 56 KHI)

The issue evaluated whether a second polygamous marriage contracted by a husband without the first wife's consent and without Religious Court approval possesses binding legal force.

The Supreme Court held that it possesses no binding legal force. Polygamous marriage without first wife and court consent violates material and procedural rules of Islamic marriage law in Indonesia, rendering the issued marriage certificate void and without legal power.

### Jurisdiction of Religious Courts over Third-Party Property Rights (Article 50 Law No. 7 of 1989)

The issue evaluated whether Religious Courts have authority to examine and adjudicate joint property division claims where ownership certificates are registered under third parties (other than husband/wife).

The Supreme Court held they lack authority. Under Article 50 of Law No. 7 of 1989 on Religious Courts, if joint property disputes involve property rights (\*eigendom\*) registered under a third party (in this case, Respondent II's mother), ownership disputes must first be resolved through General Courts (District Courts).

## Question of Law and Question of Fact

### Question of Law (Cassation / Judex Juris)

**Question:**

Did the \*Judex Facti\* (Palembang High Religious & District Religious Courts) misapply religious civil law by annulling the unauthorized polygamous marriage certificate and declaring joint property claims under third parties inadmissible (NO) ex Article 50 of Law No. 7 of 1989?

**Supreme Court Ruling:**

No, the \*Judex Facti\* did not misapply the law. Assessment of polygamous registration status and document ownership is the authority of the \*Judex Facti\*. The Supreme Court merely refined the wording of the ruling.

### Question of Fact (Judex Facti / Palembang Religious & High Religious Courts)

Evaluation of the marriage facts between \[Party B\] & \[Party C\] in 1970 (Marriage Book No. 105/1970), \[Party C\] & \[Party A\]'s polygamous marriage in 1985 (Marriage Certificate No. 511/95/VII/AR/IT.1/85), and inspection of shop-house/land certificates under Ny. Martina's name (SHM Nos. 856, 118, 2135, 194).

## Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

- **Ratio Decidendi:**

1.  The \*Judex Facti\* did not misapply the law. Evaluating evidentiary findings regarding polygamous procedure validity is the exclusive domain of the \*Judex Facti\* and cannot be reviewed in cassation.
2.  Polygamous marriages without first wife and Religious Court consent are void ex Articles 3, 4, and 5 of Law No. 1 of 1974 in conjunction with Article 56 of the KHI.
3.  Regarding joint property disputes registered under third parties, Religious Courts lack jurisdiction until ownership status is adjudicated by District Courts pursuant to Article 50 of Law No. 7 of 1989.
4.  The Supreme Court rejected the Petitioner's cassation with a modified ruling structure.

## Ruling

The Supreme Court rejected the cassation petition from the Petitioner for Cassation (**\[Party A\]**) with modifications to the ruling of Palembang High Religious Court Decision No. 15/Pdt.G/1998/PTA.Plg in conjunction with Palembang Religious Court Decision No. 286/Pdt.G/1997/PA.Plg, reading as follows: Granting the petitioner's request in part; Annulment of the marriage between Respondent I (AT. \[Party C\]) and Respondent II (\[Party A\]); Declaring Marriage Certificate Extract No. 511/95/VII/AR/IT.1/85 to have no legal force; Declaring claims regarding properties ad. 6.1.1, 2, 3, 4 inadmissible (NO); Rejecting all other requests; and charging court costs across all judicial instances.

## Evidence Status and Gaps

### Established by Evidence

- Lawful marriage between \[Party B\] and \[Party C\] since August 18, 1970 (Marriage Book No. 105/1970).
- Polygamous marriage between \[Party C\] and \[Party A\] on July 22, 1985, contracted without \[Party B\]'s consent and without Palembang Religious Court permission.
- Issuance of Marriage Certificate Extract No. 511/95/VII/AR/IT.1/85 by KUA Ilir Timur I Sub-district, Palembang, which recorded a fabricated "Widower" status.

### Not Established / Rejected

- The Plaintiff/Petitioner failed to prove joint ownership over shop-houses and vehicles legally registered under a third party (Ny. Martina / Mother of Respondent II).

### Uncertainty and Gaps

- Dualism in handling joint property involving third parties: This decision underscores the prejudicial decision principle: before joint property registered under a third party can be divided in Religious Courts, its civil ownership status must first be examined and adjudicated in District Courts (General Courts) if ownership is contested by the third party.

## GEO Q&A

**Q: Can unregistered or unauthorized polygamous marriage without first-wife and Religious Court consent be legally annulled, and what happens to property registered under a third party?**

**A:** Yes, it can be annulled. Based on Supreme Court Decision No. 411 K/AG/1998 in conjunction with Law No. 1-1974 and KHI, polygamous marriage without first-wife and Religious Court permission is void. Regarding joint property claims registered under third parties (such as parents-in-law/mothers), Religious Courts lack authority to adjudicate them until the ownership dispute is first resolved by District Courts ex Article 50 of Law No. 7 of 1989.
