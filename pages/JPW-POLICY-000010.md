# JPW-POLICY-000010

The **Malaysian Dispute Resolution and Judicial System Framework** (Stable ID: `JPW-POLICY-000010`) is a national judicial policy and research framework evaluating the common law judicial hierarchy, trial backlogs, case-management rules, specialized dispute settlement tribunals, and statutory environmental enforcement in Malaysia. Documented under IDE Asian Law Series No. 17 by Associate Professor Sharifah Suhana Syed Ahmad and Associate Professor Mary George of the Faculty of Law, University of Malaya, the policy analyzes court backlogs, High Court Order 34 case management, the Tribunal for Consumer Claims, the Industrial Court, SOCSO social security schemes, and environmental litigation under the Environmental Quality Act 1974. Note: Named entities *Cecep Mustafa* and *Reihan Abdan Syakur* are *Not found in the available sources*.

## Summary

The framework addresses the severe civil and criminal case backlogs affecting Malaysian courts in the late 1990s and 2000. It outlines six core structural pillars: (1) Common Law Judicial Hierarchy, maintaining a court structure topped by the Federal Court, Court of Appeal, and two High Courts (Malaya, Sabah & Sarawak); (2) Pre-Trial Case Management, introducing High Court Order 34 in September 2000 to streamline trials and limit witness lists; (3) Consumer Redressal, creating the Tribunal for Consumer Claims under the Consumer Protection Act 1999 for claims up to RM10,000 without lawyer representation; (4) Labour & Industrial Adjudication, combining Labour Court wage inquiries under the Employment Act 1955 with compulsory Industrial Court arbitration under the Industrial Relations Act 1967; (5) Social Security Protection, administering employment injury and invalidity benefits through SOCSO and the Social Security Appellate Board; and (6) Environmental Enforcement, executing strict statutory liability for water, air, and open burning pollution under the Environmental Quality Act 1974.

## Legal Basis

Authority and execution derive from constitutional provisions and statutory enactments:

- **Constitutional Basis:** Federal Constitution of Malaysia (Arts. 4(1), 73–77, 95B(1), 145(3), Ninth Schedule Lists I, II, III, IIIA).
- **Consumer & Commercial Statutes:** Consumer Protection Act 1999 (Act 599); Sale of Goods Act 1957; Contracts Act 1950 & Hire Purchase Act 1967.
- **Labour & Employment Statutes:** Industrial Relations Act 1967 (Act 177, Revised 1976); Employment Act 1955 (Act 265, Revised 1981); Employees' Social Security Act 1969 (Act 4); Trade Unions Act 1959.
- **Environmental Statutes:** Environmental Quality Act 1974 (Act 127, as amended by Act A636, Act A953, Act A1030, Act A1102).
- **Procedural Codes & Rules:** Rules of the High Court (Order 34).

## Background & Policy Problems

### Background

Economic growth and expanded education in Malaysia generated a surge in trading, commercial, labor, and environmental disputes during the late 20th century. Court registries became overwhelmed with active files, trial postponements, and delayed written judgments, driving reforms to computerize court files, introduce pre-trial case management, and establish specialized non-court tribunals.

### Policy Problems

- Severe Court Backlogs: Massive pendency across all levels of the court hierarchy (e.g., 159,163 civil and 338,218 criminal cases pending in Magistrates' Courts; 84,329 civil cases in Sessions Courts; 54,235 civil cases in High Courts as of December 2000).
- Judicial Overwork & Delayed Judgments: Trial judges hearing chamber matters and trials all day, leaving minimal time to draft written judgments.
- Inaccessibility for Small Claims: High litigation costs, technical civil procedure rules, and advocate fees preventing ordinary consumers from enforcing minor claims.
- Industrial Unrest: Trade disputes threatening national economic productivity, requiring mandatory arbitration mechanisms.
- Environmental Hazards: Atmospheric pollution, industrial effluent, open burning, and constitutional friction between federal environmental rules and state land/water rights.

## Objectives

1\. Clear trial court backlogs through High Court Order 34 pre-trial conferences and file computerization. 2. Provide summary, low-cost consumer dispute resolution up to RM10,000 within a 60-day target. 3. Guarantee industrial stability via compulsory labor arbitration and prohibition of strikes during proceedings. 4. Provide comprehensive social security injury coverage for workers through SOCSO. 5. Combat environmental pollution, hazardous waste dumping, and illegal open burning.

## Scope & Implementation

### Scope

Applies across all Superior Courts (Federal Court, Court of Appeal, High Courts), Subordinate Courts (Sessions Courts, Magistrates' Courts), consumer tribunals, labor courts, SOCSO boards, and environmental enforcement divisions in Malaysia.

### Key Provisions

- **Pre-Trial Case Management (Order 34):** Mandatory pre-trial conference requiring document exchange, witness limits, and show-cause notices for non-compliance.
- **Consumer Claims Jurisdiction:** Summary hearings for claims up to RM10,000 for an RM5 fee, prohibiting advocate representation.
- **Labour Court Inquiries:** Director General of Labour empowered under Sec. 69 Employment Act 1955 to issue binding cash payment orders with 8% interest.
- **Compulsory Labour Arbitration:** Ministerial discretion to refer trade disputes and dismissals to the Industrial Court, making awards binding and prohibiting strikes.
- **Open Burning Ban (Sec. 29A/29AA EQA):** Strict prohibition on open burning carrying fines up to RM500,000 or 5 years imprisonment, shifting burden of proof to landowners.

### Implementation

- **Pre-Trial Case Management Execution:** Implementation of High Court Order 34 conferences across civil registries.
- **Consumer Tribunal Operations:** Settling 1,025 claims in 2001 through hearings and conciliation.
- **Industrial Dispute Adjudication:** Industrial Court hearing 664 cases in 1998 and 701 in 2000.
- **Environmental Prosecutions:** Prosecuting 158 premises in 2000 and issuing 1,645 compound notices totaling RM2.56 million.

## Policy Problems

- Trial Backlogs & Overworked Judges: High Courts and Sessions Courts burdened by heavy dockets and delayed written judgments.
- Public EIA Non-Disclosure: Lack of statutory duty under Section 34A EQA to distribute EIA reports to the public without explicit request and fee payment.
- Legalism in Labour Adjudication: Federal Court decisions (*Syarikat Kenderaan Melayu Kelantan*, *Ramachandran*) subjecting Industrial Court awards to extensive civil court judicial review.

## Reform

- Proposed expansion of court-annexed ADR to civil family, motor accident, and property claims.
- Adoption of ISO 9002 standardized procedures for reviewing detailed EIA reports by DOE.
