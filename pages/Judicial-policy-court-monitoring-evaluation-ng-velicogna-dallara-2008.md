# Judicial-policy-court-monitoring-evaluation-ng-velicogna-dallara-2008

**The Policy Framework for Monitoring and Evaluation of Court Performance** (*Monitoring and Evaluation of Courts Activities and Performance Policy Framework*) is a comparative judicial performance monitoring and evaluation policy framework formulated by Dr. Gar Yein Ng, Dr. Marco Velicogna, and Dr. Cristina Dallara based on research conducted for the European Commission for the Efficiency of Justice (CEPEJ) of the Council of Europe across six countries (France, Italy, the Netherlands, Croatia, Serbia, and Slovenia). It maps a 5-stage evolutionary model (\*Bureaucratic Data Collection, Normative Framework, Institution Building, Evaluation and Monitoring, Accountability and Action\*) to balance judicial independence, public accountability, and the right to a fair trial (Article 6 ECHR) with the values of New Public Management (NPM).

## Summary

Published by Gar Yein Ng, Marco Velicogna, and Cristina Dallara in the \*International Journal for Court Administration\* (IJCA), this policy framework presents a comparative model of court monitoring and evaluation resulting from official CEPEJ research of the Council of Europe. This study examines how judicial systems in long-standing democracies (France, Italy, the Netherlands) and EU accession transition countries (Croatia, Serbia, Slovenia) adapt to New Public Management (NPM) values and Article 6 ECHR guarantees. The authors formulate a 5-stage evolutionary model that maps the trajectory of court transformation from rigid bureaucratic data collection entities into transparent, public-service-oriented, and accountable judicial institutions.

## Legal Basis

- **Article 6 of the European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR):** Outlines the guarantees for a fair, efficient trial and organizational accountability within the judiciary.
- **French Organic Law on Finance Laws (LOLF):** French legislation requiring public services, including courts, to account for budget allocations based on measurable performance criteria.
- **Italian Administrative Proceedings & Civil Service Reform Laws:** The legal framework for public administration efficiency and court reform in Italy.
- **EU Accession Rules:** The foundational criteria driving democratization and judicial system reform in Slovenia, Croatia, and Serbia.
- **Not found in the available sources:** Other specific formal statutory regulations are not mentioned in the source text.

## Background

Public demand for judicial efficiency and the shifting perspective that courts function similarly to ordinary public service organizations have driven the adoption of monitoring and evaluation instruments to improve service quality and public trust.

## Objectives

1\. Map the evolutionary models of court monitoring and evaluation systems across Europe. 2. Align the principles of judicial independence with public accountability within the judiciary. 3. Encourage the adoption of unit-based workload measurement systems to reduce case backlogs.

## Scope

This policy covers a comparative analysis of judicial systems in six European countries (France, Italy, the Netherlands, Croatia, Serbia, Slovenia) and serves as an evaluation reference for the CEPEJ of the Council of Europe.

## Key Provisions

- **5-Stage Evolutionary Model:**

1.  \*Stage 1. Bureaucratic Data Collection\*: Collection of basic manual/electronic procedural data for scheduling and due process.

<!-- -->

1.  \*Stage 2. Normative Framework\*: Establishment of supportive legislation based on NPM or EU accession rules.

<!-- -->

1.  \*Stage 3. Institution Building\*: Establishment of management units/bodies (such as the Netherlands Council for the Judiciary or the Statistics Directorate General & Cost/Performance Unit of the Italian Ministry of Justice).

<!-- -->

1.  \*Stage 4. Evaluation and Monitoring\*: Execution of transparent evaluations using reliable metrics free from political interference.

<!-- -->

1.  \*Stage 5. Accountability and Action\*: Utilization of performance data for budget allocation, public transparency, and policy reform.

- **Slovenian Lukenda Project (2006):** A national initiative for backlog reduction that implemented a unit-based workload measurement system in cooperation with Dutch experts.
- **Continuous Dutch Evaluation:** Best practices for internal court monitoring assessments combined with external evaluations by the Council for the Judiciary.

## Implementation

- **Multiple Case Study Methodology:** Data collection through structured interviews, CEPEJ questionnaires, and field research conducted between 2000 and 2007.
- **Three Patterns of Data Utilization:** (1) No action taken, (2) Budget/resource allocation, and (3) Active policy adjustment.

## Policy Problems

- **Resistance Based on Judicial Independence Doctrine:** The use of judicial independence arguments by certain judges to obstruct organizational management updates.
- **Politicization and Manipulation of Statistics:** The risk of careless data entry caused by staff skepticism regarding the utility of data collection.

## Reform / Change

- Modernization of data collection through the integration of Information and Communication Technology (ICT).
- Transition of courts toward public performance accountability based on empirical evidence.

### 3 Most Important Improvements

1\. **Transition from Bureaucratic Data Collection to Accountability and Action:** Direct courts not merely to collect rigid bureaucratic statistical data, but to utilize performance data for actual budget allocation and policy updates (Stage 5). 2. **Implementation of a Unit-Based Workload Measurement System:** Adopt the Slovenian Lukenda Project model to rationally and objectively calculate case allocation to cut backlogs. 3. **Enforcement of Continuous Assessment of the Monitoring System:** Cultivate periodic evaluations of the M&E instruments internally within courts and externally by judicial governing bodies (Council for the Judiciary) as practiced in the Netherlands.
