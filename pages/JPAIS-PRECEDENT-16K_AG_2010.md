## Overview

Supreme Court Decision No. 16 K/AG/2010 establishes a fundamental jurisprudence and legal standard regarding the protection of civil rights of a non-Muslim wife surviving her Muslim husband. The Supreme Court affirmed that although a spouse practicing a non-Islamic faith does not qualify as a direct heir under Islamic Inheritance Law, a non-Muslim wife who has dedicated herself through a long-standing marriage is entitled to a portion of her late husband's estate via the institution of compulsory testament (\*wasiat wajibah\*) equivalent to a wife's inheritance share, alongside her rightful half (1/2) share of the joint marital property (\*gono-gini\*).

## Case Background

The late Ir. Muhammad Armaya bin Renreng (a Muslim Testator) passed away on May 22, 2008. During his lifetime, he contracted a valid marriage with \[Party A\] (Defendant / Petitioner for Cassation) on November 1, 1990, pursuant to Marriage Certificate Extract No. 57/K.PS/XI/1990, living together harmoniously for 18 years. The Testator was survived by five Muslim heirs: his mother (\[Party B\]) and four siblings (Dra. Hj. Murnihati, Dra. Hj. Muliyahati, Djelitahati, and Ir. Arsal).

The Testator left behind joint marital assets consisting of: (1) A house and 216 m² plot of land at \[address removed\], Makassar; (2) A house and 100 m² plot of land at Kompleks BTN Tabariah G 11/13, Makassar (valued at Rp 70,000,000); and (3) A PT AIA Indonesia Life Insurance policy payout of Rp 50,000,000. The Muslim heirs filed an inheritance distribution lawsuit at the Makassar Religious Court. The Makassar Religious Court (Decision No. 732/Pdt.G/2008/PA.Mks) and the Makassar High Religious Court (Decision No. 59/Pdt.G/2009/PTA.Mks) upheld claims that ignored the non-Muslim wife's share. Dissatisfied with these rulings, the Defendant (\[Party A\]) filed a cassation petition to the Supreme Court.

## Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

- **Ratio Decidendi:** The marriage between the Defendant / Petitioner for Cassation and the Testator lasted for a considerable duration of 18 years, signifying a sustained period of dedication by the Defendant toward the Testator. Therefore, despite the Petitioner's non-Muslim status, it is equitable and just for her to receive her spousal rights, namely a 1/2 share of the joint marital property and a portion of the Testator's estate through the institution of compulsory testament (\*wasiat wajibah\*).
- **Ruling:** Granting the cassation petition filed by Petitioner for Cassation \[Party A\]; Annulling the Makassar High Religious Court Decision No. 59/Pdt.G/2009/PTA.Mks and the Makassar Religious Court Decision No. 732/Pdt.G/2008/PA.Mks; and Independently Adjudicating: (1) Declaring the Defendant (\[Party A\]) entitled to a 1/2 share of the joint marital property; (2) Declaring the Defendant entitled to a share of the inheritance estate via compulsory testament (\*wasiat wajibah\*) amounting to 15/60 parts of the total inheritance apportionment; (3) Establishing the shares of the other heirs (Mother receiving 10/60; 3 sisters receiving 7/60 each; and 1 brother receiving 14/60).
