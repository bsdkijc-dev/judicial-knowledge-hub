## 4. POLICY ANALYSIS

- **Background**: \[FACT\] The imperative to bring justice closer to citizens prompted the Spanish Ministry of the Presidency, Justice, and Parliamentary Relations to design the EVID digitalization initiative. Traditional rules requiring physical presence by law enforcement personnel, legal practitioners, and citizens at judicial headquarters created significant travel expenses and operational downtime.
- **Policy Problem**: \[FACT\]
  - **Travel Costs & Time Burden**: Mandatory physical appearances required lengthy travel for citizens living outside the court's immediate provincial jurisdiction.
  - **Security Operational Disruptions**: In-person police report ratifications forced temporary closures of Civil Guard barracks while officers were away testifying.
  - **Accessibility Barriers**: Impediments to equal access for citizens in remote regions and individuals with special needs or disabilities.
  - **Delayed Document Submissions**: Manual document delivery limited submission windows and generated procedural bottlenecks.
  - **Data Security & Transparency**: The necessity of protecting sensitive identity data while guaranteeing that hearing records cannot be modified without authorization.
- **Legal/Policy Gap**: \[FACT\] The complexity of integrating remote trial technology into the existing legal framework while ensuring that digital hearings provide equivalent legal guarantees and procedural integrity to traditional face-to-face court sessions.
- **Objectives**: \[FACT\] Modernize judicial administration; facilitate remote hearings and digital document management; guarantee non-repudiation, non-manipulation, and non-impersonation principles via blockchain registration; and eliminate physical and geographic barriers for citizens.
- **Implementation**: \[FACT\]
  - **Architecture & Deployment Options**: A cloud-based platform adaptable for deployment across public cloud, private SaaS, or on-premises infrastructure.
  - **Blockchain Technology**: Deployment of a decentralized, tamper-proof ledger to permanently and securely log every action (document submission/modification and participant verification) without single-point failure risks.
  - **Key Features**: Pseudonym-based identification for law enforcement officers to protect physical privacy, tailored accessibility tools for individuals with disabilities, and digital document delivery capabilities.
  - **Phased Adoption Process**: Execution of focus groups and stakeholder workshops in May 2024 involving judges, lawyers, officials, and citizens, alongside training sessions for legal practitioners.
- **Evidence & Impact**: \[FACT\] Substantial reduction in manual scanning and archiving workloads; time efficiency gains for law enforcement officers; data reliability guarantees via a decentralized audit trail; and improved social inclusion for remote residents.
- **Reform & Unresolved Issues**: \[FACT\] Expansion plans to roll out the platform across additional judicial practice areas in Spain; coordination with Autonomous Communities holding delegated judicial competencies; and potential platform replication by foreign judicial administrations.

## 6. MEDIAWIKI OUTPUT

## Summary

**Virtual Desktop for Digital Immediation (EVID)** (*Escritorio Virtual de Inmediación Digital*) (Stable ID: **JPW-PROJECT-002160**) is a public service cloud platform and blockchain-based digital system developed by the Spanish Ministry of the Presidency, Justice, and Parliamentary Relations. Registered under project ID PSTW-2160, EVID modernizes judicial administration by supporting remote court hearings and secure, transparent, and inclusive digital document management. EVID eliminates geographic distances, lowers travel costs for citizens and law enforcement personnel, and ensures evidential integrity through a decentralized, tamper-proof ledger.

## Legal Basis

- **Formal Statutory Basis**: Not found in the available sources. (The document indicates that integrating EVID into the existing legal framework represented a key challenge, though no specific statute number is cited).

## Background

Prior to EVID, the Spanish judicial system suffered operational inefficiencies driven by mandatory physical appearances at court headquarters. This required extensive travel for out-of-province citizens, created document submission delays, and caused temporary closures of Civil Guard barracks when officers traveled to ratify police reports.

## Objectives

1\. Enhance judicial efficiency and accessibility across all regions of Spain. 2. Facilitate remote court hearings with evidential guarantees equivalent to in-person trials. 3. Enforce non-repudiation, non-manipulation, and non-impersonation standards via blockchain. 4. Provide inclusive access for remote residents and individuals with special needs or disabilities.

## Scope

The project encompasses four core operational pillars:

- *Remote Trial Proceedings*: Conducting interactive digital hearings for judges, lawyers, law enforcement officers, and citizens.
- *Blockchain Infrastructure*: Permanently logging document transactions and participant verifications on a decentralized ledger.
- *Pseudonymized Identification*: Protecting law enforcement officers' privacy via pseudonymized identities during remote testimony.
- *Deployment Options*: Flexible hosting across public cloud, private SaaS, or on-premises environments.

## Key Provisions

- **Reliable Audit Trail**: All document submissions and modifications are permanently logged on an immutable ledger.
- **Inclusivity Protocols**: Inclusion of accessibility devices enabling individuals with disabilities to participate fully in proceedings.
- **Official Management**: Fully administered by the Spanish Ministry of the Presidency, Justice, and Parliamentary Relations.

## Implementation

Implementation was achieved through a public-private GovTech partnership with Councilbox (software/blockchain), Telefónica (ICT/telecom), and Kawaru Consulting. Deployment followed a phased approach using focus groups, stakeholder workshops in May 2024, and training sessions for legal practitioners.

## Policy Problems

- Adapting legal practitioners' established workflows from traditional to digital processes.
- Managing regional technological infrastructure disparities across Spain.
- Safeguarding sensitive data privacy during remote trial participation.

## Reform

- Plans to expand EVID across additional judicial sectors in Spain.
- System integration and coordination with Autonomous Communities holding delegated judicial powers.
- Potential international replication of the platform architecture by foreign judicial bodies.

## Relationships with Other Entities

- **Issued by**: Spanish Ministry of the Presidency, Justice, and Parliamentary Relations.
- **Developed with**: Councilbox, Telefónica, and Kawaru Consulting.
- **Applies to**: Judges, Lawyers, Law Enforcement Personnel (Civil Guard), and Spanish Citizens.
