# JPW-SITUATION-003

**Preliminary Examination of the Situation in Venezuela (ICC)** is an independent legal and factual evaluation conducted by the Office of the Prosecutor (OTP) of the International Criminal Court (ICC) since 8 February 2018 regarding alleged crimes against humanity committed within the territory of Venezuela at least since April 2017 \[\].

## Summary

This preliminary examination focuses on alleged crimes committed in the context of anti-government demonstrations and political unrest in Venezuela at least since April 2017, taking into account the background of events since February 2014 \[\].

## Legal Basis

- [Rome Statute](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Rome_Statute): Article 7 (Crimes Against Humanity), Article 12 (Jurisdiction), Article 14 (Referral by a State Party), Article 15 (Communications), and Article 53 (Preliminary Examination) \[\].
- Official referral by 6 States Parties (Argentina, Canada, Colombia, Chile, Paraguay, Peru) dated 27 September 2018 \[\].

## Background

The escalation of mass protests in Venezuela was triggered by economic crisis, food and medical shortages, and Supreme Court rulings restricting the powers of the National Assembly. The government responded to demonstrations by activating "Plan Zamora" and deploying state security forces alongside armed pro-government civilian groups known as "colectivos" \[\].

## Objectives

- Assess whether there is a reasonable basis to believe that crimes against humanity under Article 7 of the Rome Statute have been committed in Venezuela \[\].
- Prepare for the admissibility assessment phase (complementarity of national judicial systems and gravity) \[\].

## Key Provisions

- \*\*Analyzed Forms of Crimes\*\*: Murder and injury, arbitrary deprivation of liberty, torture and ill-treatment, sexual and gender-based crimes, and persecution against political opponents \[\].
- \*\*Use of Military Courts\*\*: Over 700 civilians processed through military tribunals \[\].

## Implementation

On 28 September 2018, the ICC Presidency assigned the situation in Venezuela to Pre-Trial Chamber I. The OTP gathered evidence from OHCHR (ACNUDH), OAS (OEA), IACHR (CIDH), and civil society organizations \[\].

## Policy Issues

- Mass arbitrary detentions (over 15,000 individuals since 2014) and allegations of enforced disappearances \[\].
- Excessive use of force and the participation of armed civilian "colectivos" groups in suppressing civilians \[\].
