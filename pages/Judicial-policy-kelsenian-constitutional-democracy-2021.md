# Judicial-policy-kelsenian-constitutional-democracy-2021

The **Kelsenian Constitutional Democracy & Meta-Democratic Limits Framework** (*Kerangka Teori Demokrasi Konstitusional dan Batas Meta-Demokratis Kelsenian*) is a constitutional law analytical framework evaluating [Hans Kelsen](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Hans_Kelsen)'s procedural democracy and establishing the logical requirement for substantive meta-democratic limits to prevent democratic erosion [^1].

## Summary

Authored by [Antonino Spadaro](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Antonino_Spadaro), Full Professor of Constitutional Law at Università Mediterranea di Reggio Calabria, this study analyzes internal contradictions (*aporias*) within Kelsen's Pure Theory of Law (*Reine Rechtslehre*) [^2]. Kelsen constructed democracy as a neutral, procedural, and relativistic system (*Politischer Wertrelativismus*) [^3]. Spadaro demonstrates that majority rule lacking substantive constitutional constraints risks devolving into ochlocracy, tyranny of the majority, and illiberal regimes (*démocrature*) [^4].

## Legal Basis

This framework relies on foundational constitutional instruments and principles:

- **[Italian Constitutional Court Judgment No. 1146/1988](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Italian_Constitutional_Court_Judgment_No._1146/1988)**: Establishes the existence of an "intangible hard core" of fundamental principles and inviolable rights that political majorities cannot alter [^5].
- **[Article 16 of the Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen (1789)](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Article_16_of_the_Declaration_of_the_Rights_of_Man_and_of_the_Citizen_%281789%29)**: Declares that any society lacking guaranteed rights and the separation of powers lacks a constitution [^6].
- **[UN Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948)](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Universal_Declaration_of_Human_Rights) & [US Bill of Rights](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/US_Bill_of_Rights)**: Universal value standards that precede state positive law [^7].

## Background

Hans Kelsen separated legal theory from political theology, viewing majority decision-making as a rational choice within a relativistic society [^8]. However, Kelsen acted as an *involuntarily liberal* theorist by implicitly requiring minority protections, tolerance, and freedom of thought to sustain his model [^9].

## Objectives

- Prove logically that democratic procedures require meta-democratic outer limits to remain viable [^10].
- Reject the view of the constitution as an "empty box" subject to modification by temporary political majorities [^11].
- Preserve constitutionalism as an instrument for the legal limitation of political power [^12].

## Scope

The scope covers legal philosophy, comparative constitutional theory, algocracy and digital democracy research, and democratic regression trends across Europe and the United States [^13].

## Key Provisions & Logical Implications

- **Application of Gödel's Theorem to Law**: Drawing from Kurt Gödel's Incompleteness Theorems, the internal consistency of a democratic system can only be resolved through an external factor—substantive constitutional values [^14].
- **Analysis of the Trial of Jesus**: Kelsen repeatedly cited Pontius Pilate's release of Barabbas as an example of democratic procedure [^15]. Spadaro argues this event illustrates demagogic ochlocracy rather than genuine constitutional democracy [^16].
- **Rejection of the Imperative Party Mandate**: Kelsen's idea of binding representatives to party directives (*imperative party mandate*) is rejected for undermining the representative's freedom of thought (*free mandate*) [^17].

## Implementation

Operationalized through constitutional adjudication by Constitutional Courts and the enforcement of the *counter-limits* doctrine (*controlimiti*) [^18].

## Policy Issues

- **Algocracy and Data Manipulation**: Threat of statistical data, algorithms, and political bots distorting public deliberation in digital democracies [^19].
- **Stages of Democratic Decay**: The transition from Populistic Democracy → Illiberal Democracy → *Démocrature* (autocracy disguised by elections) [^20].

## Reform / Proposed Changes

Calls for balancing democratic legitimacy "from below" (electoral/popular support) with legitimacy "from above" (science and constitutional axiological guarantees) [^21].
