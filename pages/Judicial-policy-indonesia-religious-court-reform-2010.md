# **'Access to Justice and Court Administration Reform in Indonesia’s Religious Courts' (Badilag / Supreme Court of Indonesia, 2010)**

### **Wiki Entity Page**

**Access to Justice and Court Administration Reform in Indonesia's Religious Courts** (*Program Keadilan untuk Semua* / Justice for All Program) is a series of judicial administration policies and legal reforms launched by the Directorate General of the Religious Courts (Badilag) of the Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia. This policy aims to expand the reach of justice for the poor, female heads of household, and populations in remote areas by waiving court fees (*prodeo*), conducting circuit courts (*sidang keliling*), and providing Legal Aid Posts (*Posbakum*).

## Summary

Following the transfer of organizational and financial authority to the Supreme Court under the "One Roof System" (Law No. 35 of 1999), the Religious Courts pioneered evidence-based public service reforms (\*Access and Equity Study\*). This program successfully eliminated financial and geographical barriers for tens of thousands of poor citizens, increased public trust, and linked family law registration with access to national social protection programs.

## Legal Basis

The implementation of access to justice reforms within the Religious Courts is based on the following legal foundations:

- **Law No. 7 of 1989 jo. Law No. 50 of 2009** on Religious Courts (Article 60 regarding legal aid).
- **Law No. 48 of 2009** on Judicial Power (Articles 56 and 57 regarding the right of the poor to obtain free legal aid).
- **Law No. 35 of 1999** on Amendments to the Law on Judicial Power (One Roof System).
- **Presidential Instruction No. 3 of 2010** on Equitable Development Programs (focusing on justice for women and children).
- **Supreme Court Circular (SEMA) No. 10 of 2010** on Guidelines for Providing Legal Aid in the Judiciary.
- **Presidential Regulation No. 5 of 2010** on the National Medium-Term Development Plan (RPJMN) 2010–2014.
- **National Access to Justice Strategy** (Bappenas, 2009).

## Background

Following the fall of President Soeharto in 1998, Indonesia's judicial institutions generally experienced a crisis of public confidence due to corruption and inefficiency. However, an Asia Foundation survey (2001) found that Religious Courts were considered the most honest and effective government institutions. Through the *One Roof* reform, Religious Courts utilized their autonomous authority to improve case administration and respond to the legal needs of the poor.

## Objectives

1\. Waive court fees for justice seekers who are financially incapable (*prodeo*). 2. Bring judicial services closer to residential areas in remote regions through circuit courts. 3. Provide free legal advisory and information services within court buildings (*Posbakum*). 4. Integrate the legality of family legal status (marriage/divorce) with the right to register children's birth certificates and access pro-poor social assistance.

## Scope

This policy covers the jurisdiction of Religious Courts at the regency/city level (343 working units) and High Religious Courts / Mahkamah Syar'iyah (29 working units), which handle Islamic family law cases including:

- Marriage and Divorce (accounting for 74% of all national civil cases).
- Marriage Legalization (*Isbat Nikah*).
- Inheritance, Wills, Grants, Endowments (*Waqf*), Alms (*Zakat*), and Shari'ah Economics.

## Key Provisions

- **Court Fee Waivers (Prodeo):** The court covers the costs of summoning witnesses and administration for citizens living below the poverty line.
- **Circuit Courts (Sidang Keliling):** Panels of judges and registrars travel to remote locations to hold hearings outside the court building.
- **Legal Aid Posts (Posbakum):** Provision of advocates/paralegals from Legal Aid Organizations (LBH) or Universities within the court premises.
- **SMS Reporting System:** The use of automated monthly SMS technology to monitor the usage of access to justice budgets in real-time across 372 judicial working units.

## Implementation

- **Budget Increases:** The state budget (APBN) increased Badilag's allocation for prodeo and circuit courts 18-fold, from \<Rp 1 billion (2007) to Rp 24 billion (2008), which continued in the 2010–2014 RPJMN.
- **Service Achievements (2011):** In the first 7 months of 2011, 6,243 poor citizens received *prodeo* facilities, 13,860 citizens were served by *circuit courts*, and 16,390 people consulted at 46 *Posbakum* posts.
- **Caseload Increase:** The caseload of Religious Courts increased by 77% in the 2006–2010 period as an indicator of growing accessibility and public trust.

## Policy Problems

- The average cost of a divorce case (Rp 800,000) prior to the reform was equivalent to 4 times the monthly per capita income of the poor.
- The lack of a Divorce Certificate prevented female heads of household (*PEKKA*) from proving their legal status to receive Raskin, BLT, and Jamkesmas.
- Without marriage legalization (*isbat nikah*), approximately 56%–60% of children from unrecorded marriages could not obtain a Birth Certificate.

## Reform / Change

- Transformation from isolated judicial institutions into national judicial reform leaders.
- Recognition within the Supreme Court Reform Blueprint 2010–2035.
- Replication of Badilag's service model to the General Courts under Bappenas.
