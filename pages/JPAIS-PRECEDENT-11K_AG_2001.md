## Overview

Supreme Court Decision No. 11 K/AG/2001 establishes an essential legal precedent regarding the boundaries of competence and jurisdiction of Religious Courts in disputes concerning the division and deduction of civil servant (\*PNS\*) salaries. The Supreme Court affirmed that the rule allocating half (1/2) of a defendant's salary to a former wife pursuant to Article 8 of Government Regulation No. 10 of 1983 jo. Government Regulation No. 45 of 1990 constitutes a civil servant disciplinary regulation, rather than forming part of Religious Court procedural law. The administrative action taken by a government agency to suspend or disburse a civil servant's salary constitutes a State Administrative Decision (\*KTUN\*). Consequently, once the Religious Court rules on the invalidity of the marriage certificate serving as the basis for salary suspension, claims regarding the payment or deduction of civil servant salaries must be brought before the State Administrative Court (\*PTUN\*) and are inadmissible before the Religious Courts.

## Case Background

The Original Plaintiff (Mrs. \[Party B\]) was the former wife of the Original Defendant (\[Party A\], a civil servant teacher) who divorced on December 1, 1994, at the Cilacap Religious Court (Decision No. 1312/Pdt.G/1994/PA.Clp). The divorce ruling stipulated that the Plaintiff was entitled to receive half (1/2) of the Defendant's salary until the Plaintiff remarried. However, starting in April 1994, the Defendant halted the transfer of this salary portion by submitting a copy of Marriage Certificate Extract No. 60/1995 dated February 3, 1995, issued by the Office of Religious Affairs (\*KUA\*) of Bojong Picung Subdistrict, Cianjur Regency, which stated that the Plaintiff had remarried a man named \[Party C\].

The Plaintiff, claiming she had never remarried, filed a lawsuit at the Cilacap Religious Court (Decision No. 2288/Pdt.G/1998/PA.Clp) seeking to declare the Marriage Certificate fraudulent and void, and demanding that the Defendant pay salary arrears. The Cilacap Religious Court granted the lawsuit in part by nullifying the fraudulent marriage certificate, but rejected the salary payment claims on the grounds that the Plaintiff allegedly cohabited without marriage. The Semarang High Religious Court (Decision No. 27/Pdt.G/1999/PTA.Smg) overturned the Cilacap Religious Court decision and granted all of the Plaintiff's claims, including the right to 1/2 of the civil servant salary. The Defendant subsequently filed a cassation petition to the Supreme Court.

## Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

- **Ratio Decidendi:** The allocation of half (1/2) of a defendant's salary to a plaintiff as stipulated under Article 8 of Government Regulation No. 10 of 1983 jo. Government Regulation No. 45 of 1990 concerning Civil Servant Disciplinary Regulations does not form part of Religious Court procedural law. Because the suspension or disbursement of 1/2 of the defendant's salary by the employing agency constitutes a State Administrative Decision, once the marriage certificate in the *a quo* case is declared legally void and non-binding, the plaintiff's claim for the deduction of 1/2 of the defendant's salary must be submitted to the State Administrative Court and is inadmissible before the Religious Courts.
- **Ruling:** Granting the cassation petition filed by Petitioner for Cassation (\[Party A\]); Annulling the Semarang High Religious Court decision dated September 28, 1999, No. 27/Pdt.G/1999/PTA.Smg; and Independently Adjudicating: (1) Granting the Plaintiff's lawsuit in part; (2) Declaring that the Marriage Certificate issued by KUA Bojong Picung Subdistrict, Cianjur Regency No. 60/1995 dated February 3, 1995, is invalid and possesses no legal force; (3) Declaring inadmissible the remainder of the Plaintiff's lawsuit (claims regarding the payment/deduction of 1/2 of the civil servant salary); (4) Ordering the Plaintiff and Appellant to bear procedural costs.
