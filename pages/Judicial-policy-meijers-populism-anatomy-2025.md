# Judicial-policy-meijers-populism-anatomy-2025

**The anatomy of populist ideology: How political parties define 'the people' and 'the elite** is a comparative empirical research paper authored by Maurits J. Meijers (Radboud University / University of Antwerp), Robert A. Huber (University of Salzburg), and Andrej Zaslove (Radboud University), published in the *European Journal of Political Research* (EJPR) in 2025.

## Summary

This study examines how political parties in 31 European countries define the two core concepts of populist ideology: "the people" and "the elite." Using data from the 2023 wave of the Populism and Political Parties Expert Survey (POPPA), the study finds that conceptions of "the people" are driven primarily by host ideology — nativism for the cultural dimension and economic ideology for the economic dimension — while populism itself is the main predictor determining who is considered part of "the elite."

## Background

Populist ideology centers on the opposition between "the honest people" and "the corrupt elite." However, both "the people" and "the elite" function as empty signifiers — concepts without fixed meaning that are filled in by political parties according to their own worldviews.

## Objectives

1\. Identify comparative cross-national patterns in how societal groups are classified as "the people" or "the elite." 2. Distinguish the influence of populism from host ideologies such as nativism and left-right economic position. 3. Analyze how left-wing and right-wing populist parties construct notions of political, media, economic, and socio-structural elites.

## Scope

This comparative study covers 312 political parties across 31 European countries, using the 2023 POPPA expert survey.

## Key Provisions

- **Cultural Conception of "The People":** Nativism is the primary explanatory variable excluding "citizens with a migration background" from the definition of the people (b = -0.80), with right-wing populism reinforcing this exclusion.
- **Economic Conception of "The People":** Left-right economic ideology determines the inclusion/exclusion of "very wealthy citizens," with left-wing populists explicitly excluding the wealthy from the definition of the people.
- **Conception of "The Elite":** Populism is the main predictor determining which groups are seen as elites. Right-wing populists specifically target media elites (journalists) and socio-structural elites (academics), while left-wing populists target economic elites (corporate CEOs).

## Implementation

The methodology was implemented by collecting survey data from 324 political experts across Europe, using 0–10 scales for populism and ideology components, and estimating OLS linear regression models with country-fixed effects.

## Policy Problems

1\. The challenge of understanding how political parties direct mobilization rhetoric and define legitimate "popular sovereignty." 2. The risk of attacks on media independence and academic freedom from right-wing populist movements.

## Reform / Change

Provides the first comparative empirical evidence separating the effect of populism from nativism and economic ideology in the construction of political identity.
