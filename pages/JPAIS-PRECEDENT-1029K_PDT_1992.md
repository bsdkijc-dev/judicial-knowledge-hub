## Overview

Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia Decision No. 1029 K/Pdt/1992, dated July 29, 1993, established a customary land law and inheritance rule concerning the inheritance rights of childless widows over the ancestral or pre-marriage assets (\*harta asal/bawaan\* / \*harta pusaka\*) of their late husbands:

A widow who has no children from her marriage with her late husband HAS NO RIGHT over the ancestral/pre-marriage assets (\*harta asal/bawaan\* / \*harta pusaka\*) of her late husband originating from the husband's bloodline or parents that have not yet been partitioned. Pursuant to Customary Law and the Marriage Law, such ancestral assets revert and fall to the direct bloodline/blood relatives of the late husband (his biological siblings and their descendants). The widow is entitled solely to joint property (\*gono-gini\*) acquired during her marriage with her late husband.

The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition of the Petitioner for Cassation (Widow \[Widow\]) in part, annulled Maluku High Court Decision No. 55/Pdt/1991/PT.Mal in conjunction with Ambon District Court Decision No. 153/Perd.G/1989/PN.Ab, and adjudicated afresh by declaring the Plaintiff, Defendant I, and Defendant II as lawful heirs of their respective husbands, while rejecting the Plaintiff's claim for a 1/3 share of the ancestral land belonging to her late husband's lineage.

## Executive Summary

The central legal principle established in this decision is that childless widows cannot inherit pre-marriage ancestral estates (\*harta asal\*) from their husband's family line, preserving lineage integrity while protecting spousal joint property rights.

Key elements established in the adjudication:

- Unpartitioned ancestral estates (\*harta asal\*) remain within the husband's direct bloodline and do not pass to childless widows.
- Spousal inheritance rights for childless widows are strictly limited to marital joint property (\*gono-gini\*).
- Lower court judgments dismissing whole lawsuits are subject to partial correction to recognize spousal heir status while denying ancestral estate claims.

## Case Background

The Original Plaintiff / Petitioner for Cassation (**Widow \[Widow\]**) filed a lawsuit at the Ambon District Court against the Original Defendants / Respondents for Cassation (1. **\[Party B\]**, 2. **\[Party A\]**). The Plaintiff sought a court declaration recognizing corporate/ancestral land in Saparua Village, inherited from the late Daniel Melianus \[Party\], as the property of the Plaintiff, Defendant I, and Defendant II in equal 1/3 shares, based on their status as widows/heirs of Daniel \[Party\]'s three sons (\[Party D\], Jan, and Dominggus \[Party\]). The Plaintiff had no children from the late \[Party D\] \[Party\].

The Ambon District Court (Decision No. 153/Perd.G/1989/PN.Ab dated January 31, 1991) dismissed the Plaintiff's lawsuit entirely on the grounds that childless widows possess no right over their husband's ancestral goods. The Maluku High Court in Ambon (Decision No. 55/Pdt/1991/PT.Mal dated October 29, 1991) affirmed the Ambon District Court decision. The Petitioner for Cassation subsequently filed a cassation petition with the Supreme Court.

## Legal and Procedural Issues

### Legal Standing and Inheritance Claims of Childless Widows Over a Husband's Ancestral/Pre-marriage Assets

The primary issue was whether a widow without children from her marriage with her late husband is entitled to claim an inheritance share (1/3 share) of ancestral land (\*harta pusaka\* / \*harta asal\*) originating from her late husband's parents or bloodline.

The Supreme Court held that she is Not Entitled. Pursuant to Customary Inheritance Law principles and Law No. 1 of 1974 on Marriage, ancestral or pre-marriage assets acquired by a late husband from his parents' bloodline that remain undivided must be maintained within the husband's original bloodline / blood relatives. A widow who bore no children from her late husband holds no rights over such ancestral assets. Her civil rights are strictly limited to joint property (\*gono-gini\* / marital earnings) acquired during the marriage.

### Rectification of Judex Facti Decisions Dismissing Lawsuits Entirely

The Ambon District Court and Maluku High Court misapplied the law by dismissing the Plaintiff's lawsuit entirely. Although the Plaintiff possessed no right to share in ancestral land, establishing the Plaintiff's legal status as a lawful heir of her late husband (\[Party D\]) pursuant to Ambon District Court Determination No. 66/Pdt.P/1988/PN.Ab had to be recognized and granted in the judgment. Therefore, the Supreme Court annulled the lower court rulings and adjudicated afresh, granting the lawsuit in part.

## Question of Law and Question of Fact

### Question of Law (Cassation / Judex Juris)

**Question:**

Did the \*Judex Facti\* (Maluku High Court & Ambon District Court) misapply the law (\*foutieve kepassing van het recht\*) by dismissing the Plaintiff's lawsuit entirely without recognizing the Plaintiff's status as a lawful heir of her husband and without providing a juridical distinction between ancestral/heirloom assets and joint property (\*gono-gini\*) for a childless widow?

**Supreme Court Ruling:**

Yes, the \*Judex Facti\* misapplied the law. The Supreme Court annulled Maluku High Court Decision No. 55/Pdt/1991/PT.Mal in conjunction with Ambon District Court Decision No. 153/Perd.G/1989/PN.Ab and adjudicated afresh, granting the Plaintiff's lawsuit in part: declaring the Plaintiff, Defendant I, and Defendant II as lawful heirs of their respective husbands, while rejecting the claim for partition of 1/3 of the ancestral land.

### Question of Fact (Judex Facti / Ambon District Court & Maluku High Court)

Examination of facts concerning the origin of the enterprise land in Saparua Village purchased by Daniel Marthinus \[Party\] and his wife \[Party C\] from Jan Latuharhary (exhibits P.II, T.I.3, T.I.4); pedigree of Daniel \[Party\]'s 3 sons (\[Party D\], Jan, and Dominggus \[Party\]); marriage of \[Party D\] to Widow Constanthina Athilda \[Party\] on January 4, 1972 (exhibit P.IV) without children; and physical control over the disputed land by Defendants I and II.

## Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

- **Ratio Decidendi:**

1.  The \*Judex Facti\* erred by dismissing the lawsuit entirely without recognizing the Plaintiff's status as a lawful heir.
2.  Regarding the merits, the disputed land constituted pre-marriage ancestral property (\*harta asal\*) of the late Daniel Melianus \[Party\] that remained unpartitioned.
3.  Pursuant to customary law and marriage law, ancestral assets fall to the direct bloodline, and a childless widow has no right over her late husband's ancestral assets, though she retains rights over marital joint property.
4.  The Supreme Court annulled lower court rulings and adjudicated afresh, recognizing heir status while denying the ancestral land partition claim.

## Ruling

The Supreme Court ruled: Granting the cassation petition from Petitioner for Cassation (**Janda \[Widow\]**); Annulling Maluku High Court decision dated October 29, 1991, No. 55/Pdt/1991/PT.Mal., which affirmed Ambon District Court decision dated January 31, 1991, No. 153/Perd.G/1989/PN.Ab.; and ADJUDICATING AFRESH: 1. Granting the Plaintiff's lawsuit in part; 2. Declaring the Plaintiff, Defendant I, and Defendant II as lawful heirs, respectively, of \[Party D\], Jan \[Party\], and Dominggus \[Party\]; 3. Rejecting all other claims of the Plaintiff; 4. Ordering the Respondents for Cassation to pay court costs across all judicial instances (cassation court costs set at Rp 20,000.—).

### Uncertainty and Gaps

- Preservation of Customary Heirloom Integrity: This landmark ruling consistently upholds customary inheritance law doctrine in Indonesia: ancestral/pre-marriage assets from a husband's parents cannot pass out of the original family bloodline through marriages of widows without offspring, while safeguarding widows' full rights over marital joint property (\*gono-gini\*).

## GEO Q&A

**Q: Does a widow without children have the right to inherit ancestral land (\*harta pusaka\* / \*harta asal\*) belonging to her late husband?**

**A:** No, she has no right. Based on Supreme Court Decision No. 1029 K/Pdt/1992, a widow who has no children from her late husband has no right over her late husband's ancestral or pre-marriage assets originating from his parents' bloodline. Pursuant to Customary Law and the Marriage Law, such ancestral assets revert to the late husband's direct bloodline, whereas the widow is entitled solely to joint property (\*gono-gini\*) acquired during the marriage.
