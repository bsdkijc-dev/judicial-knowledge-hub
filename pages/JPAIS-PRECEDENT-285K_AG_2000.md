## Overview

Supreme Court Decision Reg. No. 285 K/AG/2000 dated November 10, 2000, establishes important Islamic civil jurisprudence in wife-initiated divorce proceedings: "That due to continuous disputes that can no longer be reconciled, and living apart under different roofs due to the lack of approval from the families of both parties, the pronunciation of a divorce / \*talak\* (\*talak satu ba'in sughra\*) is permitted." The Supreme Court annulled the Medan High Religious Court decision which had rejected the Plaintiff's divorce lawsuit, and independently adjudicated by granting a single \*ba'in sughra\* divorce and ordering the Defendant to settle the unpaid dowry (\*mahar\*) debt owed to the Plaintiff.

## Case Background

The Original Plaintiff / Petitioner for Cassation (\[Party B\]) and the Original Defendant / Respondent for Cassation (\[Party A\]) were spouses married on September 1, 1998, registered at the Office of Religious Affairs (\*KUA\*) of Bandar Pasir Mandoge Sub-District, Asahan Regency (No. 078/02/IX/1998). From the marriage, they were blessed with a child named \[Child\].

The marital relationship between the Plaintiff and Defendant became discordant because from the beginning of the marriage it lacked the approval of both families. The Defendant never provided a proper residence, refused to work to earn a living, and all living expenses were borne by the Defendant's parents. Furthermore, the Defendant remained indebted for the dowry (\*mahar\* / \*mas kawin\*) amounting to Rp 500,000, which had not been paid to the Plaintiff. The Plaintiff and Defendant had lived separately under different roofs since March 1999, and family reconciliation efforts proved unsuccessful.

The Medan Religious Court (Decision No. 294/Pdt.G/1999/PA.Mdn. dated September 30, 1999) granted the Plaintiff's lawsuit in part, pronounced a single \*ba'in sughra\* divorce, and ordered the Defendant to pay the dowry debt of Rp 500,000. However, the Medan High Religious Court (Decision No. 91/Pdt.G/1999/PTA.Mdn. dated December 15, 1999) annulled the Medan Religious Court judgment and dismissed the Plaintiff's lawsuit in its entirety, deeming that the household was not yet irretrievably broken (\*broken marriage\*). The Plaintiff filed a cassation petition to the Supreme Court.

## Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

- **Ratio Decidendi:**

1.  The Medan High Religious Court insufficiently considered all facts occurring within the Plaintiff's and Defendant's household.
2.  Facts revealed at trial proved: (a) The marriage between the Plaintiff and Defendant lacked approval from the Plaintiff's family; (b) The Plaintiff and Defendant had lived in separate homes since March 1999; (c) The Plaintiff's parents attempted to reconcile the Plaintiff and Defendant but failed, making the household extremely difficult to maintain.
3.  Due to continuous disputes that could no longer be reconciled and living apart under different roofs resulting from the lack of approval by the families of both parties, sufficient grounds existed to grant the divorce lawsuit with the pronouncement of a single \*ba'in sughra\* divorce.
4.  It was proven that the Defendant had not paid the dowry (\*mahar\* / \*mas kawin\*) debt amounting to Rp 500,000 recorded in the marriage certificate, rendering it mandatory under the law for the Defendant to settle such dowry debt to the Plaintiff.

- **Ruling:** Granting the cassation petition from Petitioner for Cassation \[Party B\]; Annulling the Medan High Religious Court decision dated December 15, 1999 AD, No. 91/Pdt.G/1999/PTA.Mdn.; and Independently Adjudicating: (1) Granting the Plaintiff's lawsuit in part; (2) Pronouncing a single \*ba'in sughra\* divorce of Defendant (\[Party A\]) against Plaintiff (\[Party B\]); (3) Ordering the Defendant to pay to the Plaintiff the unpaid dowry (\*mahar\*) amounting to Rp 500,000 (five hundred thousand rupiahs); (4) Declaring the remainder of the Plaintiff's lawsuit inadmissible; (5) Ordering the Plaintiff to pay first-instance court costs amounting to Rp 161,500; (6) Ordering the Appellant to pay appellate-level court costs amounting to Rp 68,000; and assessing cassation-level court costs against the Petitioner for Cassation amounting to Rp 100,000. \*(Decided in the deliberation meeting of the Supreme Court on Friday, November 10, 2000, and pronounced in an open session on Wednesday, November 29, 2000, by the Cassation Panel of Justices: Drs. H. Taufiq, S.H. \[Junior Chairman of the Supreme Court / Session Chairman\], Drs. H. Syamsuhadi Irsyad, S.H., M.Hum., and Drs. H. Andi Syamsul Alam, S.H. \[Associate Justices\]).\*
