# 'International Journal for Court Administration, Vol. 4, No. 1' (IACA, 2012)

### Wiki Entity Page

**International Journal for Court Administration (Vol. 4, No. 1)** is the official peer-reviewed electronic periodical publication of the *International Association for Court Administration* (IACA) featuring comparative scientific studies on pro-poor access to justice in Indonesian Islamic courts, court management reorganization in Slovenia, Northern European judicial governance models, Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) primers, and the impact of judicial symbols \[1–6, 18, 84, 154, 191\].

## Summary

This seventh issue is led by Markus B. Zimmer (Executive Editor) and Prof. Dr. Philip M. Langbroek (Managing Editor). The publication contains five main articles and one book review analyzing judicial reforms in Indonesia, Slovenia, Australia, Northern Europe, and the United States.

## Background & Key Article Summaries

### 1. Indonesian Religious Courts Reform (Cate Sumner & Tim Lindsey)

- **Main Barrier:** Average court fees of Rp 800,000 (4 times the monthly per capita income of the poor) prevent 90% of poor female heads of household (PEKKA) from accessing courts.
- **Social Impact:** Without divorce/marriage certificates, 56% of children lack birth certificates, blocking access to social assistance (BLT, Raskin, Jamkesmas) and 9-year compulsory education.
- **Solutions & Results (2007–2011):** Badilag increased the budget for fee waivers (*prodeo*) and circuit courts (*sidang keliling*) 18-fold. In the first half of 2011 alone, \>20,000 poor citizens gained court access and 16,390 people received free legal assistance at Posbakum posts.

### 2. Constitutional Relationship of Court Management in Slovenia (Zoran Skubic)

- **Separation of Functions:** Distinguishing "Justice Administration" (*pravosodna uprava* - Executive/Ministry realm) from "Court Management" (*sodna uprava* - Judicial realm).
- **2009 Reform:** Integrated 43 Local Courts into 11 District Courts, transferred the authority to appoint Court Presidents to the Judicial Council (*Sodni svet*), and created the professional position of Director of the Court.

### 3. Court Governance Models (Tin Bunjevac - Australia & Europe)

- Critiques the executive departmental model and promotes *Integrated Management* and the *Northern European Model* (independent judicial agencies such as the SNCA in Sweden and the Raad voor de rechtspraak in the Netherlands).

### 4. Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) Primer (Markus Zimmer)

- **ADR Classification:** (1) *Facilitative* (Mediation, Conciliation); (2) *Advisory/Evaluative* (Early Neutral Evaluation, Non-Binding Arbitration, Mini-Trial); (3) *Adjudicative* (Binding Arbitration, Court Adjudication).
- Develops the *Multi-Door Courthouse* concept.

### 5. The Meaning of the Judicial Gown (Philip Langbroek)

- Judicial gowns and symbols serve to depersonalize the judge, assert neutrality, and increase public acceptance of decisions.

## Policy Problems

1\. Isolation of poor citizens from legal identity documents due to high court fees. 2. Case backlogs caused by inefficient division of court management functions. 3. Judicial dependence on the executive bureaucracy for budget management.

## Reform / Change

1\. Systematic allocation of state budgets for fee waivers, circuit courts, and legal aid posts. 2. Consolidation of first-instance courts under district courts. 3. Adoption of the Multi-Door Courthouse system and integrated independent agencies.
