# Judicial-policy-ai-odr-zeleznikow-2017

**The Zeleznikow AI and Online Dispute Resolution Policy Framework** is a judicial technology policy and evaluation framework assessing the integration of Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Online Dispute Resolution (ODR) to enhance court administration efficiency and access to justice for Self-Represented Litigants (SRLs / *pro se* litigants). Formulated by Prof. John Zeleznikow (Sir Zelman Cowen Centre, Victoria University) in the *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA), Vol. 8 No. 2 (May 2017), the framework establishes a hybrid ODR model based on the three-step Lodder-Zeleznikow approach: (1) determination of BATNA (Best Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement), (2) facilitation of dialogue/argumentation, and (3) decision analysis and integrative trade-off compromises \[1–3, 16–21, 30, 42\].

## Summary

Published by Prof. John Zeleznikow in the IJCA (May 2017), this policy framework evaluates the role of artificial intelligence and ODR technologies in addressing the crisis triggered by the surge of Self-Represented Litigants (SRLs) across Common Law judicial systems (Australia, USA, UK) \[1–3, 8\]. The SRL phenomenon has prolonged trial durations, heavily burdened court staff, and created an access-to-justice crisis for low-income citizens. Zeleznikow asserts that an effective ODR system must not merely shift face-to-face mediation onto a computer screen (as seen in the failure of the *Australian Online FDR System*), but must be a **Hybrid System** based on the Lodder-Zeleznikow three-step model \[30, 40–42\]. The study dissects real-world AI applications: the *GetAid System* (evaluating legal aid eligibility using Toulmin argument trees at Victoria Legal Aid), the *Split-Up System* (predicting the division of marital assets using 20 neural networks and 94 variables as a BATNA calculator), and the *AssetDivider System* (an integrative trade-off algorithm respecting monetary values and child welfare laws) \[27–38\].

## Legal Basis

- **Australian Family Law Act & Family Dispute Resolution (FDR) Mandate:** Regulations requiring family disputes to undergo FDR before a lawsuit can be filed in court.
- **Re F: Litigants in Person Guidelines (2001) 161 FLR 189:** Australian Commonwealth jurisprudence guidelines regarding the protection of procedural rights for SRLs in court.
- **US Legal Services Corporation (LSC) Summit Report Vision (2013):** The strategic vision for US judicial technology to provide unified legal portals and automated triage.
- **UK eJudiciary & eBundles Digital Court System Framework (2016):** An independent judicial IT framework in the UK pioneered by Sir Henry Brooke and Justice John Tanzer.
- **Not found in the available sources:** Other specific formal statutory regulations are not mentioned in the source text \[FACT\].

## Background

Legal aid budget cuts and high attorney fees have resulted in up to 88% of domestic relations cases in Arizona and up to 93% of cases in Australian tribunals lacking legal representation.

## Objectives

1\. Open broad and affordable access to justice for SRLs through the provision of interactive online legal guidance. 2. Improve the efficiency, consistency, and transparency of legal decisions to reduce public prejudice against the judiciary. 3. Facilitate out-of-court dispute resolution (ADR/FDR) through objective BATNA calculations and trade-off compromise recommendations.

## Scope

This policy encompasses the evaluation and application of AI and ODR in Australia, the United States, and England and Wales, specifically within the domains of family law and civil legal aid.

## Key Provisions

- **The Lodder-Zeleznikow Three-Step ODR Model:**
  - **Step 1 (BATNA Calculation):** Providing objective feedback on projected court outcomes if negotiations fail.
  - **Step 2 (Dialogue & Argumentation):** Resolving differences of opinion through communication instruments and argument trees.
  - **Step 3 (Decision Analysis & Trade-offs):** Using integrative trade-off algorithms to allocate assets based on priority scales and substantive legal rules.
- **Specific AI Implementations:**
  - **GetAid:** Decision trees and Toulmin arguments for determining legal aid application eligibility at Victoria Legal Aid.
  - **Split-Up:** Neural networks trained on 103 commonplace cases to predict the percentage entitlement of marital assets.
  - **AssetDivider:** Asset allocation based on value preferences (ratings sum to 100), real monetary values, and the establishment of payout items.

## Implementation

- **Development of State-Wide Legal Portals (LSC & Microsoft):** The provision of unified legal portals featuring automated triage and guided interviews.
- **Integration of ODR Automated Triage:** The automatic diversion of domestic violence cases directly to court proceedings, bypassing the ODR process.

## Policy Problems

- **Low Uptake of Imitation Face-to-Face ODR:** The failure of the *Australian Online FDR System* due to its lack of AI analysis tools (BATNA/Trade-offs).
- **Risk of Ignoring Substantive Law in Private ODR:** Commercial ODR systems based on game theory risk neglecting key legal principles such as the *paramount interest of the child*.

## Reform / Change

- Developing Hybrid ODR that integrates AI analysis (BATNA & trade-offs), automated triage, and the facilitation of online communication.
- Integrating ethics, governance, and transparency frameworks into judicial artificial intelligence.

### 3 Most Important Improvements

1\. **Adoption of an Integrated Hybrid ODR System (BATNA + Trade-offs + Online Communication):** Cease the development of ODR portals that merely imitate face-to-face mediation and replace them with hybrid systems equipped with objective AI analysis tools to assist self-represented litigants. 2. **Development of State-Wide Unified Legal Portals with Automated Triage Features:** Integrate guided interviews and document assembly systems to filter citizen cases and automatically divert sensitive matters (e.g., domestic violence) directly to the courts. 3. **Alignment of AI Trade-off Algorithms with Substantive Legal Principles:** Ensure that all AI-ODR systems incorporate mandatory legal constraints (such as the paramount interest of the child principle) into asset allocation algorithms so that the resulting compromises remain legally sound and fair \[15–16, 35–37, 42\].
