# Judicial-policy-amdc-multidoor-2003

**Abuja Multi-Door Courthouse (AMDC)** is a court-annexed Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) center established within the High Court of the Federal Capital Territory (FCT), Abuja, Nigeria, on 13 October 2003. Adopting the "Multi-Door Courthouse" concept formulated by Professor Frank Sander of Harvard Law School in 1976, the AMDC integrates intake screening, negotiation, mediation, conciliation, and arbitration into the public judicial system to reduce docket congestion, cut litigation costs, and restore the business and personal relationships of the parties involved \[11–13, 21–23, 30, 31\].

## Summary

Commissioned by the Chief Justice of Nigeria and fully funded by the High Court of the FCT Abuja under the leadership of Chief Judge L.H. Gummi, the AMDC is the second multi-door facility in Africa, following the Lagos Multi-Door Courthouse (LMDC, 2002). The AMDC offers dispute resolution pathways tailored to each case's specific needs. In practice, the AMDC has recorded an average settlement rate of 70%, with 50% of cases successfully resolved at the pre-session stage before reaching formal mediation or arbitration.

## Legal Basis

- **Section 259 of the Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria 1999:** The basis of authority for the Chief Judge of the FCT Abuja to issue the AMDC's Practice Direction.
- **Section 18 of the High Court Act (CAP 510 LFN 1990):** The statutory mandate directing courts to promote reconciliation and amicable settlement.
- **District Court Act (CAP 495 LFN 1990, Sections 26, 27, 82–88):** Rules governing referees and special arbitrators.
- **Order 19, High Court of the FCT (Civil Procedure Rules) Act (CAP 511 LFN 1990):** Procedural rules on court referrals for out-of-court dispute settlement.
- **Order 25, Rule 1, High Court of Lagos State (Civil Procedure) Rules 2004:** Pre-trial conference provisions that pioneered ADR integration in Nigeria.

## Background & the Failure of the Adversarial System

Prior to the colonial era, traditional Nigerian society employed a layered dispute resolution approach (personal negotiation → clan/kinsman mediation → chief/king arbitration) centered on restoring social relationships. The colonial legal system replaced this with a rigid adversarial framework. A surge in public legal awareness following the return to democracy in 1999 overwhelmed the capacity of traditional courts.

### Case Study: Emeka Nwana v. FCDA (2007)

The failure of the adversarial system is illustrated by the case *Nwana v. FCDA*. Mr. Nwana was dismissed by the FCDA in 1989 and filed suit in the High Court of the FCT. After proceeding through the Court of Appeal (1994) and the Supreme Court (2007), the Supreme Court set aside the appellate court's ruling and ordered a fresh hearing from the beginning (\*de novo\*) — consuming **18 years of litigation** without any substantive resolution.

## Scope & the AMDC's Three-Stage Procedure

### Case Handling Procedure

1\. **Stage 1 (Filing & Notice):** The initiating party completes Form 1 (Request Form) and a statement of issues. The ADR Registrar sends Form 2 (Notice of Referral) and Form 3 (Memorandum) to the respondent within 7 days. 2. **Stage 2 (Screening & Pre-Session):** A Dispute Resolution Officer (DRO) conducts a confidential Screening Conference (30–45 minutes) to diagnose the case, select the appropriate ADR method, and secure signatures on Form 4 (Attendance) and Form 5 (Confidentiality). 3. **Stage 3 (Scheduling & ADR Session):** The neutral (mediator/arbitrator) confirms availability within 7 days via a Disclosure Form, followed by the mediation or arbitration session.

### Case Distribution (2008/2009 Legal Year)

- Commercial Contracts: **50%**.
- Land Disputes: **20%**.
- Matrimonial/Family Cases: **10%**.
- Tenancy: **10%**.
- Labour: **4%**.
- Banking: **3%**.
- Accident Claims: **3%**.

## Policy Issues & Challenges

1\. **Resistance from the Bar Association:** Lawyers initially opposed the Multi-Door concept out of concern for losing litigation fee income. The AMDC addressed this through workshops and ongoing education. 2. **Voluntary Non-Attendance:** Some parties refuse to appear at the AMDC. To address this, the AMDC has proposed adopting English jurisprudence (\*Dunnett v. Railtrack\*) in the form of cost sanctions for parties who unreasonably refuse ADR.

## Reform & Efficiency Outcomes

- **Success Rate:** The AMDC has recorded a 70% settlement rate, with 50% resolved at the pre-session stage, cutting case resolution time from **3 months to 1 month**.
- **Legal Validity:** Mediated agreements are endorsed by an ADR Judge as legally binding consent judgments.
