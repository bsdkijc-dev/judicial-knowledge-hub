# JPW-REPORT-013

**Report on Cooperation Challenges Faced by the Court with Respect to Financial Investigations** is the official report summarizing the findings and recommendations of a 2015 workshop and panel discussion organized by the International Criminal Court in cooperation with the Principality of Liechtenstein and the International Centre for Asset Recovery (ICAR, Basel Institute on Governance), with financial support from the European Commission, to evaluate challenges and formulate recommendations for improving international cooperation in financial investigations and the tracing, freezing, and confiscation of assets.

## Summary

The report was prepared following a workshop held on 26–27 October 2015 in The Hague, and a panel discussion held on the margins of the Assembly of States Parties (ASP) session on 19 November 2015. It examines the legal framework and operational obstacles in financial investigations and sets out strategic recommendations for both States Parties and the Court aimed at tracing and confiscating illicit assets to fund victim reparations and improve judicial efficiency.

## Legal Basis

- [Rome Statute](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Rome_Statute):
  - **Article 54**: Duties and powers of the Prosecutor in investigations.
  - **Article 57(3)(e)**: Power of the Pre-Trial Chamber to issue protective/asset-freezing orders.
  - **Article 75**: Reparations orders for victims.
  - **Articles 86 and 88**: States Parties' general obligation to cooperate and to adapt domestic law accordingly.
  - **Article 93(1)(k)**: Form of international cooperation in the identification, tracing, freezing, and seizure of assets.
- [Rules of Procedure and Evidence](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Rules_of_Procedure_and_Evidence): Rule 21(5), on assessment of a suspect's financial means (indigence) for legal aid.

## Background

Rome Statute crimes are often linked to transnational organized crime and financial crime. International conventions such as UNCAC and UNTOC underscore the growing importance of asset tracing in international law enforcement. Strengthening financial-investigation capacity became especially pressing as the ICC entered the first reparations phase in the \*Lubanga\* case.

## Objectives

- Strengthen criminal evidence through financial linkage evidence, reducing reliance on witness testimony.
- Secure suspects' assets to enable confiscation orders and victim reparations.
- Ensure transparency and prevent abuse of the Court's legal aid scheme.
- Cut off perpetrators' financial capacity to continue criminal activity ("going after the money").

## Key Provisions

- **Role of the Office of the Prosecutor (OTP)**: Gathers evidence of criminal financial flows and identifies assets for potential confiscation under Articles 54 and 93(1)(k).
- **Role of the Chambers and Registry**: Chambers issue asset-freezing orders (Article 57(3)(e)), while the Registry coordinates execution of RFAs with partner states via internal MoU with the OTP and assesses a suspect's financial means under RPE Rule 21(5).
- **Cooperation Challenges**: The absence of its own police force makes the Court heavily dependent on States Parties' cooperation under Articles 86 and 88.

## Implementation

Recommendations fall into three categories:

- **For States Parties**: Adapt domestic law; designate specific financial-investigation contact points; hold informal consultations before formal RFAs; and open domestic money-laundering investigations based on Court-provided information ("reversed request").
- **For the Court**: Develop a financial cooperation guidance manual; publish Appeals Chamber jurisprudence; map domestic legal systems; and explore secure network access to financial databases.
- **For the Court and States Parties Jointly**: Conduct joint training; leverage Financial Intelligence Units (FIUs); and partner with the Egmont Group, CARIN, UNODC, StAR, Interpol, and Justice Rapid Response.

## Evidence

Financial investigations are grounded in Articles 54, 57(3)(e), 75, and 93(1)(k) of the Rome Statute and RPE Rule 21(5). In practice, asset tracing was directly applied in the first reparations phase of the \*Lubanga\* case.

## Policy Issues

- A shortage of qualified financial investigators both within the ICC and among national authorities.
- Domestic legal obstacles in some states that lack civil or criminal procedures for executing ICC asset-freezing requests.
- The absence of explicit guidance on the "nexus" limits of assets that may be frozen.
