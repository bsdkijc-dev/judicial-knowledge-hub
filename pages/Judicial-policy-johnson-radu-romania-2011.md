# Judicial-policy-johnson-radu-romania-2011

**The Effect Of EU Anti-Corruption Measures On The Romanian Judiciary** is a qualitative policy research study by Ann Johnson and Bianca Radu analyzing the effectiveness of the European Union's Cooperation and Verification Mechanism (CVM) and the role of the Superior Council of the Magistracy (CSM) in securing judicial independence and combating corruption in Romania.

## Summary

The study examines Romanian judges' perceptions of legal reform following EU accession. Although the creation of the CSM successfully protected judges' tenure and discipline from executive political dismissal, full judicial independence remains constrained by budgetary control held by the Ministry of Justice and by the lack of transparency in promotions to higher courts.

## Legal Basis

- **2003 Revision of the Constitution of Romania (June 2004):** The legal basis for establishing the CSM as an independent judicial oversight body.
- **EU Cooperation and Verification Mechanism (CVM):** A European Commission mandate to monitor judicial reform and anti-corruption efforts in Romania.
- **Articles 8–11 & 14 ECHR:** European Court of Human Rights jurisprudence binding on Romanian judges.

## Background

Following the fall of the authoritarian Ceaușescu regime in 1989, Romania experienced legal stagnation driven by informal patronage networks ("neoinstitutionalism"). EU accession in 2007 required the establishment of independent institutions to guarantee the rule of law.

## Objectives

1\. Test whether the creation of the CSM actually produced a judiciary independent from the executive. 2. Analyze the application of EU conditionality through \*Rational Choice\* and \*Neoinstitutionalism\* theory. 3. Identify structural obstacles to combating judicial corruption.

## Scope

This qualitative study is based on structured interviews with 23 judges in Cluj-Napoca and Bucharest at the levels of trial tribunals, Courts of Appeal, and the High Court.

## Key Provisions

- **Independence of Initial Recruitment:** Entry-level judges are appointed transparently through the National Institute of Magistrates (NIM) national examination.
- **Constrained Financial Autonomy:** The Ministry of Justice (MoJ) retains control over the operating budget and number of judges, which triggered a judicial strike in September 2009.
- **Opaque Promotion Process:** Interview criteria for promotion to the Court of Appeals and Supreme (High) Court are considered ambiguous and vulnerable to political intervention.
- **Standardization of Opinions & ECRIS:** Improved quality of legal analysis and digital cataloguing of decisions via the ECRIS system.

## Implementation

Implementation of the reforms is measured using the Magen & Morlino framework (Adoption, Implementation, Internalization). Findings show that formal \*adoption\* of the rules occurred, but \*implementation\* stalled on "high politics" issues such as budgets and senior promotions.

## Policy Problems

1\. \*De jure vs. De facto Implementation:\* Formal compliance ("empty compliance") without genuine transfer of financial authority to the judiciary. 2. Use of mass media by politicians to discredit judges and intervene in corruption cases. 3. Legislative ambiguity ("code law ambiguity") arising from frequent legal changes.

## Reform / Change

- Generational change, with the entry of younger judges not trained under the communist era.
- Adoption of random case assignment.
- Application of ECHR standards in written legal analysis.

## Relationships with Other Entities

- **Superior Council of the Magistracy (CSM):** Independent body overseeing judges' careers and discipline.
- **European Commission (EC):** Body overseeing CVM reporting.
- **European Court of Human Rights (ECHR):** Court that overturns national decisions not meeting human rights standards.
