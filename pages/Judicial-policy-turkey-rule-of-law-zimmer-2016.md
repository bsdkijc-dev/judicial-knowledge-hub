# Judicial-policy-turkey-rule-of-law-zimmer-2016

**The Markus Zimmer Policy Framework on Preserving Turkey's Rule of Law** is a policy framework and judicial crisis analysis detailing the impact of executive branch interventions (President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan and the AKP Government) on the independence of the judiciary and the rule of law in the Republic of Turkey. Following the 2013 corruption scandal and the failed coup attempt on July 15, 2016, this crisis was analyzed by Markus Zimmer (Executive Editor of the IJCA, October 2016) in the *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA), Vol. 8 No. 1 \[1–17\].

## Summary

Published by Markus Zimmer in the IJCA (October 2016), this policy framework examines the profound crisis afflicting judicial independence and the rule of law in Turkey \[1–5\]. Following the failed military coup on July 15, 2016, President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan and the AKP government imposed a three-month State of Emergency, exempting new executive decrees from judicial review and suspending Turkey's compliance with the European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR). Within a week, the government launched a mass purge of 60,000 to 80,000 public officials, including the suspension of 2,745 judges and court staff (with at least 1,684 judges and prosecutors placed in immediate pretrial detention) \[8–11\]. The scale of this judicial purge equates to eliminating three times the entire body of Article III federal judges in the United States. Zimmer strongly critiques the denial of due process, the refusal of access to personal legal counsel, the asset freezing of over 3,000 judges, and the mass release of 38,000 inmates to empty prison capacity for new political detainees \[11–13\].

## Legal Basis

- **Turkish State of Emergency Decrees (July 2016):** Executive regulations authorizing legislation without judicial review, extending pre-charge detention from 4 to 30 days, and freezing the assets of 3,000+ judges and prosecutors.
- **European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR):** The regional human rights convention formally derogated/suspended by the Turkish government under the State of Emergency.
- **Official Gazette Decree (August 17, 2016):** The conditional release policy for 38,000 non-violent prisoners to mitigate the prison capacity crisis.
- **Not found in the available sources:** Other specific formal statutory regulations are not mentioned in the source text \[FACT\].

## Background

The close relationship between the IACA and the Turkish judiciary dates back to the 2009 IACA International Conference in Istanbul. However, the 2013 corruption probe targeting the AKP cabinet triggered executive retaliation, resulting in the reassignment of 120 judges/prosecutors and the addition of 144 judge positions at the Supreme Court of Appeals to secure political control.

## Objectives

1\. Map and critique the executive interventions destroying judicial independence in Turkey. 2. Call for the restoration of due process guarantees, the right to legal counsel, and ECHR obligations for detained judges and prosecutors. 3. Maintain the solidarity and support of the international judicial community (IACA) for Turkey's independent judicial leaders.

## Scope

This policy covers the evaluation of executive-judicial relations, the State of Emergency, and the mass judicial purges in the Republic of Turkey \[1–17\].

## Key Provisions

- **Mass Judicial Purges and Detention:**
  - The suspension of 2,745 judges and court staff, and the pretrial detention of at least 1,684 judges and prosecutors.
- **Elimination of Judicial Review:**
  - The executive's authority to issue decrees immune from judicial oversight during the State of Emergency.
- **Financial Sanctions and Permanent Employment Bans:**
  - Freezing the assets of 3,000+ judges/prosecutors and permanently banning them from employment in the legal and judicial sectors.
- **Mass Inmate Release:**
  - Releasing 38,000 non-violent prisoners to clear prison space for newly arrested political detainees.

## Implementation

- **Enactment of the State of Emergency (July 2016) & Extension (September 2016):** The execution of executive legislation without court oversight and the extension of emergency status up to 12 months.
- **Execution of Purges by the HSYK:** The investigation and purging of 2,740+ judges/prosecutors and the rapid recruitment of 5,000 new judges/prosecutors in August 2016.

## Policy Problems

- **Paralysis of Judicial Independence:** The takeover of high judicial institutions (Yargıtay and Danıştay) and the intimidation of legal aid lawyers.
- **Human Rights Violations and Torture in Detention:** Reports from Amnesty International and HRW regarding the denial of food/medical care and the torture of detainees.

## Reform / Change

- Restore the function of judicial review over all executive decrees and halt the ECHR derogation.
- Reinstate the right to independent legal counsel and cancel automatic asset freezes for judges.

## Relationships with Other Entities

- **International Association for Court Administration (IACA):** The international judicial organization voicing concerns over the Turkish crisis.
- **Higher Council of Judges and Prosecutors (HSYK):** The executor of the mass judicial purges in Turkey.

### 3 Most Important Improvements

1\. **Restoration of the Judicial Review Mechanism Over Executive Decrees:** Permit the Turkish Constitutional Court and high courts to test the validity of all State of Emergency decrees to halt excessive executive power. 2. **Guaranteeing Full Access to Independent Legal Counsel and Halting Intimidation:** Restore due process rights for the 2,745 detained judges and court staff and protect legal aid lawyers from threats of criminalization. 3. **Revocation of Automatic Asset Freezing and Implementing Case-by-Case Evaluations:** Abolish the mass asset freezing sanctions against the 3,000+ judges/prosecutors and replace them with transparent, individualized judicial hearings in accordance with ECHR Article 6 standards.\`\`\`
