# JPW-POLICY-000006

The **Philippine Post-Marcos Restoration of Political Participation and Accountability Framework** (Stable ID: `JPW-POLICY-000006`) is a national constitutional, statutory, and institutional policy framework established following the February 1986 EDSA "People Power" Revolution. Edited by Dr. Raul C. Pangalangan and authored by legal scholars from the University of the Philippines College of Law under IDE Asian Law Series No. 13, the policy outlines the restoration of democratic governance, creation of direct democracy mechanisms, protection of human rights, and institutionalization of public accountability. Note: Named entities *Cecep Mustafa* and *Reihan Abdan Syakur* are *Not found in the available sources*.

## Summary

The framework addresses the systemic autocracy, judicial timidity, and human rights violations inherited from 14 years of martial rule under Ferdinand E. Marcos. Codified under the 1987 Constitution, it introduces four core structural pillars: (1) Re-balanced Governmental Powers, restricting executive emergency authority, strengthening Congress, and granting fiscal autonomy and "grave abuse" review powers to the judiciary; (2) Institutionalized "People Power", enabling direct popular initiative, referendum, recall, and sectoral party-list representation for marginalized groups; (3) Powerful Oversight Bodies, establishing an independent Ombudsman with statutory prosecution powers and a specialized Sandiganbayan anti-graft court; and (4) Human Rights & Social Justice, creating the independent Commission on Human Rights (CHR) and constitutionally embedding economic, social, and cultural rights.

## Legal Basis

Authority and execution derive from constitutional provisions and statutory enactments:

- **Constitutional Organic Law:** 1987 Constitution of the Republic of the Philippines; 1986 Provisional (Freedom) Constitution (Proclamation No. 3).
- **Direct Democracy & Sectoral Acts:** Republic Act No. 6735 (The Initiative and Referendum Act of 1989); Republic Act No. 7941 (Party-List System Act of 1995).
- **Accountability & Local Autonomy Acts:** Republic Act No. 6770 (The Ombudsman Act of 1989); Republic Act No. 6713 (Code of Conduct and Ethical Standards of 1989); Republic Act No. 7080 (Plunder Law of 1991); Republic Act No. 7160 (Local Government Code of 1991).
- **Social Justice Acts:** Republic Act No. 6657 (Comprehensive Agrarian Reform Law of 1988); Republic Act No. 7279 (Urban Development and Housing Act of 1992); Executive Order No. 163 (CHR Charter, 1987).

## Background & Policy Problems

### Background

In February 1986, a peaceful civilian-military uprising along EDSA ousted President Marcos after years of martial law, arbitrary detentions, and state-sanctioned abuses. President Corazon C. Aquino assumed office under a revolutionary mandate, replacing the 1973 charter with the 1986 Freedom Constitution and convening an appointive Constitutional Commission to write a charter that would "juridify" People Power and entrench democratic accountability.

### Policy Problems

- Hyper-Presidential Autocracy & Abuse: Concentration of legislative and executive powers under emergency commander-in-chief provisions.
- Judicial Timidity & Complicity: Repeated invocation of the "political question doctrine" during martial law, failing to protect citizens against arbitrary detention.
- Monopolistic Party System: Congressional elections dominated by wealthy dynastic majorities, excluding marginalized sectors (urban poor, labor, peasants, women, youth, indigenous communities).
- Rampant Graft & Impunity: Absence of an independent, non-political oversight body leaving state plunder unpunished.
- Gross Human Rights Abuses: Widespread executions, torture, and forced disappearances without independent investigative oversight.

## Objectives

1\. Prevent presidential autocracy by imposing a strict 6-year single term limit and limiting martial law declarations to 60 days subject to Congressional revocation and Supreme Court review. 2. Guarantee absolute judicial independence via automatic fiscal releases and vetting of judicial nominees through an appointive Judicial and Bar Council (JBC). 3. Give marginalized sectors (labor, peasants, urban poor, women, youth) 20% reserved representation in the House. 4. Institutionalize direct citizen lawmaking (initiative), statutory approval/rejection (referendum), and removal of local officials (recall). 5. Establish a European-style moral suasion Ombudsman armed with statutory prosecution power to combat graft. 6. Protect fundamental liberties via an independent Commission on Human Rights (CHR) and constitutionalize social justice.

## Scope & Implementation

### Scope

Applies across all branches of the national government, constitutional commissions, independent offices, and local government units in the Philippines.

### Key Provisions

- **Expanded Judicial Review:** Duty of courts to strike down "grave abuse of discretion" by any government agency (Art. VIII Sec. 1).
- **Party-List Proportionality:** Selection of 20% of House members from non-traditional sectoral organizations (R.A. 7941).
- **Ombudsman Jurisdiction:** Broad investigatory, preventive suspension, and prosecutorial authority over all public servants except impeachable officers (R.A. 6770).
- **CHR Mandate:** Independent investigation of civil/political rights violations, jail visitation, and legal aid provision (Art. XIII Sec. 17-18).
- **Local Autonomy & Recall:** Devolution of national administrative powers and creation of Preparatory Recall Assemblies to unseat unfit local leaders (R.A. 7160).

### Implementation

- **Supreme Court Jurisprudence:** Affirmation of local recall mechanics (*Garcia v. COMELEC*); rejection of presidential term limit extension petitions (*Santiago v. COMELEC*); validation of presidential succession post-EDSA 2 (*Estrada v. Arroyo*); and upholding the Plunder Law (*Estrada v. Sandiganbayan*).
- **CHR Monitoring:** National human rights education campaigns and international treaty compliance monitoring.

## Reform

- Enactment of legislative amendments to R.A. 6735 for direct constitutional initiatives.
- Statutory expansion of CHR investigative authority over socio-economic rights.
