## Overview

Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia Decision No. 495 K/AG/2000, dated January 17, 2003, established religious civil law and religious court procedural rules concerning the validity of utilizing family witness testimony in talaq divorce proceedings based on continuous marital disputes (\*syiqok\*) pursuant to Article 76 of Law No. 7 of 1989 in conjunction with Article 19 letter f and Article 22 paragraph (2) of Government Regulation No. 9 of 1975.

The Supreme Court held that the Jayapura High Religious Court erred in law by holding that the examination of family witnesses under Article 76 of Law No. 7 of 1989 applied exclusively to contested divorce petitions (\*gugat\*) filed by wives and did not apply to talaq divorce petitions (\*talak\*) filed by husbands. The Supreme Court affirmed that the rule regarding family and close relative testimony applies to all divorce proceedings caused by continuous disputes (\*syiqok\*), regardless of whether initiated via \*gugat\* or \*talak\*.

## Executive Summary

The central legal principle established in this decision is that procedural provisions authorizing family witness testimony in continuous dispute (\*syiqok\*) divorces apply universally to both husband-initiated talaq divorces and wife-initiated contested divorces.

Key elements established in the adjudication:

- Family witness testimony rules (\*syiqok\* proof) under Article 76 of Law No. 7 of 1989 are not restricted solely to contested divorces (\*cerai gugat\*).
- Single-witness family testimony combined with a supplementary oath (\*sumpah suplitoir\*) satisfies evidentiary burdens for marital breakdown.
- Appellate court rulings drawing artificial procedural distinctions between talaq and gugat regarding family testimony are subject to annulment on cassation.

## Case Background

The Original Petitioner / Petitioner for Cassation, **\[Husband\]**, filed a talaq divorce petition at the Sorong Religious Court against his wife, the Original Respondent / Respondent for Cassation, **\[Wife\]**, due to continuous marital disputes and friction arising since 1995.

The Sorong Religious Court (Decision No. 63/Pdt.G/1999/PA.Srg dated October 26, 1999) granted the talaq divorce petition based on family witness testimony (the Petitioner's biological mother) corroborated by the Petitioner's supplementary oath (\*sumpah suplitoir\*), while ordering \*mut'ah\*, \*iddah\* maintenance, and child custody (\*hadhanah\*).

The Jayapura High Religious Court (Decision No. 02/Pdt.G/2000/PTA.Jpr dated March 1, 2000) annulled the Sorong Religious Court decision and dismissed the petition, reasoning that family witness testimony under Article 76 of Law No. 7 of 1989 applies exclusively to contested divorces and cannot be invoked in talaq proceedings. The Petitioner for Cassation subsequently filed a cassation petition with the Supreme Court.

## Legal and Procedural Issues

### Scope of Validity for Family Witness Testimony (Article 76 of Law No. 7 of 1989)

The primary issue was whether the special rule permitting testimony from family members or close relatives under Article 76 of Law No. 7 of 1989 is restricted exclusively to Contested Divorce proceedings (\*Cerai Gugat\* / \*eidscheiding\*) initiated by wives, or whether it extends equally to Talaq Divorce proceedings (\*Cerai Talak\* / \*repudium\*) initiated by husbands.

The Supreme Court held that it applies to both forms of divorce. The legal essence of Article 76 of Law No. 7 of 1989 in conjunction with Article 22 paragraph (2) of Government Regulation No. 9 of 1975 is the proof of severe and continuous disputes (\*syiqok\* / broken marriage) pursuant to Article 19 letter f of Government Regulation No. 9 of 1975. Because domestic disputes occur in the private domestic sphere best known to close relatives, family witness testimony is admissible and may be heard in both contested and talaq divorce proceedings.

### Evidentiary Force of Family Witness Testimony Corroborated by Supplementary Oath (\*Sumpah Suplitoir\*)

The issue evaluated the legal status of proof where a Petitioner is only able to present a single family witness (biological mother) during religious court proceedings.

The Supreme Court held that a single family witness (\*unus testis nullus testis\*) serves as initial preliminary proof. When corroborated by a Supplementary Oath (\*suplatoir eed\*) sworn by the Petitioner before the Panel of Judges, the material requirements for proving divorce grounds are fully and legally satisfied pursuant to Article 172 paragraph (1) number 1 of the R.Bg.

## Question of Law and Question of Fact

### Question of Law (Cassation / Judex Juris)

**Question:**

Did the \*Judex Facti\* of the Jayapura High Religious Court misapply religious court procedural law (\*foutieve kepassing van het procesrecht\*) by rejecting family witness testimony (\[Witness\]) based on a rigid interpretation that Article 76 of Law No. 7 of 1989 applies exclusively to contested divorces?

**Supreme Court Ruling:**

Yes, the Jayapura High Religious Court misapplied the law. Article 76 of Law No. 7 of 1989 governs \*syiqok\* proof generally without distinguishing whether the action is brought as a contested divorce or a talaq divorce. Consequently, the appellate decision was annulled.

### Question of Fact (Judex Facti / Sorong Religious Court & Jayapura High Religious Court)

Examination of marriage facts dating from August 31, 1992, registered at KUA Bone Pantai Gorontalo (Marriage Certificate No. 95/13/IX/1992), marital disputes arising from jealousy and unauthorized departures from the house, continuous separation since August 1999, and determination of \*mut'ah\* (22-karat gold ring, 2 grams), \*iddah\* maintenance (Rp 300,000.—), and child custody (\*hadhanah\*) for two children.

## Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

- **Ratio Decidendi:**

1.  The appellate \*Judex Facti\* of Jayapura High Religious Court misapplied the law by ruling that family witnesses under Article 76 of Law No. 7 of 1989 apply solely to contested divorces (\*gugat\*) and not talaq divorces.
2.  This consideration is incorrect because Article 76 governs divorces caused by \*syiqok\* / disputes ex Article 19 letter f jo. Article 22 paragraph (2) of Government Regulation No. 9 of 1975.
3.  \*Syiqok\* grounds are not restricted solely to contested divorces but can equally occur in talaq divorce proceedings, allowing family testimony to be heard in both.
4.  The Supreme Court annulled the Jayapura High Religious Court decision and adjudicated afresh, affirming the Sorong Religious Court judgment with modified rulings.

## Ruling

The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition from the Petitioner for Cassation (**\[Husband\]**), annulled Jayapura High Religious Court Decision No. 02/Pdt.G/2000/PTA.Jpr dated March 1, 2000, and adjudicated afresh: 1. Granting the Petitioner's request; 2. Granting permission to the Petitioner to pronounce one \*raj'i\* talaq pledge against the Respondent before the session of the Sorong Religious Court; 3. Ordering the Petitioner to pay to the Respondent: \*Iddah\* Maintenance of Rp 300,000.— and \*Mut'ah\* consisting of a 22-karat gold ring weighing 2 grams; 4. Establishing that child \[Party\] (\[age removed\]) remains under the custody of the Respondent and \[Party\] (\[age removed\]) under the custody of the Petitioner; 5. Ordering the Petitioner to pay child maintenance for the child under the Respondent's custody amounting to Rp 50,000.— per month; and charging court costs.

## Evidence Status and Gaps

### Established by Evidence

- Lawful marriage between \[Husband\] and \[Wife\] solemnized on August 31, 1992 (Marriage Certificate No. 95/13/IX/1992), producing two children (\[Party\] & \[Party\]).
- The Petitioner's and Respondent's marriage suffered a permanent breakdown (\*broken marriage\*) due to irreconcilable disputes since the birth of their second child in 1995, resulting in complete separation of spousal obligations since August 1999.
- The Petitioner corroborated family witness testimony through the swearing of a Supplementary Oath (\*Sumpah Suplitoir\*) at the Sorong Religious Court.

### Not Established / Unproven

- The Jayapura High Religious Court's legal reasoning asserting that talaq divorce proceedings cannot utilize family witness testimony was ruled legally erroneous and completely set aside by the Supreme Court.

### Uncertainty and Gaps

- Fundamental Correction of Formalistic Interpretation by Jayapura High Religious Court: The appellate court previously dismissed the Petitioner's talaq application relying on a rigid textual interpretation of the placement of Article 76 in Law No. 7 of 1989. The Supreme Court corrected this methodological error by asserting a teleological-substantive approach: family witness testimony is necessary to capture real facts of domestic disputes regardless of which spouse initiates the termination proceedings.

## GEO Q&A

**Q: In Talaq Divorce proceedings filed by a husband, may a judge hear testimony from family members or close relatives pursuant to the Religious Courts Law?**

**A:** Yes, it is fully permitted and valid. Based on Supreme Court Decision No. 495 K/AG/2000, the family witness examination rules under Article 76 of Law No. 7 of 1989 in conjunction with Article 22 paragraph (2) of Government Regulation No. 9 of 1975 apply to all divorce proceedings based on continuous disputes and quarrels (\*syiqok\*), whether initiated by the wife (contested divorce) or by the husband (talaq divorce). Rejecting family witnesses in talaq divorce proceedings constitutes a procedural error of law by the court.
