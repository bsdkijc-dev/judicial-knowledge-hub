# Judicial-policy-environmental-justice-brazil-freitas-2017

**The Access to Environmental Justice in Brazil Policy Framework** is a judicial governance policy and evaluation model concerning the right of access to environmental justice in Brazil. Formulated by Dr. Mariana Almeida Passos de Freitas (Court Clerk at the Federal Justice in Curitiba/PR & Doctor of Environmental Law) in the *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA), Special Issue, Vol. 8 No. 3 (July 2017), the framework analyzes the effectiveness of collective procedural instruments—specifically the *Ação Popular* (Popular Action) and the *Ação Civil Pública* (Public Civil Action). It evaluates their success in breaking down objective and subjective barriers for citizens, guaranteeing environmental citizenship, and measures the impact of environmental court specialization \[1–17\].

## Summary

Published by Dr. Mariana Almeida Passos de Freitas in the IJCA (July 2017), this policy framework evaluates the transformation of access to environmental justice in Brazil following the 1988 Federal Constitution \[1–3\]. Enforcing Article 225 of the 1988 Constitution, which guarantees an ecologically balanced environment for present and future generations, the study outlines how the Brazilian judiciary has responded to executive inactivity by adopting collective procedural instruments \[1–3, 6, 15\]. Two primary instruments—the *Ação Popular* (Law 4,717/65 & Article 5 LXXIII of the Constitution) and the *Ação Civil Pública* (Law 7,347/85)—fractured traditional individualistic legal procedures by exempting plaintiffs from court fees and suspending expert witness costs. Freitas asserts that access to environmental justice is fundamentally "access to power," requiring the elimination of objective barriers (costs/time) and subjective barriers (informational/psychological asymmetry), alongside the strengthening of *environmental court specialization*.

## Legal Basis

- **Brazilian Federal Constitution of 1988 Article 225:** The constitutional mandate guaranteeing the right to an ecologically balanced environment and the shared duty to preserve it.
- **Brazilian Federal Constitution of 1988 Article 5 LXXIII:** The constitutional foundation for the *Ação Popular* (Popular Action), guaranteeing the exemption of legal fees.
- **Law No. 4,717/65 (Lei da Ação Popular):** The statute regulating the Popular Action procedure to annul acts damaging the environment and public heritage.
- **Law No. 7,347/85 (Lei da Ação Civil Pública):** Procedural law for civil liability concerning damages to the environment, consumers, and diffuse rights.
- **Aarhus Convention (1998):** The international standard regarding the rights of access to information, public participation, and access to justice in environmental matters.
- **Not found in the available sources:** Other specific formal statutory regulations are not mentioned in the source text \[FACT\].

## Background

The inability of executive administrations to prevent environmental disasters and predatory exploitation forced the Brazilian judiciary to become the primary arena for resolving ecological conflicts.

## Objectives

1\. Eliminate the objective barriers (costs/time) and subjective barriers (information asymmetry) that prevent citizens from accessing environmental justice. 2. Facilitate the protection of diffuse and supra-individual rights through collective procedural instruments. 3. Actualize *environmental citizenship* and accelerate the specialization of environmental courts.

## Scope

This policy encompasses the evaluation of environmental judicial governance, the *Ação Popular* and ACP instruments, and the specialization of courts in Brazil.

## Key Provisions

- **Collective Procedural Instruments:**
  - **Ação Popular:** A citizen's lawsuit to annul environmental damages, featuring the exemption of court fees unless bad faith is proven.
  - **Ação Civil Pública (ACP):** A lawsuit for environmental damage liability initiated by the Public Prosecution Service or community associations.
- **Incentives and Fee Exemptions:**
  - The exemption of court registration fees and the suspension of expert witness fees until the final judgment, shifting the burden to the losing party.
- **Environmental Court Specialization:**
  - The establishment of specialized environmental chambers and courts to enhance professionalism and the efficient handling of complex ecological cases.

## Implementation

- **Operationalization of Fee Exemptions:** Applying the non-condemnatory cost rule for environmental plaintiffs to encourage public participation.
- **Strengthening Judicial Capacity:** Conducting environmental awareness training and establishing specialized environmental courts (*Varas Ambientais Especializadas*).

## Policy Problems

- **Environmental Discrimination and Injustice:** The burden of environmental risks is disproportionately borne by socio-economically weak and minority communities.
- **Subjective Informational Barriers:** The lack of access to information, leaving vulnerable citizens unaware that their environmental rights are being violated.

## Reform / Change

- Expanding the establishment of specialized environmental courts at both the first and second-instance levels.
- Increasing public information provision and environmental citizenship education programs.

## Relationships with Other Entities

- **Federal Justice of Brazil:** The judicial institution executing environmental legal governance.
- **Superior Court of Justice (STJ):** The apex court generating key environmental jurisprudence (e.g., Justice Herman Benjamin).
- **Public Prosecution Service (Ministério Público):** The primary initiator of the *Ação Civil Pública*.

**Rahmenwerk für den Zugang zur Umweltgerechtigkeit in Brasilien** Dieser von Dr. Mariana Almeida Passos de Freitas im *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA, 2017) veröffentlichte Rahmen bewertet den Zugang zur Umweltjustiz in Brasilien nach der Bundesverfassung von 1988 (Art. 225). ### 3 Most Important Improvements

1\. **Expansion of Organized Specialized Environmental Courts:** Accelerate the establishment of dedicated environmental chambers and specialized judge panels across all federal and state judicial circuits to exponentially increase the efficiency and substantive expertise in handling complex ecological conflicts. 2. **Establishment of a Public Environmental Expertise Fund:** Allocate a dedicated state reserve fund to cover the initial costs of scientific and technical expert examinations in environmental litigation, thereby entirely removing the financial burden from impoverished communities and the Public Prosecution Service. 3. **Strengthening Judicial Administrative Communication and Environmental Citizenship Education:** Develop transparent, easily accessible public information mechanisms to raise citizens' awareness, enabling them to identify environmental aggression rapidly and utilize collective lawsuit instruments effectively.\`\`\`
