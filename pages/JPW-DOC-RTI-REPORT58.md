## Policy Analysis

- **Background & Policy Problem**:
  - National follow-up study focused on the desistance process among parolees from Japanese juvenile training schools.
  - Uses non-incarceration following release as the operational indicator for desistance.
  - Designed to provide empirical data to refine institutional correctional education, community-based probationary treatment, and post-probation support frameworks.
- **Research Methodology & Cohort**:
  - Combined a mixed-methods approach: official incarceration records, self-report questionnaires, and in-person interviews.
  - Tracked 806 parolees (727 males and 79 females) released nationally between January and March 2013 over approximately 4 years through late 2016.
- **Official Incarceration Record Findings**:
  - \*\*80.3%\*\* of participants remained free from reincarceration (both in juvenile training schools and adult prisons) over 4 years.
  - \*\*19.7%\*\* experienced reincarceration.
  - Approximately half of those reincarcerated in juvenile training schools were re-detained within the \*\*first year post-release\*\*, highlighting a critical window for intensive supervision.
  - Key risk factors and predictors: sex, age at index offense, offense type, age at first delinquency, and institutional disciplinary/reward history.
  - Stable family relationships and absence of family relational anxiety significantly correlated with non-reincarceration.
- **Self-Report Survey Results (June 2014)**:
  - Conducted ~1 year post-release across three cohorts: 69 desistance group members, 47 reincarcerated group members, and 260 non-delinquent youth.
  - The desistance group exhibited greater stability in employment, schooling, daily routines, family relationships, and ties with non-delinquent peers compared to the reincarcerated group.
  - Psychologically, the desistance group demonstrated higher self-esteem and self-control.
- **In-Person Interview Results**:
  - Three interview waves (Batch 1: 18 desisting youth at ~2 years; Batch 2: 35 reincarcerated youth at ~2 years; Batch 3: 11 desisting youth at ~4 years).
  - Desisting former inmates credited institutional experiences and probation guidance as foundational to their internal growth, expressing appreciation for genuine commitment from treatment providers.
- **Interpretations & Structural Inferences**:
  - Desistance is an active, ongoing process of internal growth sustained by trusting relationships with treatment providers, self-control reinforcement, improved family dynamics, and prosocial role adaptation in workplace settings.
  - The concentration of reincarceration within the first year (50% of reincarcerated cases) indicates that early transition from institutional confinement to community life requires robust employment safeguards and family support networks.
