#### 1. Regulatory Framework and Institutional Isomorphism

- `[SOURCE EVIDENCE]`: The Japanese civil procedure framework relies on statutory codification under the *Code of Civil Procedure* (CCP Art. 161, 162(1), 168, 169, 170, 175, 695) and administrative rules under the *Rule of Civil Procedure* (Art. 79). Under CCP Art. 162(1), the presiding judge holds explicit statutory authority to set strict submission deadlines for briefs and evidence. Under CCP Art. 695, an in-court settlement (*Wakai*) holds the exact same legal effect (*kekuatan hukum yang sama*) as a final, non-appealable court judgment.
- `[THEORETICAL INFERENCE]`: In Institutional Theory terms, this statutory foundation exerts **coercive isomorphism** over litigating parties and attorneys. By embedding strict submission deadlines and giving *Wakai* protocols full executorial force, the regulatory framework prevents tactical litigation delays and fishing expeditions.
- `[ANALYTICAL SYNTHESIS]`: In the Indonesian reform context (*RaPerMA*), court-annexed mediation under PERMA No. 1 of 2016 often suffers from **institutional decoupling**—where mediation is treated as a mere formal checklist item (*formalitas belaka*) before trial. Replicating the Japanese model requires bridging this gap by granting judges explicit statutory authority to lock issues/evidence during pre-trial preparation and giving pre-trial conciliation agreements immediate executorial enforceability without re-litigation.

#### 2. Judicial / Institutional Structure and Organizational Capacity (5M)

Applying the **5M Framework** to Judge Wakazono Rei's operational presentation reveals the organizational mechanics of Japanese pre-trial and settlement administration:

##### Man (Human Capital and Professional Roles)

- `[SOURCE EVIDENCE]`: Preparatory proceedings can be led by an **assigned associate judge (*hakim anggota yang ditunjuk*)**. *Wakai* relies on a collaborative partnership between the judge (who forms judicial conviction and proposes terms) and litigating counsel/advocates (who manage client expectations, review terms, and suggest realistic compromises).
- `[THEORETICAL INFERENCE]`: In Organizational Sociology, delegating pre-trial issue management to assigned associate judges optimizes judicial labor allocation across three-judge panels (*majelis hakim*), preventing senior judges from becoming bogged down in routine document exchanges.

##### Method (Procedural Management)

- `[SOURCE EVIDENCE]`: Issue and evidence management follows a structured 5-step loop: Setting Deadlines → Document/Evidence Submission → Closed Hearing Session → Oral Discussion / Judicial Confirmation → Setting Next Deadline. In complex cases, courts create specialized issue drafts—such as **building defect lists (*daftar cacat bangunan*)** in construction disputes or **two-stage reviews (liability first, damages second)** in Intellectual Property litigation.

##### Material (Case Briefs and Settlement Records)

- `[SOURCE EVIDENCE]`: Parties submit structured briefs (*dokumen tertulis*) containing attacking/defending arguments, relevant facts, responses to opposing claims, and reasons for dispute (CCP Art. 161 / Rule Art. 79). Upon reaching *Wakai*, the settlement deed (*Berita Acara Wakai*) explicitly details payment amounts, schedules, performance terms, waiver of future claims (*pelepasan tuntutan*), and confidentiality clauses (*kerahasiaan*).

##### Money (Trial Economy and Judicial Cost Reduction)

- `[SOURCE EVIDENCE]`: Achieving *Wakai* yields two massive institutional cost benefits for judges:
  1.  **Eliminates written judgment drafting**: Judges are freed from spending extensive time and effort writing formal judicial opinions (*tidak perlu menulis putusan*).
  2.  **Reduces appellate dockets**: Because *Wakai* settlements cannot be appealed to High Courts or the Supreme Court, overall court caseloads drop significantly across all tiers.

##### Machine / Technology (Digital Integration)

- `[SOURCE EVIDENCE]`: Participation in preparatory proceedings and *Wakai* sessions via **telephone or online video conferencing is legally recognized as formal court attendance (*kehadiran*)**.

##### Legal Culture and Social Structure

- `[SOURCE EVIDENCE]`: A defining feature of Japanese civil litigation is the **active commitment of judges as neutral third parties who actively propose settlement terms and drive compromise (*komitmen hakim yang bersifat aktif*)**.
- `[THEORETICAL INFERENCE]`: This reflects a legal culture that values pragmatic conflict resolution and relational harmony over binary, adversarial win-lose adjudication.

##### Street-Level Bureaucracy vs. Judicial Discretion

- `[THEORETICAL INFERENCE]`: Applying Lipsky's *Street-Level Bureaucracy* framework requires distinguishing frontline administrative court clerks from judges:
  - **Frontline Administrative Clerks (*Panitera/Pegawai*)**: Handle administrative filing, verify digital connection parameters for remote video/phone hearings, and record attendance. Their discretion is strictly procedural.
  - **Judges (Hakim)**: Possess **curial discretion** and judicial independence. Judges exercise discretion when deciding whether a case requires formal preparatory proceedings (complex/multi-issue cases) or can proceed directly to trial (simple/uncontested cases). During *Wakai*, judges exercise **negotiation discretion through private caucusing (*pertemuan secara terpisah*)** to hear parties' unvarnished positions, evaluate evidence, and formulate realistic settlement offers as their judicial conviction (*keyakinan hakim*) takes shape.

#### 4. Implementation and Business Experience

- `[SOURCE EVIDENCE]`: The primary institutional benefit of preparatory proceedings and *Wakai* is that they **enhance the quality of judicial reasoning (*meningkatkan kualitas pertimbangan dan keputusan*)**, focus witness examinations strictly on contested issues, accelerate resolution timelines, and deliver flexible, mutually satisfying outcomes without multi-year appellate delays.
- `[THEORETICAL INFERENCE]`: For commercial enterprises, unexpected judicial rulings and protracted appellate delays represent severe business risks. By establishing clear issue narrowing, leveraging digital video conferencing, and enforcing binding settlement deeds (*Berita Acara Wakai*), the Japanese civil procedure system delivers high regulatory predictability, low administrative burden, and high contract enforcement certainty.

### C. Synthesis of Evidence vs. Epistemological Uncertainty

#### 1. What Is Firmly Supported by Source Evidence

- Preparatory proceedings (*Proses Pra-Sidang*) are designed to manage issues and evidence, governed by CCP Art. 161, 162(1), 168, and Rule Art. 79.
- Preparatory proceedings are conducted in closed chambers, prohibit witness examination, and can be led by an assigned associate judge.
- Phone and online video conferencing are legally equivalent to physical attendance.
- Complex cases utilize specialized tools, such as building defect lists in construction suits or two-stage reviews in IP litigation.
- *Wakai* (CCP Art. 695) carries the exact same executorial force as a final court judgment.
- *Wakai* is driven by active judicial term proposals, private caucusing, and the formation of judicial conviction.
- *Wakai* frees judges from writing formal written opinions and eliminates appellate caseloads.

- **Specific Indonesian Judicial Empirical Metrics**: Source *D2.PM_Proses Pra-Sidang dan Wakai (Prof. Rei).pdf* provides the Japanese judicial framework and explains the objectives of the JPAIS KCCP program, but does **not** contain specific quantitative empirical disposition statistics for Indonesian District Courts (*Pengadilan Negeri*).
- **Non-Judicial Administrative Licensing**: The source focuses exclusively on civil procedure, pre-trial management, and in-court settlement; it contains no data on non-court business licensing software (e.g., OSS RBA) or regulatory agencies in Indonesia.

### D. Policy Recommendations for Indonesian Civil Procedure Reform (RaPerMA)

Based on Judge Wakazono Rei's presentation on Japanese civil practice:

1.  **Institutionalize Pre-Trial Issue Locking**: Amend Supreme Court Regulations (*RaPerMA*) to grant judges explicit statutory authority to set strict submission deadlines and lock issues/evidence prior to open trial hearings.
2.  **Authorize Delegated Associate Judges**: Permit assigned associate judges (*hakim anggota*) to conduct pre-trial issue management sessions to optimize judicial workload across panels.
3.  **Formalize Judicial Caucusing in Court Conciliation**: Authorize judges conducting court-annexed conciliation to hold private caucuses (*pertemuan terpisah*) with individual parties to explore realistic compromise terms.
4.  **Legitimize Remote Digital Attendance**: Codify phone and online video conference participation as legally valid court attendance in all pre-trial and conciliation proceedings.
5.  **Grant Immediate Executorial Force to Conciliation Deeds**: Ensure that settlement agreements reached during court-annexed conciliation automatically possess full executorial force equivalent to a final judgment without requiring secondary enforcement lawsuits.
