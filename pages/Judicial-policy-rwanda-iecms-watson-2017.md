# Judicial-policy-rwanda-iecms-watson-2017

**The Rwanda Integrated Electronic Case Management System (IECMS) Policy Framework** is a comprehensive e-justice digitalization and cross-agency case management policy framework developed and implemented by the Ministry of Justice of Rwanda (MoJ) in partnership with Synergy International Systems, Inc. between 2015 and 2017. Documented by Adam Watson, Regis Rukundakuvuga, and Khachatur Matevosyan in the *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA), Special Issue, Vol. 8 No. 3 (July 2017), the system fully integrates the data and case file workflows across the five primary institutions of the justice sector (National Police, Prosecution, Judiciary, Correctional Services, and Civil Litigation Service). Utilizing a Sector Wide Approach (SWAp), the framework was designed to eradicate case backlogs, increase transparency, accelerate contract enforcement, and ensure automated compliance with civil and criminal procedural law \[1–17, 20–22\].

## Summary

Published by Adam Watson, Regis Rukundakuvuga, and Khachatur Matevosyan in the IJCA (July 2017), this policy framework evaluates the successful digital transformation of Rwanda's justice sector through the implementation of the *Integrated Electronic Case Management System* (IECMS) \[1–3\]. Addressing the legacy of case delays, massive paper backlogs, and transparency vulnerabilities post-1994 genocide, the Ministry of Justice spearheaded the development of the IECMS based on a Sector Wide Approach (SWAp) \[2, 4–6, 18\]. The system seamlessly integrates the processing of criminal and civil cases across five institutions: the Police, Prosecution (NPPA), Judiciary, Correctional Services (RCS), and Civil Litigation \[4, 10–11\]. Engineered through a rigorous Business Process Reengineering (BPR) phase that produced a 500-page System Design Document, the IECMS was operationalized using a unique strategic triad: *Developing from the Beginning* (from Police to Corrections), *Deploying from the End* (deploying the courts first to "pull" data), and *Focusing Forward* (only processing new cases to prevent overwhelming staff with legacy paper files) \[8–9, 20–22\]. The IECMS notably catapulted Rwanda's ranking in the World Bank's *Doing Business* Enforcing Contracts index by 22 positions, earning Rwanda the highest Quality of Judicial Processes score in Sub-Saharan Africa.

## Background

Fragmented, paper-based justice systems fueled severe case backlogs, execution delays, and corruption risks, prompting the Rwandan government to position ICT as the central enabler of national justice sector development.

## Objectives

1\. Provide a single, integrated digital point of entry for managing cases from the point of arrest through to sentence execution. 2. Enhance transparency, accountability, equality before the law, and automatic compliance with procedural law. 3. Equip the sector with Business Intelligence and M&E tools (based on international CourTools standards) to measure clearance rates and caseloads per judge accurately.

## Scope

This policy encompasses the integration of IT systems across the 5 primary justice sector institutions throughout the entire judicial territory of the Republic of Rwanda.

## Key Provisions

- **Integration of 5 Justice Sector Institutions:**
  - Automated data exchange between the Police, Public Prosecution, Courts, Correctional Services, and Civil Litigation via single case IDs and QR codes.
- **Specialized Deployment Strategy:**
  - **Developing from the Beginning:** Software development progressing chronologically from the Police to Corrections.
  - **Deploying from the End:** Launching the court application first to trigger a "data pull" necessity from Prosecution and Police.
  - **Focusing Forward:** Processing only new cases electronically to free staff from the burden of digitizing old historical files.
- **Community-Based eJustice Infrastructure:**
  - Recruiting affordable youth facilitators and establishing e-services kiosks in cybercafés and "smart villages" to assist citizens lacking internet access.

## Implementation

- **BPR and System Design Document Formulation (2015):** The creation of a 500-page operational prototype by Synergy International Systems and the NCSC.
- **ToT Execution and Phased Rollout (2016–2017):** Training 40 technical trainers, launching a 15-court pilot in Kigali (Jan 2016), culminating in a nationwide rollout across all courts (June 2017).

## Policy Problems

- **Limited Citizen Internet Access:** Only approximately 20% of the population has access to reliable internet, hindering direct public digital engagement.
- **Institutional Resistance and Work Culture Change:** Resistance to technological adaptation from senior judges and court staff accustomed to paper-based workflows.

## Reform / Change

- Amending procedural law to formalize electronic summons and eliminate obsolete physical/manual procedural requirements.
- Interfacing the IECMS with external agencies such as Court Bailiffs, the National Identification Agency (NIDA), the Rwanda Development Board (RDB), and the Law Reform Commission.
