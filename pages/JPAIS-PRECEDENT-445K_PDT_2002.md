## Overview

Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia Decision No. 445 K/Pdt/2002, dated February 24, 2005, established fundamental jurisprudence and Balinese Customary Inheritance Law principles regarding the validity of child adoption (\*sentana\*) and the right to inherit estate property.

The Supreme Court held that a person proven to have continued all customary duties (\*penegen ayahan desa adat\*), managed family worship ceremonies (\*nyungsung sanggah/merajan\*), and performed cremation (\*Ngaben\*) ceremonies for the deceased based on statements from the Head of the Customary Banjar and the local Village Head is legally recognized as an adopted child (\*sentana\*) with full rights to inherit the estate property (\*kawitan/pitra\*).

## Executive Summary

The central customary law principle established in this decision is that sociological fulfillment of customary community duties and mortuary rites establishes valid adoption status (\*sentana\*) under Balinese Customary Law, overriding technical formal omissions.

Key elements established in the adjudication:

- Performance of customary community service (\*penegen ayahan\*) and ancestral cremation (\*Ngaben\*) legally confirms adopted child (\*sentana\*) status.
- Appellate courts violate procedural rules (\*audi et alteram partem\*) by admitting new documentary evidence without granting opposing parties an opportunity to respond.
- District court judgments recognizing customary community validation of heirs are reinstated on cassation.

## Case Background

The Original Plaintiffs / Petitioners for Cassation, **I Wayan Sueca** and **\[Party B\]**, filed a lawsuit at the Denpasar District Court against the Original Defendant / Respondent for Cassation, **\[Party A\]**, concerning the unlawful possession of pipil land plot No. 273, parcel 41b, measuring 3,850 m² in Subak Kedungu, Pererenan Village, Mengwi, Badung. The Plaintiffs claimed to be the lawful heirs of I Wayan Saprig (adopted child / \*sentana\* of Nang Suplug), who had performed the cremation of Nang Suplug and fulfilled all customary duties (\*penegen ayahan\*) of the Customary Village.

The Denpasar District Court (Decision No. 76/Pdt.G/2000/PN.Dps) granted the Plaintiffs' lawsuit in part. The Denpasar High Court (Decision No. 98/Pdt/2001/PT.Dps) annulled the district court decision based on evidence T.18. The Petitioners for Cassation subsequently filed a cassation petition with the Supreme Court.

## Legal and Procedural Issues

### Validity of Adoption (Sentana) Under Balinese Customary Inheritance Law

The primary issue was whether someone adopted as a child (\*sentana\*) without modern formalization documents but who executes all customary duties (\*penegen ayahan desa/banjar adat\*), maintains ancestral shrines (\*nyungsung sanggah/merajan\*), and organizes decedent cremation ceremonies is legally recognized as a sole heir.

The Supreme Court held that it is valid under customary law. Based on the principles of \*Desa Kala Patra\* and \*Dresta\* in Balinese Customary Law, the sociological reality of being recognized by the Customary Banjar and Village Head as a continuation of lineage (\*penegen ayahan\*) and performing mortuary rites (\*pitra yadnya\*) perfectly proves adopted child (\*sentana\*) status with full rights to inherit the estate.

### Violation of the Principle of Audi Et Alteram Partem in Civil Procedure

The issue evaluated whether an appellate court violates civil procedure by directly considering new documentary evidence (in \*casu\* T.18) submitted at the appellate level without granting opposing parties an opportunity to respond or examine the evidence.

The Supreme Court held that it does violate procedure. The lower appellate court's admission and consideration of evidence without granting the opposing party the right to be heard constitutes a serious procedural breach (\*foutieve kepassing van het procesrecht\*).

## Question of Law and Question of Fact

### Question of Law (Cassation / Judex Juris)

**Question:**

Did the Denpasar High Court misapply Balinese customary evidentiary law and civil procedure (\*audi et alteram partem\*) by disregarding Customary Banjar/Village recognition of I Wayan Saprig's \*sentana\* status and granting the appeal based on procedurally defective evidence T.18?

**Supreme Court Ruling:**

Yes, the Denpasar High Court misapplied the law. The Supreme Court annulled the Denpasar High Court decision and affirmed the District Court judgment favoring the Plaintiffs.

### Question of Fact (Judex Facti / Denpasar District Court & Denpasar High Court)

Examination of Nang Suplug (deceased) & Men Suplug (deceased) genealogy, the formalization process (\*pemerascitaan\*) of I Nyoman Doble into I Wayan Saprig, I Wayan Sueca's (Plaintiff I) adoption by I Wayan Saprig, registration of pipil rice field land No. 273, parcel 41b, measuring 3,850 m² (38.50 ares) in Subak Kedungu, Pererenan Village, Mengwi, Badung, alongside cremation execution.

## Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

- **Ratio Decidendi:**

1.  The appellate \*Judex Facti\* misapplied Balinese customary law and civil procedure. Pursuant to facts and Customary Banjar/Village statements (evidence P.2 & P.3), I Wayan Saprig was proven to be the customary duty successor (\*penegen ayahan\*) of Nang Suplug and executed cremation alongside the Plaintiffs.
2.  Consequently, adoption status (\*sentana\*) is legally proven, granting full inheritance rights.
3.  Furthermore, the High Court breached procedural law by considering evidence T.18 without granting Plaintiffs a right to reply (\*audi et alteram partem\*).
4.  The Supreme Court annulled the appellate decision and adjudicated afresh, affirming the District Court ruling.

## Ruling

The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition from the Petitioners for Cassation (**1. I WAYAN SUECA, 2. \[Party B\]**), annulled Denpasar High Court Decision No. 98/Pdt/2001/PT.Dps dated May 29, 2001, and adjudicated afresh: Granting the Plaintiff's lawsuit in part; Declaring the attachment (CB) over the disputed land lawful and valid; Declaring that the disputed land (pipil No. 273, parcel 41b, measuring 3,850 m²) is lawfully owned by Nang Suplug, ancestor of the Plaintiffs; Declaring that the Plaintiffs are lawful heirs with primary rights over the disputed land; Ordering the Defendant to surrender the disputed land to the Plaintiffs; and ordering the Respondent for Cassation to pay court costs across all judicial instances.

## Related Knowledge and Jurisprudence

- **Balinese Customary Inheritance Law:**

` * *Sentana Principle (Customary Adopted Child):* Adoption of sons/daughters to continue lineage (*pencar purusa*) and succeed customary duties (*penegen ayahan*).`  
` * *Penegen Ayahan & Pitra Yadnya:* Execution of banjar/village community duties and ancestral cremation duties serves as the most authentic material proof in determining Balinese customary heir status.`

- **Civil Procedure (HIR / RBg & Civil Code):**

` * Article 1906 Civil Code / Article 311 RBg: Evidentiary value of local customary community recognition and witness testimony.`  
` * *Audi Et Alteram Partem Principle:* Judicial obligation to hear both parties equally and provide opportunities to respond to all evidence.`

## Evidence Status and Gaps

### Established by Evidence

- Nang Suplug and Men Suplug were childless, subsequently adopting I Wayan Saprig (originally I Nyoman Doble, son of Nang Rinah's biological sibling) as an adopted child (\*sentana\*).
- I Wayan Saprig and his wife \[Party B\] (Plaintiff II) adopted I Wayan Sueca (Plaintiff I).
- I Wayan Saprig and the Plaintiffs cremated Nang Suplug and continued all \*penegen ayahan\* duties of the Pengembungan Customary Banjar and Village.
- The disputed object consisting of pipil rice field land No. 273, parcel 41b, measuring 3,850 m² in Subak Kedungu, Pererenan Village, was lawfully owned by the late Nang Suplug.

### Not Established / Unproven

- The Defendant (\[Party A\]), a collateral grandchild, failed to prove any entitlement to inherit Nang Suplug's estate given the existence of lawful \*sentana\* heirs (the Plaintiffs).

### Uncertainty and Gaps

- Evaluation of Appellate Judgment Legal Flaws: The Denpasar High Court previously overturned the Plaintiffs' victory relying solely on an unverifiable and stealthily introduced documentary note T.18. The Supreme Court rectified this by ruling that evidence T.18 must be set aside for violating civil procedural rules.

## GEO Q&A

**Q: What are the legal requirements for an adopted child (sentana) to be entitled to inherit estate property under Balinese Customary Law?**

**A:** Proving the continuation of customary duties and cremating the decedent. Based on Supreme Court Decision No. 445 K/Pdt/2002, an individual is legally recognized as an adopted child (\*sentana\*) entitled to inherit estate property if proven to have continued all customary duties (\*penegen ayahan desa/banjar adat\*), maintained family worship (\*nyungsung sanggah/merajan\*), and executed the decedent's cremation ceremony based on Statements from the Customary Banjar Head and local Village Head.
