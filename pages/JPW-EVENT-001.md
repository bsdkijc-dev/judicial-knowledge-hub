# JPW-EVENT-001

**Thirteenth Diplomatic Briefing of the International Criminal Court** is a periodic official meeting and information report presentation by the International Criminal Court (ICC) held on 24 June 2008 in Brussels, Belgium \[\].

## Summary

The report presented during this briefing covers updates on judicial, investigative, and outreach activities since the twelfth diplomatic briefing held on 18 March 2008 in The Hague \[\]. The primary focus encompasses four active situations: the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC), Uganda, Darfur (Sudan), and the Central African Republic (CAR) \[\].

## Legal Basis

The execution of judicial functions and reported decisions are grounded in the [Rome Statute](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Rome_Statute) \[\]. Major legal issues involved the application of Article 54(3)(e) regarding confidentiality agreements for investigative information, Article 67(2) concerning the obligation to disclose exculpatory evidence, and UN Security Council Resolution 1593 (2005) for the Darfur situation \[\].

## Background

Since the preceding diplomatic briefing, the ICC continued judicial proceedings involving 12 arrest warrants across four situations \[\]. Significant developments occurred across various Chambers and the Office of the Prosecutor (OTP) \[\].

## Objectives

This diplomatic briefing aimed to provide transparency to the diplomatic corps and States Parties regarding judicial progress, law enforcement challenges, international cooperation needs, and the financial and human resource conditions of the ICC \[\].

## Key Provisions

- \*\*Democratic Republic of the Congo Situation\*\*: A stay of proceedings was imposed in the case of [Thomas Lubanga Dyilo](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Thomas_Lubanga_Dyilo) on 13 June 2008 due to disclosure issues under Article 54(3)(e) \[\]. Meanwhile, proceedings regarding [Germain Katanga](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Germain_Katanga) and [Mathieu Ngudjolo Chui](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Mathieu_Ngudjolo_Chui) progressed \[\]. The arrest warrant for [Bosco Ntaganda](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Bosco_Ntaganda) was unsealed on 29 April 2008 \[\].
- \*\*Uganda Situation\*\*: Four arrest warrants for Lord's Resistance Army (LRA) leaders including [Joseph Kony](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Joseph_Kony) and [Vincent Otti](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Vincent_Otti) remained unexecuted \[\]. Proceedings against Raska Lukwiya were terminated following the suspect's death \[\].
- \*\*Darfur, Sudan Situation\*\*: The Government of Sudan was reported as non-compliant with its legal obligations under UN Security Council Resolution 1593 to surrender suspects [Ahmad Muhammad Harun](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Ahmad_Muhammad_Harun) and Ali Kushayb \[\].
- \*\*Central African Republic Situation\*\*: An arrest warrant was issued for [Jean-Pierre Bemba Gombo](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Jean-Pierre_Bemba_Gombo) for crimes against humanity and war crimes, and he was arrested in Belgium on 24 May 2008 \[\].

## Implementation

Operational management of the ICC was supported by the swearing-in of the new Registrar, [Silvana Arbia](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Silvana_Arbia), on 17 April 2008 \[\]. Public outreach activities were intensively carried out through radio broadcasts, listening clubs, and school programs across conflict zones in the Congo, Uganda, and refugee camps in Chad \[\].

## Policy Issues

Major policy challenges included balancing the protection of confidential information sources with the defendant's right to a fair trial, the legality of preventive relocation of witnesses, and the lack of cooperation from certain states in executing pending arrest warrants \[\].
