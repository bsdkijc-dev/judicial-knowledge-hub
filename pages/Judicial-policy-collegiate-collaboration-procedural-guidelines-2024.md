# Judicial-policy-collegiate-collaboration-procedural-guidelines-2024

The **Collegiate Collaboration and Procedural Guidelines Framework** (also known as the *Relying on the Courts Framework*) is a judicial management policy framework that proposes the strengthening of horizontal collegiate collaboration and the creation of procedural guidelines (*procesreglementen*) by judges. It acts as a counterbalance to strict professional hierarchies to protect internal judicial independence, increase efficiency, ensure timeliness, and maintain consistency in judicial decisions \[1, 2, 5, 11, 97–99\].

## Overview

Issued by Prof. Dr. Philip Langbroek in the *International Journal for Court Administration* (2024), this framework analyzes the negative impact of rigid judicial hierarchies that suppress the professional autonomy of judges. Through comparative studies in the Netherlands, Ukraine, and Greece, the framework demonstrates that horizontal collaboration—specifically through the drafting of procedural guidelines—can limit the use of trial postponement tactics by lawyers, strengthen internal independence, and boost public trust.

## Legal Basis

- **Article 6 of the European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR):** Guarantees the right to a fair, predictable, and timely trial.
- **Dutch Supreme Court Ruling (Hoge Raad, June 3, 2022, ECLI:NL:HR:2022:824):** Acknowledges the legal legitimacy of procedural guidelines (*procesreglementen*) established by judicial panels.
- **Opinion of Advocate-General Ruth de Bock (ECLI:NL:PHR:2021:1228):** Provides a comprehensive analysis regarding the legal nature and boundaries of judicial procedural guidelines.
- **Dutch Judiciary Organization Act 2001 (*Wet op de rechterlijke organisatie 2001*):** Statutory foundation for court organization in the Netherlands.
- **Article 90 of the Greek Constitution:** Governs the appointment of judicial leadership and the inspection system by the *Areios Pagos* (Supreme Court).

## Background and Policy Problems

The reliable administration of justice requires a balance between organizational hierarchy and social cohesion (horizontal collaboration). Without horizontal cohesion, judicial hierarchies are often exploited to control judges' behavior through the threat of disciplinary sanctions and career promotion control, which ultimately undermines internal judicial independence.

In strictly hierarchical judicial systems lacking collegiate collaboration, judges work in isolation and are vulnerable to political intervention and fear of sanctions. Lawyers and prosecutors exploit this systemic weakness to manipulate procedures via repeated postponement requests and unjustified judicial recusals. Consequently, disposition times become unpredictable and severely delayed. Furthermore, under the traditional separation of powers doctrine, judges are often perceived as lacking rule-making authority. Without explicit legislative delegation, procedural guidelines drafted by judges are vulnerable to challenges of overstepping authority.

## Implementation and Comparative Analysis

The management policy was evaluated across three European jurisdictions:

- **The Netherlands:** Operating within a high-trust society, procedural guidelines (*procesreglementen*) are widely utilized across civil, criminal, and immigration courts. This practice is supported by the *Raad voor de Rechtspraak* (Council for the Judiciary) and affirmed by Hoge Raad jurisprudence. Internal cohesion has maintained public trust in judges at approximately 70%.
- **Ukraine (Odesa & Kyiv Oblast):** The MATRA/CILC project (2019–2023) successfully facilitated local judges in drafting procedural guidelines alongside bar associations and local NGOs (Second of May Group). This was backed by the High Council of Justice to protect judges from disciplinary sanctions. The guidelines successfully restricted unjustified recusals and tightened postponement requirements (e.g., mandating substitute counsel if a lawyer is unavailable) \[49–51\].
- **Greece:** Attempts to draft procedural guidelines in Piraeus and Athens (2019–2022) were actively obstructed and sabotaged by the leadership of the *Areios Pagos* (Maria Georgiou) and judges' associations. This resistance, driven by a rigid hierarchical inspection system, led to extreme case backlogs (disposition times reaching 559–610 days), negative regulatory inflation (*polynomia* and *kakanomy*), and unilateral dismissals of judges by the Supreme Court leadership.

## Key Provisions and Proposed Reforms

- **Judges as Procedural Rule-Makers:** Judicial panels directly draft trial procedure agreements, setting document submission deadlines, speaking durations, and strict requirements for trial postponements.
- **Limiting Trial Postponements:** Mandating that absent lawyers appoint substitute counsel, except in proven emergencies.
- **Autonomy in Concrete Cases:** Judges retain the authority to deviate from procedural guidelines in specific cases if necessary in the interest of justice.
- **Multidisciplinary Organization Test:** Proposed paradigm shift from traditional rule-of-law tests to evaluating the judicial organization's characteristics based on its level of collegiate collaboration.
- **Reform of Disciplinary Inspections:** Explicit protection for internal judicial independence, urging the separation of disciplinary inspection functions from Supreme Court leadership to align with European Court of Human Rights (ECtHR) jurisprudence.
