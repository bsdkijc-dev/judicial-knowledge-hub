# Judicial-policy-laws-evolution-progress-2023

mediawiki

**Law’s Evolution and a ‘Claim to Progress’ Policy & Epistemological Framework** is a scholarly legal policy framework and normative legal philosophy explaining that the evolution of law is not driven by Darwinian cosmology, functionalist social systems, or teleological historical destiny, but by an intrinsic, performatively founded "claim to progress" (*Anspruch auf Fortschritt*) embedded within every legal decision and constitutive act.

## Overview

Formulated by [Massimo La Torre](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Massimo_La_Torre), Full Professor of Legal Philosophy at [Magna Græcia University of Catanzaro](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Magna_Gr%C3%A6cia_University_of_Catanzaro), this framework revisits the theory of legal evolution from an internal normative and performative perspective. Building upon the "claim to correctness" (*Anspruch auf Richtigkeit*) developed by [Robert Alexy](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Robert_Alexy) and [Jürgen Habermas](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/J%C3%BCrgen_Habermas), La Torre demonstrates that every legal decision carries a pragmatic performative claim that the legal situation resulting from the decision is "better" (representing normative progress) than the situation prior to its rendering.

## Statutory and Legal Basis

This framework evaluates and synthesizes several foundational theoretical bases:

- **[Performative Theory of Speech Acts](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Performative_Theory_of_Speech_Acts) (J.L. Austin)**: The performative felicity conditions requiring that a legal act normatively changes the world for the better.
- **[Claim to Correctness](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Claim_to_Correctness) (Robert Alexy & Jürgen Habermas)**: The constitutive requirement of legal correctness expanded into an explicit claim to normative progress.
- **[Inner Morality of Law](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Inner_Morality_of_Law) (Lon L. Fuller)**: The prerequisite that legal rules must be prospective (oriented toward the future) and provide stable expectations.
- **[Dynamic System Theory](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Dynamic_System_Theory) (Hans Kelsen & H.L.A. Hart)**: The concept that positive law evolves through dynamic normative leaps and secondary rules of change.
- **[Radbruch Formula](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Radbruch_Formula) (Gustav Radbruch)**: The philosophy of legal history connecting positive legal certainty with the post-1945 demands of moral justice.

## Historical Background

Many legal sociologies (such as [Niklas Luhmann's](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Niklas_Luhmann) autopoietic systems theory or F.A. Hayek's spontaneous market order) explain legal evolution as a functional adaptation mechanism devoid of normative value. La Torre rejects these functionalist paradigms and establishes a sharp distinction between law and morality: morality possesses universal and timeless validity (an act that is moral or immoral remains so across ages), whereas law is strictly bound to temporal and spatial coordinates (*tempus regit actum*). Law represents a "step forward" from a state of lawlessness (exemplified historically by the transition from the private house/*domus* to the public sphere/*agora*).

## Objectives

- Disentangle legal evolutionary theory from functionalist reductionism, historical determinism, or Darwinian evolutionary analogies.
- Establish that participants in a legal system (judges, legal scholars, citizens) operate from an internal point of view (*internal point of view*) that inherently demands justice and progress.
- Demonstrate that constitutional creations and judicial decisions constitutively pursue normative betterment compared to past states of affairs.

## Scope

The scope of this framework covers legal philosophy, judicial hermeneutics, constitutional theory, the relation between law and morality, and comparative European legal history.

## Key Provisions & Theoretical Models

1.  **Differentiation Between Law and Morality**: Morality has no "history" in the normative sense because moral principles are timeless, whereas law relies on promulgation, duration, prospective effect, and abrogation.
2.  **Radbruchian Paradigm (Omnipotence vs. Impotence of Legal Form)**: The tension between rationalist doctrines (where form dominates substance) and Savigny's historical doctrine (where law is a product of historical contingency), mediated through reflexive legal awareness.
3.  **Kant’s Four Involutionary Societal Models**: An evaluation of [Immanuel Kant's](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Immanuel_Kant) four regressive models of society—the *caravanserai* (transit traffic without community), the *Zuchthaus* (correctional facility/pure sanction), the *Tollhaus* (mental asylum/pure repression without reason), and the *cloaca* (corrupt dump/sewer)—proving that without the idea of progress, law loses all structural meaning.

## Implementation

The framework is operationalized through a hermeneutic reconstruction of judicial practice (aligned with [Ronald Dworkin's](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Ronald_Dworkin) *Law as Integrity*), wherein every resolution of a legal conflict functions as a new chapter in a "chain novel," claiming to offer the best possible interpretation of justice.

## Policy Challenges

- **Reductive Rigid Positivism**: Strict legal positivism restricting law to sovereign command (*auctoritas, non veritas facit legem*) risks alienating citizens and reducing law to mere administrative directives.
- **Tension Between Certainty and Progress**: Balancing the necessity for legal predictability (*res judicata* and stability) with the dynamic drive toward legal refinement and moral improvement.

## Proposed Reforms

Judicial practice and legislation must explicitly embrace the performative "claim to progress," ensuring that legal reasoning avoids mechanical, formalist rule-application and instead continuously strives toward moral betterment and social justice.

1.  5\. Policy Analysis Summary

- \*\*Background\*\*: Modern legal theory often treats law's evolution as an autopoietic, evolutionary, or market-driven process. La Torre argues that these functionalist models strip law of its normativity, turning it into raw administrative power.
- \*\*Policy Problem\*\*: Relying solely on positivism or functionalism risks detaching legal systems from moral validity, making them susceptible to authoritarian misuse where authority alone dictates validity (\*auctoritas, non veritas facit legem\*).
- \*\*Legal/Policy Gap\*\*: Positivism lacks an internal mechanism to explain how legal systems maintain legitimacy and generate normative improvement without relying on external metaphysical tenets.
- \*\*Objectives\*\*: Ground legal evolution in an internal performative "claim to progress" (\*Anspruch auf Fortschritt\*), ensuring every legal act aims at a normatively better state.
- \*\*Implementation\*\*: Analyzes legal adjudication through speech act theory, Alexy's claim to correctness, Radbruch's philosophy of legal history, and Kant's four regressive societal models.
- \*\*Evidence\*\*: Demonstrates that declaring a constitution or judicial decision intentionally "unjust" or "retrogressive" creates a performative self-contradiction.
- \*\*Impact\*\*: Re-establishes the internal point of view, proving that legal interpretation must strive for justice and moral betterment over mechanical rule-following.
