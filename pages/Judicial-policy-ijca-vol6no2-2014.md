# 'International Journal for Court Administration, Vol. 6, No. 2' (IACA, 2014)

### Wiki Entity Page

**International Journal for Court Administration (Vol. 6, No. 2, Source File: 17-82-PB.pdf)** is the 13th official peer-reviewed electronic periodical publication of the *International Association for Court Administration* (IACA) published on the official IACA website (www.iaca.ws). This issue features 2 professional articles, 5 comparative academic research papers, 3 book reviews, and an executive editorial regarding transitional justice and the Rwandan *Gacaca* community courts.

## Summary

This issue is led by Markus Zimmer (Executive Editor), Prof. Dr. Philip Langbroek (Managing Editor), Linda Wade-Bahr (Technical Editor), along with Prof. Dr. Andreas Lienhard, Dr. Luis María Palma, Dr. Anne Wallace, and Leisha Lister (Journal Editors). The publication presents empirical and practical analyses on the partnership between judges and court administrators, the institutional independence of the UK Supreme Court (UKSC), judicial performance assessment in Switzerland, European cross-border e-justice infrastructure (e-CODEX), judicial productivity modeling in Poland, dimensions of inter-judge knowledge sharing in the Netherlands, SERVQUAL service quality assessment in Costa Rica, and the *Gacaca* transitional courts in Rwanda.

## Background & Key Article Summaries

### 1. Executive Editorial: Transitional Justice & Rwandan Gacaca Courts (Markus Zimmer)

- Reviews the official closure of the *Gacaca* community courts in Rwanda in June 2012 after operating for 10 years (2002–2012) to try tens of thousands of perpetrators of the 1994 genocide through 9-member panels of local elders.

### 2. Court Administrators and the Judiciary - Partners (Wayne Martin AC)

- Analyzes the transition from the executive model to the judicial council model, emphasizing that the boundary between "judging" and "administering" is inseparable, thus requiring an equal partnership.

### 3. UK Supreme Court (UKSC) Case Study (William Arnold)

- Discusses the institutional independence of the UKSC based on the CRA 2005 and the *Crime and Courts Act 2013*, where the Chief Executive is appointed directly by the UKSC President and manages a budget independent from the Treasury.

### 4. Judicial Performance Assessment in Switzerland (Prof. Dr. Andreas Lienhard)

- Constitutional analysis of judicial performance assessment in Switzerland, balancing the principle of judicial independence (Articles 30/191c BV) with efficiency (Article 43a BV) and the periodic re-election system.

### 5. e-CODEX Cross-Border E-Justice & EPO Pilot in Greece (George Pangalos et al.)

- Examines the e-CODEX infrastructure (e-Delivery, e-Signature DSS-Tool, e-ID, XML e-Document) and the implementation of the *European Payment Order* pilot at the Athens Court of First Instance.

### 6. 'Hockey-Stick' Judicial Production Function Model (Kamil Jonski & Daniel Mankowski)

- Critiques the *exogenous productivity of judges* hypothesis and proves that the productivity of Polish criminal judges has a strict physical capacity limit (~38 felony cases/year) before facing case backlogs.

### 7. Four Dimensions of Inter-Judge Knowledge Sharing (Sandra Taal et al.)

- Constructs a 4-dimensional research model (technological, managerial, social, motivational) to encourage the conversion of implicit (*tacit*) knowledge into explicit knowledge to reduce unwarranted disparities in judgments.

### 8. SERVQUAL GAP 1 Analysis in Costa Rican Courts (Dr. Rodrigo Murillo)

- Measures the GAP 1 perception gap at *Sala II* of the Costa Rican Court, finding that the public prioritizes time certainty and empathy, while judicial staff tend to hide behind judicial independence to justify delays.

## Policy Problems

1\. Threats to judicial independence due to executive control over budgets and personnel. 2. The risk of quantitative performance assessments interfering with the freedom of judicial reasoning. 3. The lack of technical interoperability in cross-border debt recovery in Europe. 4. The physical time capacity limits of judges ignored by Cobb-Douglas economic models. 5. Disparities in judgments due to the lack of knowledge-sharing spaces. 6. The mismatch between public service expectations and the role perceptions of judicial staff.

## Reform / Change

1\. Adoption of the judicial council governance model and an integrated executive partnership. 2. Structuring judicial performance assessment based on managerial evaluation and peer-review without salary impacts. 3. Implementation of a decentralized e-CODEX infrastructure for automated cross-border e-filing. 4. Utilization of SERVQUAL quality standards and *Norma GICA-Justicia 2010* to restructure judicial workflows.
