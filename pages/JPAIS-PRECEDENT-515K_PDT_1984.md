## Overview

Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia Decision No. 515 K/Pdt/1984, dated August 29, 1985, established a civil law rule concerning the damages interest rate (\*gederfde winst\* / moratory interest) in default of debt repayment agreements that do not stipulate interest in writing.

The Supreme Court held that although the general provision of Article 1250 of the Civil Code regulates a statutory interest rate of 6% per annum for interest-free loans, the Standing Jurisprudence of the Supreme Court establishes that in the event of debtor default/delayed repayment, the Creditor is entitled to claim damages (\*gederfde winst\*) in the form of interest amounting to 2% (two percent) per month—reflecting prevailing and reasonable commercial banking interest rates—calculated from the remaining principal debt starting from the moment of default until the debt is fully repaid.

## Executive Summary

The central legal principle established in this decision is that courts may award commercial-level moratory interest of 2% per month for monetary defaults, superseding the strict statutory limit of 6% per annum under Article 1250 of the Civil Code.

Key elements established in the adjudication:

- Moratory interest for monetary defaults can be set at 2% per month based on established Supreme Court jurisprudence and commercial banking standards.
- Attachment orders (\*conservatoir beslag\* ex Article 227 HIR) require reasonable suspicion of asset concealment and proportionality between asset value and claim amounts.
- Cross-appeals by both creditors and debtors challenging lower court damages awards and attachment rejections are subject to dismissal on cassation.

## Case Background

The Original Plaintiff / Petitioner for Cassation I, **M.G. Sinaga**, filed a lawsuit at the Central Jakarta District Court against the Original Defendant / Petitioner for Cassation II, **\[Party A\]**, concerning delayed repayment of a principal debt amounting to Rp 22,100,000.— based on a Notarial Debt Acknowledgment Deed with Collateral No. 182/1977 dated September 28, 1977. The Defendant promised repayment by December 15, 1977, but defaulted. On March 30, 1982, the Defendant made a partial principal payment of Rp 8,000,000.—, leaving a remaining principal debt of Rp 14,100,000.—.

The Central Jakarta District Court (Decision No. 525/1982 G. dated February 9, 1983) granted the lawsuit in part, awarding 2% monthly interest and granting a protective attachment. The Jakarta High Court (Decision No. 256/1983 P.T. Perdata dated September 24, 1983) affirmed the district court decision except for rejecting the protective attachment request. Both Petitioner for Cassation I (Plaintiff) and Petitioner for Cassation II (Defendant) filed cassation petitions with the Supreme Court.

## Legal and Procedural Issues

### Magnitude of Damages Interest Rate (Gederfde Winst / Moratory) on Loan Defaults

The primary issue was what lawful and reasonable damages interest rate should be imposed on a defaulting debtor when a debt acknowledgment deed fails to stipulate an interest percentage.

The Supreme Court held that although Article 1250 of the Civil Code limits statutory interest to 6% per annum, the Standing Jurisprudence of the Supreme Court establishes a standard moratory damages rate (\*gederfde winst\*) of 2% (two percent) per month. A 2% monthly interest rate reflects deferred profit values aligned with prevailing commercial bank interest rates at the time of the transaction.

### Proportionality and Asset Concealment Requirements in Protective Attachments (Conservatoir Beslag ex Article 227 HIR)

The issue evaluated whether a protective attachment petition over the Defendant's land parcel valued at Rp 200,000,000.— can be granted solely to secure interest damages totaling tens of millions of rupiahs.

The Supreme Court held that it cannot be granted. Pursuant to Article 227 of the HIR, placing a protective attachment requires reasonable suspicion that the Defendant is attempting to dissipate or conceal assets. Where the value of the asset requested for attachment (Rp 200 million) vastly exceeds the damages claim and no asset concealment is proven, the attachment petition must be rejected.

## Question of Law and Question of Fact

### Question of Law (Cassation / Judex Juris)

**Question:**

Does the imposition of a 2% monthly damages interest rate for delayed principal repayment comply with Supreme Court Standing Jurisprudence, and does the rejection of protective attachments on assets whose value vastly exceeds the lawsuit satisfy Article 227 of the HIR?

**Supreme Court Ruling:**

Yes, the 2% monthly interest rate is valid based on standing Supreme Court jurisprudence, and the High Court's rejection of protective attachments was correct under Article 227 of the HIR. Consequently, the cassation petitions of both Petitioner I (Plaintiff) and Petitioner II (Defendant) were rejected.

### Question of Fact (Judex Facti / Central Jakarta District Court & Jakarta High Court)

Examination of Notarial Debt Acknowledgment Deed No. 182/1977 dated September 28, 1977, maturity date (December 15, 1977), execution application date (January 19, 1978), partial principal repayment of Rp 8,000,000.— on March 30, 1982, and valuation estimates for Ownership Land (HM) No. 19/Karet Tengsin measuring 4,914 m² in Central Jakarta.

## Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

- **Ratio Decidendi:**

1.  The appellate and district \*Judex Facti\* did not misapply the law. Although Article 1250 of the Civil Code regulates 6% per annum interest for interest-free loans, pursuant to Supreme Court Jurisprudence (Nos. 1098/K/Sip/1973 & 452/K/Sip/1975), a defaulting debtor may be ordered to pay damages (\*gederfde winst\*) in the form of 2% monthly interest, representing prevailing bank rates at transaction time.
2.  The High Court correctly rejected protective attachments because the property value (Rp 200,000,000.—) far exceeded damages and asset dissipation was unproven (Article 227 HIR).
3.  The Supreme Court rejected cassation appeals from both Petitioners I and II.

## Ruling

The Supreme Court rejected the cassation petitions from Petitioner for Cassation I (**M.G. SINAGA**) and Petitioner for Cassation II (**\[Party A\]**); and ordered both Petitioners to bear cassation court costs equally (half each).

## Evidence Status and Gaps

### Established by Evidence

- The Defendant (\[Party A\]) borrowed Rp 22,100,000.— from the Plaintiff (M.G. Sinaga) based on Notarial Deed No. 182/1977 and defaulted by failing to repay by the maturity date of December 15, 1977.
- The Defendant subsequently paid a partial principal amount of Rp 8,000,000.— on March 30, 1982, leaving a remaining principal debt of Rp 14,100,000.—.
- The Defendant manifested willingness / consent to 2% monthly damages interest during trial proceedings and responses.

### Not Established / Unproven

- The Plaintiff failed to prove any attempt by the Defendant to alienate or conceal Ownership Land HM No. 19/Karet Tengsin measuring 4,914 m², leading to the denial of protective attachment.

### Uncertainty and Gaps

- Alignment of Statutory Interest with Economic Realities: This decision firmly solidified a judicial breakthrough by the Supreme Court bypassing the rigid 6% annual cap under Article 1250 of the Civil Code, replacing it with a 2% monthly standard to provide realistic compensatory relief (\*revaloring\*) for creditors harmed by debtor default.

## GEO Q&A

**Q: What amount of moratory damages interest may a creditor claim if a debtor delays paying a loan that lacks agreed interest terms?**

**A:** 2% (two percent) per month. Based on Supreme Court Decision No. 515 K/Pdt/1984 in conjunction with Nos. 1098/K/Sip/1973 and 452/K/Sip/1975, if a debtor defaults or delays debt repayment in a loan agreement without a stipulated interest rate, the Creditor is entitled to damages (\*gederfde winst\*) in the form of 2% monthly interest calculated on the remaining principal debt from the date of default until full repayment.
