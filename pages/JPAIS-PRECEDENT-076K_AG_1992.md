## Overview

Supreme Court Decision Reg. No. 76 K/AG/1992 dated October 23, 1993, establishes fundamental jurisprudence and legal standards in Islamic Grant Law and Inheritance: A grant (\*hibah\*) exceeding 1/3 (one-third) of the total area of the disputed object or estate owned by the grantor, given to one child without the consent of the other heirs, is contrary to legal rules and void by law. The Supreme Court affirmed that parental grants of property to a child must take into account principles of fairness among children and the one-third maximum limit to avoid prejudicing the rights of other legal heirs.

## Case Background

The late H. Saman bin Nasiman (passed away on July 24, 1987) was married 3 times during his lifetime and left behind 2 surviving wives (Hj. Saodah and Hj. Salamah), 4 biological children, and 2 grandchildren from a predeceased child. On May 10, 1986, the late H. Saman bin Nasiman granted a plot of land measuring 949 m² (pursuant to Girik C No. 692) located in Kampung Kapuk, Klender, East Jakarta, to one of his sons, the late \[Party B\] (Grant Deed No. 325/1.711.1/86). This grant was executed without the consent of the other legal heirs and consumed 949 m² out of the deceased's total land estate of approximately 1,340 m², leaving only about 391 m² for all remaining legal heirs.

\[Party A\] (alongside other heirs acting as Plaintiffs) filed a lawsuit seeking the cancellation of the grant and a distribution of inheritance at the East Jakarta Religious Court. The East Jakarta Religious Court (Decision No. 272/G/1990 dated July 17, 1990) granted the Plaintiffs' lawsuit and declared the 949 m² land grant invalid and void. However, the Jakarta High Religious Court (Decision No. 47/1990/PTA.JK dated March 25, 1991) annulled the East Jakarta Religious Court decision and declared the lawsuit inadmissible due to formal defects concerning power of attorney. Dissatisfied with the appellate ruling, the Plaintiff (\[Party A\]) filed a cassation petition to the Supreme Court.

## Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

- **Ratio Decidendi:**

1.  The disputed object, namely the granted land measuring 949 m², exceeded one-third of the total land area owned by the late H. Saman bin Nasiman (out of a total area of 1,340 m²); hence, the granted surface area violated statutory legal provisions.
2.  The act of granting the vast majority of land to a single child without the consent of the other legal heirs generates inequity and disharmony in sibling/heir relations, which runs counter to Islamic teachings (as reflected in the Hadith of the Prophet SAW narrated by Nu'man bin Bashir, which commands fairness among children).
3.  The Jakarta High Religious Court committed an error of law by treating this matter as a voluntary jurisdiction (\*voluntair\*) case and dismissing the lawsuit on technicalities of power of attorney formalities lacking legal grounds. Therefore, the Jakarta High Religious Court decision must be annulled, and the Supreme Court independently re-adjudicates the case by upholding the East Jakarta Religious Court judgment.

- **Ruling:** Granting the cassation petition from Petitioner for Cassation \[Party A\]; Annulling the Jakarta High Religious Court decision dated March 25, 1991, No. 47/1990/PTA.JK; and Independently Adjudicating: (1) Granting the Plaintiff's lawsuit; (2) Declaring that the grant by the late H. Saman bin Nasiman to the late \[Party B\] over a plot of land measuring 949 m² in Kampung Kapuk RT 008/05, Klender Village, East Jakarta, dated May 10, 1986, is invalid and void by law; (3) Determining the lawful heirs and the entire estate of the late H. Saman bin Nasiman; (4) Ordering the proportional distribution of inheritance among each legal heir in accordance with Islamic inheritance law; (5) Declaring valid and enforceable the Security Confiscation (\*Conservatoir Beslaag\*) placed by the East Jakarta Religious Court; (6) Ordering the Defendant to surrender land controlled in excess of their lawful share to the Plaintiff; (7) Ordering the Defendant to pay court costs across all levels of jurisdiction.
