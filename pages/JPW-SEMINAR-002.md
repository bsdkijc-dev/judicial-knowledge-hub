# JPW-SEMINAR-002

**Sixth Judicial Seminar of the International Criminal Court** is an official international judicial forum organized by the International Criminal Court (ICC) on 19 January 2024 in The Hague, Netherlands.

## Summary

The seminar focused on the theme "Securing Meaningful Justice for Victims – Models and Experiences". Held in a hybrid format and funded by the European Union, the event brought together approximately 50 participants, including supreme court presidents and international/regional court presidents from 27 national jurisdictions and 8 international/regional courts \[1, 2, 4–5, 44, 45\].

## Legal Basis

- [Rome Statute](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Rome_Statute): Article 21(3) (Compliance with Human Rights), Article 68(3) (Victim Participation), Article 75 (Reparations Orders), and Article 79 (Trust Fund for Victims) \[8, 9, 21, 24–27, 50, 53\].
- [Rules of Procedure and Evidence](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Rules_of_Procedure_and_Evidence): Rule 85 (Definition of Victims) and Rules 89–93 (Victim Participation Mechanisms).

## Background

Unlike previous international criminal tribunals, the Rome Statute places victims as central stakeholders by granting explicit rights to participate and claim reparations. This seminar was convened to evaluate the ICC's evolving jurisprudence and the implementation of its groundbreaking reparation orders.

## Objectives

- Facilitate the exchange of experiences and best practices among judges from diverse jurisdictions.
- Discuss procedural status and the protection of victim rights in criminal proceedings \[6–24\].
- Evaluate the determination of principles and the implementation of victim reparation programs \[24–43\].

## Key Provisions

- \*\*Procedural Status of Victims\*\*: Applications are vetted by the Victims Participation and Reparations Section (VPRS) of the ICC Registry. Victims may present views when their personal interests are affected, represented through a \*Common Legal Representative\* \[7–12\].
- \*\*Principles of Reparations\*\*: Reparation orders under Article 75 must contain 5 essential elements and may be individual or collective in nature.
- \*\*Role of the Trust Fund for Victims\*\*: Manages donor funds to finance reparation orders and executes its independent assistance mandate \[27–29\].

## Implementation

The seminar was divided into two main sessions featuring presentations by representatives from the ICC, Canada, Sweden, the Trust Fund for Victims, the Inter-American Court of Human Rights, and the African Court on Human and Peoples' Rights \[6–40, 56–59\].

## Policy Issues

- The indigence of convicted persons, resulting in heavy reliance on TFV donor funding.
- Balancing the speed of reparation implementation with the thoroughness of victim integration.
- Varying levels of state compliance with human rights judicial decisions.
