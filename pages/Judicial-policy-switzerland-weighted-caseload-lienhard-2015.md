# Judicial-policy-switzerland-weighted-caseload-lienhard-2015

**The Swiss Combined Weighted Caseload Study and Organizational Analysis Policy Framework** is a judicial administration governance policy and judge workload measurement methodology developed by Prof. Dr. Andreas Lienhard, Daniel Kettiger, Daniela Winkler (Center of Competence for Public Management / CCPM, University of Bern), and Hanspeter Uster (2015) in the *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA). It explicitly combines a weighted caseload study with a comprehensive organizational analysis across the courts and the Public Prosecutor's Service (PPS) of Canton Basel-Stadt. This methodology serves to revalidate case weights, evaluate non-case-related work time, optimize resource allocation, and integrate qualitative aspects of judicial organizational structure \[1–3, 17, 20, 24–29\].

## Summary

Published by Prof. Dr. Andreas Lienhard et al. in the *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA), this policy framework introduces a novel methodology in Swiss judicial administration. The enactment of the new Swiss Civil Procedure Code (CPC) and Criminal Procedure Code (CrimPC) in 2011 triggered uncertainties regarding actual judicial personnel requirements. The study in Canton Basel-Stadt combined a full-year weighted caseload study with a qualitative organizational analysis based on three core elements: \*Structure\*, \*Potential\*, and \*Culture\* \[20–21\]. Lienhard et al. demonstrate that this combination yields an integrated quantitative and qualitative analysis capable of validating case weights, clarifying mathematical data distortions, evaluating the allocation of non-case-related time, and assessing the effectiveness of task delegation within courts and prosecutor's offices \[25–28\].

## Legal Basis

- **Swiss Civil Procedure Code (CPC / Schweizerische Zivilprozessordnung 2011):** The national civil procedural code that necessitated the organizational adaptation of cantonal judicial systems.
- **Swiss Criminal Procedure Code (CrimPC / Schweizerische Strafprozessordnung 2011):** The national criminal procedural code of Switzerland.
- **Mandate Decision of the Court of Appeal and Government of Canton Basel-Stadt (2011):** The official assignment foundation for conducting the workload and organizational study.
- **Not found in the available sources:** Other specific formal statutory regulations are not mentioned in the source text \[FACT\].

## Background

Historically, Switzerland has experienced a scarcity of standardized judicial workload measurements. The combination of quantitative studies and organizational analyses was developed to generate valid and qualitatively tested resource allocation planning values.

## Objectives

1\. Accurately measure the time required to process cases (*case-related workload*) and non-case activities (*non-case-related workload*). 2. Validate case weights through the analysis of organizational structure, human resource potential, and organizational culture \[16, 20–21\]. 3. Evaluate the effectiveness of workflows, ICT usage, and the delegation of authority in courts and prosecutor's offices. 4. Provide sustainable recommendations for the optimization of judicial organizations.

## Scope

This policy encompasses judicial evaluation methodologies in Switzerland, with specific applications to the Court of Appeal, Civil Court of First Instance, Criminal Court of First Instance, and the Public Prosecutor's Service (PPS) of Canton Basel-Stadt.

## Key Provisions

- **Weighted Caseload Study Methodology:**
  - Measuring time taken for procedural sequences regarding case-related work.
  - Daily self-reports over one year for case activities and 11 categories of non-case-related work (general administration, education/training, public service activities, personal/business trips) \[8–11, 20\].
- **Organizational Analysis Methodology:**
  - Focuses on 3 elements: \*Structure\* (structural organization, workflows), \*Potential\* (HR, staff allocation, ICT, space, job satisfaction), and \*Culture\* (management and communication) \[20–21\].
  - Utilizes instruments such as CAF, ISO 9001, TCPS, CourTools, and the CEPEJ Quality Questionnaire \[24, 26–27\].
  - Methodological triangulation: document analysis, 53 structured interviews (actor triangulation), and feedback loops.

## Implementation

- **Basel-Stadt Field Study (2013–2015):** The execution of daily data recording from March 2013 to February 2014 and the publication of the final report in February 2015.
- **Qualitative Validation of Case Weights:** Utilizing interview data to correct mathematical distortions caused by cross-divisional staff assistance \[25–26\].

## Policy Problems

- **Weakness of Pure Case Weights:** The risk that isolated case weights may merely reflect poor performance or inefficient workflows if not validated qualitatively.
- **Hidden Non-Case-Related Activities:** Administrative workloads and ICT projects that frequently ignore or consume unallocated judge/prosecutor working time \[8–11, 29\].

## Reform / Change

- The permanent integration of weighted caseload studies with organizational analysis as the new standard for judicial evaluation in Switzerland.
- The utilization of workload data to evaluate and optimize staff task delegation.

## Multilingual Summaries (Swiss National Languages)

### Deutsch (Schweizer Hochdeutsch)

**Rahmenwerk für die kombinierte gewichtete Fallbelastungsstudie und Organisationsanalyse in der Schweiz** Dieser von Prof. Dr. Andreas Lienhard et al. (Universität Bern) im *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA, 2015) veröffentlichte Rahmen kombiniert eine Studie zur gewichteten Fallbelastung mit einer umfassenden Organisationsanalyse. Die am Appellationsgericht und bei der Staatsanwaltschaft des Kantons Basel-Stadt durchgeführte Studie nutzt methodische Triangulation (Struktur, Potenzial, Kultur), um Fallgewichte qualitativ zu validieren und fallunabhängige Tätigkeiten (wie Verwaltung oder IT-Projekte) zu erfassen. Dieser integrierte Ansatz korrigiert mathematische Verzerrungen und optimiert die Ressourcenallokation und Aufgabendelegation.

- **Rechtsgrundlage:** Schweizerische Zivilprozessordnung (ZPO 2011); Schweizerische Strafprozessordnung (StPO 2011); Mandat des Kantons Basel-Stadt.

### Français

(Université de Berne) dans l' *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA, 2015), ce cadre combine une étude de la charge de travail pondérée avec une analyse organisationnelle globale. L'étude, menée à la Cour d'appel et au Ministère public du canton de Bâle-Ville, utilise la triangulation méthodologique (Structure, Potentiel, Culture) pour valider qualitativement les pondérations des affaires et évaluer les tâches non liées aux dossiers (comme l'administration). Cette approche intégrée corrige les distorsions mathématiques et optimise l'allocation des ressources.

- **Base Légale:** Code de procédure civile suisse (CPC 2011) ; Code de procédure pénale suisse (CPP 2011) ; Mandat du canton de Bâle-Ville.

### 3 Most Important Improvements

1\. **Integration of the Length of Judicial Proceedings Indicator:** Incorporate timeliness variables from the CEPEJ SATURN Centre into the framework to ensure that a balanced workload is directly proportional to the certainty of case resolution times \[29–30\]. 2. **Standardization of National Workload Measurement Methodologies in Switzerland:** Encourage the adoption of this combined weighted caseload and organizational analysis methodology by all Swiss cantons to construct comparative data across cantonal courts. 3. **Alignment of Workload with Substantive Judgment Quality Evaluation:** Develop advanced research methodologies that link the allocation of judicial working time with the substantive quality of civil/criminal judgments and prosecutorial decisions \[29–30\].
