# JPW-DECLARATION-002

## Guatemala Declaration on Crime of Aggression Jurisdiction — Wiki Entry

**Declaration of Non-Acceptance of ICC Jurisdiction over the Crime of Aggression by the Republic of Guatemala** is the official diplomatic declaration submitted by the Republic of Guatemala through its Ministry of Foreign Affairs on 16 January 2018, stating that Guatemala does not accept ICC jurisdiction over the crime of aggression until it completes the internal ratification process for the Kampala Amendments to the Rome Statute.

## Summary

The document sets out Guatemala's official position declining ICC jurisdiction over the crime of aggression pursuant to Article 15bis(4) of the Rome Statute. This non-acceptance remains in effect until the Guatemalan State completes its internal ratification process for the Kampala Amendments.

## Legal Basis

- [Rome Statute](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Rome_Statute):
  - **Article 5**: Crimes within the jurisdiction of the Court.
  - **Article 12**: Preconditions to the exercise of jurisdiction.
  - **Article 15bis(4)**: Procedure for submitting a declaration of non-acceptance of jurisdiction over the crime of aggression.
  - **Article 121(5)**: Provisions on amendment of the Statute.
- [Kampala Amendments](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Kampala_Amendments): Amendments relating to the definition and exercise of jurisdiction over the crime of aggression.

## Background

The declaration followed Guatemala's careful review of the relevant Rome Statute provisions and the outcome of the Assembly of States Parties' deliberations held in December 2017, which activated the Court's jurisdiction over the crime of aggression.

## Objectives

- Affirm Guatemala's national legal sovereignty over the ratification process for international treaties.
- Suspend the exercise of ICC jurisdiction over the crime of aggression with respect to Guatemalan territory or nationals until internal ratification is completed.

## Key Provisions

- **Conditional Non-Acceptance of Jurisdiction**: Guatemala does not accept ICC jurisdiction over the crime of aggression until ratification of the Kampala Amendments is completed.
- **Use of Article 15bis(4)**: Exercise of the explicit right afforded to States Parties under the Rome Statute to opt out of aggression jurisdiction by submitting a formal declaration.

## Implementation

Formally transmitted by the Guatemalan Ministry of Foreign Affairs and signed by Minister of Foreign Affairs Sandra Erica Jovel Polanco on 16 January 2018.

## Policy Issues

- The need to complete the internal legislative process at the national level for ratification of the Kampala Amendments.

## Reform / Future Directions

The declaration is conditional rather than permanent: acceptance of jurisdiction will occur automatically once Guatemala completes its internal ratification process for the Kampala Amendments.
