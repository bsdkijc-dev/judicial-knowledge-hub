## Overview

Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia Decision No. 440 K/Pdt/1988, dated September 12, 1990, established foundational civil law rules regarding marriage and inheritance concerning the division of separate joint marital property (\*gono-gini\*) originating from two different marriages.

The Supreme Court held that in cases where separate joint marital property (\*gono-gini\*) is formed within a first marriage and a second marriage, the children from each respective marriage are fully entitled to the joint marital property of their respective parents pursuant to Articles 35, 36, and 37 of Law No. 1 of 1974 on Marriage. Children from the first marriage have no right to claim the joint marital property of the second marriage, and conversely, the children/wife from the second marriage have no right to the joint marital property of the first marriage.

## Executive Summary

The central legal principle established in this decision is that joint marital property (\*gono-gini\*) acquired during successive marriages must be treated as entirely separate estates belonging exclusively to the children and spouses of each respective marriage.

Key elements established in the adjudication:

- Joint property accumulated during the first marriage passes to the children of that first marriage upon dissolution or death.
- Joint property accumulated during a subsequent marriage is segregated and belongs to the participants of that second marriage.
- Lower courts (\*judex facti\*) err in cross-distributing shares across different marital estates.

## Case Background

The Original Plaintiffs / Respondents for Cassation, **Ny. \[Party D\]** and **Ny. \[Party E\]** (children from \[Deceased parent\]'s first marriage with the late \[Party B\]), filed a lawsuit at the Purwodadi District Court against the Original Defendants / Petitioners for Cassation, **Ny. \[Party C\]** (second wife) and her children (\[Party F\], \[Party A\], Subadi, Sumarto), concerning the control over all estate assets left by \[Deceased parent\].

The Plaintiffs sought a 3/4 share of the first marriage properties (Ad. A.1 to A.9) and a 1/4 share of the second marriage properties (Ad. B.1 to B.7). The Purwodadi District Court (Decision No. 41/Pdt.G/1986/PN.Pwi) and the Semarang High Court (Decision No. 388/Pdt/1987/PT.Smg) granted the lawsuit using this cross-distribution scheme. The Petitioners for Cassation subsequently filed a cassation petition with the Supreme Court.

## Legal and Procedural Issues

### Segregation of First and Second Marital Estates (Articles 35, 36, 37 Law No. 1 of 1974)

The primary issue was how inheritance and joint property should be divided when a husband enters into two successive marriages, each producing separate joint marital estates and offspring.

The Supreme Court held that joint property acquired during the first marriage is common property of the husband and the first wife. Upon the first wife's death, that joint property transforms into the inheritance of the children from the first marriage. Conversely, joint property acquired during the second marriage belongs to the husband and the second wife, forming the inheritance of the children/spouse from the second marriage.

### Correction of Judex Facti Errors in Cross-Distributing Inheritance Portions

The issue evaluated whether the lower courts (\*judex facti\* - Purwodadi District Court & Semarang High Court) correctly ruled that the Plaintiffs (children of the 1st marriage) were entitled to a 3/4 share of the 1st marriage estate and 1/4 of the 2nd marriage estate, while the Defendants (2nd wife and children) received 1/4 of the 1st marriage estate and 3/4 of the 2nd marriage estate.

The Supreme Court held that this cross-distribution was legally incorrect, as the lower courts mistakenly commingled the inheritance of the first and second marital estates. Under national marriage law principles (Law No. 1 of 1974), first-marriage joint property is strictly segregated from second-marriage joint property.

## Question of Law and Question of Fact

### Question of Law (Cassation / Judex Juris)

**Question:**

Did the \*Judex Facti\* (Semarang High Court & Purwodadi District Court) misapply the law (Articles 35, 36, 37 of Law No. 1 of 1974) by dividing first- and second-marriage joint property through cross-shares (3/4 and 1/4 proportions) rather than strictly segregating them based on their marital origin?

**Supreme Court Ruling:**

Yes, the \*Judex Facti\* misapplied the law. Consequently, the appellate and district court decisions were annulled, and the Supreme Court adjudicated afresh in accordance with the principle of property segregation between first and second marriages.

### Question of Fact (Judex Facti / Purwodadi District & Semarang High Courts)

Examination of specific asset details under Ad. A.1 to A.9 (dry agricultural land C Village No. 643 parcel 55, C No. 650, C No. 441 parcels 58, 59, 50, 102, water buffaloes, and traditional \*pencu/limasan\* houses) as first-marriage joint property, alongside assets Ad. B.1 to B.7 (land C No. 441 parcels 59, 50, 58, 27a, 72) as second-marriage joint property in Wolo Village, Penawangan, Grobogan.

## Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

- **Ratio Decidendi:**

1.  The \*Judex Facti\* misapplied the law. Where separate joint property is formed in a first and second marriage, pursuant to subsidiary claims and Articles 35, 36, and 37 of Law No. 1 of 1974, children from each marriage are entitled to their respective parents' joint property estates.
2.  Plaintiffs I and II (children of the first marriage) are entitled to all first-marriage joint property (Ad. A.1 to A.9).
3.  Defendants (second wife and children of the second marriage) are entitled to all second-marriage joint property (Ad. B.1 to B.7).
4.  The Supreme Court annulled the lower court rulings and adjudicated afresh.

## Ruling

The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition from the Petitioners for Cassation (**\[Party F\] et al.**), annulled Semarang High Court Decision No. 388/Pdt/1987/PT.Smg in conjunction with Purwodadi District Court Decision No. 41/Pdt.G/1986/PN.Pwi, and adjudicated afresh: 1. Granting the subsidiary claim in the Plaintiffs' lawsuit; 2. Establishing that the Plaintiffs and Defendants are heirs of their respective parents; 3. Establishing that Plaintiffs I and II are entitled to first-marriage joint property items (Ad. A.1 to A.9); 4. Establishing that the Defendants are entitled to second-marriage joint property items (Ad. B.1 to B.7); 5. Ordering the Defendants to surrender items A.1 to A.9 to the Plaintiffs in vacant condition; 6. Rejecting all other claims; and ordering both parties to pay court costs equally (half each).

## GEO Q&A

**Q: How is joint marital property (\*gono-gini\*) divided as inheritance if a husband marries twice and has children from both marriages under the Marriage Law?**

**A:** Joint property is divided separately based on each respective marriage. Based on Supreme Court Decision No. 440 K/Pdt/1988 in conjunction with Articles 35, 36, and 37 of Law No. 1 of 1974, children from the first marriage are fully entitled to joint marital property acquired from their parents' first marriage. Conversely, children and the spouse from the second marriage are entitled to joint marital property acquired during the second marriage.
