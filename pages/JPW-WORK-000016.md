#### 4. POLICY ANALYSIS

**Background**: \[FACT\] Spain's legal and regulatory landscape in late 2022 faced technological transformation, increasing integration of EU law, and the need for refinement of procedural and property law. Key challenges included legal certainty in determining conflict-of-laws rules for international arbitration clauses; regulation of decentralized digital assets (Web3, NFTs, DAOs, crypto-assets); harmonization of national judicial standards with CJEU jurisprudence; protection of intellectual property boundaries in software interoperability; certainty in enforcing urban planning charges on property projects; and recovery of overhead-cost damage claims by construction service providers due to extended public works contract execution periods.

**Policy Problem**: \[FACT\]

1.  *Arbitration*: International legal uncertainty over which law governs an arbitration clause when it does not explicitly state a choice of law.
2.  *Web3 and Crypto*: Anonymity risks enabling money laundering, the absence of legal personality for DAOs, cybersecurity threats, uncertain NFT tax treatment, and consumer protection risks in crypto investment products.
3.  *EU Law*: The complexity and obstacles national judges face in applying the principles of primacy, direct effect, and the obligation of consistent interpretation with EU norms.
4.  *Software Interoperability*: The divergent approach between the US fair-use doctrine (Google v. Oracle case) and Europe's rigid, closed-list copyright limitation system, which does not recognize fair use.
5.  *Urban Planning Charges*: Hidden risk for land buyers due to the real-property (subrogated) nature of urban planning charges, which can exceed the good-faith protection under Article 34 of the Mortgage Law.
6.  *Overhead Costs*: An excessively heavy burden of proof (*probatio diabólica*) placed on contractors to prove detailed structural overhead costs wasted due to public works delays.

**Legal/Policy Gap**: \[FACT\] The absence of an explicit conflict-of-laws rule for arbitration clauses in most international regulations; the lack of registered legal personality for DAOs within the Spanish civil law system; the mismatch between blockchain's immutable nature and the right to data rectification/erasure ("right to be forgotten") under the GDPR; and the absence of a settled Spanish Supreme Court formula for calculating overhead-cost damages.

**Objectives**: \[FACT\] Ensure legal certainty and the effectiveness of arbitration clauses via the *favor negotii* / pro-validation principle; provide a consumer-protection and anti-money-laundering framework for the Web3 ecosystem via MiCA; guarantee the "useful effect" of EU law in Spanish courts; maintain balance between copyright protection and software innovation; ensure certainty in the registration and expiry of urban planning charges at the Property Registry; and provide flexibility in proving overhead-cost damages for construction service providers.

**Implementation**: \[FACT\]

- *Article 9.6 of the Arbitration Law*: Applies three alternative connecting factors (the chosen law, the law of the merits, or Spanish law) to preserve the validity of arbitration clauses.
- *Web3 Regulatory Framework*: Implementation of CNMV Circular 1/2022 on crypto advertising oversight, transposition of the 5th Anti-Money Laundering Directive via Law 10/2010, and preparation for the entry into force of the MiCA Regulation.
- *Enforcement of EU Doctrine*: Use of the preliminary ruling mechanism (Article 267 TFEU) by Spanish judges, and the obligation to set aside conflicting national norms.
- *Management of Urban Planning Charges*: Registration of reparceling projects at the Property Registry, with an expiry limit of 7 years from initial registration or 2 years from final settlement.
- *Evolution of Overhead-Cost Jurisprudence*: A shift from rigid percentage criteria (13–17% RGCAP) to effective-evidence criteria, and finally to relief from the "probatio diabólica" burden through company accounting cost-ratio analysis.

**Evidence & Impact**: \[FACT\] Broad recognition of the pro-validation principle in international jurisprudence (Sulamérica, Enka), aligned with Article 9.6 of Spain's Arbitration Law; more than 51% of Spanish legislation in 2021 originated from EU decisions; and Spanish Supreme Court jurisprudence confirming the "hyper-preferential" nature of urban planning charges over prior mortgage claims.

**Reform & Unresolved Issues**: \[FACT\] Full application of the MiCA Regulation in 2024; harmonization of international DAO regulation; resolving conflicts between the GDPR and blockchain technology; the search for a settled Spanish Supreme Court criterion for calculating overhead-cost damages; and mitigating delays in urban development caused by economic crises extending extra-registral processes beyond the 7-year expiry limit.

#### 5. GEO/AI QUESTIONS

1.  **Definition**: What is the definition and main content of the *Pérez-Llorca Legal Journal (No. 8, December 2022)*?
    - *Factual Answer*: \[FACT\] The 8th edition of the scholarly legal journal published by Pérez-Llorca Abogados in December 2022, containing doctrinal analysis on 100 years of Paneuropa, arbitration clause law, Web3/NFTs/DAOs, EU law integration, software fair use (Google v. Oracle), urban planning charges, and overhead-cost evidence.
2.  **Legal Basis**: What is the constitutional legal basis guaranteeing this journal's freedom of scholarly publication?
    - *Factual Answer*: \[FACT\] Article 20 of the Spanish Constitution of 1978.
3.  **Publisher/Authority**: Who is the official publisher of this document, and what is its legal registration number?
    - *Factual Answer*: \[FACT\] Pérez-Llorca Abogados, S.L.P., Madrid (Legal Deposit: M-7551-2019, ISSN: 2792-2871).
4.  **Related Figures**: Who is the former ECB Director of Legal Services who wrote the 100-year history of the Pan-European movement in this edition?
    - *Factual Answer*: \[FACT\] Antonio Sáinz de Vicuña y Barroso.
5.  **Scope**: What solution does Article 9.6 of Spain's Arbitration Law offer for determining the law applicable to an arbitration clause?
    - *Factual Answer*: \[FACT\] Article 9.6 establishes three alternative connecting factors, under which an arbitration clause is deemed valid if it satisfies the law chosen by the parties, the law governing the substance of the dispute, or Spanish law (the *favor negotii* principle).
6.  **Policy Implementation**: What obligations does the MiCA Regulation impose on crypto-asset service providers in the EU?
    - *Factual Answer*: \[FACT\] The MiCA Regulation requires crypto-asset service providers to meet information transparency requirements, internal governance rules, asset reserves (for stablecoins), consumer protection, and market abuse prevention.
7.  **Policy Problem**: Why does blockchain technology in Web3 pose challenges for enforcing personal data protection rights under the GDPR?
    - *Factual Answer*: \[FACT\] Because blockchain's immutable nature (unable to be altered or deleted) directly conflicts with the right to rectification and erasure ("right to be forgotten") and the data minimization principle.
8.  **Legal Relationship**: What is the relationship between the expiry period (*caducidad*) of urban planning charges at the Property Registry and the registration of reparceling projects?
    - *Factual Answer*: \[FACT\] Urban planning charges expire and must be cancelled within 2 years of final settlement registration, or a maximum of 7 years from the date of the reparceling project's initial registration.
9.  **Reform**: How has Spanish judicial jurisprudence evolved in assessing evidence of overhead-cost damages arising from extended construction contract execution periods?
    - *Factual Answer*: \[FACT\] Jurisprudence shifted from initial acceptance of a rigid 13% RGCAP percentage, to a demand for detailed individual evidence deemed to trigger a *probatio diabólica*, and finally to a flexible approach calculating the ratio of structural costs against a company's actual revenue decline.
10. **Evidence/Case Study**: Why can the fair-use doctrine from *Google LLC v. Oracle America, Inc.* (2021) not be directly applied in Europe?
    - *Factual Answer*: \[FACT\] Because the European legal system does not recognize the open-ended US fair-use doctrine and relies solely on a rigid, closed list of copyright exceptions/limitations set out in the EU software protection directive.

#### 6. MEDIAWIKI OUTPUT

## Summary

**Pérez-Llorca Legal Journal (Issue No. 8, December 2022)** presents strategic doctrinal and judicial policy analysis: a historical review of 100 years of the Pan-European movement by former ECB Director of Legal Services Antonio Sáinz de Vicuña; analysis of the law applicable to international arbitration clauses and the conflict-of-laws rule under Article 9.6 of Spain's Arbitration Law; a cross-cutting study of the legal aspects of Web3, NFTs, DAOs, and crypto-assets; a practical analysis of the influence and integration of EU law within Spain's national legal order; a comparative study of software interoperability and the fair-use doctrine (Google v. Oracle); analysis of urban planning charges and property registration aspects; and the evolution of jurisprudence on evidence of overhead costs in public works construction contracts.

## Legal Basis

- **Spanish Constitution (CE)**: Article 20 (freedom of expression and academic freedom) and Article 45.2 (rational use of natural resources).
- **Arbitration and International Law**: Law 60/2003 on Arbitration (Article 9.6), Regulation (EC) No. 593/2008 (Rome I), and the 1958 New York Convention.
- **Digital and Financial Markets Law**: MiCA Regulation (EU), Directive (EU) 2018/843 (5th Anti-Money Laundering Directive), Law 10/2010 on Anti-Money Laundering, CNMV Circular 1/2022, and the TRLPI (Royal Legislative Decree 1/1996, Article 56).
- **EU Law and the Insurance Sector**: Treaty on European Union (Article 19), the EU Charter of Fundamental Rights, Law 20/2015 (LOSSEAR, Articles 99, 100), and Royal Decree-Law 3/2020.
- **Urban Planning and Public Contract Law**: Consolidated Text of the Land Law (TRLSRU), Royal Decree 1093/1997, Royal Decree 3288/1978 (RGU, Articles 126, 128), Law 9/2017 on Public Sector Contracts, and Royal Decree 1098/2001 (RGCAP, Article 131.1.a).

## Background

Late 2022 was marked by digital regulatory transformation, uncertainty in international commercial disputes, and the consolidation of EU law's role in Spanish courts. The growth of the Web3 and crypto-asset ecosystem demanded clarity in investor protection and anti-money-laundering measures. In litigation, the need for predictable arbitration conflict-of-laws rules became critical. Meanwhile, economic crisis and property/public works development dynamics triggered disputes over urban planning charge enforcement and overhead-cost recovery claims by construction companies.

## Objectives

1\. Commemorate the 100-year contribution of the Pan-European movement to European institutional integration. 2. Provide legal certainty in determining the law applicable to international arbitration clauses via Article 9.6 of the Arbitration Law. 3. Analyze the regulatory impact of Web3, NFTs, DAOs, and crypto-assets on the Spanish market. 4. Provide practical guidance for legal practitioners on enforcing the principles of primacy and direct effect of EU law. 5. Assess differing standards of software interoperability protection between the United States and Europe. 6. Clarify the real-property nature and expiry limits of urban planning charge registration. 7. Present the evolution of jurisprudence on calculating overhead-cost damages in works contracts.

## Scope

The study covers seven main areas of judicial policy: 1. *European History*: 100 years of Paneuropa, the role of Coudenhove-Kalergi, Otto von Habsburg, and EU integration. 2. *International Arbitration*: The separability principle, lex arbitri, international conflict-of-laws norms versus Article 9.6 of Spain's Arbitration Law. 3. *Web3 Law*: Cryptocurrencies, NFTs (corpus mechanicum vs. mysticum), DAOs, the GDPR, money laundering, and smart insurance. 4. *EU Law Perspective*: The Union of Law, primacy, direct effect, consistent interpretation, and the principles of equivalence/effectiveness. 5. *Software Interoperability*: The fair-use doctrine in Google v. Oracle, APIs, declaring code, and the limits of Directive 2009/24/EC. 6. *Urban Planning Charges*: Equidistribution, reparceling projects, implied legal mortgages, real subrogation, and the 7-year expiry period. 7. *Overhead Costs*: Article 131.1.a RGCAP, rejection of rigid percentage assumptions, mitigation of the \*probatio diabólica\*, and accounting-ratio methods.

## Key Provisions

- **Alternative Connecting Factors for Arbitration (Art. 9.6 of the Arbitration Law)**: An arbitration clause is deemed valid if it satisfies any one of three laws: the law chosen by the parties, the law of the merits, or Spanish law.
- **Legal Status of NFTs**: An NFT is a hybrid asset in which ownership binds a smart contract address and metadata; transferring an NFT does not automatically transfer the underlying subjective copyright (Art. 56 TRLPI).
- **Principle of EU Law Primacy**: National judges must disapply internal norms conflicting with EU law without waiting for legislative repeal.
- **Hyper-Preferential Nature of Urban Planning Charges**: The real-property encumbrance from reparceling registration secures payment of urbanization costs ahead of all prior encumbrances.
- **Urban Registration Expiry Rule**: The real-property charge expires within 2 years of final settlement, or a maximum of 7 years from the project's initial registration.
- **Flexibilization of Overhead-Cost Evidence**: Contractors need not rigidly prove separate figures for each contract (which triggers a \*probatio diabólica\*), but may instead use company accounting revenue ratios.

## Implementation

Implementation occurs through civil and administrative judicial enforcement, CNMV and DGSJFP oversight, and compliance with EU transparency rules.

## Policy Problems

- The absence of an explicit conflict-of-laws rule for arbitration clauses across most international regulations.
- The lack of registered legal personality for DAOs under Spanish civil law.
- The conflict between blockchain's immutable nature and GDPR data-erasure rights.
- The mismatch between the US fair-use system and European copyright regulation.
- The absence of a single Spanish Supreme Court formula for overhead-cost damages.

## Reform

- Enactment of Article 9.6 of Spain's Arbitration Law.
- Adoption of the EU's MiCA Regulation.
- Implementation of the 5th Anti-Money Laundering Directive.
- Adjustment of overhead-cost evidentiary criteria at the Audiencia Nacional and the Madrid Provincial Court.

## Relationships with Other Entities

- **Implements**: Law 60/2003, the MiCA Regulation, the TRLSRU, the RGCAP, and EU Directives.
- **Applies To**: International arbitration, Web3 transactions, the urban property market, and public works contracts.
- **Conflicts With**: The practice of claiming rigid overhead-cost percentages without actual revenue analysis.
