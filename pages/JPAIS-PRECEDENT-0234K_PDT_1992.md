## Overview

Supreme Court Decision Reg. No. 234 K/Pdt/1992 dated December 20, 1993 (deliberated on June 10, 1993), establishes two fundamental civil and customary land law jurisprudence principles:

- **Letter C / Tax Records Are Not Proof of Ownership:** Recording a person's name in the Village Letter C Book or as a taxpayer (Ipeda/PBB) does not constitute proof of land ownership (\*bukti hak milik\*), but merely indicates a person's obligation to pay tax on the land under their control. Recording the name of an uncle or guardian while the true land owner is an infant does not alter the status of the land's ownership.
- **Legal Capacity to Perform Legal Actions Under Customary Law:** According to Customary Law (referring to Supreme Court Decision No. 53 K/Sip/1952 dated June 1, 1955), a person who has reached the age of 15 (fifteen) years is deemed to have reached majority and is legally competent to perform legal transactions (such as purchasing rice fields).
- **Transfer of Rights by a Non-Owner:** Because an uncle or guardian is not the lawful owner of rice fields inherited by their ward, a land purchase transaction executed by such uncle to a third party is invalid and null and void (\*nemo plus juris ad alium transferre potest quam ipse habet\*).

## Case Background

The Original Plaintiff / Petitioner for Cassation (\[Party D\], represented by counsel L.L. Korassa, S.H.) filed a lawsuit against the Original Defendants / Respondents for Cassation (Defendant I: \[Party E\], Defendant II: Sunaryo, Defendant III: Tjahyono alias Siong, Defendant IV: \[Party C\] alias Ibu \[Party A\], and Co-Defendant: \[Party F\] \[Plaintiff's sibling\]) at the Jember District Court. The object of the dispute consisted of a plot of rice field measuring approximately 1.034 Ro / 0.952 Ha (Parcel No. 25, Petok No. 403 / Letter C No. 613 S.II) in Sumberjeruk Village, Kalisat Sub-District, Jember Regency.

The disputed rice field was originally purchased by the Plaintiff's late birth mother (\[Party B\], born \[details removed\]) from \[Party G\] on May 6, 1934. In 1938, \[Party B\] passed away shortly after giving birth to the Plaintiff (\[Party D\]). The infant Plaintiff was subsequently raised by his uncle and aunt, namely \[Party A\] (\[Party B\]'s biological brother) and his wife \[Party C\] (Defendant IV). For tax payment convenience, the rice field was recorded under the name of \[Party A\] in the Village Letter C Book. After \[Party A\] passed away in 1979, the rice field was controlled and unlawfully claimed to have been purchased by \[Party E\] (Defendant I) from \[Party A\] in 1975, and was subsequently farmed by Sunaryo (Defendant II) and Tjahyono (Defendant III).

The Jember District Court (Decision No. 26/Pdt.G/1990/PN.Jr. dated December 10, 1990) granted the Plaintiff's lawsuit in part, declared the disputed rice field to be the inherited property of the late \[Party B\], affirmed that the sale and purchase between \[Party B\] and H. \[Party G\] on May 6, 1934, was valid, and declared the possession by Defendants I, II, and III to be unlawful. However, the Surabaya High Court (Decision No. 129/Pdt/1991/PT.Sby. dated April 20, 1991) annulled the Jember District Court judgment and ruled in favor of Defendant I on the grounds that the land was recorded under the name of \[Party A\] in the Village Letter C Book of 1942/1946. The Plaintiff filed a cassation petition to the Supreme Court.

## Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

- **Ratio Decidendi:**

1.  The cassation objections raised by the Petitioner for Cassation (\[Party D\]) are well-founded because the Surabaya High Court erred in applying the law, wherein the legal reasoning of the Surabaya High Court was contradictory and improperly treated the Village Letter C Book as evidence of ownership.
2.  Under prevailing law, the Village Letter C Book does not constitute proof of ownership, but merely points to the party obligated to pay taxes on the land under their control. Because the Petitioner for Cassation was an infant at the time and raised by his uncle (\[Party A\]), it was natural for taxes to be paid by and registered under \[Party A\]'s name, which does not denote \[Party A\] as the owner.
3.  The Petitioner for Cassation successfully proved ownership of the disputed rice field through Exhibit P-1, reflecting the land sale from H. \[Party G\] to \[Party B\] (the Plaintiff's mother) on May 6, 1934, corroborated by witness testimonies and acknowledged by Defendant IV (the widow of the late \[Party A\]). Under Customary Law, \[Party B\]—born \[details removed\]—was deemed an adult (ex Supreme Court Decision No. 53 K/Sip/1952) and legally competent to execute legal actions.
4.  Because \[Party A\] was not the owner of the disputed rice field, the sale and purchase transaction between \[Party A\] and Respondent for Cassation I (Defendant I / \[Party E\]) concerning the disputed rice field on July 7, 1975, is invalid and null and void.

- **Ruling:** Granting the cassation petition from Petitioner for Cassation: \[Party D\]; Annulling the Surabaya High Court decision dated April 20, 1991, No. 129/Pdt/1991/PT.Sby; and Independently Adjudicating: IN CONVENTION: IN EXCEPTION: Rejecting Defendant I's Exception; IN THE PRINCIPAL CASE: (1) Granting the Plaintiff's lawsuit in part; (2) Declaring that the disputed rice field in its entirety is the lawful inherited property of the late \[Party B\] / birth mother of the Plaintiff and Co-Defendant; (3) Declaring that the sale and purchase between the late \[Party G\] and the late \[Party B\] regarding the disputed rice field on May 6, 1934, is lawful under the law; (4) \*\*Declaring null and void all documents and deeds—including the sale and purchase deed / transfer records—concerning the disputed rice field executed between \[Party A\] and \[Party E\] (Defendant I);\*\* (5) Declaring that the control and cultivation of the disputed rice field by Defendant I, Defendant II, and Defendant III without the Plaintiff's consent is unlawful; (6) Ordering Defendants I, II, III, and anyone deriving rights from them to vacate and surrender the disputed rice field to the Plaintiff free from any encumbrances; (7) Rejecting the remainder of the Plaintiff's lawsuit; (8) Ordering the Respondents for Cassation to pay court costs across all levels of jurisdiction (assessed at Rp 20,000 at the cassation level). \*(Decided in the deliberation meeting of the Supreme Court on Thursday, June 10, 1993, and pronounced on December 20, 1993, by the Cassation Panel of Justices: H. Soeharto, SH. \[Presiding Judge\], H. Asikin Kusuma Atmadja, SH., and Soedarmadji, SH.).\*
