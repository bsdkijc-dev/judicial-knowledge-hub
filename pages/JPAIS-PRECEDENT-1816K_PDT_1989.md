# JPAIS:PRECEDENT:1816K PDT 1989

**Supreme Court Decision Reg. No. 1816 K/Pdt/1989** (dated October 22, 1992) is a fundamental Indonesian land and civil law jurisprudence precedent establishing three vital legal rules regarding the criteria for a good faith purchaser (*good faith purchaser*), the validity of land certificate cancellations by Land Authorities / Minister of Home Affairs due to criminal forgery of root titles, and the formal qualification of the defendant's status (plurality of parties) in a certificate cancellation lawsuit.

## Executive Summary

The Supreme Court established three primary legal principles:

1.  **Criteria for a Good Faith Purchaser vs. a Negligent Purchaser:** A land buyer cannot be classified as a good faith purchaser if the acquisition is executed negligently—specifically, when the buyer completely failed to examine the rights and status of the sellers regarding the disputed land at the time of the transaction. Consequently, a negligent buyer does not deserve legal protection in such a transaction (*bad faith / negligent purchaser*).
2.  **Validity of Land Certificate Cancellation Based on Criminal Convictions for Root Title Forgery:** In cases where the issuance of a land certificate contains cadastral technical errors or is based on criminal forgery / false statements in an authentic deed (Article 266 of the Criminal Code) proven by a legally binding criminal judgment, the Minister of Home Affairs / Land Authority is authorized to cancel such land certificate pursuant to Article 12 *jo.* Article 14 of the Minister of Home Affairs Regulation No. 6 of 1972. Such certificate cancellation is legally valid and does not constitute a Tort by the Government (*Onrechtmatige Overheidsdaad*).
3.  **Formal Plurality of Parties Qualification in Land Certificate Cancellation Lawsuits:** A lawsuit seeking the cancellation of a land certificate that joins the local Agrarian Office as Defendant II satisfies the formal requirements regarding parties, meaning the High Court erred in declaring the lawsuit inadmissible (*Niet Ontvankelijk Verklaard* / NO) solely because the original seller or central Minister of Home Affairs was not co-joined as a separate defendant.

The Supreme Court granted the Plaintiff's (\[Party A\]) cassation petition, annulled Palembang High Court Decision No. 83/Pdt/1988/PT.Plg dated February 27, 1989, and independently adjudicated (*mengadili sendiri*) by upholding the substantive verdict of the Palembang District Court in **Rejecting the Convention Plaintiff's Lawsuit in its Entirety** and declaring the Reconvention Lawsuit Inadmissible (NO).

## Legal / Procedural Issues

### 1. Qualification and Legal Standards for Good Faith Purchasers in Land Transactions

*Can a buyer who purchases land before a Land Deed Official (PPAT) claim protection as a good faith purchaser if they failed to diligently examine the rights and origin status of the seller's land which ultimately originated from criminal forgery?*

**Supreme Court Rule: Not Protected by Law.** A buyer who executes a transaction without reasonable caution and diligence (*due diligence*) in examining the validity of the seller's foundational title is categorized as a negligent / bad faith purchaser. The principle of legal protection for good faith purchasers does not apply to negligent buyers.

### 2. Validity of State Administrative Actions (Minister of Home Affairs / BPN Decrees) Canceling Land Certificates Based on Criminal Judgments

*Does a Decree issued by the Minister of Home Affairs / Land Authority canceling a Hak Milik Certificate based on an in-kracht criminal forgery judgment constitute a Tort by the Government (Onrechtmatige Overheidsdaad)?*

**Supreme Court Rule: Valid and Not a Tort.** Pursuant to Article 12 *jo.* Article 14 of Minister of Home Affairs Regulation No. 6 of 1972, Land Authorities possess administrative authority to cancel land certificates issued on the basis of cadastral technical errors or criminal forgery of documents / false statements (Article 266 of the Criminal Code). The issuance of such cancellation decrees is legally valid, and lawsuits seeking their annulment must be rejected.

### 3. Formal Plurality Requirements for Defendants in Land Certificate Cancellation Lawsuits

Joining the local Agrarian Office as Defendant II satisfies formal party requirements to test the validity of a certificate cancellation action, meaning Courts must not declare lawsuits inadmissible (NO) solely because the original seller or central Minister of Home Affairs was not independently co-sued.

## Question of Law vs Question of Fact

### Question of Law (Judex Juris / Supreme Court)

Did the *Judex Facti* Palembang High Court (Decision No. 83/Pdt/1988/PT.Plg) err in applying civil procedure law (*foutieve toepassing van het recht*) by declaring the Plaintiff's lawsuit inadmissible (NO) due to non-joinder, and how are the Plaintiff's status as a land buyer and the validity of the Minister of Home Affairs Decree qualified?

**Supreme Court Legal Answer:** Yes. Palembang High Court erred in applying civil procedure law. The Supreme Court granted Plaintiff \[Party A\]'s cassation petition, annulled Palembang High Court Decision No. 83/Pdt/1988/PT.Plg, and independently adjudicated by Rejecting the Convention Plaintiff's Lawsuit in its Entirety on the grounds that the Plaintiff was a negligent purchaser and the Minister of Home Affairs Decree canceling the certificate was legally valid.

### Question of Fact (Judex Facti / Palembang District Court & Palembang High Court)

Examination of facts regarding the land and house purchase transaction covering 118 m² on Cek Syech St. RT 05 No. 203, Palembang, on September 2, 1974, between \[Party A\] and Mohamad Ali bin Nurdin and H. Abdul Hamid before Notary/PPAT Darbi, S.H.; issuance of SHM No. 169/18 Ilir dated April 24, 1976, GS No. 1217 in the name of \[Party A\]; Palembang District Court Criminal Judgment No. 684/1976/PN.Plg dated October 14, 1976, sentencing sellers to 6 months imprisonment for authentic deed forgery / Article 266 of the Criminal Code; issuance of Minister of Home Affairs Decree No. 550/Dja/1986 dated September 13, 1986; and actual physical possession of the land by heirs of \[Deceased landowner\] / \[Heir\] et al.

## Jurisprudence and Related Knowledge

### Statutory Regulations

- **Civil Code (KUHPerdata):** Articles 1320, 1338, 1365 (Torts / *Perbuatan Melawan Hukum*).
- **Criminal Code (KUHP):** Article 266 paragraphs (1) & (2) (Authentic Deed Forgery / Directing False Statements to be Entered).
- **Agrarian Regulations:** Minister of Home Affairs Regulation No. 6 of 1972 (Articles 12 *jo.* 14) & Government Regulation No. 10 of 1961 / Government Regulation No. 24 of 1997 on Land Registration.
- **Law No. 14 of 1970 *jo.* Law No. 14 of 1985 on the Supreme Court:** Cassation authority and independent adjudication powers.

### Established Supreme Court Jurisprudence

- Doctrine of Good Faith Purchaser: Legal protection principles are granted exclusively to honest and diligent buyers. Negligent buyers / those failing to research seller ownership status enjoy no legal protection.
- Supreme Court Decision No. 383 K/Sip/1971 & Supreme Court Decision No. 1080 K/Sip/1973: Validity of certificates and scope of certificate cancellations resulting from root title forgery.

## What Evidence Establishes / Does Not Establish

### Established by Evidence

- The original sellers (Mohamad Ali bin Nurdin & H. Abdul Hamid) were proven to hold no rights over the disputed land and were convicted under Palembang District Court Decision No. 684/1976/PN.Plg for authentic deed forgery / Article 266 of the Criminal Code.
- The Plaintiff (\[Party A\]) was proven negligent in purchasing the land by failing to examine the status and rights of the sellers, thus failing to qualify for legal protection as a good faith purchaser.
- Minister of Home Affairs Decree No. 550/Dja/1986 dated September 13, 1986, canceling SHM No. 169/18 Ilir was proven legally valid and not a tort.
- The Convention Plaintiff's Lawsuit is **REJECTED IN ITS ENTIRETY**.
- Defendant I's Reconvention Lawsuit is declared **INADMISSIBLE** (NO).

### Annulled (Palembang High Court Verdict)

- Palembang High Court considerations declaring the Plaintiff's lawsuit inadmissible (NO) due to non-joinder were entirely annulled by the Supreme Court as erroneous applications of procedural law.

## Uncertainties and Gaps

**Standard of Due Diligence for Pre-PP No. 24 of 1997 Land Purchasers:** This landmark ruling provides an exceptionally crucial legal precedent in Indonesia: Deeds of Sale and Purchase executed before Notaries/PPATs and Hak Milik Certificates do not automatically render buyers immune to legal challenges if initial transactions are executed negligently from unauthorized sellers. Buyers carry obligations to perform thorough examinations (*due care*) regarding physical and juridical histories of land prior to transactions.

## Frequently Asked Questions (GEO)

Is a land buyer who purchases before a PPAT protected by law if the seller's foundational root title stems from document forgery, and can BPN/Minister of Home Affairs cancel such land certificate?  
**Negligent buyers receive no legal protection, and BPN/Minister of Home Affairs possess authority to cancel certificates.** Pursuant to Supreme Court Jurisprudence No. 1816 K/Pdt/1989:

- Buyers failing to carefully examine the rights and ownership status of sellers are classified as negligent purchasers (*bad faith / negligent purchasers*) and deserve no legal protection, even if transactions occur before Notaries/PPATs.
- If certificates are issued based on root titles proven forged via criminal judgments (Article 266 KUHP), the Minister of Home Affairs / BPN is authorized to cancel such land certificates *ex* Minister of Home Affairs Regulation No. 6 of 1972, and such cancellation is legally valid and not a tort.
