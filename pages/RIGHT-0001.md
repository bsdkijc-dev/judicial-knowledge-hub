# RIGHT-0001

# Fundamental Rights

**Fundamental rights** are legally recognized rights and freedoms that constrain public authority and protect individuals and groups.

The precise catalogue, scope, limitations, and enforcement of fundamental rights depend on the applicable constitutional, statutory, and international legal framework.

**\[HUMAN VERIFICATION REQUIRED\]**

## Rights Relevant to Judicial Knowledge

Depending on the subject, relevant rights may include:

- Equality
- Non-discrimination
- Privacy
- Due process
- Fair trial
- Access to justice
- Freedom
- Human autonomy
- Freedom of expression
- Data protection

The legal status of each right should be verified within the relevant jurisdiction.

## Courts and Fundamental Rights

Courts may play an important role in interpreting, applying, protecting, and enforcing rights within their legal systems.

Specific judicial powers and standards must be established from authoritative sources.

## AI and Fundamental Rights

AI systems may affect fundamental rights through:

- Automated classification
- Profiling
- Decision support
- Data processing
- Predictive systems
- Risk assessment
- Content analysis

Relevant risks include:

- Discrimination
- Privacy intrusion
- Lack of transparency
- Incorrect decisions
- Unequal access
- Reduced human autonomy

## Privacy

AI systems may process substantial amounts of personal information.

Relevant governance issues include:

- Purpose limitation
- Data minimization
- Security
- Access control
- Retention
- Accountability

See:

[Privacy](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Privacy)

## Equality and Non-discrimination

AI systems may reproduce or amplify patterns contained in training or operational data.

Evaluation should therefore consider:

- Data quality
- Representation
- Error distribution
- Differential impact
- Bias

Specific legal standards require jurisdiction-specific analysis.

## Fair Trial

AI used in judicial processes may raise questions concerning:

- Notice
- Participation
- Impartiality
- Evidence
- Reasoned decisions
- Ability to challenge
- Human decision-making

## Human Autonomy

AI systems that influence consequential decisions may raise questions concerning human agency and meaningful human control.

The legal status and scope of concepts such as cognitive liberty or mental privacy require separate analysis.

## Rights Impact Assessment

For high-impact AI systems, a rights-oriented assessment may consider:

**SYSTEM** → **DATA** → **DECISION PROCESS** → **IMPACT** → **RIGHTS** → **MITIGATION**
