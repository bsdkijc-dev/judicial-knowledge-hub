# JPW-POLICY-000027

The **System for People's Procuratorates of China Framework** (Stable ID: `JPW-POLICY-000027`) is a national constitutional, statutory, and operational judicial policy framework establishing the status, structure, personnel qualifications, and legal supervision scope of the procuratorial branch in the People's Republic of China. Codified under Article 129 of the 1982 PRC Constitution, the Organic Law of People's Procuratorates (revised 1979), and the Public Procurators Law, the framework defines procuratorates as state organs for legal supervision exercising authority independently under vertical line leadership. As of December 31, 1997, the system comprised 3,841 procuratorial organs (including 1 Supreme People's Procuratorate, 31 Provincial, 397 Branch/City, 3,097 County, 161 Local Branches, 81 Military, and 72 Railway Procuratorates) and 221,912 total personnel. Note: Named entities *Cecep Mustafa* and *Reihan Abdan Syakur* are *Not found in the available sources*.

## Summary

The framework traces its origins to October 1, 1949 and the Provisional Organic Rules approved by Chairman Mao Zedong on December 20, 1949. It outlines six core structural pillars: (1) Constitutional Status, defining procuratorates as state organs for legal supervision responsible to People's Congresses and higher procuratorates; (2) Vertical Line Leadership, mandating that lower procuratorates operate under the leadership of higher procuratorates and the unified command of the SPP; (3) Organizational Hierarchy, establishing four general tiers plus special Military and Railway Procuratorates; (4) Procurator Qualifications & 12 Grades, enforcing the Public Procurators Law, public exams, nepotism avoidance rules, and 12 professional grades headed by the Procurator-in-Chief; (5) Four Supervision Targets, authorizing oversight over state organs, state functionaries, citizens, and foreigners; and (6) Six Content Domains of Legal Supervision, covering Law Discipline, Investigation, Criminal Trial, Executions, Civil Trial, and Administrative Trial.

## Legal Basis

Authority and execution derive from primary constitutional provisions, organic statutes, professional codes, and procedural laws:

- **Constitutional Charter:** Article 81 of the 1954 Constitution; Article 129 of the 1982 PRC Constitution.
- **Organic & Professional Statutes:** Organic Law of the People's Procuratorates (1954, revised 1979); Public Procurators Law of the PRC.
- **Procedural Codes:** Criminal Code & Criminal Procedure Code; Civil Procedure Code (Article 14); Administrative Procedure Code (Article 10).
- **Historical Directives:** Provisional Organic Rules approved by Chairman Mao Zedong (Dec 20, 1949).

## Background & Policy Problems

### Background

Initially patterned after Soviet legal models, the Supreme People's Procuratorial Administration was created alongside the PRC on October 1, 1949. The 1954 Constitution formally created the "People's Procuratorate" structure. Re-established under the 1979 Organic Law and 1982 Constitution, the system expanded into a national legal supervision network.

### Policy Problems

- Abuse of State Power & Official Malfeasance: Preventing treason, state security threats, and official crimes committed by state functionaries (dereliction of duty, bribery, corruption, abuse of power).
- Ensuring Uniform Enforcement of Laws: Overseeing state organs, police forces, and courts to maintain legal unity and eliminate local administrative interference.
- Procedural Legality Across Judicial Operations: Supervising police arrests, criminal trials, prison sentence executions, civil rulings, and administrative litigation.
- Professionalization & Nepotism Control: Establishing rigorous qualifications, public examinations, 12 professional grades, and strict conflict of interest/nepotism avoidance rules for procurators.

## Objectives

1\. Maintain national unity, the socialist legal system, and public order. 2. Protect socialist property and citizens' personal and democratic rights. 3. Supervise state functionaries to suppress corruption, bribery, and dereliction of duty. 4. Guarantee procedural legality in police arrests, criminal trials, prison sentences, civil rulings, and administrative proceedings.

## Scope & Implementation

### Scope

Applies across all levels of People's Procuratorates, public procurators, public security organs, courts, prisons, state functionaries, citizens, and foreigners in the PRC.

### Key Provisions

- **12 Professional Grades:** Ranging from Procurator-in-Chief (Procurator-General of SPP) down through 12th grade procurators.
- **Six Supervision Categories:** Codified operational rules for Law Discipline, Investigation, Criminal Trial, Executions, Civil Trial, and Administrative Trial.
- **Prohibition of Concurrent Posts:** Strict ban on procurators holding posts in People's Congress Standing Committees, administrative organs, courts, enterprises, or practicing as lawyers.

### Implementation

- **National Procuratorial Network:** Operating 1 SPP, 31 Provincial, 397 City/Branch, 3,097 County, 161 Local Branches, 81 Military, and 72 Railway Procuratorates.
- **Personnel Force:** Managing 221,912 total personnel (115,586 chief procurators and procurators) as of December 31, 1997.

## Policy Problems

- Detailed Procedural Gaps in Administrative Supervision: Translating Article 10 Administrative Procedure Law rules into specific practice.
- Local Administrative Interference: Protecting local procuratorates from local administrative organ influence.
- Preventing Official Corruption: Investigating malfeasance, bribery, and abuse of power among state functionaries.

## Reform

- Enacting the Public Procurators Law and public exam entry requirements.
- Implementing 12-grade professional rankings.
- Strengthening supervision over civil and administrative court judgments.

## Related Key Figures & Institutions

### Key Figures

- **Chairman Mao Zedong:** Approved the Provisional Organic Rules of the Supreme People's Procuratorial Administration on December 20, 1949.
- **Procurator-General of the SPP:** Highest procuratorial officer in the PRC, holding the grade of Procurator-in-Chief.
