# JPW-POLICY-000025

The **Outline of the Five-Year Reform Plan of the People's Courts (1999–2003)** (Stable ID: `JPW-POLICY-000025`) is a national judicial reform policy framework issued by the Supreme People's Court of the People's Republic of China establishing core reform principles, trial model reforms, judge empowerment, separation of filing/enforcement/supervision from trial operations, and administrative court management reforms. Analyzed by Prof. Xin Chunying (Deputy Director of the Institute of Law at the Chinese Academy of Social Sciences - CASS) in collaboration with IDE-JETRO, the policy operationalizes the 15th CPC Party Congress resolutions, the 2nd session of the 9th National People's Congress Resolution, and the 4th Constitutional Amendment ("run the country by law and construct a socialist country rule of law"). Note: Named entities *Cecep Mustafa* and *Reihan Abdan Syakur* are *Not found in the available sources*.

## Summary

The framework addresses the rapid expansion of litigation following China's market-oriented economic reforms, where 1997 court filings reached nearly 5 times the 1986 volume. It outlines six core structural pillars: (1) Constitutional Framing, establishing the goal of "shaping a stronger judicial power under the existing constitutional structure"; (2) Article 4 Five Principles, mandating Party leadership, the People's Congress system, independent trial according to law, national legal unity, and selective adaptation of foreign court management models; (3) Trial Model Reform, empowering individual presiding judges and collegiate benches while regulating the power of adjudication committees; (4) Procedural Separation, separating case filing, enforcement, and judicial supervision from trial divisions; (5) Overcoming Enforcement Crises, tackling the 50% unenforced civil/economic ruling rate and RMB 2,590 billion unexecuted award backlog recorded in mid-1999; and (6) Institutional Problem Agenda, addressing judge qualifications, appointment mechanisms, judicial local protectionism, and court financial arrangements.

## Legal Basis

Authority and execution derive from constitutional amendments, legislative resolutions, and central directives:

- **Constitutional Charter & Amendments:** Constitution of the People's Republic of China, including the 4th Constitutional Amendment enacted by the 2nd session of the 9th NPC ("run the country by law and construct a socialist country rule of law").
- **National People's Congress Resolutions:** Resolution on the Annual Report of the Supreme People's Court by the 2nd session of the 9th National People's Congress.
- **Party Directives:** Resolutions of the 15th National Congress of the Communist Party of China incorporating judicial reform into political reform.
- **Historical Central Directives:** Central Government Circular by the State Council on Strengthening People's Judicial Work (1950).

## Background & Policy Problems

### Background

Before the mid-1980s, Chinese courts operated primarily as an instrument for "proletariat dictatorship" under Mao Zedong's formula on social contradictions rather than acting as a neutral judgment organ. Economic opening, private business growth, and rising rights awareness led to explosive case growth by the late 1990s. Despite enacting 300+ laws and 9,000+ regulations by 1998, weak enforcement persisted, with only 20% of laws implemented and 85,000 unenforced court rulings accumulating RMB 2,590 billion by mid-1999. The Supreme People's Court enacted the Five-Year Reform Plan to meet these challenges.

### Policy Problems

- Severe Enforcement Crisis ("Execution Difficulty"): 50% of civil and economic court rulings were unenforced or delayed, accumulating massive unexecuted award backlogs.
- Discrepancy Between Written Law and Practice: Weak real-life implementation of promulgated statutory laws.
- Spreading Judicial Corruption & Local Protectionism: Financial dependency of local courts on local governments causing external interference and improper judge-litigant relations.
- Over-Centralization in Adjudication Committees: Over-reliance on administrative adjudication committees rather than empowering presiding trial judges.

## Objectives

1\. Shape a stronger judicial power under the existing constitutional structure. 2. Align court management mechanisms with market economy demands and due process. 3. Empower individual judges and collegiate benches while restricting adjudication committee intervention. 4. Separate case filing, enforcement, and supervision from trial operations. 5. Guarantee equal protection of client rights under the rule of law.

## Scope & Implementation

### Scope

Applies across all levels of People's Courts (Supreme People's Court, Higher Courts, Intermediate Courts, Basic Courts), judicial administrators, judges, and trial divisions in China.

### Key Provisions

- **Five Principles (Article 4):** Mandatory adherence to Party leadership, People's Congress system, independent trial, legal unity, and selective foreign model adoption.
- **Judge & Bench Empowerment:** Granting direct decision-making authority to presiding trial judges and collegiate benches.
- **Functional Division Separation:** Establishing independent court divisions for filing, trial, enforcement, and supervision.

### Implementation

- **Supreme People's Court Issuance:** Promulgating the Five-Year Reform Plan across national court hierarchies.
- **Trial Model Execution:** Transitioning trial procedures and reforming internal administrative management.

## Policy Problems

- Enforcement Backlog ("Execution Difficulty"): RMB 2,590 billion in unenforced court awards.
- Judicial Local Protectionism: Financial and administrative interference by local governments.
- Judicial Corruption: Loss of public trust due to improper judge-litigant relations.

## Reform

- Restructuring internal court administration and trial divisions.
- Reforming judge recruitment, appointment, and qualification standards.
- Establishing independent enforcement mechanism divisions.
