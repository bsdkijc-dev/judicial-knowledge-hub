# JPW-WORK-JIPLP-ED-2006-JPL087

**Editorial: IP litigation, the new money-spinner** is a legal editorial authored by [Jeremy Phillips](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Jeremy_Phillips) and published in the [Journal of Intellectual Property Law & Practice](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Journal_of_Intellectual_Property_Law_%26_Practice) (Vol. 1, No. 8) by [Oxford University Press](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Oxford_University_Press) in 2006 [Editorial Publication](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Editorial_Publication) \[1–10\].

## Summary

This editorial reviews the commercial phenomenon where intellectual property (IP) litigation yields substantial financial returns and media exposure for both winning and losing parties (\*win-win situation\*) [IP Litigation Benefits](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/IP_Litigation_Benefits) \[1–10\]. The author cites as prime examples \*The Da Vinci Code\* copyright dispute and Tesco's parallel import trade mark clash with Levi Strauss [IP Case Examples](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/IP_Case_Examples) \[1–10\].

## Legal Basis

- **Fair Dealing (UK Copyright Law)**: A legal doctrine permitting excerpts from litigated texts and judicial decisions in academic commentaries or trial review books without infringing copyright [Fair Dealing](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Fair_Dealing) \[1–10\].
- **Regional Trade Mark Exhaustion Policy (European Union)**: Regulations prohibiting unauthorized parallel imports of branded goods from outside the European Economic Area (EEA), as enforced in *Levi Strauss v Tesco* (Joined Cases C-414/99 to C-416/99) [Trade Mark Exhaustion](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Trade_Mark_Exhaustion) \[1–10\].

## Background

IP litigation is widely recognized for its exorbitant costs, which place heavy financial demands on litigants [Litigation Costs](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Litigation_Costs) \[1–10\]. However, intensive media interest throughout proceedings frequently generates promotional value far exceeding the legal fees incurred [Judicial Publicity](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Judicial_Publicity) \[1–10\].

## Objectives

1.  Demonstrate how IP litigation provides direct and indirect economic rewards for participating parties [Financial Returns](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Financial_Returns) \[1–10\].
2.  Analyze the impact of trial-generated publicity on consumer product sales [Product Marketing](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Product_Marketing) \[1–10\].
3.  Evaluate corporate reputation outcomes within EU trade mark exhaustion disputes [Corporate Image](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Corporate_Image) \[1–10\].

## Key Provisions

- **Baigent and Leigh v Random House Case**: A copyright infringement suit brought by the authors of *The Holy Blood and the Holy Grail* against the publisher of *The Da Vinci Code* was dismissed, yet sales for both books surged dramatically [Da Vinci Code Dispute](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Da_Vinci_Code_Dispute) \[1–10\].
- **Judicial Encrypted Code (Smithy Code)**: Mr Justice Peter Smith inserted a secret code within his judgment text, attracting widespread public and media fascination [Smithy Code](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Smithy_Code) \[1–10\].
- **Levi Strauss v Tesco Case**: Although Tesco lost its suit over gray-market Mexican jeans, it established a favorable public reputation as a "Champion of the Consumer" opposing European differential pricing [Tesco Levi Dispute](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Tesco_Levi_Dispute) \[1–10\].

## Policy Issues

- **Burden of Judicial Expenses**: The need for procedural streamlining in court litigation, despite secondary promotional benefits [Judicial Efficiency](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Judicial_Efficiency) \[1–10\].
- **Consumer Welfare vs Trade Mark Monopolies**: Conflict between brand owners' rights to control supply channels and consumer demands for competitive pricing [Distribution Conflict](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Distribution_Conflict) \[1–10\].
