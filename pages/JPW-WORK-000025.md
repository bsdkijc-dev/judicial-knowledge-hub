## 4. POLICY ANALYSIS

- **Background**: \[FACT\] Dialnet has supported open access (\*acceso abierto\*) to scientific literature since its inception; however, not all documents indexed within Dialnet's bibliographic database are directly accessible in full-text format.
- **Policy Problem**: \[FACT\] Restricted authorization permissions from publishers and/or authors of scientific documents prevent Dialnet in certain cases from hosting full-text attachments, limiting available records to bibliographic references and journal summaries/table of contents (\*sumario\*).
- **Legal/Policy Gap**: Not found in the available sources.
- **Objectives**: \[FACT\] Provide access to full-text scientific documents for all users (both registered and unregistered across Dialnet and Dialnet Plus) through secure, structured download mechanisms.
- **Implementation**: \[FACT\]
  - **Two Document Access Pathways**:
    - External links redirecting to external host servers or web pages where the document is stored, marked by a "Texto Completo" arrow icon.
    - Direct PDF file downloads hosted locally on Dialnet servers.
  - **Summary Color-Coding System**:
    - Gray color: Indicates articles linked to external website pages.
    - Yellow color: Indicates PDF files hosted directly on Dialnet servers.
- **Evidence & Impact**: \[FACT\] Full-text document access is supported across all scientific literature categories including journal articles, collective book chapters, doctoral theses, books, working papers (\*documentos de trabajo\*), and conference proceedings (\*actas de congresos\*).
- **Reform & Unresolved Issues**: Not found in the available sources.

## 6. MEDIAWIKI OUTPUT

## Summary

**Full-Text Document Access in Dialnet** (*Centro de asistencia de Dialnet - Acceso a documentos a texto completo*) (Stable ID: **JPW-WORK-000025**) is a service policy and operational guideline provided by the Dialnet Help Center (\*Centro de asistencia de Dialnet\*). The policy outlines the procedures and terms for accessing full-text scientific documents (\*texto completo\*). While Dialnet is committed to supporting open access (\*acceso abierto\*), document availability depends on explicit authorization from publishers and authors. Registered and unregistered users across Dialnet and Dialnet Plus can retrieve documents (journal articles, doctoral theses, books, working papers) via external links or direct downloads from Dialnet servers.

## Legal Basis

- **Formal Statutory Basis**: Not found in the available sources.

## Background

Since its creation, Dialnet has supported the open access movement for scientific literature. However, not all academic works indexed in Dialnet's directories contain attached full-text files due to copyright restrictions or missing publisher authorizations.

## Objectives

1\. Facilitate open dissemination of scientific literature (\*acceso abierto\*). 2. Provide a consistent full-text access mechanism for users across Dialnet and Dialnet Plus. 3. Offer clear visual transparency regarding document storage locations.

## Scope

The scope of accessible scientific documents includes:

- Journal articles (\*artículos de revistas\*)
- Collective book chapters (\*capítulos de libros colectivos\*)
- Doctoral theses (\*tesis doctorales\*)
- Books (\*libros\*)
- Working papers (\*documentos de trabajo\*)
- Conference proceedings (\*actas de congresos\*)

## Key Provisions

- **Authorization Requirement**: Full-text files are provided only when authorized by publishers and/or authors; without authorization, records display bibliographic references and summaries only.
- **User Access Terms**: All users across Dialnet and Dialnet Plus (registered or unregistered) may access available full-text documents free of charge.

## Implementation

Full-text retrieval is executed through two methods: 1. **External Links (Enlaces Externos)**: Outer links marked with the text "Texto Completo" and an arrow symbol. 2. **Direct Downloads (Descarga Directa)**: Direct PDF downloads hosted on Dialnet servers. 3. **Summary Color Coding**: Gray summaries indicate links to external host sites, whereas yellow summaries indicate files hosted directly on Dialnet servers.

## Policy Problems

- Absence of publisher/author authorization for a portion of indexed bibliographic records.
