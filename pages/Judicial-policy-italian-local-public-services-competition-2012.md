# Judicial-policy-italian-local-public-services-competition-2012

The **Italian Local Public Services Competition Regulation Framework** is a regulatory and policy framework governing local public service markets in Italy. Analyzed by Fabio Merusi, it evaluates the implementation of the "competition for the market" model, limitations on in-house company exceptions, and market oversight by the Italian Competition Authority (Antitrust Authority) [^1].

## Summary

Authored by [Fabio Merusi](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Fabio_Merusi), Professor of Administrative Law at "Guglielmo Marconi" University, this study examines the regulatory dynamics of Italian local public services following the 2008/2009 reforms and the June 2011 referendum [^2]. Due to the historical fragmentation of Italian municipalities originating from the 1859 Rattazzi Law, European Union law introduced "competition for the market" through periodic tenders rather than direct competition within the market [^3].

## Legal Basis

The framework relies on national legislation, constitutional rulings, and popular referendums:

- **[Article 23-bis of Law No. 112/2008](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Article_23-bis_of_Law_No._112/2008)**: Initial provision establishing competitive tendering obligations for local public services [^4].
- **[June 2011 Referendum](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/June_2011_Referendum) & [Law No. 148/2011](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Law_No._148/2011)**: Popular repeal of Article 23-bis, which was subsequently re-enacted in near-identical form via Legislative Decree No. 138/2011 (Law No. 148/2011) [^5].
- **[Article 114 & Repeal of Article 130 of the Italian Constitution](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Article_114_%26_Repeal_of_Article_130_of_the_Italian_Constitution)**: Constitutional foundation for local autonomy, creating legal friction with the Antitrust Authority's ex-ante oversight powers [^6].
- **[Constitutional Court Judgment No. 326/2010](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_Judgment_No._326/2010)**: Jurisprudence establishing market competition as a horizontal state legislative competence [^7].

## Background

Implementing the EU competition model encountered significant structural obstacles in Italy because fragmented local municipalities prevented the formation of viable relevant markets [^8]. Consequently, municipalities frequently established controlled joint-stock companies (in-house companies) to bypass competitive tendering and public procurement rules [^9].

## Objectives

- Ensure transparency, impartiality, and efficiency in managing local public services of economic interest [^10].
- Restrict direct, non-competitive awards to in-house companies exclusively to exceptional economic or geographical situations [^11].
- Secure high-quality private participation in public-private joint ventures by enforcing a minimum 40% private equity requirement [^12].

## Scope

The scope of this framework includes Italian administrative law, European Union competition law, public procurement rules ([Legislative Decree 163/2006](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Legislative_Decree_163/2006)), regional autonomy guarantees, and Constitutional Court jurisprudence [^13].

## Core Provisions & Supervisory Mechanisms

- **Competition for the Market**: Public service operations must be awarded via open competitive tenders, requiring the separation of network ownership from operational management (unbundling) [^14].
- **Antitrust Authority Approval**: Municipalities seeking to retain an in-house company must publish market analysis justifications and obtain Antitrust Authority approval, except for services valued under €200,000 [^15].
- **Private Partner Requirements**: Private partners in joint ventures must be selected through public competitive procedures, perform active managerial roles, and hold at least 40% equity share [^16].

## Implementation

Implementation is carried out through municipal tender drafting, calculating investment amortization schedules to set contract durations, and market supervision by the Antitrust Authority [^17].

## Policy Issues

- **Constitutional Oversight Conflicts**: Ex-ante Antitrust Authority review was challenged as an unconstitutional infringement on local autonomy under Article 114 following the repeal of Article 130 [^18].
- **Subversion of the 2011 Referendum**: Re-enacting competition rules shortly after the popular referendum created prolonged regulatory instability for service providers [^19].

## Reforms & Modifications

The framework drives ongoing reforms to clarify tender termination criteria, resolve asset amortization schedules, and settle property transfer disputes upon contract expiration [^20].
