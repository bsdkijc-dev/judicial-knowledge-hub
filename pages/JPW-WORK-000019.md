#### 4. POLICY ANALYSIS

**Background**: \[FACT\] Legal developments in Spain, Portugal, and the EU in mid-2024 were shaped by digital regulatory transformation, corporate sustainability reporting obligations, housing market reform, and evolving labor rights dynamics. Key issues analyzed included the risk of algorithmic discrimination and the efficiency of bias-mitigation models in AI systems (such as Google Gemini and DALL-E-2); clarifying the legal nature of unit-linked life insurance contracts as hybrid/complex products and their use as financing collateral; implementing new sustainability-statement reporting standards (ESRS 1) under the CSRD Directive; the practical application and new procedural requirements of Law 12/2023 on Housing in lease disputes and mortgage enforcement; the efficiency of accordion-operation capital transactions in public limited companies; conflict between the Spanish Workers' Statute and Article 7 of ILO Convention 158 regarding pre-dismissal hearing obligations; and controversy over employer authority versus workers' rights in setting schedules and days off under flexible work regimes in Portugal.

**Policy Problem**: \[FACT\]

1.  *Algorithmic Discrimination*: The "black box" phenomenon, proxy discrimination (e.g., postal code or salary), automation bias, and overcompensation failures through diversity prompts lacking objective criteria.
2.  *Unit-Linked Insurance*: Ambiguous definition and classification of unit-linked products across the EU, disputes over the presence of biometric risk/actuarial technical basis, and investment-value fluctuation risk when used as loan collateral.
3.  *ESRS 1 Reporting*: Extreme administrative burden from 1,102 sustainability reporting data points, complexity in applying the "double materiality" principle (impact and financial), and the absence of a settled reasonable-assurance audit criterion.
4.  *Housing Law*: Ambiguity in the "large-scale landlord" (*gran tenedor*) registration procedure, delays in eviction enforcement due to mandatory vulnerability-study reports, rent price caps in "tension zones," and market shift toward seasonal/temporary leases.
5.  *Accordion Operation*: The need to simplify BORME publication formalities and the 1-month creditor-opposition waiting period when the resulting capital does not decrease.
6.  *ILO Hearing Right*: The absence of a pre-dismissal hearing rule in the Spanish Workers' Statute for non-union-represented workers, prompting labor courts to annul dismissals under the "conventionality control" doctrine.
7.  *Flexible Work in Portugal*: Conflicting interpretations between lower/appellate courts and the Portuguese Supreme Court over whether workers may independently determine fixed Saturday-Sunday days off under a flexible work scheme.

**Legal/Policy Gap**: \[FACT\] The absence of a harmonized definition of "unit-linked" in EU insurance law; the lack of sanction regulation and reasonable-assurance audit standards for ESRS reporting; the absence of protection for small landlords (non-*gran tenedor*) facing economic vulnerability when tenants default; the absence of an explicit pre-dismissal hearing rule under Article 55 ET in Spain; and ambiguity in the text of Article 56 of the Portuguese Labor Code regarding authority to set days off.

**Objectives**: \[FACT\] Promote transparent, human-rights-oriented AI governance; provide legal certainty for unit-linked insurance product structures used as collateral; ensure faithful representation and comparability of corporate sustainability reports; strengthen housing's social function without stifling investment; facilitate corporate capital restructuring; guarantee workers' right to a defense before dismissal; and balance workers' family-life reconciliation with employers' managerial authority.

**Implementation**: \[FACT\]

- *AI Governance*: Use of red-teaming methods, RLHF, and exploration of Constitutional AI.
- *Unit-Linked Insurance*: Establishing a minimum death-risk portion (approximately 5%) per DGT and LIRPF criteria, and using pledge instruments or designating an irrevocable beneficiary.
- *ESRS 1*: Applying internal due-diligence processes and double-materiality assessment per EFRAG guidance.
- *Housing Law*: Implementing a "large-scale landlord" certificate registration system via the Association of Registrars and reference-index verification by the Ministry of Housing.
- *Accordion Operation*: Using publication and opposition-period exceptions under Article 334.2 LSC and DGSJFP jurisprudential doctrine.
- *Spanish Labor*: Application of "conventionality control" by the TSJ of the Balearic Islands (Ruling 68/2023), directly applying Article 7 of ILO Convention 158.
- *Portuguese Labor*: Enforcement of STJ Ruling 12/10/2022, granting workers the right to fixed Saturday-Sunday days off to care for children.

**Evidence & Impact**: \[FACT\] Google Gemini's diversity-prompt failure in early 2024, triggering a temporary suspension of its image feature; DGT and CJEU rulings (González Alonso and Towarzystwo cases) confirming unit-linked insurance status where biometric risk is present; mandatory ESRS reporting for large European companies beginning 1 January 2024; a 15% decline in permanent rental listings and a 56% surge in seasonal rentals following the Housing Law; and legal uncertainty in Spain from differing criteria among Superior Courts of Justice regarding the pre-dismissal hearing obligation.

**Reform & Unresolved Issues**: \[FACT\] Full implementation of the EU AI Act and additional risk-criteria guidance; adoption of ESRS reasonable-assurance audit standards by the European Commission by 2028; resolution of 8 pending unconstitutionality challenges against the Housing Law before the Spanish Constitutional Court; formulation of unifying jurisprudence (*casación para la unificación de doctrina*) by the Spanish Supreme Court on Article 7 of ILO Convention 158; and doctrinal criticism of the Portuguese STJ's corrective interpretation as potentially violating separation-of-powers principles.

#### 5. GEO/AI QUESTIONS

    - *Factual Answer*: \[FACT\] Article 20 of the Spanish Constitution of 1978.
3.  **Publisher/Authority**: Who is the official publisher of this document, and what is its legal registration number?
    - *Factual Answer*: \[FACT\] Pérez-Llorca Abogados, S.L.P., Madrid (Legal Deposit: M-7551-2019, ISSN: 2792-2871).
4.  **Related Figures**: Who is the human rights expert and Chair of OLAF's Supervisory Committee who wrote the analysis of AI algorithmic bias in this edition?
    - *Factual Answer*: \[FACT\] Teresa Anjinho.
5.  **Scope**: What are the three main concepts analyzed in the European Sustainability Reporting Standard (ESRS 1) in this edition?
    - *Factual Answer*: \[FACT\] The concept of double materiality (impact and financial), due diligence, and the qualitative characteristics of sustainability information.
6.  **Policy Implementation**: What is the minimum death-risk requirement for a unit-linked contract to be recognized as life insurance under Spanish DGT and LIRPF criteria?
    - *Factual Answer*: \[FACT\] The contract must include an additional capital-at-risk portion of at least 5% above the investment value of the mathematical provision throughout the contract term.
7.  **Policy Problem**: Why can the accordion operation procedure (simultaneous capital reduction and increase) be exempted from the BORME publication requirement and the 1-month creditor-opposition period?
    - *Factual Answer*: \[FACT\] Because the operation is treated as a single unified whole; if the resulting capital equals or exceeds the initial capital, creditors' security position is not harmed (creditor neutrality).
8.  **Legal Relationship**: What was the impact of the TSJ of the Balearic Islands ruling (Ruling 68/2023) on disciplinary dismissal procedures in Spain?
    - *Factual Answer*: \[FACT\] The court applied conventionality control and directly enforced Article 7 of ILO Convention 158, declaring a disciplinary dismissal without a pre-dismissal hearing unfair, even though the Spanish Workers' Statute does not explicitly require it.
9.  **Reform**: How does Law 12/2023 on Housing change the formal requirements for mortgage enforcement and eviction applications filed by "large-scale landlords"?
    - *Factual Answer*: \[FACT\] Large-scale landlords must attach an asset ownership certificate, declare the property's status as a primary residence, and prove they have requested an economic vulnerability study and mediation process through the administration before filing suit.
10. **Evidence/Case Study**: How did the Portuguese Supreme Court (STJ 12/10/2022) rule on the flexible-work-schedule dispute for workers with children under 12?
    - *Factual Answer*: \[FACT\] The Portuguese STJ ruled that the right to a flexible schedule includes the worker's authority to set fixed Saturday and Sunday days off to support family life, overriding the employer's managerial authority.

## Summary

**Pérez-Llorca Legal Journal (Issue No. 11, May 2024)** presents strategic doctrinal and judicial policy analysis: an Article 20 opinion piece on algorithmic discrimination, data bias, and the "black box" impact of artificial intelligence, by former Portuguese Deputy Ombudsman Teresa Anjinho; a technical study of the legal nature of unit-linked life insurance contracts and their use as credit collateral, by Jesús Almarcha; a comprehensive analysis of the double-materiality and due-diligence concepts in European Sustainability Reporting (ESRS 1) under the CSRD Directive, by José María de Paz; an evaluation of the procedural requirements and market impact of Law 12/2023 on Housing, by Aránzazu Barandiarán and Isabella Hardiman; simplification of the accordion-operation procedure for public limited companies, by Laura Llanos and Marta Monroy; application of conventionality control regarding the pre-dismissal hearing obligation under Article 7 of ILO Convention 158, by Manel Hernández Montuenga; and a comparative analysis of a Portuguese Supreme Court ruling (STJ 12/10/2022) on the right to flexible work schedules for workers with children, by Inês Arruda and Miguel Riço dos Santos.

## Background

2024 was marked by a leap in EU regulation of technology risk, mandatory environmental transparency, and evolving social-rights disputes in Spain and Portugal. The emergence of generative AI models (such as Google Gemini) exposed the dangers of algorithmic bias and automation bias. In finance, the complexity of unit-linked life insurance structures required clarity on biometric risk boundaries. In ESG, ESRS 1's entry into force compelled companies to adopt double-materiality analysis. Meanwhile, Spain's property market faced procedural uncertainty from the Housing Law, companies sought smoother accordion-operation transactions, Spanish labor courts began testing dismissal compliance against ILO Convention 158, and Portuguese courts debated the limits of family-friendly schedule flexibility.

## Objectives

1\. Highlight the importance of independent oversight of AI algorithmic bias. 2. Provide doctrinal analysis establishing unit-linked insurance status and its use as collateral. 3. Guide companies in preparing sustainability statements under ESRS 1 standards. 4. Identify procedural obstacles and market dynamics arising from Law 12/2023 on Housing. 5. Facilitate corporate accordion operations without excessive formality. 6. Affirm the importance of pre-dismissal hearings in disciplinary terminations per ILO standards. 7. Clarify workers' rights regarding flexible scheduling and days off in Portugal.

## Scope

The study covers seven main pillars: 1. *Algorithmic Discrimination*: Data bias, proxy discrimination, automation bias, RLHF, red-teaming, and Constitutional AI. 2. *Unit-Linked Insurance*: Investment component versus biometric risk, DGT criteria (5% additional capital), and collateral forms (pledge, irrevocable beneficiary, assignment). 3. *ESRS 1 Reporting*: Information requirements (1,102 data points), double materiality, due diligence, and five qualitative characteristics. 4. *Housing Law*: LEC procedural requirements, large-scale landlord criteria, vulnerability studies, rent price limits, and the shift to temporary leasing. 5. *Accordion Operation*: Simultaneous capital reduction and increase, exemption from balance-sheet audit, and exemption from creditor opposition under Article 334.2 LSC. 6. *ILO Hearing Right*: Article 7 of ILO Convention 158, Article 55 ET, and enforcement of conventionality control by the TSJ of the Balearic Islands. 7. *Portuguese Flexible Schedule*: Article 56 of the Labor Code, STJ Ruling 12/10/2022, the right to set Saturday-Sunday days off, and criticism of separation-of-powers concerns.

## Key Provisions

- **Handling of Algorithmic Bias**: Using diversity prompts without objective criteria risks shifting discrimination onto other protected groups.
- **Unit-Linked Insurance Requirements**: A contract must have an actuarial technical basis and a minimum 5% death-risk capital portion to be recognized as life insurance.
- **ESRS 1 Double-Materiality Principle**: Companies must report sustainability issues meeting either the impact-materiality or financial-materiality criterion.
- **Prerequisites for Large-Scale Landlord Claims**: A large-scale landlord must attach an ownership registration certificate and proof of a vulnerability-study request before filing an eviction/mortgage-enforcement claim.
- **Publication Exemption for Accordion Operations**: If the resulting capital equals or exceeds the initial capital, BORME publication and the creditor-opposition period are not required.
- **Requirement of Pre-Dismissal Hearing (ILO Convention 158)**: A disciplinary dismissal without a prior opportunity for written defense may be declared unfair.
- **Portuguese Flexible Work-Schedule Right**: A worker caring for a child under 12 may request fixed Saturday-Sunday days off under a flexible work scheme.

## Implementation

Implementation occurs through AEPD and AI regulator oversight, DGSFP and EIOPA solvency oversight, EFRAG sustainability reporting, registrar certification via the Association of Registrars, and judicial enforcement by the Supreme Court, the TSJ of the Balearic Islands, and the Supreme Court of Justice of Portugal.

## Policy Problems

- Lack of algorithmic transparency protected by trade secrecy (the "black box" effect).
- Divergent interpretation of unit-linked boundaries among EU Member States.
- Administrative fatigue for companies collecting 1,102 ESRS 1 data points.
- Delays in eviction proceedings and mass shift of rental units to the temporary market due to the Housing Law.
- Legal uncertainty in Spain from the absence of a pre-dismissal hearing rule under Article 55 ET.
- Conflict between workers' flexible-schedule rights and employers' managerial authority in Portugal.

## Reform

- Passage of Delegated Regulation (EU) 2023/2772 (ESRS).
- Enactment of Law 12/2023 on the Right to Housing.
- Consolidation of DGSJFP jurisprudence simplifying accordion operations.
- Application of the conventionality control doctrine regarding ILO Convention 158.
