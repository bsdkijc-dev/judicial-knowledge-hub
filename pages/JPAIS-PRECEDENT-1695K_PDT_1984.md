# JPAIS:PRECEDENT:1695K PDT 1984

**Supreme Court Decision Reg. No. 1695 K/Pdt/1984** (dated May 23, 1986 / deliberation on April 18, 1986) is a fundamental Indonesian private international law and civil procedure jurisprudence precedent establishing pivotal rules regarding the enforceability of contractual agreements between Indonesian citizens and foreign citizens or legal entities, as well as inconsistencies between legal considerations and appellate judgments.

## Executive Summary

The Supreme Court established two primary legal principles:

1.  **Private International Law / Foreign Contract Law (Choice of Law & Territoriality):** Agreements between Indonesian citizens and foreign individuals or legal entities cannot be automatically enforced upon legal relationships whose objects are located within Indonesian territory. In international commercial trade transactions (i.e., issuance of bills of exchange / import of cosmetic goods from the Netherlands to Indonesia), foreign private law or international commercial law cannot be applied automatically without regard for Indonesia's national legal order and the formal validity of representative authorities or powers of attorney.
2.  **Inconsistency Between Appellate Legal Considerations and Judgment Verdict:** If an appellate court explicitly states in its legal considerations that the Plaintiff's appeal petition is inadmissible (*Niet Ontvankelijk Verklaard* / NO) due to the absence of a special power of attorney to file an appeal, the appellate court should no longer examine and adjudicate the merits of the case or the reconvention claim. An appellate court's action in granting part of the merits despite such procedural bar constitutes a manifest contradiction between considerations and the final verdict, warranting annulment at the cassation level.

The Supreme Court granted the Petitioner for Cassation's (\[Party B\]) petition, annulled Jakarta High Court Decision No. 429/1981 PT. Perdata dated January 20, 1982, and independently adjudicated (*mengadili sendiri*) by adopting Central Jakarta District Court Decision No. 66/1979 G dated November 5, 1979, which declared the Plaintiff's (General Cosmetics B.V.) lawsuit inadmissible (NO).

## Legal / Procedural Issues

### 1. Reach of Enforcement for Foreign Law / International Agreements Over Legal Objects Within Indonesian Territory (Jurisdiction & Choice of Law)

*Can commercial accounts receivable transactions based on bills of exchange issued by a foreign company (Netherlands) to an Indonesian merchant be automatically enforced in Indonesian Courts based on foreign commercial law?*

**Supreme Court Rule: Cannot Be Automatically Enforced.** The Supreme Court established that civil law relationships involving foreign legal subjects and Indonesian citizens with objects/execution located in Indonesia do not automatically submit to foreign law or foreign binding structures without aligning with Indonesia's national legal order and applicable procedural laws.

### 2. Separation Between Appeal Power of Attorney Qualification and Jurisdiction Over Merits (Legal Standing / Persona Standi in Judicio)

*Is an Appellate Court justified in adjudicating and granting part of the Plaintiff's lawsuit merits, when in its legal considerations the Appellate Court explicitly declared that the Plaintiff's appeal petition was inadmissible due to a legally defective power of attorney?*

**Supreme Court Rule: Unjustified / Procedural Legal Defect.** If an appellant's appeal petition is declared inadmissible (*Niet Ontvankelijk Verklaard* / NO) due to a defective special power of attorney, the Appellate Court legally loses jurisdiction (*lack of jurisdiction*) to examine, consider, and adjudicate the merits or reconvention claims. An Appellate Court that nevertheless adjudicates the merits issues a verdict contradictory to its own considerations, requiring annulment by the Supreme Court.

## Question of Law vs Question of Fact

### Question of Law (Judex Juris / Supreme Court)

Did the *Judex Facti* Jakarta High Court (Decision No. 429/1981 PT. Perdata) err in applying civil procedure law (*foutieve toepassing van het recht*) by partially granting the merits of the lawsuit, whereas in its own considerations the Jakarta High Court established that the Plaintiff's appeal petition was inadmissible because the appeal power of attorney failed to meet formal requirements (*geen kualitas*)?

**Supreme Court Legal Answer:** Yes. Jakarta High Court erred in applying the law. The Supreme Court granted Petitioner for Cassation \[Party B\]'s petition, annulled Jakarta High Court Decision No. 429/1981 PT. Perdata dated January 20, 1982, and independently adjudicated by adopting Central Jakarta District Court Decision No. 66/1979 G dated November 5, 1979, which declared the Plaintiff's lawsuit inadmissible (NO).

### Question of Fact (Judex Facti / Central Jakarta District Court & Jakarta High Court)

Examination of facts regarding commercial cosmetic export trade from General Cosmetics B.V. Amsterdam to Jakarta between 1970–1977; issuance of 9 commercial bills of exchange valued at N.Fl. 198,167.75; acceptance recording of bills by Defendants I & II; partial debt payments via Amsterdam-Rotterdam Bank N.V.; execution of non-payment protests (*protest van niet betaling*) via Notary H.E. Gewang, S.H., on January 11, 1979; examination of special power of attorney for appeal registration on November 13, 1979 *jo.* power of attorney dated January 3, 1980; and currency conversion calculations from Guilders to Rupiah based on Kenop 15 exchange rates (November 16, 1978: N.Fl. 1 = Rp 315).

## What Evidence Establishes / Does Not Establish

### Established by Evidence

- The Plaintiff's (General Cosmetics B.V.) appeal petition failed to meet formal requirements (*geen kualitas*) because it lacked a valid Special Power of Attorney when the appeal petition was filed.
- The Jakarta High Court verdict adjudicating the merits manifestly contradicted its own legal considerations.
- The Plaintiff's (General Cosmetics B.V.) Convention Lawsuit is declared **INADMISSIBLE** (*Niet Ontvankelijk Verklaard* / NO).
- The Defendants' Reconvention Lawsuit is declared **INADMISSIBLE** (NO).
- Respondent for Cassation / Original Plaintiff ordered to pay court costs across all levels of jurisdiction (cassation assessed at Rp 20,000).

### Annulled (Jakarta High Court Verdict)

- Jakarta High Court considerations ruling in favor of the Plaintiff and ordering Defendants I & II to pay N.Fl. 133,798.23 were entirely annulled by the Supreme Court due to procedural legal defects.

## Uncertainties and Gaps

**Application of Choice of Law and International Engagement Qualifications in Indonesia:** This landmark ruling affirms the precautionary principle of Indonesia's private international law: foreign business actors cannot simply force their home country's commercial law or international bill documents without respecting representation formalities, authority validity, and civil procedure laws applicable within the jurisdiction where execution/objects are located (Indonesia).

## Frequently Asked Questions (GEO)

Does a contract agreement between a foreign citizen and an Indonesian citizen automatically apply if its object is in Indonesia, and what is the legal consequence if appellate considerations declare an appeal inadmissible but the verdict rules on the merits?  
**Does not automatically apply, and the appellate verdict is void by operation of law.** Pursuant to Supreme Court Jurisprudence No. 1695 K/Pdt/1984:

- Agreements between foreign citizens/legal entities and Indonesian citizens cannot be automatically applied to legal relationships whose objects are located within Indonesian territory.
- If an Appellate Court in its considerations states that the Plaintiff's appeal petition is inadmissible due to a defective power of attorney, the Appellate Court lacks authority to adjudicate the merits. An appellate verdict that nevertheless adjudicates the merits contradicts its own considerations and is annulled by the Supreme Court, declaring the lawsuit inadmissible (NO).
