## Overview

Supreme Court Decision No. 44 K/AG/1998 dated February 19, 1999, establishes an essential legal principle in Islamic Civil Law regarding divorce based on continuous disputes and quarrels. The Supreme Court outlined that when continuous and irreconcilable quarrels are proven based on witness testimony and valid evidence during trial, and both parties have separated domiciles while reconciliation efforts have failed, a divorce lawsuit initiated by a wife (\*cerai gugat\*) may be granted.

## Case Background

The Original Plaintiff / Petitioner for Cassation (\[Party A\], \[age removed\], beauty salon owner) and the Original Defendant / Respondent for Cassation (\[Party B\], \[age removed\], on-call driver) were husband and wife legally married on February 26, 1983 (Marriage Certificate of the Office of Religious Affairs (\*KUA\*) of Kras Subdistrict, Kediri, No. 654/37/1983) and blessed with 3 children. Starting in January 1997, their household became disharmonious because the Defendant refused to maintain steady employment, frequently became angry when advised to work, and arbitrarily forced the closure of the Plaintiff's salon business, leading to a police report filed at the Kras Sector Police (\*Polsek\*). The Plaintiff and Defendant subsequently separated their domiciles.

The Kediri Regency Religious Court (Decision No. 455/Pdt.G/1997/PA.Kab.Kdr dated June 11, 1997) granted the Plaintiff's lawsuit and ruled for a divorce via single \*raj'i talak\*. However, the Surabaya High Religious Court (Decision No. 142/Pdt.G/1997/PTA.Sby dated October 7, 1997) annulled the Kediri Religious Court decision and rejected the Plaintiff's lawsuit on the grounds that witness statements lacked clarity and police report documents possessed no probative weight. Dissatisfied with the appellate ruling, the Plaintiff filed a cassation petition to the Supreme Court.

## Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

- **Ratio Decidendi:**

1.  Because continuous and irreconcilable quarrels have been proven based on witness testimony and evidence presented at trial, a decree granting a divorce between the Plaintiff and the Defendant is legally permissible.
2.  The Surabaya High Religious Court committed an error by issuing a ruling lacking adequate legal reasoning (\*onvoldoende gemotiveerd\*) and misapplying evidentiary rules when rejecting testimony from close neighboring witnesses and police report evidence that clearly demonstrated the existence of disputes and the forced closure of the Plaintiff's salon by the Defendant.
3.  Reconciliation efforts undertaken by the court and family proved unsuccessful, and the Defendant was unwilling to present family members or witnesses to pursue reconciliation; hence, the Plaintiff's cassation petition must be granted and the Surabaya High Religious Court ruling annulled.

- **Ruling:** Granting the cassation petition from Petitioner for Cassation \[Party A\]; Annulling the Surabaya High Religious Court decision dated October 7, 1997, No. 142/Pdt.G/1997/PTA.Sby; and Independently Adjudicating: (1) In Exception: Rejecting the Defendant's exception; (2) In the Merits: 1. Granting the Plaintiff's lawsuit; 2. Pronouncing a single \*raj'i talak\* by Defendant \[Party B\] against Plaintiff \[Party A\]; 3. Ordering the Plaintiff to pay first-instance court costs amounting to Rp 70,500 (seventy thousand five hundred rupiahs); 4. Ordering the Defendant-Appellee to pay appellate-level court costs amounting to Rp 48,000 (forty-eight thousand rupiahs).
