## Core Definition

The \*\*Japanese\*\* language portal is the specialized bilateral lexicography and comparative translation module within the Global Explainer repository, dedicated to standardizing approved Japanese legal and procedural terminology—particularly concerning civil conciliation (\*Chotei\*), judge-led preparatory proceedings (\*bengon junbi tetuzuki\*), and judicial training frameworks (\*Shihō Kenshūjo\*)—for comparative Pustrajak research publications and bilateral exchanges.

- \*\*Simple Definition:\*\* The official Japanese language portal and specialized legal dictionary that standardizes how Japanese legal and court administration terms (such as \*Chotei\* mediation) are translated and explained for comparative judicial research.
- \*\*Legal / Technical Definition:\*\* The institutional lexicon ensuring that all comparative policy papers, Pustrajak research briefs, and Japanese Mediation Series publications maintain rigorous functional doctrinal equivalence between Japanese civil code concepts and Indonesian judicial practice without semantic drift.
- \*\*Plain-Language Explanation: **It acts as the official Japanese-Indonesian linguistic guide for the judiciary and research partners, ensuring absolute precision in every comparative cross-border legal text.**

## Why It Matters

- \*\*Supports Comparative Legal Research:\*\* Facilitates deep cross-border analysis of Japan’s highly successful civil mediation (\*Chotei\*) system and case management models.
- \*\*Ensures Accuracy in Bilateral Exchanges:\*\* Standardizes terminology used during JICA Knowledge Co-Creation Programs and high-level judicial delegations between Indonesia and Japan.
- \*\*Prevents Doctrinal Distortion:\*\* Establishes approved translations for specialized Japanese procedural terms, avoiding misleading literal interpretations.
- \*\*Enhances RAG AI Search Precision:\*\* Standardizes Japanese and bilingual vector embeddings across the knowledge repository, ensuring researchers querying comparative civil procedure receive verified translations.

## Key Bilateral Lexicographical Domains Detailed

### 1. Civil Conciliation & Mediation Terminology (Leksikon Mediasi Sipil / Chotei)

- \*\*Core Focus:\*\* Standardizing approved terminology for Japanese civil conciliation (\*Chotei\*), settlement conferences, and preparatory proceedings (\*bengon junbi tetuzuki\*).
- \*\*Why It Matters:\*\* Provides the precise vocabulary needed to evaluate how Japanese mediation models can inform Indonesian court-annexed mediation reforms.

### 2. Judicial Training & Apprentice Lexicon (Glosarium Pelatihan Yudisial)

- \*\*Core Focus:\*\* Approving terminology for the Research and Training Institute of the Supreme Court of Japan (\*Shihō Kenshūjo\*) and unified legal apprenticeship structures.
- \*\*Why It Matters:\*\* Supports comparative analysis of judicial capacity building and training curricula (\*Badan Diklat\*).

### 3. Comparative Civil & Procedural Vocabulary (Kosakata Prosedur Perdata Komparatif)

- \*\*Core Focus:\*\* Establishing precise bilingual phrasing for civil code concepts, trial schedules, and case tracking shared across Japanese and Indonesian civil law traditions.
- \*\*Why It Matters:\*\* Maintains academic and professional rigor in comparative procedural studies.

## Debates & Controversies

- Debate: Direct Japanese Concept Retention vs. Complete Localization

` * *Position A (Concept Retention):* Specialized Japanese terms like *Chotei* should be retained in Japanese with clear explanatory footnotes to preserve their unique cultural and procedural nuance.`  
` * *Position B (Complete Localization):* Every foreign term must be translated immediately into standard Indonesian or English equivalents to avoid confusing readers.`  
` * *Evidence:* Editorial debates within comparative mediation monographs.`  
` * *Current Status:* Balanced under Pustrajak guidelines: foundational foreign terms (*Chotei*, *Shihō Kenshūjo*) are retained with precise functional definitions provided in the portal.`

## Common Misconceptions

- \*Myth:\* The Japanese portal is a general tourist dictionary for spoken Japanese conversation.

` * *Reality:* The Japanese portal is a rigorous institutional lexicon containing specialized comparative civil conciliation, mediation, and judicial training terminology.`  
` * *Source:* Pustrajak Lexicographical Standards.`

- \*Myth:\* Japan and Indonesia share identical court administrative structures because both belong to the civil law family.

` * *Reality:* While both are civil law jurisdictions, Japan features unique administrative systems like *Chotei* mediation and the *Shihō Kenshūjo* training institute, which require careful comparative lexicography.`  
` * *Source:* Comparative Law Doctrine.`
