## Overview

Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia Decision No. 880 K/Pdt/2003, dated January 29, 2004, established a fundamental civil and constitutional/political law rule concerning the absolute judicial jurisdiction of general courts over internal political party management disputes:

District Courts lack absolute jurisdiction to adjudicate political party management disputes, which constitute internal organizational matters and internal mechanisms of political parties. Disputes concerning the organizational leadership of political parties must be resolved through internal party mechanisms in accordance with the applicable Statutes and Bylaws (\*AD/ART\*) of the political party. The Supreme Court annulled Mataram High Court Decision No. 176/Pdt/2002/PT.Mtr in conjunction with Mataram District Court Decision No. 134/Pdt.G/2001/PN.Mtr, and adjudicated afresh by declaring the Plaintiff's lawsuit inadmissible (\*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard\* / NO).

## Executive Summary

The central jurisdictional principle established in this decision is that general courts must observe the doctrine of non-intervention in internal political party disputes, leaving leadership contests to internal party tribunals and statutory bylaws.

Key elements established in the adjudication:

- General courts lack absolute jurisdiction (\*kompetensi absolut\*) to examine leadership disputes within political parties.
- Internal party disputes over regional leadership boards and regional congresses (\*Musda\*) must be settled via party \*AD/ART\* mechanisms.
- District court judgments accepting jurisdiction over internal party matters are subject to reversal on cassation, resulting in dismissal as inadmissible (NO).

## Case Background

The Original Plaintiff / Respondent for Cassation (Regional Leadership Board of Golkar Party West Lombok Result of Accelerated Musda VIII: Drs. TGH. Muchlis Ibrahim & H. Abdul Kasim) filed a lawsuit at the Mataram District Court against the Original Defendants / Petitioners for Cassation (1. West Lombok Golkar DPD Result of Musda VII: Lalu Koeshardi, SH & Raden Putra Kesuma, SH; 2. NTB Golkar DPD; 3. DPP Golkar). The Plaintiff sought legal recognition of the accelerated Musda VIII held on January 6, 2001, and the annulment of Interim Replacement (\*PAW\*) appointment decrees issued by Defendant II.

The Defendants raised an exception that the Mataram District Court lacked absolute jurisdiction because leadership disputes constitute internal party affairs governed by \*AD/ART\*. The Mataram District Court (Decision No. 134/Pdt.G/2001/PN.Mtr dated July 20, 2002) rejected the exception and granted the Plaintiff's lawsuit in part. The Mataram High Court (Decision No. 176/Pdt/2002/PT.Mtr dated November 14, 2002) affirmed the district court decision with modifications. The Petitioners for Cassation subsequently filed a cassation petition with the Supreme Court.

## Legal and Procedural Issues

### Absolute Jurisdiction of General Courts over Internal Political Party Disputes

The primary issue was whether General Courts (District Courts, High Courts, Supreme Court) have legal authority to examine, adjudicate, and decide disputes concerning the validity of leadership boards and dual outcomes of Regional Congresses (\*Musda\*) of a political organization (\*Political Party\*).

The Supreme Court held that they lack authority (\*Absolute Jurisdiction Dismissed\*). Disputes concerning leadership validity, interim replacements (\*PAW\*), or Regional Congress (\*Musda\*) validity constitute internal organizational disputes and affairs of political parties. Resolution of such internal disputes must be pursued through forums and mechanisms stipulated within the Statutes and Bylaws (\*AD/ART\*) of the respective political party. General District Courts possess no jurisdictional competence (\*kompetens absolut\*) to try such matters, requiring the Plaintiff's lawsuit to be declared inadmissible (\*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard\* / NO).

## Question of Law and Question of Fact

### Question of Law (Cassation / Judex Juris)

**Question:**

Did the \*Judex Facti\* (Mataram High Court & District Court) misapply the law or exceed their jurisdictional limits (\*foutieve kepassing van het recht\* / exceeding authority ex Article 30 of Law No. 14/1985) by accepting and adjudicating a civil unlawful act lawsuit concerning internal political party leadership validity?

**Supreme Court Ruling:**

Yes, the \*Judex Facti\* misapplied the law. Political party management matters are internal party affairs, rendering General Courts unauthorized to adjudicate. The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition of the Petitioners for Cassation, annulled Mataram High Court Decision No. 176/Pdt/2002/PT.Mtr in conjunction with Mataram District Court Decision No. 134/Pdt.G/2001/PN.Mtr, and adjudicated afresh by declaring the Plaintiff's lawsuit inadmissible (NO).

### Question of Fact (Judex Facti / Mataram District Court & High Court)

Examination of facts concerning the vote of no confidence by 12 Sub-District Consultative Councils (\*DPK\*) of Golkar Party in West Lombok; execution of accelerated Musda VIII on January 6, 2001, at Jayakarta Hotel; election of Drs. TGH. Muchlis Ibrahim and H. Abdul Kasim; issuance of the Decree on Composition and Personnel of Interim Replacement (\*PAW\*) for West Lombok Golkar DPD by DPD I NTB (Decree No. Kep-003/DPD/GOLKAR/IV/2001); and the absence of written approval from the DPD I NTB Chairman in convening Musda VIII.

## Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

- **Ratio Decidendi:**

1.  The \*Judex Facti\* of Mataram High Court and District Court misapplied the law and exceeded their jurisdictional authority.
2.  Pursuant to the Plaintiff's \*posita\*, the object of dispute concerned management matters constituting internal affairs of the respective political party.
3.  Under organizational and political legal principles, District Courts lack authority to adjudicate internal political party management disputes.
4.  The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition, annulled lower court rulings, and adjudicated afresh, declaring the Plaintiff's lawsuit inadmissible (\*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard\* / NO).

## Ruling

The Supreme Court ruled: Granting the cassation petition from Petitioners for Cassation (1. **WEST LOMBOK GOLKAR DPD RESULT OF MUSDA VII**, 2. **NUSA TENGGARA BARAT GOLKAR DPD**, 3. **DPP GOLKAR**); Annulling Mataram High Court Decision No. 176/Pdt/2002/PT.Mtr dated November 14, 2002, in conjunction with Mataram District Court Decision No. 134/Pdt.G/2001/PN.Mtr dated July 20, 2002; and ADJUDICATING AFRESH: Declaring Plaintiff's lawsuit inadmissible (\*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard\*); and ordering the Respondent for Cassation / Original Plaintiff to pay court costs across all judicial instances (cassation court costs set at Rp 500,000.—).

## Evidence Status and Gaps

### Established by Evidence

- The object of dispute filed by the Plaintiff (West Lombok Golkar DPD Result of Musda VIII) was purely an internal political party management dispute.
- District Courts lack absolute jurisdiction to adjudicate internal political party management disputes.
- Plaintiff's lawsuit was declared inadmissible (\*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard\* / NO) and Plaintiff was charged with court costs.

### Not Established / Inadmissible

- The substantive validity of leadership claims between Musda VII Results and Musda VIII Results was neither examined nor adjudicated by the Supreme Court because Courts lack authority to try internal party disputes.

### Uncertainty and Gaps

- Principle of Judicial Non-Intervention in Internal Political Organization Disputes: This landmark ruling firmly establishes the internal party dispute doctrine (\*internal party dispute\*): general courts are barred from meddling in political party leadership affairs, fostering democratic maturity by compelling internal resolution through Party Tribunals / party \*AD/ART\* mechanisms.

## GEO Q&A

**Q: Do District Courts have authority to adjudicate dual leadership disputes within internal political parties?**

**A:** No authority (\*Absolute Jurisdiction Dismissed\*). Based on Supreme Court Decision No. 880 K/Pdt/2003, District Courts lack authority to adjudicate political party management disputes constituting internal party affairs. Political party organizational leadership disputes fall within the internal domain mandatorily resolved through mechanisms set forth in the party's Statutes and Bylaws (\*AD/ART\*), rendering lawsuits filed in District Courts inadmissible (\*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard\* / NO).
