# Judicial-policy-italian-administrative-procedure-act-1990

The **Italian Administrative Procedure Act Policy Framework** (officially centered on *Law no. 241 of August 7, 1990 / Legge 7 agosto 1990, n. 241*) is the foundational statutory and policy framework in Italian administrative law governing adjudicatory administrative procedures, transparency, public participation, administrative simplification, and principles of good governance [^1].

## Overview

Authored by [Aldo Sandulli](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Aldo_Sandulli), Full Professor of Administrative Law at [University "Suor Orsola Benincasa" of Naples](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/University_%22Suor_Orsola_Benincasa%22_of_Naples), this analysis evaluates the two-decade evolutionary trajectory of Law no. 241 of 1990 [^2]. Drafted by an expert academic commission appointed in 1979 by Minister for Civil Service [Massimo Severo Giannini](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Massimo_Severo_Giannini) and chaired by [Mario Nigro](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Mario_Nigro), the Act fundamentally transformed the relationship between public administration and citizens from an authoritarian paradigm to a transparent, participatory, and structured interaction [^3].

## Legal Basis

- **[Law no. 241 of August 7, 1990](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Legge_7_agosto_1990%2C_n._241)**: The core Italian administrative procedure statute establishing statutory rights to public participation, document access, strict procedural deadlines, and administrative simplification mechanisms [^4].

## Background

The original bill was completed by the Nigro Commission in 1982, but faced an eight-year political and bureaucratic delay before enactment by the Italian Parliament in 1990 [^5]. The statutory architecture of Law no. 241/1990 established general principles focused on adjudicatory administrative procedures affecting individual legal positions, while general regulatory acts were intentionally excluded from its direct scope [^6].

## Objectives

- Guarantee transparency via public access to administrative documents (\*accesso agli atti\*) and ensure public participation in administrative decision-making [^7].
- Maintain a structural balance between administrative efficiency (\*simplification\*) and citizen rights (\*guarantees\*) [^8].
- Uphold constitutional principles of impartiality and good administration (\*imparzialità e buon andamento\*) [^9].

## Scope

Applies across all individual adjudicatory administrative procedures in Italy. However, general administrative and regulatory acts, emergency measures, and specified large-scale infrastructure projects are excluded or subject to special regimes [^10].

## Key Provisions & Mechanisms

- **Participation and Transparency Rights**: Mandates advance notice of administrative proceedings (\*comunicazione di avvio del procedimento\*) and establishes a general right of access to administrative documents [^11].
- **Conference of Services (Conferenza di Servizi)**: A statutory coordination mechanism enabling multiple administrative bodies to make collective determinations efficiently [^12].
- **Procedure Officer (Responsabile del Procedimento - RUP)**: Requires the designation of a specific individual officer responsible for guiding each procedure to timely completion [^13].

## Implementation

Operationalized throughout Italian public administration bodies and routinely reviewed and interpreted by administrative courts, primarily the Italian Council of State (\*Consiglio di Stato\*) [^14].

## Policy Issues

- **Patchwork Effect**: Frequent piecemeal amendments by the legislature have eroded the original structural coherence and spirit of the Act [^15].
- **Marginalization of Democratic Guarantees**: A shifting procedural focus prioritizing economic acceleration and litigation reduction over citizen procedural guarantees [^16].
- **Flight from General Rules**: Increasing statutory exemptions created for emergency procedures and major infrastructure, allowing administration to bypass standard Law 241/1990 guarantees [^17].

## Proposed Reforms (De Jure Condendo)

Aldo Sandulli recommends three structural reform steps:

1.  Implement a moratorium on makeshift emergency legislative amendments [^18].
2.  Conduct empirical mapping and comparative legal research on contemporary Italian administrative practices [^19].
3.  Establish a new expert academic commission (modeled on the Nigro Commission) to draft a balanced modern Administrative Procedure Act [^20].
