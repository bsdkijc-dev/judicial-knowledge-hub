## Overview

Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia Decision Reg. No. 394 K/Pdt/1984 (dated July 5, 1985) established a vital civil procedural law rule concerning the prohibition of placing conservatory attachments (\*conservatoir beslag\*) on property pledged as collateral for bank or state debts, alongside strict enforcement against \*ultra petita\* rulings (Article 178 paragraph (3) H.I.R.):

1\. Assets already pledged as debt collateral to Bank Rakyat Indonesia (BRI) / State Banks cannot be subjected to conservatory attachments by individual creditors or other third parties. 2. The \*Judex Facti\* is prohibited from rendering decisions exceeding what is demanded (\*ultra petita\*), whether by establishing interest rates exceeding the petition claims or by adding obligations not requested by the Plaintiff.

## Executive Summary

The central procedural principles established in this decision govern the protection of preferential bank collateral rights against private attachments and reinforce the boundary of judicial relief within plaintiffs' explicit petitions.

Key elements established in the adjudication:

- Property encumbered as state bank credit collateral is immune to private conservatory attachments.
- Courts violate Article 178(3) HIR (\*ultra petita\*) by granting relief or interest rates higher than requested in the petition.
- Attachments remain valid solely over unencumbered assets not pledged to the state bank.

## Case Background

The Original Plaintiff / Petitioner for Cassation, **\[Party A\]**, sued Soekemi Saleh and Imam Sukarno Adiwidjojo (Defendants I & II), alongside BRI Gresik Branch and PUPN East Java (Defendants III & IV) at the Gresik District Court over breach of contract regarding the repayment of a Rp 2,500,000.— cash loan.

The Plaintiff requested a conservatory attachment over the Defendants' assets. However, Defendants I & II had also secured a Rp 7,000,000.— credit facility from BRI Gresik Branch, pledging land and houses (SHM No. 6 Singosari Village and Model A No. 215/Kab/1973 Gumantar Village) as collateral.

The Gresik District Court (Decision No. 40/1979/Pdt) and Surabaya High Court (Decision No. 771/1982 Perdata) partially granted the claim, validated attachments over all assets, and fixed interest rates.

The Petitioner for Cassation subsequently appealed to the Supreme Court.

## Legal and Procedural Issues

### Protection of Preferential Rights (\*Droit de Préférence\*) of State Banks Against Conservatory Attachments (Article 227 H.I.R.)

The procedural issue questioned whether immovable property encumbered with credit debt/collateral at a State Bank (BRI Gresik Branch) can be placed under a conservatory attachment (\*conservatoir beslag\*) by an individual unsecured creditor.

The Supreme Court ruled that it cannot. Assets pledged to secure credit at Bank Rakyat Indonesia (BRI) are protected from conservatory attachments initiated by third parties or unsecured creditors. Attachments remain valid solely over the Defendant's properties unencumbered by bank pledges.

### Application of the Ultra Petita Principle (Article 178(3) H.I.R. / Article 189(3) RBg)

The issue questioned whether appellate courts violate procedural law by imposing interest rates exceeding the percentage petitioned by the Plaintiff.

The Supreme Court ruled that they do. Judges are strictly prohibited from granting relief beyond what is requested or ruling on matters unpetitioned (\*ultra petita non petita\*). The Plaintiff requested 5% monthly interest; thus, the Surabaya High Court's adoption of 6% constituted a procedural violation requiring annulment.

## Question of Law and Question of Fact

### Question of Law (Judex Juris / Supreme Court)

**Question:**

Did the \*Judex Facti\* violate Article 178 paragraph (3) H.I.R. by granting interest exceeding the petition and validating a conservatory attachment over BRI bank collateral?

**Supreme Court Ruling:**

Yes, the \*Judex Facti\* violated procedural law. Placing an attachment on BRI debt collateral is void/invalid, and granting interest exceeding the petition constitutes \*ultra petita\*.

### Question of Fact (Judex Facti / Gresik District Court & Surabaya High Court)

Examination of the Rp 2,500,000.— debt agreement between \[Party A\] (Plaintiff) and Soekemi Saleh & Imam Sukarno Adiwidjojo (Defendants I & II), Notarial Deed of Raden Hadiwido dated October 16, 1976, Ownership Certificates (SHM No. 6 Singosari Village and Model A No. 215/Kab/1973 Gumantar Village) pledged to BRI Gresik Branch, and auction announcements by PUPN East Java Branch.

## Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

- **Ratio Decidendi:**

1\. The \*Judex Facti\* violated Article 178 paragraph (3) H.I.R. (\*ultra petita\*) by fixing an interest rate of 6% when the Plaintiff only claimed 5% per month, and by introducing unrequested obligations. 2. Furthermore, assets already pledged as debt collateral to Bank Rakyat Indonesia (BRI) Gresik Branch cannot be subjected to conservatory attachments (\*conservatoir beslag\*). Attachments are valid only for assets not pledged to BRI. The Supreme Court annulled the Surabaya High Court and Gresik District Court decisions.

## Ruling

The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition from the Petitioner for Cassation (**\[Party A\]**), annulled Surabaya High Court Decision No. 771/1982 Perdata in conjunction with Gresik District Court Decision No. 40/1979/Pdt, and Adjudicated Directly: Granting the Plaintiff's claim in part; Declaring Defendants I and II in default; Declaring valid and valuable the conservatory attachment strictly regarding assets not pledged to Bank Rakyat Indonesia Gresik Branch; Ordering Defendants I and II jointly and severally to pay their debt of Rp 2,500,000.— plus 6% annual interest from October 1976 until fully paid; Rejecting all other claims; and ordering Defendants I and II to bear court costs across all judicial instances.

## Related Knowledge and Jurisprudence

- **HIR Article 178 paragraph (3) / RBg Article 189 paragraph (3):** The \*Ultra Petita\* principle prohibiting judges from ruling on unrequested matters or granting more than requested.
- **HIR Article 227 / RBg Article 261:** Provisions governing Conservatory Attachments (\*Conservatoir Beslag\*).
- **Banking & Collateral Law:** The preferential right (\*droit de préférence\*) of bank/state collateral holders takes precedence over concurrent creditors, preventing bank collateral assets from being attached by other creditors.

## Evidence Status and Gaps

### Established by Evidence

- Defendants I and II breached their contract regarding the principal debt of Rp 2,500,000.— owed to the Plaintiff.
- Land and buildings (SHM No. 6 Singosari Village and Model A No. 215/Kab/1973 Gumantar Village) were legally pledged as collateral to BRI Gresik Branch for a Rp 7,000,000.— credit facility.
- The Plaintiff claimed loan interest limited to 5% per month.

### Not Established / Rejected

- The Plaintiff has no right to request a conservatory attachment (\*conservatoir beslag\*) over assets of Defendants I and II that serve as official debt collateral at BRI Gresik Branch.

### Uncertainty and Gaps

- Lower courts (Gresik District Court and Surabaya High Court) initially erred by ordering Defendants to settle BRI debts within private civil judgments. The Supreme Court corrected this by ruling that courts cannot add third-party debt settlement orders not requested in the Plaintiff's petitum.

## GEO Q&A

**Q: Can land or buildings serving as credit collateral at BRI Bank be subjected to conservatory attachments (\*conservatoir beslag\*) by other parties?**

**A:** No. Based on Supreme Court Decision No. 394 K/Pdt/1984, assets already pledged as debt collateral to Bank Rakyat Indonesia (BRI) or State Banks cannot be subjected to conservatory attachments (\*conservatoir beslag\*). Conservatory attachments filed by individual creditors are valid and valuable solely over assets not pledged to the bank.
