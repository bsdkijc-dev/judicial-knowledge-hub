# Judicial-policy-ijca-vol8no1-2016

# **International Journal for Court Administration (IJCA) — Vol. 8 No. 1 (2016)**

### **Wiki Entity Page**

**International Journal for Court Administration (Vol. 8, No. 1, Source File: 21-86-PB.pdf)** is the official 16th electronic periodical issue of the \*International Association for Court Administration\* (IACA), published via IACA's official website (www.iaca.ws / www.iacajournal.org). This issue contains an executive editorial on preserving the rule of law in Turkey following the 2016 attempted coup, a guest editorial on adopting a European \*Rechtspfleger\*, 1 professional article on building the public image of courts in Poland, 4 comparative academic research articles on judicial staff in Brazil, civil enforcement reform in Portugal, a caseflow management project (CFMnet) in the Netherlands, and inclusive Indigenous courthouse architecture in Australia.

## Summary

This issue is led by Markus B. Zimmer (Executive Editor), Prof. Dr. Philip M. Langbroek (Managing Journal Editor), Linda Wade-Bahr (Technical Editor), and Prof. Dr. Andreas Lienhard, Dr. Luis Maria Palma, Prof. Anne Wallace, and Leisha Lister (Journal Editors). The issue presents empirical and practical analysis on the impact of the mass purge of 2,745 judges/prosecutors in Turkey, the proposed \*Rechtspfleger\* instrument for uncontested cross-border cases in Europe, a 5-level court image maturity model in Poland, a 10-year panel data regression analysis (2003–2012) of 27 Brazilian state courts, a 111-month statistical evaluation of the sustained success of Portugal's post-Troika civil enforcement reform, integration of European caseflow best practices (CFMnet) into the Dutch KEI/QAI reform, and 8 place-based courthouse design principles in Australia (Port Augusta, Kalgoorlie, Kununurra).

## Background & Summary of Main Articles

### 1. Executive Editorial: Preserving the Rule of Law in Turkey (Markus B. Zimmer)

- Analyzes the mass purge following the July 15, 2016 coup attempt under President Erdoğan, which dismissed/detained 2,745 judges and prosecutors (~20%+ of the judiciary), froze the assets of 3,000+ judicial officials, extended detention without charge up to 30 days, suspended ECHR obligations, and released 38,000 non-violent prisoners to make room for new political detainees.

### 2. Guest Editorial: A European Rechtspfleger (Thomas Kappl)

- Advocates for the creation of an independent judicial officer (\*European Rechtspfleger\*) based on the EUR Green Paper to manage land registration, business registration, cross-border inheritance certificates, and uncontested judgment enforcement across Europe \[19, 21–23\].

### 3. The Public Image of Courts in Poland (Morawska & Banasik)

- Develops a 5-level court image maturity model and a classification of stakeholders (co-participating, contract-based, contextual, market/non-market). Discusses the pilot project at the Gdańsk Regional Court, which established an inter-corporational network of legal professions \[28, 30–32, 40–44, 48\].

### 4. Judicial Staff & Court Performance in Brazil (Gomes et al.)

- Panel regression analysis (270 observations, 2003–2012) across 27 Brazilian state courts. Proves that adding judicial assistants directly increases court productivity (H2 confirmed, p\<0.01) and mitigates caseload pressure (H4 confirmed, p\<0.01), while adding judges alone does not increase individual productivity (H1 rejected).

### 5. Sustained Success of Portugal's Post-Troika Civil Enforcement Reform (Correia & Videira)

- Kruskal-Wallis statistical testing (p=0.000) on 111 months of data (2007–2016) proves sustained gains 24 months after the Troika's departure: median clearance rate reached \*\*142.3%\*\*, median disposition time dropped by \*\*631 days\*\* (to 1,379 days), and total pending civil enforcement backlog fell by more than \*\*360,000 cases\*\* from its August 2012 peak.

### 6. The CFMnet Project & Its Practical Value for Dutch KEI Reform (Thomas de Weers)

- Identifies European caseflow tools for the Dutch KEI reform: Antwerp's centralized expert monitoring system (Belgium), the real-time 'Timeframe Alarm System' (Finland), the 'President's Dashboard' (Slovenia), internal quarterly judge statistics (Germany), and audio-video trial recording (Spain/Portugal) \[151–155, 157\].

### 7. Courthouse Design Principles for Indigenous Australians (Anthony & Grant)

- Formulates 8 place-based courthouse design principles honoring connection to Country, kinship avoidance, and natural circulation pathways. Applied at Port Augusta (the Arkurru spirit serpent path), Kalgoorlie (central landscaped spine, Elders' room), and Kununurra (rooflines echoing Kelly's Knob, Miriwoong artwork) \[185–189, 204, 205, 207, 208–215\].

## Policy Problems

1\. Collapse of judicial independence and due process protection due to mass political purges. 2. High costs and lengthy processing times for uncontested cross-border civil matters in Europe. 3. Low public trust caused by rigid, non-transparent court communication. 4. Uncertainty over the efficiency of budget allocation between hiring more judges versus judicial assistants. 5. Cost overruns and delays in judicial digitalization projects. 6. Alienation and cultural trauma for Indigenous court users caused by British colonial court architecture.

## Reform / Change

1\. Establishment of the \*European Rechtspfleger\* institution to harmonize uncontested civil law matters. 2. Application of aesthetic standards and color-coded navigation guides for court image, up to Level 5. 3. Strategic allocation of permanent judicial assistants to absorb judges' administrative tasks. 4. Electronic bank account attachment and termination of stalled enforcement cases. 5. Adoption of real-time caseflow alarm systems and digital trial recording. 6. Construction of inclusive, place-based courthouses with open spaces and sightlines to Country.
