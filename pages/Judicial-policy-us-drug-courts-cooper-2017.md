# Judicial-policy-us-drug-courts-cooper-2017

**The Drug Treatment Courts and Their Progeny in the U.S. Policy Framework** is a therapeutic criminal justice and long-term evaluation policy framework concerning Drug Treatment Courts (DTC) and their derivatives (such as Juvenile Drug Courts, Family Treatment Courts, Tribal Healing to Wellness Courts, and Veterans Treatment Courts) in the United States. Formulated by Caroline Cooper (American University Justice Programs Office) in the *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA), Vol. 8 No. 3 (July 2017), the framework analyzes the 25+ year evolution since the founding of the Miami Drug Court in 1989. It evaluates the application of Differentiated Case Management (DCM) and the NADCP/BJA's 10 Key Components, proposing reforms to rebalance legal supervision with the principles of therapeutic jurisprudence \[1–9, 14–18, 28–32\].

## Summary

Published by Caroline Cooper in the IJCA (July 2017), this policy framework evaluates the 25+ year journey of Drug Treatment Courts (DTC) in the United States since their inception in Miami in 1989 \[1–3\]. Developed as a pragmatic response to the crisis of mandatory sentencing laws and the "revolving door" cycle of incarcerating drug addicts, the DTC model applies Differentiated Case Management (DCM) and therapeutic jurisprudence by providing intensive outpatient treatment as a diversion from prosecution \[1–5, 9\]. The DTC model eventually spawned four primary derivatives: Juvenile Drug Courts, Family Treatment Courts (driven by ASFA 1997), Tribal Healing to Wellness Courts, and Veterans Treatment Courts \[14–18\]. However, Cooper identifies severe challenges, including the erosion of fidelity to the NADCP/BJA's 10 Key Components, a problematic shift toward a probation-based model that strips defendants of public defender representation, subjective selection processes that trigger racial disparities, and the persistent rejection of Medication Assisted Treatment (MAT) \[11–13, 19–27\]. The proposed reforms emphasize drafting a *Unifying Mission Statement*, securing felony record expungements, and integrating therapeutic jurisprudence into university curricula and Continuing Legal Education (CLE) \[30–39\].

## Legal Basis

- **Sixth Amendment & Due Process Clause (U.S. Constitution):** Constitutional guarantees of the right to a speedy and public trial, a fair hearing, and the assistance of counsel.
- **Mandatory Sentencing Laws (1980s Federal & State Laws):** Strict sentencing regulations that triggered mass incarceration and catalyzed the creation of the alternative DTC model.
- **Adoption and Safe Families Act (ASFA) of 1997 (Public Law 105-89):** The legal foundation enforcing strict 15/22-month timelines for foster care, which spurred the creation of Family Drug Treatment Courts.
- **Defining Drug Courts: The 10 Key Components (NADCP/BJA 1997):** The foundational national standard defining the mandatory elements for operating a DTC in the United States.
- **Not found in the available sources:** Other specific formal statutory regulations are not mentioned in the source text \[FACT\].

## Background

The failure of mass incarceration policies to rehabilitate drug addicts compelled judicial leaders in Miami in 1989 to leverage the coercive power of the criminal justice system to mandate participation in medical treatment.

## Objectives

1\. Halt the revolving-door cycle of incarcerating drug addicts through intensive medical rehabilitation backed by judicial supervision. 2. Ensure the protection of defendants' constitutional rights and legal sovereignty within a non-adversarial team approach. 3. Eliminate the collateral consequences of permanent criminal records for participants who successfully graduate from the program.

## Scope

This policy covers the evaluation of DTCs at the Federal, State, and Tribal levels within the US, as well as their international adoption in over 20 countries.

## Key Provisions

- **The 10 Key Components (NADCP/BJA):**
  - Integrating drug treatment with criminal justice processing, maintaining a non-adversarial approach, ensuring early intervention, conducting frequent abstinence testing, and providing continuous evaluation.
- **Four DTC Progeny Permutations:**
  - Juvenile Drug Courts (focusing on adolescent development), Family Treatment Courts (ASFA 1997), Tribal Healing to Wellness Courts (10 Tribal Components), and Veterans Courts (addressing trauma & PTSD).
- **Medication Assisted Treatment (MAT) Mandate:**
  - A federal mandate prohibiting DTCs from denying participants the use of certified medical treatments (e.g., methadone/buprenorphine).

## Implementation

- **Enforcement of Evidence-Based Eligibility Criteria:** Abolishing subjective selection or "team voting" and replacing it with scientific risk/need assessment instruments.
- **Academic Partnerships and CLE Training:** Integrating DTC principles into law schools, medical schools, public health programs, and Continuing Legal Education.

## Policy Problems

- **Loss of Public Defender Representation in the Probation Model:** Probationers in DTCs often lack public defender representation, making them highly vulnerable to program termination and incarceration without due process.
- **Racial and Demographic Disparities:** Unsystematic and subjective selection criteria have led to disproportionately low enrollment rates for minority groups (particularly African Americans).

## Reform / Change

- Redrafting a *Unifying Mission Statement* and establishing clear pathways for the expungement of felony records.
- Expanding the indicators of success from mere recidivism rates to comprehensive prosocial recovery indicators (desistance measures).

### Deutsch (Schweizer Hochdeutsch)

**Drogenbehandlungsgerichte und ihre Ableger in den USA: Ein politischer Rahmen** Dieser von Caroline Cooper im *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA, 2017) veröffentlichte Rahmen bewertet die 25-jährige Entwicklung von Drogenbehandlungsgerichten (DTC) in den USA, die 1989 in Miami begannen. Das DTC-Modell, eine Alternative zu den strengen "Mandatory Sentencing Laws", verbindet gerichtliche Aufsicht mit intensiver medizinischer Behandlung (Therapeutic Jurisprudence). Aus dem Modell entstanden Jugendgerichte, Familiengerichte und Veteranengerichte. Cooper kritisiert jedoch die Abkehr von den "10 Key Components", die mangelnde rechtliche Vertretung im Bewährungsmodell (Verletzung des Due Process), rassistische Ungleichgewichte durch subjektive Auswahlverfahren und die anfängliche Ablehnung der medikamentengestützten Behandlung (MAT).

### Français

**Les tribunaux de traitement de la toxicomanie et leurs dérivés aux États-Unis : Cadre politique** Publié par Caroline Cooper dans l' *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA, 2017), ce cadre évalue les 25 années d'évolution des tribunaux de traitement de la toxicomanie (DTC) aux États-Unis, initiés à Miami en 1989. Ce modèle, une alternative aux lois de peines minimales obligatoires ("Mandatory Sentencing Laws"), associe la supervision judiciaire à un traitement médical intensif (Jurisprudence Thérapeutique). Le modèle a donné naissance aux tribunaux pour mineurs, aux tribunaux de la famille et aux tribunaux pour vétérans. Cependant, Cooper critique l'érosion de la fidélité aux "10 Key Components", le manque de représentation légale dans le modèle de probation (violation de la procédure régulière), les disparités raciales dues à des processus de sélection subjectifs, et le refus initial du traitement assisté par médicaments (MAT).

- **Base Légale:** 6ème amendement de la Constitution américaine ; ASFA 1997 ; 10 Key Components (NADCP/BJA 1997).

### 3 Most Important Improvements

1\. **Redrafting a Unifying Mission Statement & Restoring the Right to Legal Counsel:** Re-align the visions of the criminal justice and public health systems, and guarantee the mandatory presence of a public defender during all DTC phases to fiercely protect defendants' due process rights. 2. **Implementing Evidence-Based Eligibility Criteria & Mandating MAT Adoption:** Eliminate subjective selection mechanisms or "team voting" to eradicate racial disparities, and mandate the integration of Medication Assisted Treatment (MAT) in strict accordance with modern medical science. 3. **Establishing Cross-Disciplinary Academic Partnerships & Prosocial Desistance Measures:** Integrate Therapeutic Jurisprudence and Procedural Justice curricula into law, medical, and CLE programs, and measure DTC success through prosocial indicators (employment, family stability, health) rather than relying solely on recidivism statistics.\`\`\`
