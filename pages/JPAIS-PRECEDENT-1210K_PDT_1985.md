**Supreme Court Decision No. 1210 K/Pdt/1985** is an Indonesian civil-law precedent establishing three fundamental civil procedure rules concerning the prohibition of using voluntary jurisdiction (*voluntaire jurisdictie*) for land rights determinations, the legal consequences of nullifying such petitions, and the appropriate handling of third-party objections.

The decision establishes that district courts have no statutory authority to determine ownership or land rights through unilateral petition procedures without a dispute. Consequently, such orders are null and void (*batal demi hukum*), requiring disputes over customary land boundaries to be brought via separate contentious civil suits.

## Executive Summary

The dispute arose in Jayapura, Papua (Irian Jaya), where **\[Party A\]** (Ondoafi Hedam Ayapo) filed a voluntary petition (No. 118/PN/Pdt.P/1983) at the Jayapura District Court seeking recognition of customary land rights without a contesting opposing party. The district court granted the petition.

**\[Party B\]** (Ondoafi Besar Yoka Hebeibulu) subsequently filed an objection (*verzet*) challenging the order because it overlapped with customary lands in Abepura-Sentani. The lower courts examined the objection, with the High Court ruling in favor of the challenger.

The Supreme Court held that the lower courts exceeded their jurisdiction. Voluntary jurisdiction cannot be used to establish land titles or ownership rights. The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition, annulled the lower-court decisions, declared the original district court petition order null and void, and ruled that all examination proceedings regarding the objection were invalid, mandating that land disputes be settled through standard contentious lawsuits.

## Case Background

\[Party A\] filed a voluntary petition (No. 118/PN/Pdt.P/1983) at the Jayapura District Court, which was granted on 5 December 1983, recognizing customary control and rights (including the right to control and inherit) over *ulayat* land in Ayapo and surrounding areas.

\[Party B\] challenged this order by filing an objection (*verzet* No. 22/Pdt/Plw/1984/PN.Jpr), arguing that the order unilaterally annexed customary lands belonging to the Yoka Hebeibulu community in Abepura-Sentani.

The Jayapura District Court ruled that the land was jointly owned, and upon appeal, the High Court of Irian Jaya (Decision No. 55/Pdt/1984/PT.Jpr) modified the judgment to grant the land entirely to the challenger. \[Party A\] then filed a cassation petition to the Supreme Court.

## Procedural History

### First Instance: Jayapura District Court (Petition)

The Jayapura District Court issued Order No. 118/PN/Pdt.P/1983 on 5 December 1983, granting the voluntary petition for land rights.

### Objection Proceeding: Jayapura District Court

The Jayapura District Court decided Case No. 22/Pdt/Plw/1984/PN.Jpr on 29 October 1984, ruling that the disputed land was jointly owned.

### Appeal: High Court of Irian Jaya at Jayapura

The High Court of Irian Jaya decided Appeal No. 55/Pdt/1984/PT.Jpr on 2 January 1985, modifying the first-instance judgment and declaring the land to belong entirely to the challenger (*Pelawan*).

### Cassation: Supreme Court

\[Party A\] filed a cassation petition. The Supreme Court granted the petition, annulled the lower-court decisions, and independently declared the initial voluntary petition order null and void.

## Legal Issues

The case raised the following principal legal issues:

1.  Whether a district court has the authority to receive, examine, and grant petitions for land ownership or customary land rights through voluntary jurisdiction (*voluntaire jurisdictie*) without a contentious dispute.
2.  What the legal consequences are when a district court issues a voluntary land rights order without statutory backing.
3.  Whether third-party objections (*verzet*) can be filed against a voluntary judicial order or whether a separate contentious lawsuit is required.

## Supreme Court's Reasoning

### 1. Inadmissibility of Voluntary Jurisdiction for Land Rights

Acting on its own legal grounds (*ambtshalve*), the Supreme Court ruled that district courts examining and deciding land rights petitions without an active dispute exercise voluntary jurisdiction without legal basis. There is no statutory provision authorizing district courts to grant land ownership or title via ex-parte petitions. Such applications should initially be declared inadmissible (*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard* / NO).

### 2. No Objection (*Verzet*) Lies against an Order on a Petition

Under civil procedure law, an objection (*verzet*) cannot be filed against an order issued on a petition. A party who considers its land rights affected by such an order therefore cannot use the objection procedure to challenge it.

### 3. Nullity of the Voluntary Order and Subsequent Proceedings

Because the Jayapura District Court petition order lacked legal foundation, it was declared null and void (*batal demi hukum*). Consequently, all subsequent examination proceedings in the objection lawsuits handled by the district and high courts were also rendered null and void.

### 4. Requirement of Contentious Civil Lawsuits

Customary land disputes and boundary conflicts between tribes cannot be resolved via ex-parte petitions or irregular objection procedures, but must be pursued through separate, contentious civil lawsuits (*gugatan perdata kontradiktur*).

## Ratio Decidendi

The core *ratio decidendi* may be stated as follows:

> **District courts have no statutory authority to adjudicate land ownership or customary rights via voluntary petition procedures; any such order issued without legal basis is null and void (*batal demi hukum*), and any property or boundary disputes must instead be litigated through separate contentious civil lawsuits.**

## Holding

The Supreme Court:

1.  **Granted** the cassation petition of \[Party A\].
2.  **Annulled** the High Court of Irian Jaya Decision No. 55/Pdt/1984/PT.Jpr.
3.  **Annulled** the Jayapura District Court Decision No. 22/Pdt/Plw/1984/PN.Jpr.
4.  **Adjudicated the case itself** (*mengadili sendiri*):
    1.  Declared that the Challenger (*Pelawan*) is the rightful challenger (*Pelawan yang benar*).
    2.  Declared Jayapura District Court Petition Order No. 118/PN/Pdt.P/1983 dated 5 December 1983 to be **null and void**.
    3.  Declared that the Jayapura District Court lacked authority to examine the original petition.
    4.  Declared all examination proceedings by the district and high courts in the objection case (No. 22/Pdt/Plw/1984/PN.Jpr jo. No. 55/Pdt/1984/PT.Jpr) to be **null and void**.
5.  Ordered the Cassation Respondent (\[Party B\]) to pay court costs at all judicial levels (cassation costs set at IDR 20,000).

## Legal Significance

This precedent is a landmark ruling regarding civil procedure and agrarian law in Indonesia, establishing that:

- Courts are strictly prohibited from issuing declaratory property titles or land rights through voluntary jurisdiction (*voluntaire jurisdictie*).
- Ex-parte land petitions are an absolute procedural nullity.
- No objection (*verzet*) lies against an order on a petition, and objection proceedings built on a void order are themselves void.
- Land and customary rights (*hak ulayat*) disputes must be settled via contentious (adversarial) civil lawsuits (*gugatan kontradiktur*).
