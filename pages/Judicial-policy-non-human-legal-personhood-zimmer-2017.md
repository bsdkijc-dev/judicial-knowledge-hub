# Judicial-policy-non-human-legal-personhood-zimmer-2017

**The Data-Informed Case Management for Special Immigrant Juvenile Status Cases Policy Framework** is a judicial governance and data-informed case management framework for handling Special Immigrant Juvenile Status (SIJS) predicate finding requests in state trial courts. Formulated by Danielle Fox, Hisashi Yamagata, Lili Khozeimeh, Marianne Hendricks, Madeleine Jones, and Rick Dabbs (Montgomery County Circuit Court, Maryland, USA) in the *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA), Vol. 8 No. 3 (July 2017), the framework analyzes the transformation of custody and guardianship case management for unaccompanied alien children (UAC). It traces the evolution from a single-judge handling model (pre-2014) and a two-judge model (2014–2015), to a *three-judicial officer model* (2015–2016) driven by a dedicated SIJS database and automated scheduling to guarantee legal certainty, drastically reduce case backlogs, and uphold the rule of law \[1–36\].

## Summary

Published by the management and research team of the Montgomery County Circuit Court (Maryland) in the IJCA (July 2017), this policy framework evaluates the state trial court's response to the massive influx of Unaccompanied Alien Children (UAC) requesting factual predicate findings for Special Immigrant Juvenile Status (SIJS) \[1–5\]. Based on federal laws (INA 1990 and TVPRA 2008) and state legislation (Maryland Law Chapter 96 of 2014), state courts are mandated to issue predicate orders confirming the impossibility of parental reunification due to abuse, neglect, or abandonment \[5–8\]. Analyzing 939 SIJS cases (May 2013–September 2016), the study maps the evolution of case management from a reactive, attorney-driven single-judge model to a highly structured **Three-Judicial Officer Model** (two judges for guardianship and one magistrate for custody). This model is supported by court-managed automated scheduling (within a 125-day timeframe) and the generation of automated *critical review sheets* \[13–22, 25\]. These reforms successfully catapulted the court's clearance rate from 32% to 90% and slashed the average age of pending cases by 44% (from 233 days to 130 days).

## Legal Basis

- **Immigration and Nationality Act (INA) of 1990 (8 U.S.C. § 1101(a)(27)(J)) & TVPRA 2008:** US federal regulations establishing SIJS immigration relief for immigrant children subjected to parental abuse, neglect, or abandonment.
- **Maryland Code, Family Law § 1-201(b)(10) & Maryland Law Chapter 96 (2014):** State legislation expanding the equity jurisdiction of Maryland courts up to the age of 21 specifically for SIJS factual predicate findings.
- **Maryland Rules of Procedure (Rules 2-121–2-126, 10-201, & 16-307):** Court rules governing the service of process (including international service), guardianship procedures, and the authority of family case managers.
- **Not found in the available sources:** Other specific formal statutory regulations are not mentioned in the source text \[FACT\].

## Background

A surge in unaccompanied child immigration from Central America triggered a massive influx of SIJS requests in Maryland family courts. This influx threatened to overwhelm the court's docket capacity if not aggressively managed using data-informed strategies.

## Objectives

1\. Ensure the issuance of SIJS factual predicate findings is timely, consistent, and accurate to support the rule of law. 2. Slash the accumulation of case backlogs and reduce the age of pending cases to comply with Maryland’s family case time standards. 3. Transform judicial administration through Differentiated Case Management (DCM) and the active utilization of performance data.

## Scope

This policy encompasses the evaluation of the governance of custody and guardianship cases involving SIJS requests at the Montgomery County Circuit Court, Maryland, from 2013 to 2016.

## Key Provisions

- **Three-Judicial Officer Model:**
  - Assigning two independent judges specifically for guardianship + SIJS cases, and one dedicated magistrate for custody + SIJS cases.
- **Court-Managed Automated Scheduling:**
  - Reclaiming scheduling control from attorneys; the IT system automatically schedules custody and SIJS hearings within 125 days after valid service of process is achieved.
- **Pre-Trial File Verification & Critical Review Sheets:**
  - Rigorous screening of international service and translated documents by case managers, followed by the generation of automated critical review sheets prior to hearings.

## Implementation

- **Evolution of the Case Management System:** The transition from a single-judge model (2007–2013), to a two-judge approach (2014–2015), and finally to the three-judicial officer model with special dockets (2015–2016).
- **Development of a Dedicated SIJS Database:** The creation of a secondary support database that pulls data from the court's main system to automate reporting and generate critical working documents.

## Policy Problems

- **Challenges with International Service of Process:** The extreme difficulty of lawfully serving legal summons to parents residing in foreign countries.
- **Vulnerability to Staff Turnover:** Operational disruptions and temporary dips in case closures resulting from the turnover of key case management personnel.

## Reform / Change

- Establishing a cross-functional Technical Advisory Board to continuously identify business efficiency gaps and monitor data.
- Institutionalizing ongoing training for court staff and clerks regarding the vital role of state trial courts in juvenile immigration law.

## Relationships with Other Entities

- **USCIS & HHS/ORR:** The federal immigration authorities receiving the SIJS predicate findings from the state court.
- **National Center for State Courts (NCSC):** The organization developing national guidelines for handling juvenile immigration cases in state courts.
