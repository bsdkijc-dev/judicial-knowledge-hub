# Judicial-policy-rooze-differentiated-ecms-2008

**Differentiated Use of Electronic Case Management Systems** is an analytical framework and judicial ICT policy study authored by Dr. Erwin J. Rooze (former adviser to the Netherlands Council for the Judiciary). Based on international qualitative research (2006–2008) across European countries and Australia, the framework applies **contingency theory** to classify judicial ICT applications into three levels of sophistication (Basic, Medium, Advanced), four operational components (Administrative, Logistics, Procedural, Content Management), and three caseflow tracks (Mass, Regular, Complex Caseflows) in order to optimize judicial efficiency and procedural justice \[1, 2, 4, 10–15, 25–28, 34, 35\].

## Summary

Drawing on empirical experience in the Netherlands, Austria, England, Singapore, and Western Australia, Dr. Rooze argues that no single ICT system suits every type of case ("no one best way to organize"). Courts must align ICT investment with the specific characteristics of their caseload: mass caseflows require advanced logistics automation to the point of forming a fully-fledged e-court, while complex caseflows require advanced digital content management, which has been shown to cut judges' analysis time by **50%** \[10–12, 27, 28, 33, 36, 37\].

## Background & Contingency Theory

The concept of case management originated in the United States to shift control of case progress from lawyers to the courts. Drawing on organizational contingency theory (Lawrence & Lorsch 1967; Galbraith 1973), Rooze argues that a court's structure and ICT support needs depend on its external environment and its specific area of law (civil, criminal, commercial, administrative) \[9–13\].

## The Four Components of Case Management (ECMS)

1\. **Administrative Management:** Case registration, unique case-number assignment, file location tracking, and management reporting. 2. **Logistics Management:** Guiding caseflow from intake to archiving, milestone planning, capacity allocation (courtrooms, judges, staff), and tracking/tracing. 3. **Procedural Management:** Enforcing procedural rules, verifying jurisdiction, checking court fee payments, milestone signaling, and automated notifications. 4. **Content Management:** Dynamic text templates, official document drafting, audio/video recording, digital archiving, and automated decision-making for uncontested claims.

## Three Levels of ICT Sophistication

- **Basic Level (Electronic Case Administration):** Focused on reliable electronic databases, schedule printing, and basic reporting.
- **Medium Level (Caseflow Support):** Integrating calendars and judge/staff data to automate milestone tracking and backlog alerts.
- **Advanced Level (Digital Handling & e-Court):** Fully paperless digital file handling, self-service web filing, and automated document generation.

## Differentiation by Caseflow Track

| Caseflow Track       | Case Characteristics                                                                 | Key ICT Component                                     | Real-World Examples                                                             |
|----------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Mass Caseflow**    | High volume, low complexity (e.g., traffic violations, small money claims).          | Advanced Logistics Management & e-Court Automation    | Money Claim Online (England) & Mahnverfahren (Austria).                         |
| **Complex Caseflow** | Low volume, high complexity (e.g., intellectual property, international cybercrime). | Advanced Content Management & Digital File Analysis   | 50% reduction in judicial analysis time in the Netherlands & Western Australia. |
| **Regular Caseflow** | Medium volume, medium complexity.                                                    | Procedural Management to maintain standard scheduling | Use of advanced web forms in Singapore.                                         |

## Policy Issues & Implementation Realism

1\. **Failure of Judicial ICT Projects:** Many judicial ICT projects in Europe have failed due to excessive optimism and rigid system designs that ignore case-type contingency (Velicogna 2007). 2. **Preventing Wasted Investment:** High-tech ICT investment in mass caseflows should focus on administrative efficiency, while investment in complex caseflows should focus on judges' knowledge work \[31–33\].
