# Judicial-policy-ijca-vol3no2-2010

# 'International Journal for Court Administration, Vol. 3, No. 2' (IACA, 2010)

### Wiki Entity Page

**International Journal for Court Administration (Vol. 3, No. 2)** is the official peer-reviewed electronic periodical publication of the *International Association for Court Administration* (IACA) featuring comparative scientific studies on the economic analysis of judicial incentives (law and economics), institutional reform of the UK Supreme Court, the application of information technology for dispute resolution, and administrative justice efficiency \[1–6, 16, 65, 147, 176\].

## Summary

This sixth issue is led by Markus B. Zimmer (Executive Editor) and Prof. Dr. Philip M. Langbroek (Managing Editor). The publication contains four main policy papers from Europe analyzing judicial incentive schemes, the establishment of the UK Supreme Court, the matrix of judicial roles in IT implementation, and the efficiency of administrative justice in Macedonia.

## Background & Key Article Summaries

### 1. Judicial Incentives & Economic Analysis (Zajc & Kovač - Slovenia & Europe)

- **Principal-Agent Theory:** Judges act as utility-maximizing agents, but high monitoring costs (\$C_d \> B_p\$) mean that salary increases do not directly impact productivity \[20–22, 24, 25\].
- **Danger of Using Punishments (Sticks):** The use of sticks (dismissals/sanctions) triggers a *multiplication effect* that risks being abused by authoritarian rulers and threatens judicial independence \[35–37\].
- **Use of Carrots (Rewards):** Encouraging productivity through promotion prospects (rank-ordered tournaments), peer reputation, and legacy.
- **Finnish Quality Benchmarks (2006):** Measures 40 criteria across 6 aspects: The Process, The Decision, Treatment of the Public, Promptness, Competence, and Organization \[39–45\].

### 2. Establishment of the UK Supreme Court (Gavin Drewry - United Kingdom)

- Established under the *Constitutional Reform Act 2005*, ending the judicial functions of the House of Lords and launching the UK Supreme Court on October 1, 2009.
- Functions as a non-ministerial department under Chief Executive Jenny Rowe and President Lord Phillips, fully separated from Parliament and the Ministry of Justice.

### 3. Judicial Roles Matrix & Information Technology (Dory Reiling - Netherlands)

- **4 Judicial Roles:**
  - **Title Provision (Group 1):** 35% of cases (Undefended money claims, zero-sum, certain outcome) \$\rightarrow\$ Primary target for automation (e.g., MCOL in the UK, Mahnverfahren in Germany) \[147–150, 154, 158–160\].
  - **Notarial (Group 2):** 30% of cases (Family/labor, win-win, certain outcome) \$\rightarrow\$ Web guidance features.
  - **Settlement (Group 3):** 9% of cases (Mediation/negotiation, win-win, uncertain outcome) \$\rightarrow\$ Negotiation software.
  - **Judgment (Group 4):** 8% of cases (Legal dispute hearings, zero-sum, uncertain outcome) \$\rightarrow\$ Electronic case files (e-file) & knowledge management \[151, 154, 168–170\].

### 4. Administrative Justice Efficiency in Macedonia (Pelivanova & Dimeski)

- Transformed the Anglo-Saxon model in the Supreme Court to a Continental model by establishing the Administrative Court (2007) and Higher Administrative Court (2010/2011).
- Addressed case backlogs (10,340 unresolved cases in 2009) through the recruitment of judicial assistants and specialized training.

## Digital Technology Implementation

The deployment of 4-channel audio/video Electronic Recording (ER) with time-stamped log notes and a keyword-searchable central directory accelerates transcript preparation and the appeals process \[127–134\].

## Policy Problems

1\. Difficulty in quantitatively measuring the quality of judicial decisions without undermining judicial independence. 2. Institutional anachronism of apex courts merged with legislative bodies. 3. E-justice technology mismatches due to a failure to map caseflow functions.

## Reform / Change

1\. Adoption of the Finnish Quality Benchmarks instrument for court evaluation. 2. Full structural separation of the UK Supreme Court from Parliament. 3. Automation of mass claims (title group) and the application of digital files for complex cases.
