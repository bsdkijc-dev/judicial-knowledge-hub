#### 4. POLICY ANALYSIS

**Background**: \[FACT\] Youth in Spain (ages 15–29) face prolonged structural vulnerability in the labor market, marked by unemployment rates far higher than adult age groups. The impact of successive economic crises has positioned youth as perpetual victims struggling to access decently paid work.

**Policy Problem**: \[FACT\]

1.  *High and Involuntary Temporariness*: Spain recorded involuntary temporariness among youth under 24 at 44.2% in 2022 (far above the Eurozone average of 16.5%).
2.  *Shift to "Fijo-Discontinuo" Contracts*: Although Royal Decree-Law 32/2021 statistically reduced fixed-term contracts, companies shifted to "fijo-discontinuo" (fixed but discontinuous) contracts, which do not guarantee real work continuity, fueling underemployment (workers wanting and able to work more hours but limited to fewer).
3.  *Overqualification*: Spain recorded the EU's highest youth overqualification rate at 35.8% in 2023 (EU average: 22%).
4.  *Brain Drain and Mental Health*: A lack of recognition for qualifications drives talent abroad, and 55.6% of youth facing economic hardship experience mental health problems.

**Legal/Policy Gap**: \[FACT\] Employment policy has focused too narrowly on contract-form changes and fiscal incentives without addressing structural root causes: insufficient investment in new high-qualification sectors, misalignment between educational curricula and real labor market needs, and weak integration of dual vocational training (*formación dual*).

**Objectives**: \[FACT\] Guarantee access to stable, decent work; reduce real temporariness and underemployment; curb overqualification through dual training; and meet the targets of the ILO's 2030 Agenda and the Youth Guarantee Plus Plan 2021–2027.

**Implementation**: \[FACT\]

- *ET Reform via RDL 32/2021*: Strict restriction on the use of temporary contracts (Art. 15 ET) and restructuring of training contracts into two modalities (Art. 11 ET: alternating training and professional practice).
- *Law 3/2023 on Employment*: Designation of youth (particularly those with low qualifications or no degree) as a "priority group requiring special attention" in active employment policy (Arts. 50, 53.3).
- *Youth Guarantee Plus Plan 2021–2027*: Implementation of a decent-work program to promote long-term (permanent) hiring and combat job precariousness.

**Evidence & Impact**: \[FACT\] INE (Labor Force Survey) and Eurostat data show a decline in the percentage of temporary contracts after 2021, but job precariousness persists due to the surge in "fijo-discontinuo" contract use and continued high overqualification (35.8%).

**Reform & Unresolved Issues**: \[FACT\] Integration of educational curricula with industry economic needs; expansion of dual training; establishment of employment guidance offices in depopulated rural areas; incentives for study in growth sectors (circular economy, sustainable tourism, sustainable agriculture); and specific protection for doubly vulnerable groups (young women, migrants, people with disabilities).

#### 6. MEDIAWIKI OUTPUT

## Summary

**Youth Employment and Hiring Following Recent Political-Legislative Actions: A Critical Commentary** is a labor law scholarly work written by Isabella Marie Iturrizaga of Universidad de Málaga (UMA). The study presents a critical evaluation of the effectiveness of Spain's political-legislative employment reforms in addressing youth job precariousness. The research analyzes the impact of Royal Decree-Law 32/2021 on restricting fixed-term contracts (Art. 15 ET) and restructuring training contracts (Art. 11 ET), the role of Law 3/2023 on Employment and the Youth Guarantee Plus Plan 2021–2027, and recommendations from the International Labour Organization (ILO). The study highlights that despite a statistical decline in temporariness, precariousness persists due to a shift toward "fijo-discontinuo" contracts, rising underemployment, and continued high overqualification in Spain.

## Legal Basis

The regulations and legal reforms analyzed in this publication are grounded in the following Spanish and international legal framework:

- **Spanish Constitution (CE)**: Article 35 (the right and duty to work) and Article 40 (mandate for full-employment policy and vocational training).
- **Royal Legislative Decree 2/2015 (Workers' Statute — ET)**: Article 11 (training contracts: alternating training and professional practice) and Article 15 (rules restricting fixed-term contracts).
- **Royal Decree-Law 32/2021, of 28 December**: Labor reform to ensure job stability and reduce temporariness.
- **Law 3/2023, of 28 February, on Employment**: Article 50 and Article 53.3 (designation of youth as a "priority group requiring special attention").
- **Youth Guarantee Plus Plan 2021–2027**: A national decent-work program for Spanish youth.
- **ILO Initiatives**: The Youth Employment Programme (2005), the Decent Work for Youth Initiative, and recommendations from the 2019 Global Youth Employment Forum.

## Background

Spanish youth have historically been the group most affected by successive economic crises. Difficulty accessing decently paid work has created economic vulnerability, affecting mental health and delaying personal independence.

## Objectives

1\. Analyze the effectiveness of recent legislative reforms in improving youth working conditions. 2. Identify the real impact of temporary-contract restrictions on job stability. 3. Assess the level of overqualification among Spanish youth compared to the EU average. 4. Promote alignment between educational curricula and real labor market needs. 5. Propose inclusive active employment policies for vulnerable youth groups.

## Scope

The study covers analysis across four main pillars: 1. *Constitutional and Regulatory Framework*: Application of Articles 35 and 40 CE, the ET, RDL 32/2021, and Law 3/2023. 2. *International Initiatives (ILO)*: Evaluation of the application of the 2019 Global Forum's 6 recommendations in Spain. 3. *Temporariness and Underemployment*: The shift from temporary contracts to "fijo-discontinuo" modalities and its implications. 4. *Overqualification*: Eurostat data (35.8%), the "brain drain" phenomenon, and the potential of dual training.

## Key Provisions

- **Restriction of Fixed-Term Contracts (Art. 15 ET)**: Employment contracts are presumed to be for an indefinite term; fixed-term contracts are permitted only for specific production reasons or worker replacement.
- **Dual Training Contract Structure (Art. 11 ET)**: Comprises the alternating-training contract (combining paid work and training) and the training contract for obtaining professional practice.
- **Special Attention Priority (Arts. 50 & 53.3, Law 3/2023)**: Youth, particularly those with low qualifications or no degree, are classified as a priority group in active employment policy.

## Implementation

Implementation occurs through active employment policy execution by the Public Employment Service, dual-training programs at universities and vocational centers, and compliance oversight by the Labor Inspectorate.

## Policy Problems

- Use of "fijo-discontinuo" contracts as a substitute for temporary contracts, without providing real income continuity.
- High overqualification (35.8%), fueling professional frustration and talent flight abroad.
- Misalignment between the education system and the skill needs of new industrial sectors.

## Reform

- Enactment of Royal Decree-Law 32/2021.
- Enactment of Law 3/2023, of 28 February, on Employment.
- Implementation of the Youth Guarantee Plus Plan 2021–2027.

## Relationships with Other Entities

- **Implements**: The Spanish Constitution (Arts. 35, 40), RDL 32/2021, and Law 3/2023 on Employment.
- **Applies To**: All young workers (ages 15–29) and employers in Spain.
- **Conflicts With**: The practice of using "fijo-discontinuo" schemes to mask underemployment.
