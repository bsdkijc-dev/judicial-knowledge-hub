# Judicial-policy-attorney-impact-civil-cases-castelliano-2018

**The Castelliano, Watanabe & Netto Empirical Impact of Attorneys Policy Framework** is an empirical judicial policy and civil attorney performance evaluation model formulated by Caio Castelliano de Vasconcelos, Eduardo Watanabe, and Waldir Leôncio Netto in the *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA), Vol. 9 No. 2 (July 2018). The framework leverages a natural experiment at the Office of the Solicitor General of the Union (SGU - *Procuradoria-Geral da União*) in Brazil, based on the pure random assignment of cases, to analyze 30,821 judicial decisions handled by 386 Federal Attorneys across 70 specialized teams. It discovers that attorneys significantly influence the win rate in half of the teams (43% at sig \< 0.05 and 59% at sig \< 0.10) and establishes that there is no statistical correlation between an attorney's experience/seniority and the outcome of civil cases \[1–68\].

## Summary

Published by Caio Castelliano de Vasconcelos, Eduardo Watanabe, and Waldir Leôncio Netto in the IJCA (July 2018), this policy framework fills the severe scarcity of empirical evidence regarding the impact of attorneys in civil litigation. To overcome the methodological hurdle of the case-selection effect (where private attorneys select easy-to-win cases, skewing performance metrics), the study utilizes a natural experiment at the SGU in Brazil, where 30,821 civil cases were assigned purely randomly by an IT system to 386 Federal Attorneys organized into 70 specialized teams. Logistic regression analysis reveals that in approximately half of the teams (43% at sig. \< 0.05 and 59% at sig. \< 0.10), attorney performance had a statistically significant impact on the win rate, with the maximum odds (Max Odds) of winning varying dramatically—up to 10.98 times higher between attorneys on the same team. Furthermore, Pearson correlation testing proves the absence of a statistical correlation between an attorney's seniority/experience and their civil case win rate, a phenomenon the authors attribute to the trade-off between increasing experience and diminishing workplace motivation over time.

## Background

It is highly difficult to isolate a lawyer's pure advocacy skills from the inherent characteristics of a civil case due to the lack of random case assignment in private law firms, leaving the true impact of civil attorneys largely unmeasured in global judicial literature.

## Objectives

1\. Scientifically isolate and measure the absolute impact of an attorney's work on civil case outcomes by eliminating confounding variables through random assignment. 2. Verify the degree of *judge sensitivity* to variations in the quality of civil attorney advocacy. 3. Test the statistical correlation between an attorney's seniority/experience and the case win rate.

## Scope

This policy encompasses the empirical evaluation of 30,821 judicial decisions handled by 386 Federal Attorneys within 70 specialized SGU teams across the Federative Republic of Brazil.

## Key Provisions

- **Random Case Assignment Natural Experiment:**
  - Utilizing an IT system's automated random distribution to eliminate confounding variables and the case-selection effect.
- **Pretreatment Balance Testing (k-means Gap Statistic):**
  - Statistical testing that proved 95.26% of the teams were perfectly homogeneous regarding the distribution of judges, confirming the pure randomness of the sample.
- **Significant Findings on Judicial Outcomes:**
  - Statistical significance was found in 43% of teams (sig. \< 0.05) and 59% of teams (sig. \< 0.10), proving that judges are highly sensitive to attorney advocacy in half of all case types.
- **Independence of Seniority from Case Outcomes:**
  - The lack of a Pearson correlation between attorney seniority and the win rate due to the shifting dynamics of work motivation.

## Implementation

- **SGU Dataset Filtering (2011–2014):** Processing 93,263 initial defenses down to 30,821 resolved cases to guarantee strict statistical validity.
- **Application of Logistic Regression Models:** Calculating the *goodness of fit* and the *Max Odds* ratio of winning among attorneys within the same specialized team.

## Policy Problems

- **Traditional Case-Selection Bias:** Private attorneys tend to cherry-pick easily winnable cases, obscuring the measurement of their true pure advocacy skills.
- **Bureaucratic Reliance on Pure Seniority Criteria:** Public sector promotion and compensation systems often glorify tenure and seniority without regarding objective performance evidence.

## Reform / Change

- Adapting performance-based case assignment processes by allocating the best-performing attorneys to high-value, strategic civil cases.
- Mandating that high-win-rate attorneys share their successful advocacy strategies and defense drafts with their peers to elevate organizational performance.

### Deutsch (Schweizer Hochdeutsch)

**Empirisches Rahmenwerk von Castelliano, Watanabe & Netto zur Wirkung von Anwälten** Dieser von Caio Castelliano de Vasconcelos, Eduardo Watanabe und Waldir Leôncio Netto im *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA, 2018) veröffentlichte Rahmen bewertet empirisch den Einfluss von Zivilanwälten auf Gerichtsurteile. Um den Auswahleffekt (Case-Selection Effect) zu umgehen, nutzt die Studie ein natürliches Experiment: die IT-gestützte, rein zufällige Fallzuweisung an 386 Bundesanwälte (SGU) in Brasilien. Die Analyse von 30.821 Urteilen zeigt, dass die Leistung des Anwalts in etwa der Hälfte der Teams (43 % bei sig \< 0,05) die Gewinnquote signifikant beeinflusst, wobei die Gewinnchancen (Max Odds) bis zu 10,98-fach variieren. Die Studie beweist zudem, dass keine statistische Korrelation zwischen der Dienstaltersstufe/Erfahrung eines Anwalts und seinem Erfolg besteht, was auf einen Motivationseinbruch bei älteren Anwälten hindeutet.

- **Rechtsgrundlage:** Bundesverfassung Brasiliens 1988; Interne Vorschriften der SGU; IT-System der Bundesgerichte.
