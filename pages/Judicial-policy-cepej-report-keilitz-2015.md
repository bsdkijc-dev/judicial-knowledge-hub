# Judicial-policy-cepej-report-keilitz-2015

**The CEPEJ European Judicial Systems Evaluation Policy Framework** is a transnational judicial evaluation policy framework and comparative performance benchmarking model developed by the European Commission for the Efficiency of Justice (CEPEJ) and reviewed by Ingo Keilitz (CourtMetrics & College of William and Mary, 2015) in the *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA). Based on the *European Judicial Systems – Edition 2014 (2012 Data)* report (521 pages, 45 Council of Europe member states), it establishes a 208-indicator survey utilizing home-grown evidence-based data to measure efficiency, budget allocation, e-justice, leadership, and the quality of judicial services \[2–6, 12–13\].

## Summary

Published by Ingo Keilitz in the *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA), this policy framework evaluates the fifth edition of the CEPEJ Report, *European Judicial Systems – Edition 2014 (2012 Data)*. Collecting data from 45 Council of Europe countries through a verified 208-indicator survey, the report presents an in-depth analysis of judicial budgets, e-justice, procedural simplification, case clearance rates, and the glass ceiling constraints faced by female judges. Keilitz demonstrates the superiority of the CEPEJ methodology, which relies on valid, home-grown government data, compared to global donor indices (such as WJP or UNDP) \[12–13\]. This methodology was successfully replicated to benchmark the judicial reform project in Kenya (*Judicial Performance Improvement Project*) under Chief Justice Willy Mutunga.

## Legal Basis

- **Statute of the Council of Europe & CEPEJ Mandate:** The institutional foundation establishing CEPEJ as the expert body for European judicial evaluation.
- **CEPEJ 208-Item Survey Framework:** The standardized instrument for measuring judicial efficiency and quality.
- **Kenya Judicial Performance Improvement Project (2012):** The judicial transformation framework of Kenya based on CEPEJ benchmarks.
- **Not found in the available sources:** Other specific formal statutory regulations are not mentioned in the source text \[FACT\].

## Background

The critical need for transparent and measurable judicial performance data prompted the Council of Europe to establish CEPEJ. The CEPEJ biennial report has become the primary reference for policymakers and legal researchers worldwide \[2–3\].

## Objectives

1\. Provide a verified database regarding judicial efficiency and quality across 45 European countries \[2–3\]. 2. Facilitate comparative analysis (benchmarking) of judicial performance at both regional and global levels \[10–11\]. 3. Encourage the utilization of home-grown data to support sustainable judicial reforms \[12–13\].

## Scope

This policy encompasses the evaluation of judicial systems across 45 Council of Europe member states, budgetary indicators, e-court operations, and the comparative application of judicial performance metrics outside Europe (specifically, Kenya).

## Key Provisions

- **208-Indicator Evaluation Methodology:**
  - Measurements include the supply side (e.g., budgets and court buildings) as well as the demand side (e.g., case clearance rates and disposition times).
  - Data is collected by national correspondents and directly verified by the CEPEJ expert team.
- **Key Trends Output (Chapter 18 of the CEPEJ Report):**

1.  Judicial budgets remain a public priority despite economic crises, though investments are uneven.
2.  Expansion of e-justice and e-courts (e-filing, automated case registration, electronic signatures, and jurisprudence databases).
3.  Implementation of simplified procedures for undisputed civil claims and minor criminal offenses.
4.  The excessive length of judicial proceedings remains a primary challenge.
5.  The glass ceiling phenomenon for women in the judiciary persists.

## Implementation

- **Publication of the 2014 CEPEJ Report:** A 521-page biennial evaluation containing over 250 tables and figures \[2–3\].
- **Benchmarking the Kenyan Judiciary:** Replication of the CEPEJ methodology by Ingo Keilitz to measure the performance of the *Judicial Performance Improvement Project* under Chief Justice Willy Mutunga.

## Policy Problems

- **Excessive Length of Proceedings:** Delays in case handling that severely disrupt access to justice.
- **Dominance of Top-down Donor Indicators:** The risk of reform failure due to the reliance on third-party global indices (WJP/UNDP) that fail to reflect local realities \[12–13\].

## Reform / Change

- The adoption of home-grown performance management systems by national judiciaries.
- The critical need for procedural simplification and the expansion of e-court automation.

### 3 Most Important Improvements

1\. **Systematic Adoption of Demand-side Indicators:** Mandate courts to routinely measure case clearance rates and case disposition times to combat case backlogs effectively. 2. **Shift from External Donor Indicators to Home-grown Data:** Encourage national judiciaries to build independent Performance Management Directorates to produce evaluations aligned with actual domestic needs. 3. **Expansion of E-Justice and E-Courts Implementation:** Accelerate judicial digitization (e-filing, automated registration, electronic signatures) to shorten the duration of proceedings and increase overall efficiency.
