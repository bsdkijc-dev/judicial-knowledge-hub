## 4. Policy Analysis

Background  
Expanding direct and indirect participation by retail investors for wealth accumulation and retirement planning has elevated capital market fairness and investor protection into a core public interest. However, recurring mass financial losses — such as the KIKO currency option crisis, Woori Power Income Fund, DSME accounting fraud, and the Lime and Optimus private equity fund redemption suspensions — highlight systemic vulnerabilities in South Korea's post-hoc collective redress mechanisms.

<!-- -->

Policy Problem  
**Protracted Civil Litigation & Evidentiary Imbalance**: Collective investor lawsuits under general civil procedure take years to resolve (averaging 10 to 53+ months for 1st instance judgments) due to severe evidentiary asymmetry (증거편재), where critical financial engineering, valuation, and sales data are held exclusively by financial institutions.

**Underutilization of Securities Class Actions**: Over 19 years since its enactment in 2005, only 12 securities class action lawsuits were filed in South Korea (averaging \< 0.63 cases/year). Appeals against class certification decisions take an average of 57.54 months — consuming 85% of total trial duration — because immediate appeals automatically suspend main proceedings.

**Inadequate Regulatory ADR Mechanics**: Financial Dispute Mediation (금융분쟁조정) provides low-cost resolution but lacks legally binding collective enforcement (편면적 구속력 or statutory collective mediation), forcing uncompensated investors back into court if financial institutions reject non-binding recommendations.

<!-- -->

Legal/Policy Gap  
Financial law combines private contract law and public regulatory law. While regulatory authorities collect disgorged profits or administrative fines (과징금), these funds revert entirely to the national treasury (국고) rather than compensating victimized investors.

<!-- -->

Objectives  
Streamline judicial evidence collection, reduce class certification appellate delays, concentrate common-issue dockets, and establish a statutory mechanism allowing financial regulators to disgorge illicit gains via court actions and distribute them directly to injured investors.

<!-- -->

Implementation & Proposed Reforms  
**Securities Class Action Act Amendments**: Amend Article 15 to specify that immediate appeals against class certification decisions do NOT suspend main court proceedings (excluding suspension of execution), allowing trial courts to conduct evidence gathering while certification is appealed. Introduce modern evidence discovery tools: Material Production Orders (자료제출명령), Deposition/Statement Recording (진술녹취제도), mandatory record transfers from regulatory bodies, and unified financial transaction queries via Korea Exchange (한국거래소 일괄조회).

**Practical Judicial Management**: Implement "Lead Case / Common Issue Concentration" (한 재판부 관련 사건 병합/집중심리), establish Pre-trial Case Explanation Hearings (사건설명기일), expand Expert Commissioner participation (전문심리위원), and extend judge assignment terms (사무분담 장기화).

**Capital Markets Act Amendment (Long-term)**: Insert Articles 420-2 and 420-3 to establish an Action for Restitution / Disgorgement of Unjust Enrichment (부당이득환수의 소). This empowers the Financial Services Commission (FSC) to sue non-compliant financial entities in court to disgorge profits or recoup investor losses, appointing court-supervised distribution managers (분배관리인) to disburse funds directly to affected investors.

<!-- -->

Evidence & Empirical Data  
**Civil Lawsuits**: Average 1st instance trial duration ranged from 10.1 months (Woori Power Income) to 53.89 months (DSME).

**Securities Class Actions**: Out of 12 total filed cases, average class certification resolution took 57.54 months, main case disposition took 67.66 months, and complete distribution took 89.0 months. Attorney fees averaged 22.71% of recovery, while net investor payouts averaged 74.62% of executed recovery.

**International Benchmarks**: US SEC Fair Funds distributed \$903.86 million annually (2017–2023); UK FCA enacted mandatory Consumer Redress Schemes (CONRED) for Arch cru and British Steel Pension cases.

## 6. MediaWiki Output

**A Study on the Improvement of the Collective Redress Procedures for Investors** is an official judicial policy research report published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of South Korea's Supreme Court.

## Summary

This document is an official judicial policy research report published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of South Korea's Supreme Court. It comparatively and empirically analyzes collective redress mechanisms for investors through general civil litigation, securities class actions, and regulatory alternative dispute resolution (ADR). It formulates practical court case-management guidance along with draft amendments to the Securities Class Action Act and the Capital Markets Act.

## Legal Basis

The legal frameworks analyzed include:

- **Capital Markets Act (자본시장법)**: Articles 1, 125, 162, 175, 177, 179, and proposed draft Articles 420-2 & 420-3.
- **Financial Consumer Protection Act (금융소비자보호법)**: Articles 1, 17–21, 33, 41, 42, 44.
- **Securities Class Action Act (증권관련집단소송법)**: Articles 3, 11, 12, 15, 17, 30–34, and proposed amendment drafts.
- **Civil Act & Civil Procedure Act of Korea (민법 / 민사소송법)**: Articles 51, 53, 65, 202-2, 344–350, 750.

## Background

Growing public participation in financial investment has made capital market fairness a critical public value. However, mass-loss cases such as KIKO, the Woori Power Income Fund, DSME accounting fraud, and the Lime and Optimus fund suspensions demonstrate the slow pace of investor redress through the courts.

## Objectives

1.  Address evidentiary imbalance (증거편재) and shorten the duration of investor lawsuits.
2.  Amend the Securities Class Action Act so that appeals against class certification no longer halt examination of the main case.
3.  Establish an Action for Restitution of Unjust Enrichment (부당이득환수의 소) so regulators can seize illegal profits and distribute them directly to investors.

## Scope

- **Domestic Empirical Study**: Statistical analysis of civil cases, 12 securities class actions (2005–2023), and Financial Dispute Mediation Committee mediations.
- **Four-Country Comparative Study**: United States (SEC Fair Funds & Class Actions), United Kingdom (FCA Redress Schemes & Restitution Orders), Germany (KapMuG & VDuG), and Japan (Consumer Court Procedures Special Measures Act & Designated ADR bodies).

## Key Provisions

- **Removal of Appeal Suspension Effect**: Immediate appeals (즉시항고) against class certification no longer suspend examination of the main case's evidence.
- **Strengthened Evidence Tools**: Adoption of Material Production Orders (자료제출명령), the Deposition/Statement Recording System (진술녹취제도), and integrated data transfer from the Korea Exchange (KRX).
- **Regulator Restitution Action**: The Financial Services Commission (FSC) is empowered to sue violators in court and appoint distribution managers (분배관리인) to disburse funds to victims.

## Implementation

Courts adopt integrated case management (Lead Case Concentration), Pre-trial Case Explanation Hearings (사건설명기일), expanded use of Expert Commissioners (전문심리위원), and extended judge assignment terms in corporate/securities divisions.

## Policy Issues

- Administrative fines (과징금) and criminal forfeiture proceeds currently go to the national treasury (국고) rather than compensating investor losses.
- The lengthy class certification process (averaging 57.54 months) undermines the deterrent function of private enforcement (사적제재).

## Reform / Amendments

- Amendment of the Securities Class Action Act (Articles 15, 17, 32, 33, 34).
- Amendment of the Capital Markets Act (addition of Articles 420-2 and 420-3).
