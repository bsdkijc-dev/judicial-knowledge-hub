# `judicial-policy-swiss-performance-assessment-lienhard-2014`

**The Swiss Judicial Performance Assessment and Constitutional Policy Framework** is a judicial governance and judicial performance evaluation policy framework formulated by Prof. Dr. Andreas Lienhard (University of Bern / Centre of Competence for Public Management, 2014) based on the Swiss National Science Foundation (SNSF) research project 'Principles of Good Court Management in Switzerland'. It analyzes the constitutional alignment between the obligation to guarantee justice (efficiency, effectiveness, self-government, supervision, and term-of-office re-appointment) and the protection of judicial independence (Articles 30 & 191c of the Swiss Federal Constitution) as well as individual data privacy protection (Article 13 Paragraph 2 of the Swiss Federal Constitution) \[1, 4, 11, 30, 32, 52–53\].

## Summary

Published by Prof. Dr. Andreas Lienhard in the *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA), this policy framework addresses the challenges of assessing judicial performance in Switzerland. Amidst surging workloads and limited public budgets, courts are increasingly pressured to prove their efficiency and service quality. Lienhard asserts that evaluating judicial performance is constitutionally justifiable and necessary as part of the state's obligation to guarantee justice and facilitate judicial self-government. However, such evaluations face strict constitutional boundaries: establishing binding performance targets that interfere with decision-making is strictly prohibited, evaluating the material substance of rulings is forbidden, and the privacy of individual judges' performance data must be rigidly protected in accordance with Article 13 Paragraph 2 of the Swiss Federal Constitution.

## Legal Basis

- **Swiss Federal Constitution (Schweizerische Bundesverfassung / BV):**
  - \*Article 29 Paragraph 1 BV\*: Prohibition of judicial delays and the right to prompt proceedings.
  - \*Article 29 Paragraph 2 BV\*: Right to fair treatment and the obligation to provide adequate legal justification.
  - \*Article 30 Paragraph 1 & Article 191c BV\*: Guarantee of an independent and impartial court and the protection of judicial independence.
  - \*Article 43a Paragraph 5 & Article 126 Paragraph 1 BV\*: Principles regarding the economical fulfillment of state tasks and balanced financial management.
  - \*Article 168 Paragraph 1 & Article 188 Paragraph 4 BV\*: The election of federal judges by the United Federal Assembly for fixed terms of office.
  - \*Article 169 Paragraph 1 & Article 170 BV\*: Parliamentary oversight and the evaluation of state measure effectiveness.
  - \*Article 188 Paragraph 3 BV\*: The autonomy and self-government of the Federal Supreme Court.
  - \*Article 13 Paragraph 2 BV\*: The protection of privacy and personal data against misuse.
- **Swiss Cantonal Regulations:** Article 127 of the Geneva Cantonal Constitution of 2012 (Magistrates Supervisory Council) and various parliamentary motions in the Canton of Lucerne.
- **International Standards:** \*CCJE Opinion No. 17 (2014)\* on the Evaluation of Judges' Work and \*CEPEJ Quality Checklists\*.
- **Not found in the available sources:** Other specific formal statutory regulations are not mentioned in the source text.

## Background

Increasing judicial workloads combined with the application of New Public Management (NPM) principles have triggered heightened demands for judicial accountability and transparency. Incidents such as a federal supreme court judge not being re-elected due to severe work delays, alongside cantonal constitutional reforms, drove the necessity for a constitutionally compliant judicial evaluation framework in Switzerland \[3, 5, 8–10\].

## Objectives

1\. Balance the constitutional alignment between the state's guarantee of justice and the protection of judicial independence. 2. Provide objective instruments for caseload management and equitable automated case allocation. 3. Establish objective, evidence-based guidelines for parliamentary committees during the term-of-office re-appointment process to prevent partisan politicization. 4. Regulate procedures for staff appraisal interviews, status discussions, and peer reviews without violating the privacy rights of individual judges.

## Scope

This policy encompasses the governance evaluation of the federal judiciary and the 26 cantons in Switzerland, and features a comparative analysis of judge evaluation systems across the European Union, Germany, the United States, Austria, the Netherlands, France, and Italy \[1, 11, 40–45\].

## Key Provisions

- **Two Constitutional Sides of Judicial Performance Assessment**:

1.  \*Constitutional Support (Permissibility)\*: Performance evaluation is permissible and necessary to fulfill the constitutional principles of efficiency (Art. 29/43a BV), internal supervision, and providing a basis for term re-appointment considerations (Art. 168/188 BV).
2.  \*Constitutional Barriers (Limits)\*: Judicial independence prohibits binding quantitative performance targets that might influence a judge's rulings; data privacy (Art. 13 BV) prohibits the open, public publication of an individual judge's performance statistics.

- **Operational Provisions of Evaluation**:
  - \*Evaluation Subjects\*: Strictly limited to case management efficiency, trial duration, leadership, and social/organizational competence. The substantive or material content of judicial decisions cannot be subjected to administrative evaluation.
  - \*Prohibition of Salary Linkage\*: Evaluation data must not be utilized as a determinant for salary increases to prevent the creation of false incentives that could corrupt the judicial process.
  - \*Right to Rebuttal and Appeal\*: Judges possess the right to be heard, to provide responses, and to file appeals to an independent body regarding their evaluation results.

## Implementation

- **Automated Case Allocation (Bandlimat)**: The implementation of an automated case distribution application in the Swiss Federal Administrative Court to ensure an equitable distribution of workload.
- **Status Discussions & Peer Review**: Annual status discussions are held in the Canton of Bern, and voluntary collegial feedback (peer review) is utilized at the Federal Criminal Court.

## Policy Problems

- **Risk of Implicit Pressure**: The danger that quantitative evaluations may indirectly pressure judges to resolve cases too hastily, thereby sacrificing procedural justice and careful legal analysis.
- **Politicization of Re-appointment**: The reality that without objective performance data, the re-election of judges by parliament risks being dominated solely by partisan political interests.

## Reform / Change

- Autonomous initiatives by judicial bodies to proactively design internal evaluation methods before such systems are forced upon them by external parliamentary bodies.
- Refinement of confidentiality procedures to guarantee that individual judge evaluation data is restricted solely to internal court management boards.

### Deutsch (Schweizer Hochdeutsch)

**Leistungsbeurteilung an Gerichten - Der Schweizer Fall** Dieser von Prof. Dr. Andreas Lienhard (Universität Bern) im *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA, 2014) veröffentlichte Rahmen analysiert die Leistungsbeurteilung von Richtern in der Schweiz aus verfassungsrechtlicher Sicht. Die Studie untersucht das Spannungsfeld zwischen der Rechtsweggarantie (Effizienz, Wirtschaftlichkeit, Wiederwahl) und dem Schutz der richterlichen Unabhängigkeit (Art. 30 & 191c BV) sowie dem Datenschutz (Art. 13 BV). Der Rahmen lehnt verbindliche Leistungsziele ab, die in die Entscheidungsfindung eingreifen könnten, unterstützt aber Beurteilungen des Fallmanagements und der Sozialkompetenz.

### Français

**Évaluation des performances dans les tribunaux - Le cas suisse** Publié par le Prof. Dr. Andreas Lienhard (Université de Berne) dans l' *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA, 2014), ce cadre analyse l'évaluation des performances des juges en Suisse d'un point de vue constitutionnel. L'étude examine la tension entre la garantie de la justice (efficacité, rentabilité, réélection) et la protection de l'indépendance judiciaire (Art. 30 & 191c Cst.) ainsi que la protection des données (Art. 13 Cst.). Le cadre rejette les objectifs de performance contraignants qui pourraient interférer avec la prise de décision, mais soutient l'évaluation de la gestion des cas et des compétences sociales.

Il quadro respinge gli obiettivi di prestazione vincolanti che potrebbero interferire con il processo decisionale, ma sostiene la valutazione della gestione dei casi e delle competenze sociali.
