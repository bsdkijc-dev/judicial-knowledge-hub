# Judicial-policy-india-pocso-special-courts-2012

# 'Special Courts for Children; Lessons Learnt From India' (Ferrao, 2024)

### Wiki Entity Page

**India's POCSO Special Courts and Child Protection Framework** is a specialized judicial administration policy and framework in the Republic of India established under the *Protection of Children from Sexual Offences (POCSO) Act 2012*. It is designed to adjudicate crimes of sexual violence and exploitation against children under the age of 18 through *child-friendly court procedures*.

## Summary

Enacted in 2012 in response to the high rate of sexual crimes against children in India (\>350 cases per day), this framework mandates the establishment of Special Courts in every district. The policy alters traditional criminal procedure law by requiring *in-camera* recording of testimonies, prohibiting direct visual confrontation between the child and the accused, and integrating national ICT monitoring systems (ITSSO, NDSO, CCTNS). Evaluations show significant progress in sentencing, but the system remains constrained by a backlog of over 243,000 cases and high acquittal rates.

## Legal Basis

- **Protection of Children from Sexual Offences (POCSO) Act 2012 (Act No. 32 of 2012):** Effective since June 19, 2012.
- **POCSO Act Amendment 2018:** Introduced capital punishment for aggravated penetrative sexual assault.
- **Indian Penal Code 1860 (IPC)**.
- **Code of Criminal Procedure 1973 (Cr.P.C.):** Section 173 (90-day investigation timeframe) and Section 232.
- **Supreme Court of India Jurisprudence:** *Sampurna Behura vs. Union of India* (2018) and SMW (Crl.) No. 1/2019.
- **High Courts Jurisprudence:** *Soumen Biswas vs. State of West Bengal* (Calcutta HC), *X vs. State of NCT of Delhi* (Delhi HC), and *Hanumantha Mogaveera vs. State of Karnataka* (Karnataka HC).

## Background

India has 444 million children under the age of 18. The National Crime Records Bureau (NCRB) report noted that sexual crimes account for 38% of all criminal offenses against children. The general judicial framework was deemed too intimidating and triggered deep trauma for child victims, necessitating an independent and responsive specialized judicial system.

## Objectives

1\. Stop the re-traumatization of child victims during the judicial process. 2. Ensure the completion of investigations within 2 months and trials within 1 year. 3. Break direct visual confrontation between the victim and the accused in court. 4. Provide medical, psychological, and financial rehabilitation and compensation for victims.

## Scope

This policy applies throughout the jurisdiction of the Republic of India. As of March 2024, 409 Special Courts are operational across 30 States and Union Territories, having resolved 229,361 cases. Jurisdiction covers crimes of penetrative sexual assault and child sexual exploitation.

## Key Provisions

- **Child-Friendly Testimony Recording (S.33 & S.24):** The child's statement is recorded at home or a comfortable place without police uniforms, and must be completed within 30 days of case registration.
- **Separation of the Accused (S.36):** Strict prohibition on bringing together or showing the accused to the child during the trial.
- **Mandatory Reporting (S.19):** Criminal obligation for anyone to report suspected sexual crimes against children.
- **No Statute of Limitations:** Victims can report sexual crimes at any time, even after reaching adulthood.

## Implementation

- **Application of Vulnerable Witness Facilities:** Construction of *Vulnerable Witness Deposition Courts* in Delhi (Kakardooma, Saket, Dwarka), Hyderabad, and Bengaluru, equipped with playrooms and transparent dividers.
- **Judicial ICT Systems:** Real-time file tracking through ITSSO and extraction of repeat offenders' track records via the *National Database on Sexual Offenders* (NDSO).

## Policy Problems

- **Case Backlogs:** Over 243,000 cases were pending in Special Courts as of 2023.
- **High Acquittal Rates:** Caused by the retraction of testimonies due to intimidation, crimes involving family members, or consensual adolescent relationships.
- **Failure in Compensation Payment:** The Supreme Court noted that in 99% of cases, victims did not receive interim/final compensation from legal aid funds.

## Reforms / Changes

- **Prohibition of Adjournments:** The Calcutta High Court affirmed the prohibition of trial adjournments when a child witness is present, and categorized strikes by advocates refusing to cross-examine a child as professional misconduct and criminal contempt.
- **Increased Compensation Value:** The Delhi High Court established an enhanced compensation scheme of Rs 10.5 Lakh for victims with severe injuries.
- **Specialized Medical & Mental Units:** The Karnataka High Court mandated the establishment of child handling units in every District Hospital with all psychological rehabilitation costs borne by the state.

## Relationships with Other Entities

- **Ministry of Law and Justice & Department of Justice:** Budget allocators for Fast Track Special Courts.
- **District Legal Services Authority (DLSA):** Managers of victim compensation funds.
- **Child Welfare Committee (CWC):** Social assistance and child rehabilitation agency.
