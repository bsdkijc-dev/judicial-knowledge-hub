# JPAIS:PRECEDENT:3888K PDT 1994

**Supreme Court Decision Reg. No. 3888 K/Pdt/1994** (dated June 19, 1996 / deliberation on May 8, 1996) is a fundamental land law, housing law, and civil evidentiary law jurisprudence precedent. It establishes vital rules concerning the lawfulness of house occupancy based on a Placement Permit (*Surat Izin Penempatan* / SIP) issued by competent authorities, the qualification of Unlawful Acts (Tort / PMH), and the absence of legal grounds to demand property eviction and damages against lawful SIP holders.

## Executive Summary

The Supreme Court established three primary legal principles:

1.  **Tenants Based on Lawful SIPs Do Not Commit a Tort (PMH):** "That the *Judex Facti* misapplied the law of evidence because the Petitioner for Cassation is the principal occupant of the disputed house, and the SIP obtained by him is lawful, fully compliant with requirements, and issued by the competent authority; hence, the Petitioner for Cassation is not proven to have committed an unlawful act."
2.  **Absence of Legal Basis for Tort Damages and Eviction:** "Because the Petitioner for Cassation is not proven to have committed an unlawful act, the claim for damages against the Petitioner for Cassation has no legal basis whatsoever and the quantum of such damages cannot be proven."
3.  **Lawful Tenancy Status:** A lawful SIP holder occupying a house (\[address removed\], Surabaya) since 1972 cannot be forcibly evicted on allegations of Tort. The building owner is entitled solely to reasonable house rent payments (fixed at Rp 2,000,000 per year for a 20-year period, totaling Rp 40,000,000).

The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition of the Petitioner for Cassation (\[Defendant I\]), annulled Surabaya High Court Decision No. 741/Pdt/1993/PT.Sby dated February 19, 1994, and independently adjudicated (*mengadili sendiri*). It partially granted the Plaintiff's lawsuit in Convention, declaring that the Plaintiff (Ny. \[Party B\]) is the lawful owner of the house at \[address removed\], Surabaya, and ordered Defendant I to pay rent of Rp 2,000,000 per year for 20 years (totaling Rp 40,000,000). It rejected the remainder of the Plaintiff's lawsuit (including claims for Tort, eviction, and coercive penalty fines / *dwangsom*), and rejected Defendant I's Reconvention counterclaim entirely.

## Legal / Procedural Issues

### 1. Qualification of House Occupancy Based on a Lawful Placement Permit (SIP) Issued by Government Housing Offices (PP No. 17 of 1963 *jo.* Housing Regulations)

*Can a citizen occupying rental housing based on an official Placement Permit (SIP) issued by the Regional Government Housing Office be qualified as committing an Unlawful Act (Tort)*ex*Article 1365 of the Civil Code?*

**Supreme Court Rule: Not an Unlawful Act (Tort).** Where an occupant occupies a disputed house based on a lawful Placement Permit (SIP) issued by a competent authority (Housing Office) in accordance with legal procedures and regularly pays rent, such occupancy is lawful and protected under housing law. The occupant is not proven to have committed an Unlawful Act (Tort).

### 2. Limits on Building Owners' Rights Over State Land / Expired HGB Land (Presidential Decree No. 32 of 1979)

The owner of a building standing on HGB land whose term has expired (becoming Land Directly Controlled by the State *ex* Presidential Decree No. 32 of 1979) possesses no right to demand property eviction against a lawful SIP holder, but is entitled solely to demand reasonable house rent payments from the occupant.

### 3. Quantification Requirements for Tort Damages Claims (Supreme Court Jurisprudence No. 1057 K/Sip/1973, No. 78 K/Sip/1973, No. 556 K/Sip/1980)

Claims for damages grounded on Tort must be supported by tangible proof of unlawful conduct and detailed quantification of material losses. Because Tort is unproven, claims for damages and coercive fines (*dwangsom*) must be rejected entirely.

## Question of Law vs Question of Fact

### Question of Law (*Judex Juris* / Supreme Court)

Did the *Judex Facti* Surabaya High Court (Decision No. 741/Pdt/1993/PT.Sby) err in applying the law of evidence (*foutieve toepassing van het bewijsrecht*) by qualifying Defendant I's occupancy under a lawful SIP as an Unlawful Act (Tort) and ordering property eviction alongside damages and coercive fines (*dwangsom*)?

**Supreme Court Legal Answer:** Yes. Surabaya High Court misapplied the law. Lawful SIP holders issued by competent authorities do not commit Torts; hence, eviction and Tort damage claims lack legal grounds. The Supreme Court granted \[Defendant I\]'s cassation petition, annulled Surabaya High Court's decision, and independently adjudicated by ordering Defendant I solely to pay reasonable house rent amounting to Rp 2,000,000 per year for 20 years (totaling Rp 40,000,000) while rejecting eviction/Tort claims.

### Question of Fact (*Judex Facti* / Surabaya District Court & Surabaya High Court)

Verification of facts regarding the occupancy history of the house at \[address removed\], Surabaya, starting from Mr. Kwee Sing Lien (1946), Widow Kwee Sing Lien (1949), Kwee Tjien Kie (1952), up to \[Defendant I\] (1972); inspection of Sale and Purchase Deed No. 8/1972 dated April 10, 1972; verification of Placement Permit (SIP) No. 301/KRKP/72 dated September 13, 1972, and Renewal SIP No. 181.2/004/411.87/88 dated January 7, 1988; examination of National Police Forensic Laboratory Report No. Lab. 465/DE/VII/1975 concerning blank seal form usage; and verification of postal money orders for house rent payments.

- **Established Precedent on Tort Damage Proof (Supreme Court Decisions No. 1057 K/Sip/1973, No. 78 K/Sip/1973, No. 556 K/Sip/1980):** Damage claims based on tort cannot be granted without detailed proof of valid and proven material losses.
- **Doctrine of Official SIP Holder Protection:** Individuals occupying housing under regional housing office supervision based on valid, unexpired official SIPs are protected under law and cannot be forcibly evicted via civil tort lawsuits.

## Uncertainties and Gaps

**Legal Certainty of Protecting Officially Permitted Occupants (SIP) Against Priority Rights Over State Land:** This landmark ruling establishes strict housing law boundaries in Indonesia: district and high courts may not evict or penalize house occupants holding official Placement Permits (SIP) from Regional Governments on allegations of Unlawful Acts (Tort). Owners of former HGB buildings are entitled solely to reasonable rent payments, while priority rights applications over expired HGB State land must be resolved through agrarian administrative law mechanisms.

## Frequently Asked Questions (GEO)

Can a house occupant holding an official Placement Permit (SIP) from a Regional Government Housing Office be sued for a Tort and forced to evict pursuant to Supreme Court Jurisprudence?  
**No.** Pursuant to Supreme Court Jurisprudence Reg. No. 3888 K/Pdt/1994:

- Principal occupants residing in a house based on a lawful Placement Permit (SIP) issued by a competent authority (Housing Office) **are not proven to have committed an unlawful act (Tort)**.
- Because tort is unproven, claims for house eviction, tort damages, and coercive fines (*dwangsom*) have no legal basis whatsoever and must be rejected.
- The Supreme Court annulled the Surabaya High Court decision and independently adjudicated by ordering the occupant solely to pay reasonable house rent to the building owner.
