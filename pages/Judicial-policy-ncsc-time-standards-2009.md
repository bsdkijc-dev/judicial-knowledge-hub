# Judicial-policy-ncsc-time-standards-2009

**Case Processing Time Standards** in American state and local trial courts is a judicial management policy framework developed by the National Center for State Courts (NCSC), the American Bar Association (ABA), the Conference of Chief Justices (CCJ), and the Conference of State Court Administrators (COSCA). The framework sets reasonable timelines for resolving civil, criminal, domestic relations, juvenile delinquency, child protection, and probate cases, aiming to eliminate unnecessary delay and ensure public accountability.

## Summary

Adopted gradually since the 1970s to cover 41 states and the District of Columbia by 2007, time standards serve as a tool for motivation, measurement, management, and the development of court information systems. The standards distinguish between intermediate time standards for each procedural stage and overall time standards spanning from filing to disposition.

## Legal Basis

- **US State Court Administrative Rules:** Applied across 41 states and Washington, D.C., either mandatorily or as advisory/elective guidelines.
- **Federal Child Protection Statute (ASFA 1997):** The Adoption and Safe Families Act of 1997 (P.L. 105-89; 42 U.S.C. § 675(5)(B)), which mandates limits on the duration a child may remain in foster care.
- **ABA Standards Relating to Trial Courts:** 1992 Edition, Sections 2.50, 2.51C, 2.52, and 2.55.
- **Trial Court Performance Standards (TCPS 1990):** Developed by the NCSC and the Bureau of Justice Assistance (BJA) of the US Department of Justice.

## Background

National research in the US demonstrates that court delay is unrelated to court size or the number of filings per judge, but instead depends on the court leadership's commitment to effective caseflow management. Simply adding new judgeships without effective caseflow management does not resolve case backlogs.

## Objectives

1\. Ensure prompt and fair justice that meets public expectations. 2. Reduce the volume and age of the pending case inventory (backlog). 3. Ensure trial date certainty and control the cost of access to justice. 4. Maintain a balance between procedural speed and substantive fairness (speedy justice without sacrificing substantive fairness).

## Scope & ABA Standards Table

### Overall Time Standards (ABA)

| Case Type              | 90% Completed | 98% Completed | 100% Completed |
|------------------------|---------------|---------------|----------------|
| **General Civil**      | 12 Months     | 18 Months     | 24 Months      |
| **Domestic Relations** | 3 Months      | 6 Months      | 12 Months      |
| **Felony**             | 120 Days      | 180 Days      | 365 Days       |
| **Misdemeanor**        | 30 Days       | --            | 90 Days        |

### Child Protection Cases (ASFA 1997 & NCJFCJ)

- Preliminary protective hearing: Must be held within 72 hours of a child's removal from parental custody.
- Adjudication: Must be completed within 60 days of removal.
- Permanency planning hearing: Must be held within 12–14 months.
- Petition for termination of parental rights: Mandatory when a child has been in foster care for 15 of the last 22 months.

## Key Provisions & Management Tools

- **Strict Continuance Policy:** Postponements are granted only for good cause shown, submitted in writing. A continuance rate exceeding 20% indicates excessive court leniency that undermines attorney preparedness.
- **Differentiated Case Management (DCM):** A "triage" system distinguishing simple, standard, and complex cases so that judicial resource allocation is properly targeted.
- **Four Core NCSC CourTools Measures:**

1.  **Clearance Rate:** The percentage of outgoing cases relative to incoming cases.
2.  **Time to Disposition:** The percentage of cases resolved within the established time standard.
3.  **Age of Pending Caseload:** The average age of currently active pending cases.
4.  **Certainty of Trial Date:** The certainty of the scheduled trial date and evidence submission.

## Policy Issues

1\. **Saturation Point of Capacity:** Even a highly efficient court can reach a saturation point if a surge in caseload is not matched by adequate support staffing. 2. **Continuance Abuse:** Lax continuance policies increase attorney costs, waste witnesses' time, and fuel public frustration. 3. **Risk of "Foster Care Drift":** Children risk being left adrift in the temporary care system without a clear path to adoption if permanency timelines are not strictly enforced.

## Reform / Implications

- Institutionalization of time standards across 41 US states, along with the integration of NCSC's CourTools indicators for the ongoing performance evaluation of judicial panels.
- Implementation of ASFA regulations across all 50 states by 2007 to ensure permanency certainty for children.
