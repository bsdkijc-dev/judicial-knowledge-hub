# Judicial-policy-schulz-langbroek-media-discourses-2010

**The Managing Discourses of Disapproval and Court-Media Public Relations Framework** is a judicial communication policy and management framework that analyzes and manages discourses of disapproval and disrespect from the mass media and politicians towards the judiciary. It aims to maintain the independence, reputation, and legitimacy of judicial institutions through communication instruments such as court outreach programs, judicial spokespersons, public education, and the management of camera access in courts \[1–5\].

## Summary

Based on the work of Pamela D. Schulz (*Courts and Judges on Trial*, Lit Verlag 2010) as reviewed by Philip Langbroek, this framework dissects the complex relationship among the judiciary, the mass media, and politicians. The framework explains how the populist press and politicians launch attacks on court rulings, and it offers public communication instruments such as judicial spokespersons, court outreach, and community education to safeguard judicial integrity.

## Legal Basis

- **Rules of Judicial Conduct:** Prohibits judges from openly discussing colleagues' decisions in the media and demands reluctance in responding to media attacks regarding concrete cases.
- **Principle of Judicial Independence:** Guarantees that judges are bound only by the law and the facts presented in court, not by the pressure of public opinion or the media.

## Background

The advent of the internet and the expansion of social media have created unprecedented collective surveillance of justice. While judges require time to carefully formulate balanced decisions, the mass media operates in a time-driven competitive frenzy, producing shallow news framing that quickly becomes ammunition for political attacks.

## Objectives

1\. Fortify the judiciary against the threat of colonization by the mass media and politicians. 2. Equip court leadership with public relations management skills and the ability to analyze discourse patterns. 3. Build proactive public communication channels without violating the ethics of judicial confidentiality.

## Scope

This framework was developed based on Anglo-Saxon media studies and interviews with politicians in Australia. However, it is highly transferable to Continental European jurisdictions, such as the Netherlands, France, Denmark, and Belgium, which also face populist press and political movements.

## Key Provisions

- **Anatomy of Media Attack Discourse:** The media and politicians frequently form alliances to criticize the judiciary to boost popularity or viewership ratings.
- **Discursive Barriers:** There is a significant gap between the technical legal language of the courts and the populist language of the news media.
- **Judicial Reluctance:** The ethical imperative for judges to refrain from engaging in open debates in the mass media.

## Implementation

Courts are recommended to adopt five primary communication instruments: 1. **Communication Plans:** Formulating structured communication strategies to anticipate and respond to negative news framing. 2. **Judicial Spokespersons:** Appointing and training designated officials authorized to provide official explanations of rulings without violating judicial ethics. 3. **Court Outreach Programs:** Opening avenues for productive interaction with community stakeholders. 4. **Community Education:** Educating the public on the fundamental differences between fair legal proceedings and media opinion shaping. 5. **Cameras in Courts:** Managing the visual transparency of trials in a measured manner.

## Policy Problems

- **Media Inconsistency:** The media prioritizes audience appeal over a consistent stance on public interest issues (as seen in the Dutch child protection trial case study, where the media shifted from criticizing inaction to criticizing over-intervention).
- **Risk of Judicial Colonization:** Unmanaged media pressure can dictate policy or intimidate judicial independence.

## Reform / Change

- Implementation of communication management and discourse analysis training for leading judges and court managers.
- Establishment of professional and structured judicial public relations units.
