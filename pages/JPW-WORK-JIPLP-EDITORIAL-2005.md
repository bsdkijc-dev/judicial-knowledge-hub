# JPW-WORK-JIPLP-EDITORIAL-2005

**Editorial: Play the game, kill the competition** is a legal editorial authored by [Jeremy Phillips](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Jeremy_Phillips) and published in the [Journal of Intellectual Property Law & Practice](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Journal_of_Intellectual_Property_Law_%26_Practice) (Vol. 1, No. 2) by [Oxford University Press](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Oxford_University_Press) on December 5, 2005 [Editorial Publication](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Editorial_Publication).

## Summary

This editorial critiques the regulatory preparations leading up to the [London 2012 Olympic Games](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/London_2012_Olympic_Games) that provided excessive commercial protections to official sponsors [Olympic Preparations](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Olympic_Preparations). The author challenges the tendency of lawmakers to restrict legitimate business competition through the creation of special anti-*ambush marketing* laws [Competition Critique](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Competition_Critique).

## Legal Basis

- **Formal Statutory Basis**: Not found in the available sources [Legal Basis](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Legal_Basis) \[1–9\].
- **IP vs. Competition Principles**: The text emphasizes that Olympic sponsor protections extend far beyond reasonable limits established under standard intellectual property law [Competition Restrictions](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Competition_Restrictions).

## Background

Staging the London 2012 Olympic and Paralympic Games required substantial corporate sponsorship revenue [Olympic Sponsorship](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Olympic_Sponsorship). To command high sponsorship fees, organizers offered special legal protections against marketing activities by competitors [Exclusive Protections](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Exclusive_Protections).

## Objectives

1.  Highlight the adverse impacts of anti-*ambush marketing* regulations on fair competition [Competition Impact](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Competition_Impact).
2.  Address the disregard for consumer interests in receiving diverse commercial messaging [Consumer Interests](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Consumer_Interests).
3.  Request a transparent explanation from the European Commission regarding competition-based justifications for such laws [European Commission Explanation](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/European_Commission_Explanation).

## Key Provisions

- **Circular Protection Pattern**: Higher sponsorship fees drive demands for broader legal protections, which in turn artificially inflate the market value of future sponsorship packages [Rights Circularity](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Rights_Circularity).
- **Stigmatization of Competition**: Applying the pejorative label *ambush marketing* to outlaw creative competitor marketing strategies that do not infringe on standard IP rights [Marketing Stigmatization](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Marketing_Stigmatization).
- **Illustrative Comparison**: The use of a hypothetical bus advertisement scenario (\*Acme vs. Zenith\*) to contrast legitimate commercial ingenuity with actual IP infringement [Acme Zenith Scenario](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Acme_Zenith_Scenario).

## Policy Issues

- **Overprotection**: Granting exclusive rights to major corporations (such as Nike or Adidas) that exclude competitors without robust competition law justifications [Overprotection](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Overprotection).
- **Consumer Harm**: Restricting choices and information available to consumers purchasing athletic goods post-Olympics [Consumer Disadvantage](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Consumer_Disadvantage).
