# JPW-EVENT-002

**ACCJ-ACT Roundtable on the Criminalisation of Environmental Harm and the Crime of Ecocide (2021)** (*Diskusi Meja Bundar Kriminalisasi Kerusakan Lingkungan dan Kejahatan Ekosida ACCJ-ACT (2021)*) is an academic forum and legal policy roundtable co-organised by the Amsterdam Center for Criminal Justice (ACCJ) and the Amsterdam Centre for Transformative Private Law (ACT) of the University of Amsterdam on 22 October 2021 \[1–4\].

## Summary

This event discussed the feasibility and legal mechanisms for criminalising acts that harm the environment, including the formulation of the legal definition of the crime of ecocide and the application of the ICC Rome Statute as an enforcement instrument \[2–4\].

## Legal Basis

- [Rome Statute](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Rome_Statute): The international criminal law framework analyzed to assess the scope of individual criminal liability for the crime of ecocide.
- \*\*Expert Drafting Panel Report\*\*: The proposed legal definition of the crime of ecocide formulated by an international panel of experts (Stop Ecocide Foundation).

## Background

The event was motivated by ongoing international legal policy debates regarding the necessity of penal sanctions for large-scale environmental destroyers and initiatives to establish ecocide as an international crime.

## Objectives

- Analyze the proposed legal definition of ecocide drafted by the Expert Drafting Panel.
- Discuss the role of international criminal law in addressing climate change.
- Assess the limits of individual criminal liability under the ICC Rome Statute.

## Key Provisions

- \*\*Keynote Speech by Prof. Philippe Sands\*\*: Detailed discussion regarding the definition of the crime of ecocide.
- \*\*Criminal Law as a Climate Solution\*\*: Presentation by Prof. Harmen van der Wilt on the effectiveness of international criminal law.
- \*\*Liability under the Rome Statute\*\*: Presentations by Dr. Tomas Hamilton and Marc Tiernan regarding criteria for individuals who can be held criminally responsible for ecocide.

## Implementation

The roundtable was successfully held on 22 October 2021 at the University of Amsterdam, involving academics, PhD researchers, and professors of international law \[2–4\].

## Policy Issues

- The absence of official recognition of ecocide within the ICC Rome Statute.
- The determination of secondary liability frameworks for perpetrators of environmental damage.
