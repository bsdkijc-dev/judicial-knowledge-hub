# Judicial-policy-dark-side-court-budgeting-2013

**The Dark Side Of Court And Justice System Budgeting In Hard Times Policy Framework** is a judicial budgeting policy and fiscal efficiency impact analysis formulated by Markus Zimmer (Executive Editor of the IJCA). It dissects the negative impacts ("dark side") of budget cuts, sequestration, and budget shortfalls in the United States, covering disparities in indigent defense funding, the involuntary debt accumulation placed upon poor inmates, and the systemic abuse of civil asset forfeiture laws.

## Summary

Published by Markus Zimmer in the *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA) Vol. 5 No. 2 (October 2013), this policy framework critiques the phenomenon of judicial budget cuts in the United States. Amidst various judicial innovations to "do more with less", Zimmer uncovers the dark elements of fiscal policy that are counterproductive to fundamental values of justice. The framework highlights the discriminatory impact of a \$50 million cut to Federal Defender Services, the imposition of involuntary debt on 80% of indigent defendants, and the rampant abuse of civil asset forfeiture laws by local law enforcement to fund non-judicial expenses.

## Legal Basis

- **U.S. Budget Control Act / Congressional Federal Funds Sequestration (2013):** Federal automatic budget cut regulations that severely slashed the spending authority of the federal judiciary.
- **Civil Asset Forfeiture Laws (United States):** Regulations allowing law enforcement to seize money and property based merely on the suspicion of a criminal offense, without requiring a formal conviction.
- **Other Specific Formal Statutory Regulations:** Not found in the available sources.

## Background

Although the 2013 fiscal recovery eased budget pressures in some U.S. states, the residual impact of prolonged funding cuts continued to leave problems such as the slashing of court services and the closure of local courthouses in California and Mississippi. The execution of automatic budget cuts (sequestration) by the U.S. Congress forced 87 out of 94 Chief Federal District Court Judges to send a formal protest letter in August 2013, declaring that judicial operations had been "slashed to the bone".

## Objectives

1\. Critique judicial budgeting policies that sacrifice principles of justice for short-term fiscal efficiency. 2. Raise awareness among the public and court administrators regarding the impact of abusive judicial funding schemes on impoverished citizens. 3. Push for the reform of fair and proportional judicial funding schemes without triggering recidivism. 4. Correct the abuse of civil asset forfeiture laws by local law enforcement.

## Scope

This policy covers the evaluation of the budgeting of federal, state, and local criminal justice systems (*state & local courts*) in the United States.

## Key Provisions

- **Disparate Sequestration Budget Cuts:** A \$50 million budget cut was imposed on the *Federal Defender Services* (triggering furloughs, layoffs, and cuts to contract attorney rates), while the prosecuting offices (*U.S. Attorneys Offices*) did not experience commensurate cuts.
- **Involuntary Debt Accumulation on Indigent Defendants:** The imposition of cost-shifting schemes (fines, processing fees, cell rental fees, public defender fees, and monthly interest) on criminal defendants, 80% of whom are classified as indigent.
- **Confiscation of Inmate Family Funds:** The correctional system's practice of taking up to one-third of the funds sent by families to inmates, exacerbating the cycle of poverty.
- **Abuse of Civil Forfeiture:** The seizure of citizens' cash, cars, and homes without formal charges, diverted for deviant expenditures such as official bonuses, luxury car rentals, Halloween costumes, popcorn machines, a \$1,000 donation to a Baptist church for a District Attorney's re-election campaign, a \$100,000 police boat, and a \$20,000 beach party.

## Implementation

- **Protest of 87 Chief District Judges:** Open protests by the leadership of federal district courts against the judicial budget cuts implemented by the U.S. Congress in August 2013.
- **Case Disclosures by the Brennan Center & Sarah Stillman:** The publication of empirical sociological evidence regarding the impact of judicial fines on recidivism, and the abuse of civil asset forfeiture (based on minority profiling) in *The New Yorker* magazine.

## Policy Problems

- **Asymmetric Impacts of Budget Cuts:** The slashing of legal aid funds for public defenders amidst an undiminished volume of criminal prosecutions by prosecutors.
- **Perpetuation of Poverty and Recidivism:** The burden of judicial fines and accumulating debt that triggers the failure of former inmates to reintegrate into society.

## Reform / Change

- Ending the discriminatory allocation of budget cuts between prosecutors and public defenders.
- Abolishing excessive judicial administration fee schemes placed on indigent defendants to prevent them from spawning recidivism.
- Radically reforming civil asset forfeiture laws to eliminate corrupt financial incentives for law enforcement.
