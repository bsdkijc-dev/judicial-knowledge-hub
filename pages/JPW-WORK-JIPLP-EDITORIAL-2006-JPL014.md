# JPW-WORK-JIPLP-EDITORIAL-2006-JPL014

**Editorial: When the figures just don’t figure** is a legal editorial authored by [Jeremy Phillips](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Jeremy_Phillips) and published in the [Journal of Intellectual Property Law & Practice](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Journal_of_Intellectual_Property_Law_%26_Practice) (Vol. 1, No. 4) by [Oxford University Press](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Oxford_University_Press) in 2006 [Editorial Publication](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Editorial_Publication) \[1–9\].

## Summary

This editorial evaluates statistical data compiled by [Jean Pire](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Jean_Pire) (Gevers & Partners, Belgium) regarding the failure rates of legal practitioners in complying with procedural formalities for Community Trade Marks at [OHIM](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/OHIM) [OHIM Statistics](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/OHIM_Statistics) \[1–9\]. The data demonstrates that practitioners from Spain, Germany, and the United Kingdom account for the vast majority of these procedural defaults [Procedural Imbalance](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Procedural_Imbalance) \[1–9\].

## Legal Basis

- **OHIM Regulations**: The administrative procedural framework of the Office for Harmonisation in the Internal Market governing Community Trade Mark applications [OHIM Rules](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/OHIM_Rules) \[1–9\].
- **Principle of Formal Protection**: Arbitrary procedural rules within OHIM regulations designed to protect innocent parties [Formal Principles](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Formal_Principles) \[1–9\].

## Background

In European intellectual property practice, formality failures are often assumed to be random administrative errors [IP Formalities](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/IP_Formalities) \[1–9\]. However, empirical records from the Gevers database reveal a heavy geographic concentration of defaults \[1–9\].

## Objectives

1.  Detail the statistical disparities in OHIM formality failure rates \[1–9\].
2.  Refute conventional explanations rooted in outdated 20th-century national stereotypes \[1–9\].
3.  Formulate working hypotheses regarding practitioner strategies and multinational corporate client behavior \[1–9\].
4.  Promote further empirical legal research \[1–9\].

## Key Provisions

- **Distribution of Formality Defaults**: Spain accounts for 31.6%, Germany for 30.2%, and the United Kingdom for 10.8% of total formality failures at OHIM \[1–9\].
- **Greek Failure Rate**: Greek practitioners recorded the lowest default rate at 0.12% \[1–9\].
- **Practitioner Qualifications**: Practitioners in Spain, Germany, and the UK possess demonstrated high capability, rigorous training, and extensive experience handling large case volumes \[1–9\].

## Implementation

- **Official Publication**: Published in JIPLP Vol. 1 No. 4, p. 229, DOI: 10.1093/jiplp/jpl014 \[1–9\].
- **Data Source**: Derived from decisions of the Board of Appeal, Opposition Division, and Cancellation Division at OHIM maintained in the Gevers & Partners database \[1–9\].

## Policy Issues

- **The Non-Compliance Enigma**: Formality failures cannot be attributed to lack of qualification or cultural stereotypes \[1–9\].
- **Multinational Client Strategy**: Well-funded global corporations may intentionally test procedural points as part of a broader portfolio management strategy \[1–9\].
