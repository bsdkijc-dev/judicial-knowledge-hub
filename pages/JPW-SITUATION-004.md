# JPW-SITUATION-004

**Preliminary Examination of the Situation in the Plurinational State of Bolivia (ICC)** is an independent legal and factual assessment conducted by the Office of the Prosecutor (OTP) of the International Criminal Court since 9 September 2020, following a referral by the Bolivian government concerning alleged crimes against humanity committed in Bolivian territory in August 2020.

## Summary

The preliminary examination was triggered by an official referral from the Bolivian government under Article 14(1) of the Rome Statute. The allegations focus on coordinated road blockades organized by supporters of the \*Movimiento al Socialismo\* (MAS) party that prevented the delivery of medical supplies and medical oxygen at the height of the COVID-19 pandemic.

## Legal Basis

- [Rome Statute](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Rome_Statute): Article 7(1)(k) (Crimes Against Humanity — Other Inhumane Acts) and Article 14(1) (Referral by a State Party).
- Bolivia ratified the Rome Statute on 27 June 2002 (jurisdiction effective from 1 September 2002).

## Background

In August 2020, MAS party leaders and supporters called for nationwide road blockades to pressure the government into setting a presidential election date. The blockades lasted nine days and paralyzed medical logistics and distribution routes.

## Objectives

- Assess whether the obstruction of medical oxygen supplies meets the subject-matter jurisdiction (\*competencia material\*) threshold under Article 7(1)(k) of the Rome Statute.
- Determine whether a reasonable basis exists to proceed to the admissibility analysis stage.

## Key Provisions

- **Nature of the Alleged Crime**: Inhumane acts intentionally causing great suffering or serious injury to physical or mental health (Article 7(1)(k)).
- **Health Impact**: More than 40 people reportedly died from lack of medical oxygen during the blockades.
- **Nature of Conduct**: The blockades were organized through a structured, logistically coordinated system rather than spontaneous action.

## Implementation

The ICC Presidency assigned the Bolivia situation to Pre-Trial Chamber III on 15 September 2020. The OTP began independent collection and analysis of evidence.

## Policy Issues

- Bolivia has not codified crimes against humanity in its national Penal Code.
- Alleged co-optation of domestic judicial institutions, hindering internal law enforcement.
