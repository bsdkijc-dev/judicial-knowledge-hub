# Judicial-policy-ijca-inaugural-2008

# 'International Journal for Court Administration, Vol. 1, No. 1' (IACA, 2008)

### Wiki Entity Page

**International Journal for Court Administration (Inaugural Issue 2008, Source File: 15-70-PB.pdf)** is the first official peer-reviewed electronic periodical publication of the *International Association for Court Administration* (IACA), published on the official IACA website (www.iaca.ws). This issue features comparative scientific studies on Italian criminal procedure reform, Abu Dhabi judicial restructuring, judicial productivity in India, the development of court administrators in Canada and Russia, European judicial quality evaluation (CEPEJ), court monitoring and evaluation frameworks, and the principles of fair electronic trials (e-justice).

## Summary

This inaugural issue is led by Markus B. Zimmer (Executive Editor) alongside Prof. Dr. Philip M. Langbroek and Dr. Barry Mahoney (Co-Managing Editors). The journal is designed as an international communications vehicle for exchanging experiences and empirical research on court governance across common law, continental civil law, and Shari'ah-based legal systems.

## Background & Key Article Summaries

### 1. Italian Criminal Procedure Reform (Marco Fabri)

- Analyzes the failure of the 1989 Italian Code of Criminal Procedure's transition from an inquisitorial model to an adversarial model due to legal formalism. A rigid 48-hour police reporting rule caused case backlogs in prosecutor offices, while plea bargaining saw extremely low adoption.

### 2. Abu Dhabi Judicial Modernization (Lawrence Groo)

- Examines judicial restructuring based on Law No. 23 of 2007, which formed an independent Judicial Council, consolidated administration under Court Presidents, and established a Judicial Academy.

### 3. Judicial Productivity in India vs. Australia (Barry Walsh)

- Compares the disposal capacity of Delhi judges (701 cases/year) with Australian judges (1,511 cases/year). Low productivity in India is driven by an extremely low civil settlement rate (5% vs. 70%+), lack of outcome date certainty, and fragmented trials featuring 20–40 adjournments.

### 4. The Role of the Court Administrator in Canada & the US (Ryder-Lahey & Solomon)

- Traces the development of the court administrator profession from the 1950s in the US to Canada (Deschenes Report 1981, *Valente* Decision 1985, Zuber Report 1987), formatting an "executive partnership" between Chief Judges and Court Administrators.

### 5. Court Administrators in Russia (Irina Mikheeva)

- Analyzes the role of court administrators based on the 1998 Judicial Department Law. Identifies a "dual subordination" problem where administrators are appointed by the Judicial Department but report to the Court Chairperson, frequently limiting their role to facility maintenance.

### 6. CEPEJ European Judicial Evaluation Experience (Pim Albers)

- Analyzes CEPEJ's comparative evaluation methodology across 47 Council of Europe member states, tracking court budgets, judges' salaries, SATURN time indicators, and the division between *action time* and *standstill time*.

### 7. Court Monitoring & Evaluation Model (Ng, Velicogna, & Dallara)

- Formulates a 5-stage developmental model for court monitoring systems: *Bureaucratic Data Collection* \$\rightarrow\$ *Normative Framework* \$\rightarrow\$ *Institution Building* \$\rightarrow\$ *Monitoring & Evaluation* \$\rightarrow\$ *Accountability & Action*.

### 8. Principles of Fair Electronic Trials / E-Justice (Ronald van den Hoogen)

- Establishes 13 principles of fair e-justice based on ECHR Article 6: Equal access, Continuity, Quality of information, Reliability, Freedom of the press, Public accessibility, Online publishing, Transparency, Computerized administration, Expeditious handling, Chain-computerisation, Equality, and Right to attend.

## Policy Problems

1\. Legal reform failures caused by ignoring organizational contexts and actor capacities. 2. Case backlogs resulting from fragmented trials and the absence of outcome date certainty. 3. Dual subordination conflicts in court personnel management. 4. Risks of violating the right to a fair trial amid e-justice automation.

## Reform / Change

1\. Adoption of the "executive partnership" model between Chief Judges and professional Court Administrators. 2. Consolidation of court management under independent Judicial Councils. 3. Transition toward continuous trials and the adoption of standard e-justice principles.
