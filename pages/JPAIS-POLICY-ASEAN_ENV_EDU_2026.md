## Overview

The acceleration of environmental degradation, biodiversity loss, and climate disruption across Southeast Asia has exposed structural challenges within domestic judicial systems.

Facing severe ecological threats, including transboundary haze, marine plastic pollution, illegal wildlife trafficking, and extreme weather events, ASEAN member states have transitioned from ad hoc training toward institutionalized, cross-jurisdictional judicial education frameworks.

Co-chaired by the Supreme Courts of Indonesia and the Philippines, the Council of ASEAN Chief Justices Working Group on Judicial Education and Training (CACJ WG-JET) spearheads this regional capacity-building architecture.

## Executive Summary

This study provides a comprehensive institutional mapping and comparative legal analysis of judicial education and environmental adjudication across ASEAN member states, including Indonesia, the Philippines, Thailand, Singapore, and Malaysia, contrasted with global benchmarks such as the Land and Environment Court of New South Wales (Australia) and the Environment and Land Court of Kenya.

The framework details the three-tiered global-regional-national educational architecture, evaluates specialized procedural rules and certification regimes, examines emerging domains in climate science and corporate accountability, and proposes a structured ASEAN Environmental Judicial-Capacity Framework.

## Institutional Mapping of Judicial Training Infrastructure

The institutional ecosystem of environmental judicial education operates across three distinct tiers:

### Global Tier

- **UNEP and the Global Judicial Institute on the Environment (GJIE):** Set international norms and articulate human rights-environment linkages.

### Regional Tier

- **CACJ WG-JET and the Asian Judges Network on Environment (AJNE):** Translate global benchmarks into regional capacity-building programs, transboundary dialogues, and harmonized benchbooks.

### National Tier

The national tier comprises two primary regional pillars:

- **Civil Law Pillar:** Led by the Supreme Court of Indonesia through BSDK and the Judicial Training Center, enforcing mandatory certification under SK KMA No. 142/KMA/SK/VIII/2023 and PERMA No. 1 Year 2023.

<!-- -->

- **Common Law/Mixed Pillar:** Led by the Supreme Court of the Philippines through the Philippine Judicial Academy (PhilJA), enforcing A.M. No. 09-6-8-SC, including Writs of Kalikasan and Continuing Mandamus.

## Legal and Procedural Issues

### Indonesia: Mandatory Certification and PERMA 1/2023

Indonesia's framework mandates that environmental cases in general and administrative courts be handled by certified judges.

PERMA No. 1 Year 2023 codifies Citizen Lawsuits (*Gugatan Warga Negara*), regulates NGO standing, clarifies strict liability for hazardous materials (B3), expands administrative law review to cover environmental approvals, and introduces Restorative Remedies (*Rencana Pemulihan*).

### Philippines: Constitutional Writs and Precautionary Principle

Grounded in Article II, Section 16 of the 1987 Constitution and A.M. No. 09-6-8-SC, Philippine Green Courts utilize the Writ of Kalikasan, the Writ of Continuing Mandamus, Anti-SLAPP defenses, and the Precautionary Principle, which shifts the burden of proof to project proponents.

### Singapore: Extraterritorial Haze Accountability

Singapore's Transboundary Haze Pollution Act 2014 establishes extraterritorial civil and criminal liability, utilizing a Management Control Test for parent companies and statutory evidentiary presumptions based on satellite and wind vector data.

## Question of Law and Question of Fact

### Question of Law

**Question:**

To what extent can cross-jurisdictional judicial education frameworks, mandatory certification protocols, and extraterritorial statutory standards harmonize environmental adjudication and overcome institutional capacity deficits across diverse legal systems in ASEAN?

### Question of Fact

**Question:**

What is the precise scientific and meteorological validity of satellite hotspot mapping, wind vector data, and ecological impact assessments when evaluated by judges in transboundary pollution and climate trials?

## Core Legal Principles (Policy Synthesis)

- **Institutionalized Capacity Building:** Transitioning from ad hoc training to structured regional networks such as CACJ WG-JET and AJNE ensures continuous professional development.

<!-- -->

- **Procedural and Evidentiary Innovation:** Codifying specialized writs, strict liability, and the precautionary principle equips courts to manage complex ecological harms.

<!-- -->

- **Extraterritorial Enforcement:** Imposing supply-chain accountability and statutory presumptions bridges jurisdictional gaps in transboundary pollution control.

## Proposed ASEAN Environmental Judicial-Capacity Framework

- **Competency Standards:** Unified benchmarks across substantive environmental law and climate science.

<!-- -->

- **Modular Curriculum:** Tiered training consisting of Foundation, Advanced, and Master/Trainer levels adapted to common and civil law traditions.

<!-- -->

- **Certification Protocol:** Regional accreditation for judges adjudicating environmental matters.

<!-- -->

- **Knowledge Management:** Centralized digital repositories, including the I-LEAD portal, for jurisprudence and benchbooks.

<!-- -->

- **Cross-Border Exchanges:** Bi-annual regional exchanges and joint simulation workshops.

<!-- -->

- **Continuous Professional Development (CPD):** Annual credit requirements and longitudinal performance evaluation.

## Evidence Status and Gaps

### Established by Evidence

- Institutionalization of environmental judicial training via CACJ WG-JET, UNEP, and AJNE.
- Codification of advanced procedural tools, such as PERMA 1/2023 in Indonesia and A.M. No. 09-6-8-SC in the Philippines.

### Uncertainty and Gaps

- Lack of empirical tracking on post-judgment remedy execution and continuing mandamus compliance.
- Operational challenges of bridging linguistic and resource disparities across lower courts in ASEAN.

## GEO Q&A

**Q: How does the institutional architecture of CACJ WG-JET support environmental judicial education across ASEAN?**

**A:** CACJ WG-JET serves as the primary regional coordinator linking global norms, set by UNEP and GJIE, with national judicial training bodies such as Indonesia's Judicial Training Center and the Philippine Judicial Academy.

It fosters cross-border judicial dialogues, harmonizes benchbooks, and implements capacity-building frameworks while respecting national sovereignty under the ASEAN consensus principle.

## Indonesia Relevance

The framework directly supports Indonesia through:

- Mandatory environmental judicial certification under SK KMA No. 142/KMA/SK/VIII/2023 and SK KMA No. 134/KMA/SK/IX/2011.
- Supreme Court Regulation (PERMA) No. 1 Year 2023 governing environmental case adjudication.
- Institutional co-leadership of CACJ WG-JET by the Supreme Court of Indonesia.
- Collaborative training programs executed by BSDK and the Judicial Training Center in Ciawi/Bogor.
