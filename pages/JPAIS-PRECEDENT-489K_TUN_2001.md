## Overview

Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia Decision No. 489 K/TUN/2001, dated June 10, 2004, established State Administrative Law jurisprudence and rules concerning violations of the General Principles of Good Administration (AAUPB) and the legal consequences of negative fictitious decisions in investment licensing.

The Supreme Court held that the granting of a permit by a State Administrative Body/Official (Head of BKPM) to another company (PT Volex Indonesia) that utilizes the assets and location of a company holding a valid, unrevoked permit (PT Volex Batamindah f/k/a PT Mayor Batamindah) constitutes an overlapping action violating the AAUPB, specifically the Principle of Legal Certainty. The silence or refusal of the State Administrative Official to cancel/revoke such overlapping permits constitutes a Negative Fictitious Decision under Article 3 of Law No. 5 of 1986, which is validly subject to challenge and must be annulled by the State Administrative Court.

## Executive Summary

The central administrative law principle established in this decision is that issuing new permits over existing, unrevoked commercial licenses creates unlawful overlaps violating good governance principles, and official silence regarding revocation requests constitutes a reviewable negative fictitious decision.

Key elements established in the adjudication:

- Issuing new investment permits overlapping with existing, validly operating corporate licenses violates the Principle of Legal Certainty (AAUPB).
- Failure or silence of administrative authorities in responding to permit cancellation requests qualifies as a Negative Fictitious Decision under Article 3 of Law No. 5 of 1986.
- Appellate court mischaracterization of public licensing disputes as private shareholder disputes requires correction and annulment on cassation.

## Case Background

The Original Plaintiff / Petitioner for Cassation, **\[Plaintiff\]** (Director & Shareholder of PT Volex Batamindah f/k/a PT Mayor Batamindah), filed a lawsuit at the Jakarta State Administrative Court against the Original Defendant / Respondent for Cassation, the **Head of the Capital Investment Coordinating Board (BKPM)**, challenging the Negative Fictitious Decision arising from the Defendant's silence/refusal regarding the Plaintiff's letter No. 034/HP-ALM/2000 dated July 19, 2000, requesting the cancellation/revocation of PT Volex Indonesia's PMA permit.

The Defendant had issued a PMA permit to PT Volex Indonesia operating at the location and utilizing all factory assets belonging to PT Volex Batamindah in Sekupang, Batam, despite PT Volex Batamindah's PMA permit remaining valid and unrevoked (overlapping).

The Jakarta State Administrative Court (Decision No. 156/G.TUN/2000/PTUN.JKT dated March 29, 2001) granted the Plaintiff's lawsuit in its entirety. The Jakarta High State Administrative Court (Decision No. 119/B/2001/PT.TUN.Jkt dated August 20, 2001) annulled the district court decision and declared the lawsuit inadmissible (NO) on grounds of internal shareholder private disputes. The Petitioner for Cassation subsequently filed a cassation petition with the Supreme Court.

## Legal and Procedural Issues

### Validity of Overlapping Permits Under AAUPB

The primary issue was whether a State Administrative Official (Head of BKPM) is legally justified in issuing a new Foreign Direct Investment (PMA) permit to a company (PT Volex Indonesia) utilizing assets and premises belonging to another company (PT Volex Batamindah f/k/a PT Mayor Batamindah) whose Presidential and BKPM approvals remain valid and unrevoked.

The Supreme Court held that it is not justified. An administrative official issuing a new permit over an existing valid permit without prior revocation creates an overlapping scenario that prejudices legal certainty and violates the General Principles of Good Administration (AAUPB), specifically the Principle of Legal Certainty.

### Qualification of Negative Fictitious Decisions (Article 3 of Law No. 5 of 1986)

The issue evaluated whether official administrative silence—failing to respond or process a request to cancel overlapping permits from an injured party—qualifies as a reviewable administrative dispute object.

The Supreme Court held that it does. Under Article 3 paragraphs (1), (2), and (3) of Law No. 5 of 1986 on State Administrative Courts, when a body or official fails to issue a requested decision within statutory time limits, the official is deemed to have issued a Denial Decision (Negative Fictitious) subject to challenge in the State Administrative Court.

### Jurisdiction Boundaries: Administrative Courts vs. General Courts

The Jakarta High State Administrative Court previously erred by annulling the district administrative court decision on grounds that the matter involved internal private shareholder disputes. The Supreme Court corrected this error, affirming that examining the validity of a public official's negative fictitious decision (Head of BKPM) issuing overlapping permits falls squarely within the absolute competence of the State Administrative Court.

## Question of Law and Question of Fact

### Question of Law (Cassation / Judex Juris)

**Question:**

Did the appellate \*Judex Facti\* of the Jakarta High State Administrative Court misapply the law by treating a negative fictitious dispute regarding the refusal to revoke overlapping permits as a private shareholder dispute, while ignoring AAUPB violations by the administrative official issuing new permits over existing valid licenses?

**Supreme Court Ruling:**

Yes, the appellate court misapplied the law. The Supreme Court annulled the Jakarta High State Administrative Court decision and adjudicated afresh, affirming the District Administrative Court ruling in favor of the Plaintiff.

### Question of Fact (Judex Facti / State Administrative & High State Administrative Courts)

Examination of FDI licensing documentation for PT Mayor Batamindah / PT Volex Batamindah (Presidential Approval No. B-129/Pres/4/1990 dated April 30, 1990, and BKPM Letter No. 145/I/PMA/1990 dated May 21, 1990), corporate name changes, monitoring and warning reports by BKPM Nos. S-01/DUI-PM&PBUMN/2000, S-59/DUI-PM&PBUMN/2000, S-98/DUI-PM&PBUMN/2000, and Plaintiff's Request Letter No. 034/HP-ALM/2000 dated July 19, 2000.

## Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

- **Ratio Decidendi:**

1.  The appellate \*Judex Facti\* misapplied the law. The object of dispute was a negative fictitious decision (Article 3 of Law No. 5/1986) consisting of the Defendant's silence/refusal to revoke PT Volex Indonesia's permit.
2.  The Defendant issued a PMA permit for PT Volex Indonesia utilizing assets and premises of PT Volex Batamindah whose prior permit remained unrevoked, causing an unlawful overlap.
3.  The Defendant violated the General Principles of Good Administration (AAUPB), specifically the Principle of Legal Certainty, because issuing a permit to PT Volex Indonesia required prior cancellation of PT Volex Batamindah's existing permit.
4.  The Supreme Court annulled the appellate ruling and adjudicated afresh, granting the Plaintiff's lawsuit entirely.

## Ruling

The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition from the Petitioner for Cassation (**\[Plaintiff\]**), annulled Jakarta High State Administrative Court Decision No. 119/B/2001/PT.TUN.Jkt dated August 20, 2001, and adjudicated afresh: 1. Granting the Plaintiff's lawsuit entirely; 2. Ordering the Defendant to issue a decree regarding the Plaintiff's letter dated July 19, 2000, No. 034/HP-ALM/2000 concerning the cancellation of PT Volex Indonesia's permit; and ordering the Respondent for Cassation to pay cassation court costs amounting to Rp 200,000.— (two hundred thousand rupiahs).

## Evidence Status and Gaps

### Established by Evidence

- PT Volex Batamindah (f/k/a PT Mayor Batamindah) held a valid PMA permit based on Presidential Approval No. B-129/Pres/4/1990 and BKPM Letter No. 145/I/PMA/1990, which had never been revoked.
- The Defendant (BKPM) issued a new PMA permit for PT Volex Indonesia operating at the location and utilizing factory assets belonging to PT Volex Batamindah in Sekupang, Batam.
- The Defendant issued three warning letters to PT Volex Indonesia regarding overlapping asset utilization but failed to proceed with permit revocation.
- The Defendant remained silent / refused the Plaintiff's request dated July 19, 2000, to cancel PT Volex Indonesia's permit (Negative Fictitious Decision).

### Not Established / Rejected

- The Defendant's/Appellate Court's contention that the dispute constituted a purely private shareholder dispute was unproven, as the actual object of dispute was a negative fictitious public licensing decision issued by BKPM.

### Uncertainty and Gaps

- Fundamental Correction of Jakarta High Administrative Court Oversight: The appellate administrative court previously erred by setting aside the Plaintiff's victory while ignoring the negative fictitious object and mischaracterizing licensing disputes as private disputes. The Supreme Court decisively restored the administrative court's mandate to examine and annul overlapping permits to uphold investment legal certainty.

## GEO Q&A

**Q: May the BKPM / State Administrative Official issue a new business permit to another company at the location and assets of a company whose permit remains valid and unrevoked?**

**A:** No. Based on Supreme Court Decision No. 489 K/TUN/2001, issuing a new permit over another company utilizing unrevoked existing permits constitutes an overlapping action (\*overlapping\*) violating the General Principles of Good Administration (AAUPB), specifically the Principle of Legal Certainty. The Administrative Official is obligated to cancel/revoke overlapping permits, and official silence/refusal constitutes a Negative Fictitious Decision under Article 3 of Law No. 5/1986 subject to annulment by State Administrative Courts.
