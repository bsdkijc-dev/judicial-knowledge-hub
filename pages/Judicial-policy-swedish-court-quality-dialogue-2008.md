# Judicial-policy-swedish-court-quality-dialogue-2008

**The Internal and External Dialogue: A Method for Quality Court Management Framework** is a policy framework and judicial quality management methodology based on comprehensive participatory ("bottom-up") engagement. It combines internal dialogue (anonymous interviews and group discussions involving judges and administrative staff) with external dialogue (structured discussions with prosecutors, lawyers, and court users) to enhance public trust, streamline case workflows, improve job satisfaction, and elevate the quality of trial services.

## Summary

Published by Marie B. Hagsgård (Court of Appeal of Western Sweden) in the *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA), this policy framework details a method for improving court quality based on participatory dialogue. Designed to overcome the traditional resistance of judges to top-down management and external advisors, the method leverages the professional expertise of all court personnel. The two-stage approach (internal dialogue followed by external dialogue) has proven highly successful in reducing case turnaround times, cutting case backlogs, and significantly increasing staff job satisfaction.

## Background

Courts are highly professional organizations where independent judges hold a central role. Efforts to introduce quality management systems frequently encounter obstacles due to judges' tendencies to resist managerial interventions. In 2003, the *Court of Appeal of Western Sweden* initiated a method of dialogic interviews and discussions that proved highly effective. Consequently, it was adopted by the annual meeting of Swedish court managers in 2004 as a national model.

## Objectives

1\. Utilize the professional know-how and experience of all judges and staff to identify organizational weaknesses. 2. Build a sense of ownership and commitment among all court personnel toward the change process. 3. Incorporate the perceptions and needs of external users (prosecutors, advocates, defendants, witnesses, victims) into court action plans \[1, 20–25\]. 4. Improve case turnaround times, reduce case balances, and increase public trust in the judiciary.

## Scope

This policy was initiated at the *Court of Appeal of Western Sweden* (110 employees, 36 judges, 4,000 cases/year) and replicated across six other Swedish courts (Court of Appeal of Skåne and Blekinge, Administrative Court of Appeal of Stockholm, and the District Courts of Hässleholm, Borås, Vänersborg, and Göteborg), with comparative international references in Finland, Denmark, and the Netherlands.

## Key Provisions

- **Stage 1: Internal Dialogue**
  - *Anonymous Individual Interviews:* In-depth interviews (1.5 hours) conducted by internal interviewers (such as junior judges) with at least half of the staff population to honestly uncover operational weaknesses and proposals.
  - *Participatory Group Discussions:* Processing interview results in small groups (5–8 people) and departmental groups (20–25 people) to formulate priority recommendations.
  - *Reasoned Decisions by Leadership:* The chief judge establishes an annual action plan based on staff proposals and transparently communicates the reasoning behind the decisions.
- **Stage 2: External Dialogue**
  - *Professional Dialogue:* Periodic meetings with prosecutors and lawyers to improve civil and criminal trial workflows \[20–22\].
  - *Non-Professional User Interviews:* Qualitative interviews with defendants, plaintiffs, and witnesses regarding their treatment and the clarity of trial information \[24–26\].
- **Principle of Internal Before External:** Internal dialogue must strictly precede external dialogue to give professional staff time to reflect on internal work quality before receiving critiques from outside parties.

## Implementation

- **Delegation of Case Preparation:** Training and delegating case file preparation tasks from judges to court secretaries (saving judges 1–1.5 hours per week).
- **Improvement of User Information Facilities:** Creating FAQ sheets and orientation maps, posting photos of the courtroom layout outside doors, providing identity nameplates for judges/clerks at trial desks, and optimizing schedule delay notifications in waiting rooms \[28–30\].

## Policy Problems

- **Skepticism Toward External Consultants:** The limitations of top-down command reform models, which often trigger hidden resistance from independent judges.
- **Anxiety of Witnesses and Defendants:** A lack of preliminary information regarding trial procedures and locations, which causes severe anxiety for witnesses and lay users.

## Reform / Change

- Adoption of the bottom-up participatory approach as a standard for judicial management in Sweden.
- Development of continuous constructive feedback training modules between senior and junior judges.

Der "Bottom-up"-Ansatz wurde entwickelt, um den traditionellen Widerstand von Richtern gegen "Top-down"-Management und externe Berater zu überwinden, indem das professionelle Know-how aller Gerichtsmitarbeiter genutzt wird. ### 3 Most Important Improvements

1\. **Institutionalization of Anonymous Interview Training for Internal Quality Teams:** Develop specialized interview technique modules for judges and administrative staff to ensure the objective collection of data regarding organizational weaknesses. 2. **Drafting National Standards for Delegating Case Preparation to Secretaries:** Adopt the successful case preparation delegation scheme from the Court of Appeal of Western Sweden across all first-instance courts to save judges' working hours. 3. **Enhancing Information Facilities and Witness Protection in Courtrooms:** Uniformly implement the provision of orientation maps, FAQ sheets, courtroom layout photos, and identity nameplates at trial desks to improve the comfort and reduce the anxiety of court users.
