# JPAIS:PRECEDENT:2064K PDT 1991

**Supreme Court Decision Reg. No. 2064 K/Pdt/1991** (dated February 28, 1994 / deliberation on February 24, 1994) is a fundamental Indonesian land and civil evidence law jurisprudence precedent establishing a crucial rule regarding the perfect evidentiary force (*volledig bewijskracht*) of Land Rights Certificates (*Sertifikat Hak Milik*) and the inability of witness testimonies grounded in conjecture or indication (*legenbewijs / aanwijzingen*) to defeat them.

## Executive Summary

The Supreme Court established two primary legal principles:

1.  **Perfect Evidentiary Force of Land Rights Certificates:** A Land Rights Certificate (*Sertifikat Hak Milik*) issued by authorized land authorities in accordance with applicable legal procedures (e.g., Government Regulation No. 10 of 1961 on Land Registration) constitutes an authentic instrument possessing perfect evidentiary force (*volledig bewijskracht*).
2.  **Witness Testimonies / Conjectures (*Legenbewijs / Aanwijzingen*) Cannot Overturn Certificates:** An Appellate Court errs in applying the law of evidence when it overturns or invalidates a legally issued Land Rights Certificate solely by relying on witness testimonies providing conjecture or indications (*legenbewijs / aanwijzingen*) regarding past oral purchase transactions. Standalone witness testimonies are insufficient to break down authentic certificate evidence issued pursuant to legal procedure.

The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition of the Petitioners for Cassation (\[Party B\], Wardinah, Warda — heirs of the late H. Tompo), annulled Ujung Pandang High Court Decision No. 112/Pdt/1991/PT.Uj.Pdg dated March 16, 1991, and independently adjudicated (*mengadili sendiri*) by adopting Polewali District Court Decision No. 36/Pdt.G/1990/PN.Pol dated November 12, 1990—partially granting the Plaintiffs' lawsuit, declaring the disputed 8,273 m² rice field as the property of H. Tompo inherited by the Plaintiffs, and ordering the Defendant (\[Defendant\]) to surrender the disputed rice field.

## Legal / Procedural Issues

### 1. Hierarchy of Land Evidentiary Force (Land Rights Certificate vs. Witness Testimony / *Legenbewijs*)

*Can oral witness testimonies asserting that a land transaction occurred with other parties decades prior undermine the legal force of a Land Rights Certificate officially issued pursuant to Government Regulation No. 10 of 1961?*

**Supreme Court Rule: Cannot Overturn.** A Land Rights Certificate represents an authentic instrument issued by authorized land authorities following measurement procedures, land history research, and unprotested public announcements. This certificate possesses perfect evidentiary force (*volledig bewijskracht*). Witness testimonies constituting conjecture or indications (*legenbewijs / aanwijzingen*) carry no legal weight capable of defeating the validity of such authentic certificates.

### 2. Open Acknowledgment of Certificate Procedure Validity (*Praesumptio Iustae Causa* / Estoppel)

A defendant who never raises objections during the land registration announcement process (3 months) and never legally files a lawsuit to cancel a certificate is deemed by law to acknowledge the validity of such land rights certificate.

## Question of Law vs Question of Fact

### Question of Law (*Judex Juris* / Supreme Court)

Did the *Judex Facti* Ujung Pandang High Court (Decision No. 112/Pdt/1991/PT.Uj.Pdg) err in applying the law of evidence (*foutieve toepassing van het bewijsrecht*) by setting aside Land Rights Certificate No. 7613252 in the name of H. Tompo and ruling in favor of the Defendant based solely on oral witness testimonies?

**Supreme Court Legal Answer:** Yes. Ujung Pandang High Court misapplied the law of evidence. The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition of the Petitioners for Cassation (\[Party B\] et al.), annulled Ujung Pandang High Court Decision No. 112/Pdt/1991/PT.Uj.Pdg dated March 16, 1991, and independently adjudicated by adopting Polewali District Court Decision No. 36/Pdt.G/1990/PN.Pol dated November 12, 1990, partially granting the Plaintiffs' lawsuit.

### Question of Fact (*Judex Facti* / Polewali District Court & Ujung Pandang High Court)

Examination of facts regarding the origins of the 8,273 m² rice field in Ugi Baru, Ugi Baru Village, Wonomulyo Subdistrict, Polewali Mamasa Regency, initially pawned by La Malappe in 1962 for Rp 25,000 to \[Party A\] & H. Tompo; subsequent purchase of the land in 1963 by H. Tompo for Rp 60,000; issuance of SHM No. 7613252 on April 11, 1983, in the name of H. Tompo; cultivation relationships maintained by the Defendant (\[Defendant\]) under a sharecropping arrangement until the passing of \[Defendant's mother\] in 1989; and testimonies of witnesses (\[Party C\], \[Witness\], \[Witness\], H. Siraja, Maddiwali, Rusdi).

## Jurisprudence and Related Knowledge

### Statutory Regulations

- **Government Regulation No. 10 of 1961 on Land Registration (*jo.* Gov. Reg. No. 24 of 1997):** Articles 3, 6 (Announcement and Certificate Issuance Procedures).
- **HIR / RBg:** Article 164 HIR / Article 284 RBg (Civil Evidence Instruments: Authentic Documents, Witnesses, Presumptions, Confessions, Oaths).
- **Civil Code (KUHPerdata / BW):** Articles 1867, 1868, 1870 (Evidentiary Force of Authentic Deeds / Certificates).
- **Law No. 14 of 1970 *jo.* Law No. 14 of 1985 on the Supreme Court:** Independent Adjudication Powers at Cassation Level.

### Established Supreme Court Jurisprudence

- **Doctrine of Certificate *Volledig Bewijskracht*:** Land certificates issued in accordance with procedure serve as binding and perfect authentic evidence instruments.
- **Doctrine of *Legenbewijs* Inability to Defeat Authentic Deeds:** Individual witness testimonies unsupported by counter-documentary evidence lack sufficient legal strength to defeat registered title rights established in certificates.

## What Evidence Establishes / Does Not Establish

### Established by Evidence

- The disputed 8,273 m² rice field in Ugi Baru Village is proven to be the property of H. Tompo based on Land Rights Certificate No. 7613252 dated April 11, 1983.
- The Plaintiffs (\[Party B\], Wardinah, Warda) are legally proven to be the biological heirs entitled to the disputed land.
- The Defendant (\[Defendant\]) is proven to have occupied the disputed rice field without right and committed a tort upon refusing to share harvests beginning in 1989.
- The Defendant is ordered to surrender the disputed rice field to the Plaintiffs.

### Annulled (Ujung Pandang High Court Verdict)

- The Defendant's assertion that the disputed rice field belonged to his father (\[Party A\]) based on oral witness accounts was **UNPROVEN** in law and entirely annulled by the Supreme Court.

## Uncertainties and Gaps

**Protecting Legal Certainty for Registered Right Holders Against Oral Claims:** This landmark ruling underscores legal certainty concerning land registration in Indonesia: Holders of Land Rights Certificates who acquire their rights lawfully through formal registration procedures remain protected from unilateral ownership claims grounded solely in oral testimony (*legenbewijs*). Rebuttal evidence attempting to shake a certificate must demonstrate procedural registration flaws or authentic documentary proof bearing equivalent evidentiary weight.

## Frequently Asked Questions (GEO)

Can oral witness testimonies cancel or undermine a Land Rights Certificate legally issued under the law?  
**Cannot Overturn Certificates.** Pursuant to Supreme Court Jurisprudence No. 2064 K/Pdt/1991:

- A Land Rights Certificate issued by land authorities pursuant to prevailing land registration procedures (Gov. Reg. No. 10/1961) constitutes an authentic instrument possessing perfect evidentiary force (*volledig bewijskracht*).
- Witness testimonies constituting conjecture or indications (*legenbewijs / aanwijzingen*) **CANNOT DEFEAT THE PERFECT EVIDENCE** of a Land Rights Certificate.
- High Courts invalidating land certificates based solely on oral witness accounts commit errors in evidentiary law, leading to their judgments being annulled by the Supreme Court.
