# Judicial-policy-finnish-topdown-europeanization-2022

The **Europeanization of Finnish Constitutional Culture and Fundamental Rights Framework** (*Kerangka Kebijakan Europeanisasi Budaya Konstitusi dan Hak Fundamental Finlandia*) is a judicial policy and constitutional law analytical framework demonstrating that constitutional culture and fundamental rights in Finland were shaped top-down through the integration of European Union norms and the jurisprudence of the European Court of Human Rights (ECtHR) and the Court of Justice of the European Union (CJEU)[^1].

## Overview

Authored by [Tomi Tuominen](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Tomi_Tuominen), Lecturer in Law at the [University of Lapland](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/University_of_Lapland), this paper critiques the bottom-up approach utilized by the [European Law Institute](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/European_Law_Institute) (ELI) project in identifying common constitutional traditions (Article 6(3) TEU)[^2]. Tuominen demonstrates that Finland did not export local traditions to the EU, but rather acted as a receiving end, absorbing European legal norms into the deep structure of its constitutional culture following its accession to the Council of Europe (1989) and the EU (1995)[^3].

## Legal Basis

The framework is grounded in key domestic and European legal instruments:

- **[Article 6(3) & Article 4(2) TEU](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Article_6%283%29_%26_Article_4%282%29_TEU)**: EU Treaty provisions regarding common constitutional traditions and national constitutional identity[^4].
- **[Constitution of Finland 1999](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Finland_1999) (731/1999)**: Framework for *ex ante* constitutional review by the Parliamentary Committee (Section 74) and *ex post* judicial review by courts (Section 106)[^5].
- **[European Convention on Human Rights](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/European_Convention_on_Human_Rights) (ECHR)**: Incorporating European fundamental rights standards into domestic law since 1990[^6].
- **[EU Charter of Fundamental Rights](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/EU_Charter_of_Fundamental_Rights)**: Evaluative benchmark in criminal and administrative proceedings[^7].

## Background

Finnish constitutional history evolved through four distinct eras: the Swedish era (since the 13th century), the Grand Duchy of the Russian Empire era (1809–1917), the post-1917 independence and Nordic welfare state era, and the post-1989 Europeanization era[^8]. In 2012, Section 1 of the Constitution was amended to explicitly confirm that "Finland is a Member State of the European Union"[^9].

## Objectives

- Demonstrate that fundamental rights protection in Finland developed top-down through European jurisprudence integration[^10].
- Analyze the operational dynamics of Finland's hybrid constitutional review system[^11].
- Evaluate the alignment of Finnish law with European standards regarding free expression, market freedoms, and judicial independence[^12].

## Scope

The scope encompasses Finnish constitutional law, European Union law, ECtHR/CJEU case law, and the institutional frameworks of both general and administrative court systems[^13].

## Key Provisions & Hybrid Review

- **Ex Ante Review by the Parliamentary Committee (Perustuslakivaliokunta)**: A political committee that consults constitutional law professors to assess the constitutionality of legislative bills before enactment[^14].
- **Ex Post Judicial Review (Section 106)**: Courts may disapply an act of Parliament only if it manifests an "evident conflict" with the Constitution[^15].
- **Harmonized Interpretation (Verfassungskonformen Auslegung)**: Courts and committees adopt the doctrine of interpreting domestic legislation in conformity with the ECHR and EU law[^16].

## Judicial Evidence (Landmark Case Law)

- **Judgment KKO 2012:58 (Jussi Halla-aho)**: The Supreme Court convicted politician Halla-aho of blasphemy and ethnic agitation, applying ECtHR hate speech benchmarks[^17].
- **Judgments KKO 2018:51 & 2018:81**: Balancing privacy rights against freedom of expression on social media based on *von Hannover v. Germany* jurisprudence[^18].
- **CJEU Judgment Viking Line (Case C-438/05)**: Reviewing the conflict between EU freedom of establishment and fundamental labor rights within collective bargaining[^19].

## Implementation

Implemented through daily judicial reasoning in the Supreme Court (KKO) and Supreme Administrative Court (KHO), as well as the establishment of the National Courts Administration (*Tuomioistuinvirasto*) in 2018 to ensure judicial autonomy[^20].

## Policy Challenges

- **Politicization Concerns**: Criticisms regarding academic dominance and political dynamics within the Parliamentary Committee[^21].
- **Fiscal and EU Market Conflicts**: Ongoing tension between national vehicle taxation/alcohol monopoly policies and EU internal market freedoms[^22].

## Reforms

The 2012 Constitutional Amendment entrenched EU membership into Section 1 and formally eliminated the practice of using exceptive acts (*poikkeuslaki*)[^23].
