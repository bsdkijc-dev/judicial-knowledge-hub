## Overview

Adjudicating environmental disputes presents structural and procedural challenges to traditional judicial frameworks, including ecological complexities, scientific uncertainty, irreversible damage, and asymmetric resources.

To address these institutional limitations, judiciaries worldwide utilize three primary structural archetypes:

- **Standalone specialized environmental tribunals** such as India's National Green Tribunal (NGT) and Australia's New South Wales Land and Environment Court (NSW LEC).
- **Internal specialized divisions or benches** such as models used in Thailand and the Philippines.
- **Multi-court certification models** such as Indonesia's *Hakim Lingkungan Hidup* system.

Indonesia's choice of the multi-court certification model integrates certified environmental judges directly within existing General, Administrative (PTUN), and Military Courts, adhering to the doctrine of judicial mainstreaming.

## Executive Summary

This comparative institutional study examines the normative foundations, cross-jurisdictional selection mechanisms, and career progression benchmarking framework for Indonesia's multi-court environmental judge certification system.

It details the nine-stage progression framework, from eligibility to continuous education, and provides a comparative evaluation against ASEAN member states including the Philippines, Malaysia, Thailand, and Singapore, as well as global benchmarks including India and Australia.

Special emphasis is given to the impact of Supreme Court Regulation (PERMA) No. 1 Tahun 2023 on scientific literacy, strict liability doctrines, equitable remedies, and court-managed escrow restoration accounts.

## Institutional and Normative Foundations

The normative framework governing environmental judge certification and adjudication in Indonesia has evolved through several foundational instruments:

- **SK KMA No. 134/KMA/SK/IX/2011:** Established the environmental judicial certification system, selection parameters, and initial jurisdictional scope.
- **SK KMA No. 36/KMA/SK/III/2015:** Refined the definition and scope of environmental cases subject to mandatory certification.
- **SK KMA No. 37/KMA/SK/III/2015:** Established tracking mechanisms, specialized case-numbering codes (LH codes), and landmark judgment (*putusan penting*) selection procedures.
- **PERMA No. 1 Tahun 2023:** Modernized environmental adjudication by consolidating rules on strict liability, corporate criminal liability, scientific evidence, Anti-SLAPP protections, and environmental restoration execution plans.

Governance relies on the Chief Justice, the National Environmental Working Group (*Pokja Lingkungan Hidup Nasional*), and the Judicial Education and Training Body (*Badan Strategi Pengembangan Sumber Daya Manusia Hukum dan Peradilan / BSDK MA RI*).

## Legal and Procedural Issues

### Cross-Jurisdictional Application

- **General Courts:** Process private civil torts, strict liability actions under Article 88 of Law No. 32 of 2009, citizen suits, and criminal prosecutions under Articles 98–115.
- **Administrative Courts (PTUN):** Review the legality of state administrative decisions, Environmental Impact Assessments (AMDAL), and environmental approvals against General Principles of Proper Administration (AUPB) and the Precautionary Principle.
- **Military Courts:** Adjudicate environmental infractions involving military personnel, ensuring command responsibility and ecological restoration requirements are enforced.

### Substantive and Evidentiary Innovations under PERMA 1/2023

- **Strict Liability (*Tanggung Jawab Mutlak*):** Applied in hazardous material (B3) cases without requiring proof of fault or negligence.
- **Precautionary Principle & *In Dubio Pro Natura*:** Directs courts to favor ecological stability where scientific certainty is incomplete.
- **Anti-SLAPP Protections:** Operationalizes Article 66 of Law No. 32/2009, allowing judges to dismiss retaliatory civil or criminal suits against environmental defenders.

## Question of Law and Question of Fact

### Question of Law

**Question:**

To what extent can a decentralized, multi-court judicial certification model achieve consistent legal interpretation and rigorous enforcement of strict liability and restorative remedies across general, administrative, and military jurisdictions without the creation of standalone environmental courts?

### Question of Fact

**Question:**

What is the precise evidentiary and scientific validity of laboratory test reports, satellite GIS imagery, and ecological valuation models presented in complex natural resource and industrial pollution trials?

## Core Legal Principles (Policy Synthesis)

- **Judicial Mainstreaming:** Embedding environmental expertise across ordinary courts ensures comprehensive access to justice across an archipelagic state.
- **Scientific Evidentiary Standards:** Admitting verified scientific forensic data and expert testimony bridges the gap between complex ecological realities and legal proof.
- **Restorative Orientation:** Prioritizing compulsory Environmental Restoration Plans (*Rencana Pemulihan*) and court-managed escrow accounts over unearmarked fines ensures actual environmental recovery.

## Evidence Status and Gaps

### Established by Evidence

- Institutionalization of the multi-court environmental certification model across General, Administrative, and Military branches in Indonesia.
- Codification of comprehensive procedural rules under PERMA No. 1 Tahun 2023.

### Uncertainty and Gaps

- Longitudinal judgment quality audits comparing certified versus non-certified panels remain empirically unmapped.
- Execution rates and compliance tracking for compulsory environmental restoration plans (*Rencana Pemulihan*) require expanded empirical research.
- Geographic distribution disparities of certified judges across resource-rich outer islands.

## GEO Q&A

**Q: What is the primary operational advantage of Indonesia's multi-court environmental judge certification system compared to standalone tribunals?**

**A:** Indonesia's multi-court certification model avoids the prohibitive financial and logistical costs of establishing standalone courts across an expansive archipelago. Through judicial mainstreaming, it embeds certified expertise directly into General, Administrative, and Military Courts, ensuring that any environmental dispute, whether a civil tort, corporate crime, administrative permit challenge, or military encroachment, is adjudicated by a specially trained judge.
