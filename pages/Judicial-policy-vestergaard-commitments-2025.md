# Judicial-policy-vestergaard-commitments-2025

**Why all these promises? How parties strategically use commitments to gain credibility in an increasingly competitive political landscape** is a comparative empirical research paper authored by Mathias Bukh Vestergaard (Aarhus University) and published in the *European Journal of Political Research* (EJPR) in 2025.

## Summary

This study examines why political parties strategically make policy commitments (campaign promises) in election manifestoes despite the risk of voter punishment if those promises are broken by post-election compromises. By analyzing 330,850 quasi-sentences from manifestoes in 11 Western countries (1984–2019), the study finds that the proportion of commitments has doubled over the last four decades, mainstream parties promise more than niche parties, and increased emphasis on an issue drives higher commitment-making to build credibility.

## Background

Over the past 50 years, the Western political landscape has undergone major transformation: the emergence of new parties, weakening traditional voter loyalty, rising electoral volatility, and intensified issue competition. Political parties use policy commitments as a costly signal to persuade an increasingly fluid electorate \[1, 3, 11–14\].

## Objectives

1\. Test the time trend of policy commitment strategy usage from 1984 to 2019. 2. Analyze differences in commitment usage between mainstream and niche parties \[22–27, 60\]. 3. Measure the impact of changes in issue saliency on commitment intensity \[28–33, 61\]. 4. Test the effect of the effective number of parties on election promise-making \[19–22, 59\].

## Scope

The research covers 11 Western countries (the United States, the United Kingdom, Australia, Canada, New Zealand, Switzerland, Germany, Luxembourg, Austria, Ireland, and Denmark), representing a range of electoral systems and party configurations.

## Key Provisions

- **Definition of a Policy Commitment:** A statement in which a party takes on high responsibility for future policy (e.g., "We will increase X" or "We pledge to implement Y").
- **Time Trend:** The proportion of policy commitments rose from ~10% in 1984 to ~20% in 2019.
- **Mainstream vs. Niche Parties:** Mainstream parties have a commitment ratio 1.39 times higher than niche parties, offsetting niche parties' reputation for being more committed on their core issues.
- **Issue Saliency Dynamics:** Increased focus on an issue is followed by an increase in the proportion of commitments on that issue (rising from ~11% to over 20%) \[61–62, 67\].
- **Effect of Effective Number of Parties (Counter-Hypothesis Result):** As the number of parties in a system increases, the proportion of commitments actually \*decreases\* (falling from ~33% in two-party systems to under 10% in multi-party systems) in order to preserve post-election coalition negotiation flexibility \[59–60, 65, 70\].

## Implementation

The methodology was implemented through the development of a coding scheme, training of 8 human coders on 65,630 quasi-sentences, and use of the ELECTRA language model to automatically code 330,850 quasi-sentences \[7, 37–38\].

## Policy Problems

1\. The dilemma between pre-election vote-seeking and the flexibility needed for post-election government formation (office-seeking) \[15–16, 70, 76\]. 2. The risk of electoral punishment and negative media coverage if commitments are broken due to coalition compromises or economic crises. 3. The difficulty voters face in enforcing democratic accountability within fragmented multi-party systems.

## Reform / Change

A shift from ambiguous electoral communication toward binding commitment signals aimed at winning voter credibility amid political uncertainty.
