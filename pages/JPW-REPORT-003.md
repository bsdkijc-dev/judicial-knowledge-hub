# JPW-REPORT-003

**Report on Preliminary Examination Activities (2020)** is the official annual report published by the Office of the Prosecutor (OTP) of the International Criminal Court on 14 December 2020, summarizing analysis and preliminary-examination activity concerning situations of international crimes under the Rome Statute for the period 6 December 2019 to 11 December 2020.

## Summary

The report covers the completion of four preliminary examinations (Palestine, Iraq/UK, Nigeria, Ukraine), the opening of two new state referrals (Bolivia, Venezuela II), and ongoing assessment of several other situations, including Colombia, Guinea, the Philippines, and Venezuela I, all at Phase 3 (Admissibility).

## Legal Basis

Preliminary examinations are conducted under Article 53(1) of the [Rome Statute](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Rome_Statute), which sets out the evaluation criteria of jurisdiction (temporal, territorial/personal, subject-matter), admissibility (complementarity and gravity), and the interests of justice.

## Background

A preliminary examination may be triggered through three principal channels: (i) Article 15 communications from individuals, groups, states, or NGOs; (ii) a referral from a State Party or the UN Security Council; or (iii) a declaration accepting jurisdiction under Article 12(3) of the Rome Statute.

## Objectives

The report's core aims are to promote transparency in the preliminary examination process, explain the reasoning behind decisions to proceed or not proceed toward investigation, and support positive complementarity by encouraging genuine national proceedings.

## Key Provisions

The OTP applies a four-phase filtering process:

- **Phase 1**: Initial assessment of all Article 15 communications received.
- **Phase 2**: Assessment of jurisdictional preconditions — territorial/personal and subject-matter jurisdiction.
- **Phase 3**: Admissibility assessment, focused on national judicial complementarity and gravity of the crimes.
- **Phase 4**: Assessment of the interests of justice.

## Implementation

During the reporting period, the OTP received 813 Article 15 communications. Of these, 612 were found manifestly outside the Court's jurisdiction, 104 related to situations already under examination, 71 related to ongoing investigations or prosecutions, and 26 required further analysis ("WFA communications").

## Policy Issues

Key challenges include the OTP's lack of investigative powers during the preliminary examination stage, operational constraints from the COVID-19 pandemic, the absence of any fixed timeline under the Rome Statute, and the need to address delays in domestic proceedings in certain states.
