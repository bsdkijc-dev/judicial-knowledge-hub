# Judicial-policy-polish-court-image-morawska-2016

**The Polish Court System Public Image Policy Framework** is a judicial management and public image development policy framework formulated by Sylwia Morawska (Warsaw School of Economics) and Przemysław Banasik (Gdańsk University of Technology) in the *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA), Vol. 8 No. 1 (October 2016). It evaluates the paradox between the objective efficiency of Polish courts at the EU level (*EU Justice Scoreboard 2013*) and the internal crisis of public trust (image crisis). It establishes a 5-level court image maturity model and a framework for territorial inter-corporational legal networks \[1–15, 25–26\].

## Summary

Published by Sylwia Morawska and Przemysław Banasik in the IJCA (October 2016), this policy framework addresses the public trust crisis experienced by the Polish judicial system \[1–4\]. Although the European Commission's *EU Justice Scoreboard 2013* ranked Polish courts as the 5th most efficient in civil/commercial cases and 3rd in administrative cases within the European Union, domestic public perception views the courts as inefficient, rigid, and opaque. This study presents the results of a modern management pilot project (2013–2014) across 60 courts, funded by the European Social Fund and the National School of Judiciary and Prosecution (KSSiP) \[12–13\]. Morawska and Banasik formulated a 5-level court image maturity model—ranging from the complete absence of navigation facilities (Level 1) to the adoption of formal aesthetic documents, logo standardization, and an internal color-coding navigation system (Level 5) \[15–20\]. The model implemented at the Regional Court in Gdańsk (*Sąd Okręgowy w Gdańsku*) successfully achieved Level 4 and established the first inter-corporational legal network in Poland \[22–26\].

## Background

Following the collapse of the socialist regime in the 1980s, Polish courts adopted judicial independence but retained a classic socialist bureaucratic management style that alienated justice seekers.

## Objectives

1\. Overcome the courts' public image crisis in Poland through modern management approaches. 2. Facilitate the transformation of courts from closed bureaucracies into transparent, user-friendly public institutions. 3. Provide a set of measurement indicators (maturity levels) for district, regional, and appellate courts.

## Scope

This policy encompasses 318 district courts, 45 regional courts, and 11 appellate courts in Poland, utilizing a pilot project sample of 60 courts.

## Key Provisions

- **Court Stakeholders Matrix:**
  - Categorizing stakeholders into *Co-participating* (Court Presidents, Judges, Staff), *Contract-based* (Litigants/Parties), and *Context-related* (Prosecutors, Prisons, KSSiP, Lawyers, Media, Citizens).
- **5-Level Image Maturity Model:**
  - **Level 1:** No satisfaction surveys, no navigation maps, no standard logos, no aesthetic documents.
  - **Level 2:** Presence of satisfaction surveys and directional maps/diagrams.
  - **Level 3:** Regular staff training and standardized logos/graphics.
  - **Level 4:** Possession of a formal document outlining aesthetic standards for the court's image.
  - **Level 5:** Full implementation of an internal color-coding navigation system.
- **Territorial Inter-Corporational Networks:**
  - Collaborative forums among legal professionals (judges, advocates, notaries, academics) to transfer management innovations via a *bottom-up* approach.

## Implementation

- **Execution of the KSSiP Pilot Project (2013–2014):** Trial of modern management techniques across 60 courts (3 appellate, 19 district, 38 local).
- **Application of the Regional Court in Gdańsk Model:** Achieving Level 4 maturity and establishing the Gdańsk Chapter of Legal Professions.

## Policy Problems

- **The Efficiency vs. Trust Paradox:** High EU objective efficiency rankings that inversely contrast with a severe local public trust crisis.
- **Absence of Standard Aesthetic Documents:** Rigid and confusing courthouse layouts and graphics that disorient visitors.

## Reform / Change

- Integrating *bottom-up* approaches (voluntary court innovations) with *top-down* directives (Ministry of Justice regulations).
- Adopting annual judicial staff satisfaction surveys alongside continuous service registration surveys for justice seekers.

### 3 Most Important Improvements

1\. **Adoption of National Standard Aesthetic and Navigation Documents (Level 5 Achievement):** Mandate all courts in Poland to adopt formal aesthetic documents and internal color-coding navigation systems to facilitate ease of public accessibility. 2. **Institutionalization of Territorial Inter-Corporational Legal Networks Nationwide:** Expand the Gdańsk Territorial Inter-Corporational Network model across all regional court jurisdictions to facilitate continuous communication among advocates, notaries, and academics. 3. **Implementation of Continuous and Integrated Satisfaction Surveys:** Integrate satisfaction surveys for both justice seekers and judicial staff directly into the courts' IT systems to provide real-time data for ongoing management improvement.
