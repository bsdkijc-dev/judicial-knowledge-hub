# JPW-POLICY-000020

The **UNDP Asian Judicial Environmental Justice Needs Assessment Framework** (Stable ID: `JPW-POLICY-000020`) is a multi-jurisdictional empirical needs assessment and judicial policy framework evaluating the structural obstacles, training deficits, procedural challenges, capacity gaps, and support requirements for judges adjudicating environmental justice cases across Asian jurisdictions. Published by the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP), the survey captures judicial feedback from judges across Lao PDR (42.3%), Indonesia (27.6%), Philippines (18.4%), Thailand (4.6%), and India (1.9%). The framework highlights that 84.0% of judges have received no formal training on environmental justice, outlining key UNDP intervention pathways including specialized environmental procedure curriculums, dedicated environmental chambers, scientific capacity building, and judge security measures. Note: Named entities *Cecep Mustafa* and *Reihan Abdan Syakur* are *Not found in the available sources*.

## Summary

The framework synthesizes survey findings from Asian judges (65% female, 35% male) regarding environmental litigation. It outlines six core structural pillars: (1) Caseload Identification, documenting water pollution, climate change, air pollution, land degradation, and biodiversity loss as the most frequent environmental court cases; (2) Judicial Training Deficit, revealing that 84.0% of judges lack formal environmental justice training; (3) Statutory Adequacy Assessment, recording that 55.6% view environmental laws as only "somewhat adequate" and 22.7% as "inadequate"; (4) Adjudication Obstacles, identifying government alternative deficits, lack of writ jurisprudence, weak enforcement, corruption, conflicting agency mandates, and inadequate monitoring infrastructure; (5) Judicial Security & Scientific Challenges, detailing risks of harassment, lack of evidence handling security, lack of climate change understanding, and lack of expert witnesses; and (6) Access to Justice Barriers, highlighting how poverty, remoteness, unresponsive authorities, and formalistic court rulings impede affected communities.

## Legal Basis

Specific statutory legal bases are *Not found in the available sources*. The framework operates as an empirical needs assessment and international policy guide informing UNDP support programs, court administration budgets, and judicial academy curriculums.

## Background & Policy Problems

### Background

Increasing environmental degradation across Asia has led to complex environmental litigation before regular courts. However, judges frequently lack formal training in environmental law and environmental science, operating within courts burdened by weak enforcement, conflicting agency mandates, and inadequate monitoring infrastructure. UNDP initiated this needs assessment to evaluate judicial challenges and establish structured support mechanisms.

### Policy Problems

- High Untrained Judge Ratio: 84.0% of surveyed judges have received no formal training on environmental justice.
- Harassment & Security Risks: Judges face personal security risks when handling environmental evidence and encounter harassment risks during litigation.
- Scientific Knowledge Barriers: Lack of judicial understanding regarding environmental science, climate change, and multidisciplinary legal connections.
- Severe Access Barriers: Poverty, remoteness, unresponsive authorities, and formalistic decision-making restrict access to justice for vulnerable communities against state entities and large corporations.

## Objectives

1\. Evaluate structural and procedural obstacles in environmental adjudication. 2. Develop environmental procedure curriculums, textbooks, and specialized training. 3. Enhance judicial capacity in environmental science and climate change. 4. Promote digital infrastructure, field data collection, and expert consultation. 5. Support dedicated environmental justice chambers and legal-technical assistance.

## Scope & Implementation

### Scope

Applies across courts, court administrations, judicial training institutes, and environmental chambers in Lao PDR, Indonesia, Philippines, Thailand, India, and international development frameworks.

### Key Provisions

- **Curriculum & Textbook Creation:** Standardizing environmental procedure learning materials.
- **Dedicated Environmental Chambers:** Establishing specialized judicial chambers for environmental cases.
- **Scientific & Tech Capacity Building:** Equipping courts with digital case management, expert consultation, and field data support.

### Implementation

- **Survey Execution:** Surveying judges across Lao PDR, Indonesia, Philippines, Thailand, and India.
- **Ten UNDP Support Avenues:** Implementing specialized training, educational materials, knowledge sharing, legal-technical support, networking, policy establishment, community engagement, scientific capacity, access to information, and dedicated chambers.

## Policy Problems

- High Un-trained Judge Ratio: 84.0% of judges lack formal environmental justice training.
- Judge Security & Harassment: Risks faced when handling environmental evidence.
- Access Barriers for Impoverished Citizens: Unresponsive authorities and remote court access.

## Reform

- Creating specialized environmental curriculums.
- Establishing specialized environmental courts/chambers.
- Strengthening scientific and climate change training for judges.
