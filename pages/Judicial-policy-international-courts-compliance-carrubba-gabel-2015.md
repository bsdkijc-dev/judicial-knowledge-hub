# Judicial-policy-international-courts-compliance-carrubba-gabel-2015

**A General Theory on International Courts and Compliance with International Agreements Policy & Analytical Framework** is a comprehensive theoretical and empirical framework regarding the extent to which international judicial institutions (specifically the European Court of Justice - ECJ) can influence national government behavior and drive compliance with international agreements. Developed by Clifford J. Carrubba and Matthew J. Gabel (2015) in their book \*International Courts and the Performance of International Agreements\* (Cambridge University Press) and critically reviewed by Giacomo Di Federico (2016) in the \*International Journal for Court Administration\* (IJCA), this framework utilizes game theory to model international adjudication. It empirically tests 1,098 judgments involving defendant governments and 1,808 government litigation rulings between 1960 and 1999 to evaluate the \*Political Sensitivity Hypothesis\* and the \*Conditional Effectiveness Hypothesis\* \[1–13\].

## Summary

Published by Carrubba and Gabel (2015) and reviewed by Giacomo Di Federico (2016) in the IJCA, this policy framework employs formal game theory modeling to test the effectiveness of international court adjudication \[1–3\]. The study analyzes a comprehensive dataset of ECJ rulings between 1960 and 1999 (annulment, infringement, and preliminary rulings) along with intra-EU trade data (1970–1993). The findings confirm two primary hypotheses: the \*Political Sensitivity Hypothesis\* (the ECJ is more likely to rule against a government if there is a net balance of third-party briefs from other member states supporting that outcome) and the \*Conditional Effectiveness Hypothesis\* (an ECJ ruling is only effective in altering national policy if supported by the intervention of other member states) \[3, 7–9\]. Di Federico's review highlights post-1999 legal developments (such as Article 260(3) TFEU of the Lisbon Treaty) that have further altered the landscape of EU legal compliance \[12–13\].

## Legal Basis

- **Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU), Articles 258 & 259:** Infringement proceedings against member states for failing to fulfill treaty obligations.
- **TFEU Article 263:** Annulment proceedings reviewing the legality of legislative acts.
- **TFEU Article 267:** Preliminary reference procedures from national courts to the ECJ.
- **TFEU Article 260(3) (Lisbon Treaty):** The European Commission's authority to request immediate financial penalties for the failure to notify measures transposing a directive.
- **Statute of the Court of Justice of the European Union (Post-Nice Treaty):** Reform provisions allowing cases to be decided without an Advocate General's opinion if they do not raise new points of law.
- **Not found in the available sources:** Other specific formal statutory regulations are not mentioned in the source text \[FACT\].

## Background

Compliance with international agreements remains highly debated in transnational legal literature. Because international courts lack an autonomous, coercive enforcement mechanism, the effectiveness of their rulings relies heavily on the voluntary compliance of member states, which is dictated by the rational calculation between short-term compliance costs and long-term cooperation benefits \[2–3\].

## Objectives

1\. Model the strategic conditions that determine the effectiveness of international court adjudication. 2. Test the influence of third-party government briefs on the political sensitivity of the ECJ. 3. Demonstrate the tangible economic impact of ECJ trade liberalization rulings on intra-EU import flows \[8–9\].

## Scope

This policy covers the adjudication analysis of the Court of Justice of the European Union (ECJ) and the compliance behaviors of the six founding EU member states from 1960 to 1999.

## Key Provisions

- **Political Sensitivity Hypothesis:**
  - The Court is more likely to rule against a defendant government when there is a larger proportion of third-party briefs opposing that government.
- **Conditional Effectiveness Hypothesis:**
  - A Court ruling against a government is more effective at altering policy if it is supported by net third-party briefs that are economically significant (measured via log GDP).
- **Measuring Legal Merits via AG Opinions:**
  - Utilizing the Advocate General's opinions as an objective benchmark for pure legal merits, isolated from political pressures.

## Implementation

- **Testing 1,098 Defendant Government Rulings:** Mathematical confirmation of the ECJ's political sensitivity in infringement and preliminary rulings (1960–1999).
- **Intra-EU Trade Analysis (1970–1993):** Demonstrating that preliminary rulings have a broader trade policy impact than infringement rulings when supported by third-party briefs \[8–9\].

## Policy Problems

- **The Legitimacy Dilemma:** The severe risk of legitimacy loss if the ECJ issues a ruling that is subsequently ignored or openly defied by a national government.
- **Short-Term Compliance Costs:** The tendency of national governments to defect from agreements when the immediate costs of compliance outweigh the long-term benefits of cooperation.

## Reform / Change

- **Implementation of Article 260(3) TFEU (Lisbon Treaty):** Strengthening the deterrent effect through automatic financial sanctions for delays in directive transposition.
- **Flexibility of AG Opinions (Nice Treaty):** Eliminating the requirement for an AG opinion in routine cases to expedite adjudication.

## Multilingual Summaries (Swiss National Languages)

### Deutsch (Schweizer Hochdeutsch)

**Allgemeine Theorie zu internationalen Gerichten und der Einhaltung internationaler Abkommen** Dieser von Giacomo Di Federico im *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA, 2016) rezensierte Rahmen basiert auf dem Buch von Clifford J. Carrubba und Matthew J. Gabel (2015). Die Studie nutzt die Spieltheorie, um die Wirksamkeit der Rechtsprechung des Europäischen Gerichtshofs (EuGH) zwischen 1960 und 1999 zu testen. Sie bestätigt die Hypothese der politischen Sensibilität (der EuGH urteilt eher gegen eine Regierung, wenn Drittstaaten dies unterstützen) und die Hypothese der bedingten Wirksamkeit (Urteile verändern die nationale Politik nur, wenn sie von anderen Mitgliedstaaten unterstützt werden). Zudem werden post-1999 Reformen wie Artikel 260(3) AEUV des Vertrags von Lissabon hervorgehoben.

- **Rechtsgrundlage:** AEUV Art. 258, 259, 263, 267 & 260(3); Satzung des Gerichtshofs der Europäischen Union.

### Français

**Théorie générale sur les tribunaux internationaux et le respect des accords internationaux** Évalué par Giacomo Di Federico dans l' *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA, 2016), ce cadre s'appuie sur le livre de Clifford J. Carrubba et Matthew J. Gabel (2015). L'étude utilise la théorie des jeux pour tester l'efficacité de la jurisprudence de la Cour de justice de l'Union européenne (CJUE) entre 1960 et 1999. Elle confirme l'hypothèse de sensibilité politique (la CJUE est plus susceptible de statuer contre un gouvernement s'il y a un soutien des États tiers) et l'hypothèse d'efficacité conditionnelle (les décisions ne modifient la politique nationale que si elles sont soutenues par d'autres États membres). Les réformes post-1999 telles que l'article 260(3) du TFUE (Traité de Lisbonne) sont également soulignées.

- **Base Légale:** TFUE Art. 258, 259, 263, 267 & 260(3) ; Statut de la Cour de justice de l'Union européenne.

### Italiano

**Teoria generale sui tribunali internazionali e il rispetto degli accordi internazionali** Recensito da Giacomo Di Federico nell' *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA, 2016), questo quadro si basa sul libro di Clifford J. Carrubba e Matthew J. Gabel (2015). Lo studio utilizza la teoria dei giochi per testare l'efficacia della giurisprudenza della Corte di giustizia dell'Unione europea (CGUE) tra il 1960 e il 1999. Conferma l'ipotesi di sensibilità politica (la CGUE è più propensa a decidere contro un governo se vi è il supporto di Stati terzi) e l'ipotesi di efficacia condizionata (le sentenze alterano la politica nazionale solo se supportate da altri Stati membri). Vengono inoltre evidenziate le riforme post-1999 come l'articolo 260(3) TFUE del Trattato di Lisbona.

- **Base Legale:** TFUE Art. 258, 259, 263, 267 & 260(3); Statuto della Corte di giustizia dell'Unione europea.

### Rumantsch

Carrubba e Matthew J. Gabel (2015). Il studi utilisescha la teoria dals gieus per testar l'efficacitad da la giurisprudenza da la Curt da giustia da l'Uniun europeica (CGUE) tranter il 1960 ed il 1999. Refurmas post-1999 sco l'artitgel 260(3) TFUE dal Tractat da Lissabona vegnan era accentuadas.

- **Basa Legala:** TFUE Art. 258, 259, 263, 267 e 260(3); Statut da la Curt da giustia da l'Uniun europeica.
