# Judicial-policy-delfino-szymanski-platform-work-2024

The **Policy Framework on Trade Union Participation and Platform Work Regulation: EU and US Perspectives** (*Platform Work and Trade Union Participation: European and American Perspectives*) is a comparative labour law analysis formulated by Massimiliano Delfino and Charles Szymanski, published in *The Italian Law Journal* (Vol. 10 Nos. 01–02/2024, pp. 73–97).[^1]

The study evaluates the role of trade unions and social dialogue in regulating platform work (the [gig economy](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/gig_economy)) and [algorithmic management](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/algorithmic_management) / Artificial Intelligence (AI) systems across the [European Union](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/European_Union) and the [United States](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/United_States).[^2] It contrasts worker consultation and information rights under the EU Draft Directive on Platform Work, the [EU AI Act](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/EU_AI_Act), the 2020 Autonomous Framework Agreement on Digitalisation, and the 2022 Central Government Administrations Agreement against US legal hurdles, including worker misclassification, the [ABC test](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/ABC_test), the [PRO Act](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/PRO_Act), federal antitrust prohibitions, [California Proposition 22](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/California_Proposition_22), and SAG-AFTRA's non-collective consultation model for social media influencers.[^3]

## Summary

Authored by [Massimiliano Delfino](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Massimiliano_Delfino) (Full Professor of Labour Law at the [Federico II University of Naples](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Federico_II_University_of_Naples)) and [Charles Szymanski](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Charles_Szymanski) (Professor of Law at the [University of Bialystok](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/University_of_Bialystok)), this comparative work examines how trade unions in the European Union and the United States respond to algorithmic management and platform work challenges.[^4]

In the European Union, trade unions utilize institutional tripartite social dialogue to shape supranational regulations.[^5] Instruments like the EU Draft Directive on Platform Work and Article 26(7) of the EU AI Act guarantee worker representatives information and consultation rights before automated management systems are deployed.[^6] Conversely, in the United States, trade unions face structural obstacles because platform workers are typically classified as independent contractors excluded from the [National Labor Relations Act](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/National_Labor_Relations_Act) (NLRA).[^7] Efforts in the US remain constrained by Senate filibuster rules blocking the PRO Act, federal antitrust prohibitions against collective bargaining by independent contractors (e.g., *City of Seattle*), and heavily funded corporate ballot initiatives like California Proposition 22.[^8]

## Legal Basis

### European Union Instruments & Regulations

- **[Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Treaty_on_the_Functioning_of_the_European_Union) (TFEU)**: Article 153(1)(b) (Social Policy), Article 114 (Internal Market), Article 16(2) (Data Protection), Articles 154 & 155 (Social Dialogue and Autonomous Framework Agreements), Article 288 (Legal Effects of Regulations vs. Directives).[^9]
- **[Charter of Fundamental Rights of the European Union](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Charter_of_Fundamental_Rights_of_the_European_Union) (CFREU)**: Article 27 (Workers' Right to Information and Consultation).[^10]
- **EU Draft Directive on Platform Work (March 2024 Text)**: Articles 6, 9, 13, and 14 (Collective Information and Consultation Procedures).[^11]
- **[EU Artificial Intelligence Act](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Artificial_Intelligence_Act) (2024 Text)**: Article 26(7) (High-Risk AI Information Obligations in Workplace Contexts).[^12]
- **Directive 2002/14/EC**: Framework for informing and consulting employees.[^13]
- **Autonomous Agreements**: European Autonomous Framework Agreement on Digitalisation (2020) and Autonomous Agreement on Digitalisation in Central Government Administrations (2022).[^14]

### United States Legislation & Jurisprudence

- **[National Labor Relations Act](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/National_Labor_Relations_Act) (NLRA) (29 U.S.C. § 151 et seq.)**: § 152(3) (Exclusion of Independent Contractors), §§ 153, 159, 160(b).[^15]
- **[Fair Labor Standards Act](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Fair_Labor_Standards_Act) (FLSA) (29 U.S.C. § 201 et seq.)**: § 203(r)(1) (Minimum Wage and Overtime Protections).[^16]
- **[Sherman Antitrust Act](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Sherman_Antitrust_Act_of_1890) & Federal Antitrust Law**: Prohibitions on price-fixing and collective action by independent contractors.[^17]
- **Proposed Legislation & State Acts**: [Protecting the Right to Organize Act](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Protecting_the_Right_to_Organize_Act) (PRO Act), [California Assembly Bill 5](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/California_Assembly_Bill_5) (AB 5), and [California Proposition 22](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/California_Proposition_22).[^18]
- **Key Jurisprudence**:
  - *CJEU / General Court*: Joined Cases *EPSU v. Commission* (C-928/19 P & T-310/18); Case C-256/01 *Allonby*; Case C-413/13 *FNV Kunsten*.[^19]
  - *US Federal Courts & NLRB*: *Chamber of Commerce v. City of Seattle* (9th Cir. 2018); *SuperShuttle DFW, Inc.* (367 NLRB No. 75, 2019); *The Atlanta Opera, Inc.* (372 NLRB No. 95, 2023); *Janus v. AFSCME* (138 S. Ct. 2448, 2018).[^20]

## Key Pillars of Policy Analysis

1\. **Algorithmic Information and Consultation Rights in the EU**: The EU framework mandates transparency regarding automated decision-making and algorithmic management (Articles 9 & 13 of the Draft Directive). Employers must consult trade unions prior to implementing high-risk AI systems (EU AI Act Art. 26(7)) and cover expert costs for companies exceeding 250 workers.[^21] 2. **Antitrust Barriers and Classification Hurdles in the US**: Under NLRA § 152(3), independent contractors cannot engage in protected collective bargaining. Attempts by local municipalities (such as Seattle) to authorize collective bargaining for gig workers face federal antitrust preemption unless protected under the state action doctrine.[^22] 3. **Trade Union Adaptation Strategies**: While EU unions utilize institutional dialogue and legislative reforms, US trade unions advocate for federal adoption of the ABC test via the PRO Act and explore alternative non-collective service models, such as [SAG-AFTRA](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/SAG-AFTRA)'s standardized influencer agreements.[^23]

## Sintesi

Scritto da [Massimiliano Delfino](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Massimiliano_Delfino) (Professore Ordinario di Diritto del Lavoro presso l'[Università degli Studi di Napoli Federico II](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Universit%C3%A0_degli_Studi_di_Napoli_Federico_II)) e [Charles Szymanski](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Charles_Szymanski) (Professore di Diritto presso l'[Università di Białystok](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Universit%C3%A0_di_Bia%C5%82ystok)), questo saggio comparato analizza la risposta sindacale di fronte alle sfide della gestione algoritmica e del lavoro digitale.[^33]

Nell'Unione Europea, i sindacati utilizzano il dialogo sociale istituzionalizzato per promuovere normative sovranazionali.[^34] Strumenti quali la proposta di Direttiva sul lavoro su piattaforma e l'Articolo 26(7) dell'EU AI Act garantiscono ai rappresentanti dei lavoratori diritti di informazione preventivi e consultazione prima dell'introduzione di algoritmi di gestione.[^35] Al contrario, negli Stati Uniti le organizzazioni sindacali incontrano gravi impedimenti strutturali dovuti alla classificazione dei lavoratori delle piattaforme come lavoratori autonomi (*independent contractors*), categoria esclusa dal [National Labor Relations Act](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/National_Labor_Relations_Act) (NLRA).[^36] Le iniziative statunitensi rimangono bloccate dal fenomeno del *filibuster* al Senato contro il PRO Act, dai limiti del diritto antitrust federale alla contrattazione collettiva dei lavoratori autonomi (es. caso *City of Seattle*) e dal ricorso ai referendum finanziati dalle multinazionali del settore (es. Proposition 22 in California).[^37]

## Pilastri Fondamentali dell'Analisi

1\. **Diritti di Informazione Algoritmica nell'UE**: Il quadro europeo impone trasparenza sui sistemi decisionali automatizzati (Artt. 9 e 13 della proposta di Direttiva). I datori di lavoro devono consultare i sindacati prima di adottare sistemi di IA ad alto rischio (EU AI Act Art. 26(7)) e coprire i costi dei periti per le imprese con più di 250 dipendenti.[^50] 2. **Ostacoli Antitrust e Qualificazione del Rapporto negli USA**: Ai sensi del NLRA § 152(3), i lavoratori autonomi non godono del diritto alla contrattazione collettiva. I tentativi di regolamentazione locale (come l'ordinanza di Seattle) sono soggetti a nullità per contrasto con il diritto antitrust federale, salvo applicazione della *state action doctrine*.[^51] 3. **Evoluzione delle Strategie Sindacali**: Mentre i sindacati europei sfruttano la via legislativa e il dialogo sociale, le organizzazioni statunitensi promuovono il test ABC tramite il PRO Act e sviluppano modelli di tutela individuale, come i contratti tipo di [SAG-AFTRA](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/SAG-AFTRA) per gli influencer.[^52]
