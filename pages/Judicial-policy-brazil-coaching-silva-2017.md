# Judicial-policy-brazil-coaching-silva-2017

**The Illinois Mental Health Courts Policy and Evaluation Framework** is a specialized judicial (problem-solving/specialty court) policy framework and statewide comprehensive evaluation regarding Mental Health Courts (MHCs) in Illinois. Researched by Arthur J. Lurigio, Monte D. Staton, Shanti J. Raman, and Lorena Roque (2015) in the *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA), the framework is based on a 2010–2013 study of 9 operational MHCs (which expanded to 21 by 2014) funded by the ICJIA and the US Department of Justice (BJA). It integrates the principles of therapeutic jurisprudence, the CSG's 10 Essential Elements, cross-disciplinary team approaches, ACA/CountyCare enrollment, and a 4-year recidivism analysis to divert offenders with serious mental illness (PSMI) from prison into community clinical care \[1–39\].

## Summary

Published by Arthur J. Lurigio et al. in the *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA), this policy framework presents the first statewide examination of Mental Health Courts (MHCs) in Illinois. Reviewing the expansion from 9 operational MHCs in 2010 to 21 by 2014, the study confirms that the majority of MHCs in Illinois operate in accordance with the CSG's 10 Essential Elements \[8, 10, 16–18\]. Utilizing a non-adversarial hybrid team approach (comprising judges, prosecutors, public defenders, probation officers, and TASC), MHCs have successfully diverted PSMIs from incarceration \[18–20\]. A 4-year recidivism analysis of 210 participants demonstrated positive outcomes, with MHC 1 achieving a 70% survival rate free from re-arrest \[30–31\]. Lurigio et al. recommend the mass enrollment of participants into the *Affordable Care Act (ACA) / CountyCare* health insurance schemes to secure continuous funding for treatment \[31–32\].

- **US Bureau of Justice Assistance (BJA) Mental Health Court Program Grant Authorizations:** The foundation for federal funding and technical guidance for mental health courts.
- **Council of State Governments (CSG) 10 Essential Elements of a Mental Health Court:** The standard reference framework for the structure and operation of MHCs \[8, 16–18, 21\].
- **Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act (ACA) / CountyCare Directives:** Policies expanding public health insurance to finance the medical/psychiatric care of participants \[31–32, 38\].

## Background

The increasing population of people with serious mental illness (PSMI) in prisons spurred the innovation of Mental Health Courts. Based on therapeutic jurisprudence, MHCs aim to break the cycle of repeated incarceration through community medical intervention \[2–3\].

## Objectives

1\. Divert offenders with serious mental illness (PSMI) from prison to community treatment facilities. 2. Reduce criminal recidivism rates while improving public safety and the clients' quality of life. 3. Build a flexible and coordinated non-adversarial judicial workgroup model \[4, 18–20, 23\]. 4. Integrate participants into federal health insurance programs (ACA / CountyCare) \[31–32\].

## Scope

This policy encompasses the evaluation of all judicial circuits (23 circuits) in the State of Illinois, with a focus on 9 operational MHCs in urban counties.

## Key Provisions

- **Implementation of the CSG 10 Essential Elements:**
  - Requiring voluntary participation (informed consent), pre/post-adjudication referrals, and screening for clinical criteria (Axis I/II & co-occurring disorders) \[17–18\].
- **Non-Adversarial Hybrid Team Approach:**
  - Hybrid collaboration between judges, prosecutors, public defenders, probation officers, and TASC (Treatment Alternatives for Safe Communities) case managers \[18–20, 23\].
- **Graduated Sanctions and Incentives Scheme:**
  - The use of a *fish bowl draw* (e.g., movie tickets/vouchers) for positive behavior, alongside graduated sanctions (from community service to brief jail time) for rule violations.
- **Application of Evidence-Based Practices (EBPs):**
  - Integration of modules such as Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT), Motivational Interviewing, and Supportive Employment \[27–28\].

## Implementation

- **Operational Growth (2004–2014):** An increase in operational MHCs in Illinois from 2 programs in 2004 to 21 programs in 2014 \[9–10, 14–15\].
- **Integration of ACA/CountyCare Enrollment:** Active enrollment of MHC participants into health insurance schemes to ensure the continuity of therapy \[31–32\].

## Policy Problems

- **Rural Scarcity:** The lack of mental health service providers in rural areas, which hinders the establishment of MHCs \[11–12\].
- **Racial/Ethnic Disparities:** The overrepresentation of African-Americans and the underrepresentation of Latinos in MHC participation.

## Reform / Change

- Full adoption of ACA/CountyCare health insurance enrollment for all citizens involved in the criminal justice system \[31–32\].
- The establishment and utilization of the *Illinois Mental Health Court Database System* as an integrated data repository \[35–36\].

### 3 Most Important Improvements

1\. **Development of Regional Mental Health Facilities in Rural Areas (Rural Regional Consortia):** Encourage the consolidation of resources between smaller judicial circuits to ensure that PSMI citizens in rural areas gain equal access to MHCs comparable to urban regions \[11–12\]. 2. **Full Transition to ACA/CountyCare Enrollment Upon MHC Admission:** Ensure that all new participants are immediately enrolled in federal health insurance schemes to guarantee the availability of medication and long-term psychiatric interventions \[31–32\]. 3. **Optimization of the Illinois Mental Health Court Database System:** Integrate a unified data entry system across all Illinois MHCs to monitor clinical outcomes, EBP compliance, and real-time reductions in re-arrest rates \[35–36\].
