## Policy Analysis

- **Background & Policy Problem**:
  - **FACT**: Foreign entries into Japan rose dramatically from ~18,000 in 1950 to a historic record of 31,187,179 in 2019.
  - **FACT**: Overstaying foreign residents grew for six consecutive years, reaching 82,892 individuals as of January 1, 2020.
  - **FACT**: Technical intern trainee abscondments spiked to 8,796 cases in 2019, primarily due to wage issues and intermediary agency misconduct.
  - **FACT**: Unlawful employment (12,816 cases in 2019) and human rights violations by illegal brokers undermined fair labor practices.
- **Policy Objectives**:
  - **FACT**: Facilitate entry of tourists and skilled foreign workers to support Japan's tourism-oriented national strategy and industrial labor demands.
  - **FACT**: Provide legal protections and status stability for technical trainees and victims of human trafficking or domestic violence.
  - **FACT**: Strengthen enforcement against illegal residents and undocumented workers through structured deportation procedures.
- **Key Provisions & Reforms**:
  - **FACT**: **Specified Skilled Worker Status**: Operationalized "Specified Skilled Worker (i)" and "(ii)" categories starting April 1, 2019.
  - **FACT**: **Technical Intern Training Reform**: Established the Organization for Technical Intern Training (OTIT) under Act No. 89 of 2016 on November 1, 2017.
  - **FACT**: **Departure Order System**: Permits qualifying overstayers to leave voluntarily without detention at immigration centers.
  - **FACT**: **Anti-Abscondment Measures**: Ministry of Justice directive (Nov 12, 2019) barred sending and supervising organizations with high abscondment rates from accepting new trainees.
  - **FACT**: **Refugee Protection Framework**: Enforced the 1981 Refugee Convention standards, incorporating provisional stay permits and independent refugee examination counselors.
- **Implementation Schedule & Indicators**:
  - **FACT**: Mid-to-long-term and special permanent residents reached a record high of 2,933,137 individuals (2.32% of Japan's total population) by late 2019.
  - **FACT**: In 2019, 19,386 foreign nationals were processed for deportation, with 9,597 officially deported (8,967 via self-funded voluntary departure).
