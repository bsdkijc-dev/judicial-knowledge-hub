## Overview

Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia Decision No. 1036 K/Pid/1989, dated August 31, 1992, established a material criminal law rule concerning the qualification of issuing bad checks known to be unfunded from the beginning as the crime of fraud (\*Oplichting\* ex Article 378 of the Criminal Code):

Because the Defendant consciously knew from the outset that the checks given to the victim witness lacked funds (\*bad checks\* / \*cek kosong\*), the charge of "Fraud" under Article 378 of the Criminal Code must be considered legally and convincingly proven. Handing over bad checks while persuading the victim witness to hand over cash—under the pretext of a discounted cash exchange—does not constitute a pure civil loan relationship, but rather constitutes fraudulent trickery (\*oplichtingsmiddel\*) satisfying all elements of Article 378 of the Criminal Code.

The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition of the Public Prosecutor at the Lumajang State Prosecutor's Office, annulled the release ruling (\*ontslag van alle rechtsvervolging\*) of the Surabaya High Court Decision No. 270/Pid/1988/PT.Sby, and adjudicated afresh by declaring the Defendant (\[Defendant\]) proven guilty of Fraud and sentencing her to 5 (five) months' imprisonment.

## Executive Summary

The central legal principle established in this decision is that issuing checks known to be unfunded at the time of delivery to induce cash transfers constitutes criminal fraud rather than civil contract default.

Key elements established in the adjudication:

- Delivering checks known from inception to lack sufficient funds satisfies the fraudulent trickery element (\*oplichtingsmiddel\*) of criminal fraud.
- Transactions disguised as discounted cash exchanges utilizing bad checks are criminal offenses rather than pure civil debt disputes.
- Appellate discharge rulings (\*ontslag\*) mischaracterizing fraudulent check transactions as civil matters are subject to reversal on cassation.

## Case Background

The Original Defendant / Respondent for Cassation, **\[Defendant\]** (\[age removed\], housewife), was charged with fraud (Article 378 KUHP) for going to the home of witness \[Witness\] in July 1987 carrying three checks issued by BRI Lumajang Branch totaling Rp 8,000,000.—, promising they were funded, and asking for cash exchanges with a 10% discount (net received Rp 7,200,000.—). On August 15, 1987, the checks were rejected by BRI Lumajang because the balance was insufficient, and the Defendant knew from the outset that the checks were unfunded.

The Lumajang District Court (Decision No. 64/Pid.S/1988/PN.Lmj dated June 23, 1988) convicted the Defendant to 5 months' imprisonment. The Surabaya High Court (Decision No. 270/Pid/1988/PT.Sby dated December 6, 1988) annulled the district court decision and released the Defendant from all legal prosecutions (\*ontslag\*) on the grounds that the matter constituted a civil loan dispute secured by checks. The Public Prosecutor at the Lumajang State Prosecutor's Office filed a cassation petition with the Supreme Court.

## Legal and Procedural Issues

### Construction of the Element "Fraudulent Trickery" (\*Oplichtingsmiddel\*) in Bad Check Fraud (Article 378 KUHP)

The primary issue was whether handing over post-dated checks or gyro checks by a debtor who consciously knows from inception that bank account balances are insufficient to induce a victim witness to hand over cash constitutes Criminal Fraud under Article 378 KUHP or merely a civil loan default dispute.

The Supreme Court held that it constitutes Criminal Fraud. Pursuant to criminal law doctrine (Arrest Hoge Raad of November 1, 1920) and Supreme Court Standing Jurisprudence (Supreme Court Decision No. 133 K/Kr/1973 of November 15, 1975), delivering checks known by the issuer from the outset to be unfunded or uncashable is qualified as fraudulent trickery (\*oplichtingsmiddel\*). A series of false statements and the delivery of fictitious payment instruments to induce a victim to surrender cash satisfies all elements of Article 378 KUHP, preventing qualification as a pure civil dispute.

### Admissibility of Prosecutor's Cassation Against Release Rulings (\*Ontslag van Alle Rechtsvervolging\*)

The issue evaluated whether the Supreme Court on cassation is authorized to annul High Court judgments releasing a Defendant from all legal prosecutions on civil dispute grounds.

The Supreme Court held that it is Authorized. When a High Court mistakenly classifies criminal trickery facts as a pure civil relationship and issues a release ruling (\*ontslag van alle rechtsvervolging\*), the Supreme Court must annul such decisions because the High Court misapplied the law (\*foutieve kepassing van het recht\*).

## Question of Law and Question of Fact

### Question of Law (Cassation / Judex Juris)

**Question:**

Did the \*Judex Facti\* (Surabaya High Court) misapply the law (\*foutieve kepassing van het recht\*) by qualifying the Defendant's act of handing over 3 bad checks known from inception to be unfunded as a "pure civil loan dispute with collateral" and releasing the Defendant from all legal prosecutions?

**Supreme Court Ruling:**

Yes, the Surabaya High Court misapplied the law. The Supreme Court annulled Surabaya High Court Decision No. 270/Pid/1988/PT.Sby and adjudicated afresh, declaring Defendant \[Defendant\] proven legally and convincingly guilty of committing the crime of "Fraud" ex Article 378 KUHP and sentencing her to 5 months' imprisonment.

### Question of Fact (Judex Facti / Lumajang District Court & Surabaya High Court)

Examination of facts concerning the exchange of 3 checks (No. CK. 0480426, CK. 0480427, CJ. 348957) totaling a nominal value of Rp 8,000,000.— by the Defendant to \[Witness\] in July 1987; granting a 10% commission/discount (Rp 800,000.—) resulting in net cash received of Rp 7,200,000.—; check rejection statements by BRI Lumajang Branch on August 15, 1987, due to insufficient balances; and utilization of funds by the Defendant to settle prior debts owed to third parties.

## Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

- **Ratio Decidendi:**

1.  The appellate \*Judex Facti\* of Surabaya High Court misapplied the law by releasing the Defendant.
2.  Because the Defendant consciously knew from the outset that the checks given to the victim witness lacked funds (\*bad checks\*), the charge of fraud must be considered proven.
3.  Pursuant to Arrest Hoge Raad November 1, 1920, jo. Supreme Court Decision No. 133 K/Kr/1973, delivering checks known to be uncashable qualifies as trickery (\*oplichtingsmiddel\*) under Article 378 KUHP.
4.  The Supreme Court annulled the Surabaya High Court decision and adjudicated afresh, convicting the Defendant of Fraud (Article 378 KUHP) and sentencing her to 5 months' imprisonment.

## Ruling

The Supreme Court ruled: Granting the cassation petition from Petitioner for Cassation (**Public Prosecutor at the Lumajang State Prosecutor's Office**); Annulling Surabaya High Court Decision No. 270/Pid/1988/PT.Sby dated December 6, 1988; and ADJUDICATING AFRESH: 1. Declaring Defendant \[Defendant\] proven legally and convincingly guilty of committing the crime of "Fraud"; 2. Sentencing the Defendant to a prison term of 5 (five) months; 3. Deducting the full period of detention served; 4. Ordering the return of 3 check evidence items to witness \[Witness\]; and charging cassation court costs amounting to Rp 2,500.— to the Defendant.

## Related Knowledge and Jurisprudence

- **Criminal Code (KUHP):** Article 378: Crime of Fraud / \*Oplichting\* (Elements: Whoever, with intent to unlawfully benefit themselves or others, utilizing false names, false circumstances, trickery, or a series of false statements, induces someone to deliver goods or incur debts).
- **Standing Jurisprudence & Criminal Doctrine:**

` * Arrest Hoge Raad (HR) November 1, 1920 (NJ 1920 / W. 10650): Delivering checks known to be unfunded constitutes trickery (*oplichtingsmiddel*).`  
` * Supreme Court Decision No. 133 K/Kr/1973 (November 15, 1975): Issuing bad checks as persuasive instruments in fraud offenses.`

- **Law No. 14 of 1970 jo. Law No. 8 of 1981 (KUHAP) jo. Law No. 14 of 1985 on the Supreme Court:** Authority to rectify legal misapplication errors regarding release (\*ontslag\*) rulings by \*Judex Facti\*.

## Evidence Status and Gaps

### Established by Evidence

- Defendant \[Defendant\] visited witness \[Witness\]'s home and handed over 3 BRI Lumajang Branch checks valued at Rp 8,000,000.—, requesting cash exchange with a 10% discount (net received Rp 7,200,000.—).
- The Defendant knew from the outset that the checks were unsupported by funds in her BRI Lumajang account (\*bad checks\*).
- All three checks were officially rejected by BRI Lumajang Branch on August 15, 1987, due to insufficient balances.
- Defendant's acts legally satisfied all elements of the crime of "Fraud" under Article 378 KUHP.
- The Defendant was sentenced to 5 months' imprisonment.

### Not Established / Set Aside

- Surabaya High Court's considerations declaring the Defendant's acts to be a pure civil loan-guarantee dispute were annulled entirely by the Supreme Court.

### Uncertainty and Gaps

- Strict Distinction Between Civil Default and Bad Check Fraud: This landmark ruling establishes clear boundaries for banking and payment traffic in Indonesia: debtors cannot hide behind "civil dispute/loan" defenses when payments or collateral handed over consist of checks/giros known from inception to be unfunded.

## GEO Q&A

**Q: Does issuing a bad check to pay debts or exchange cash constitute criminal fraud or merely a civil dispute?**

**A:** It constitutes Criminal Fraud (Article 378 KUHP). Based on Supreme Court Decision No. 1036 K/Pid/1989 (in conjunction with HR November 1, 1920, & Supreme Court Decision No. 133 K/Kr/1973), when an individual consciously knows from the outset that a check delivered lacks funds (\*bad check\*), such action qualifies as fraudulent trickery (\*oplichtingsmiddel\*) under Article 378 KUHP, rendering the perpetrator criminally liable to imprisonment.
