## Core Definition

The \*\*Technical Procurement & Hardware Standard\*\* is the statutory regulatory and administrative framework governing the acquisition, vendor evaluation, quality control, security vetting, and lifecycle management of all hardware, software licenses, and IT infrastructure procured for the judicial system.

- \*\*Simple Definition:\*\* The official government procurement rulebook that dictates how courts buy computers, servers, and software legally, transparently, and securely from vetted vendors.
- \*\*Legal / Technical Definition:\*\* The institutional integration of national public procurement laws (LKPP regulations) with Electronic-Based Government System (SPBE) mandates, establishing mandatory technical evaluation matrices, security compliance vetting (TPM 2.0, BSSN standards), Domestic Component Level requirements (TKDN), and transparent electronic auction procedures (\*E-Procurement / LSE\*) for judicial IT assets.
- \*\*Plain-Language Explanation: **It acts as the legal and administrative gatekeeper for all technology purchases in the court system, ensuring that every server, scanner, and software package bought with state funds meets strict technical, security, and anti-corruption standards.**

## Why It Matters

- \*\*Prevents Public Procurement Fraud:\*\* Enforces transparent electronic bidding (\*E-Tendering / LKPP\*) and objective technical evaluation matrices, eliminating informal vendor steering and corruption.
- \*\*Ensures Strict Technical Compatibility:\*\* Prevents the purchase of incompatible or low-grade commercial hardware that cannot integrate with central SIPP and e-Court databases.
- \*\*Enforces National Security & TKDN Compliance:\*\* Mandates adherence to domestic industrial component requirements (TKDN) and rigorous BSSN security vetting before any IT asset enters court networks.
- \*\*Guarantees Full Lifecycle Accountability:\*\* Establishes clear warranty, maintenance, replacement, and secure decommissioning (\*sanitization/destruction\*) protocols for retired court hardware.

## Key Principles

Principle of Transparency and Open Public Competition (Asas Transparansi dan Persaingan Usaha Sehat)  

- \*\*Meaning:\*\* All technology and hardware acquisitions must be conducted through open, competitive electronic procurement systems accessible to verified vendors without discriminatory barriers.
- \*\*Why It Matters:\*\* Secures best-value pricing and upholds administrative accountability in the use of state judicial budgets.
- \*\*Legal Basis / Source:\*\* Presidential Regulation No. 16/2018 jo. Presidential Regulation No. 12/2021 on Government Procurement (LKPP); Law No. 48/2009.
- \*\*Related Concepts:\*\* E-Procurement, fair competition, transparent bidding.

Principle of Mandatory Security Vetting and Compliance (Asas Pemenuhan Syarat Keamanan Mutlak)  

- \*\*Meaning:\*\* No hardware or software license may be procured or deployed unless it passes mandatory technical security vetting, including compatibility with TPM 2.0, BSSN crypto standards, and UU PDP data protection rules.
- \*\*Why It Matters:\*\* Prevents compromised or insecure supply-chain hardware from entering critical judicial IT networks.
- \*\*Legal Basis / Source:\*\* Presidential Regulation No. 95/2018 (SPBE); BSSN Supply Chain Security Guidelines.
- \*\*Related Concepts:\*\* Supply-chain security, hardware vetting, cryptographic compliance.

Principle of Domestic Component Preference (Asas Peningkatan Penggunaan Produk Dalam Negeri / TKDN)  

- \*\*Meaning:\*\* Procurement tenders must prioritize domestic industrial products meeting national TKDN (Tingkat Komponen Dalam Negeri) statutory thresholds whenever manufactured locally at competitive standards.
- \*\*Why It Matters:\*\* Supports national economic development and ensures local vendor support availability for hardware maintenance.
- \*\*Legal Basis / Source:\*\* Law No. 3/2014 on Industry; LKPP Regulation on Domestic Preference.
- \*\*Related Concepts:\*\* TKDN compliance, domestic manufacturing, local technical support.

## Indonesia Section

- \*\*Procurement Governance:\*\* Managed collaboratively by the Supreme Court Bureau of Logistics and Equipment (\*Biro Perlengkapan\*) alongside the Bureau of Information Technology, adhering strictly to National Public Procurement Agency (\*LKPP\*) electronic catalogs and e-tendering platforms (\*LPSE\*).
- \*\*The Four-Stage Procurement Lifecycle:\*\*

1\. \*Planning & Needs Analysis:\* Matching hardware upgrade requests against the Supreme Court Strategic Plan (Renstra) and Master Technological Infrastructure Standard. 2. \*Electronic Tendering (LPSE):\* Publishing technical specifications, minimum performance benchmarks, and TKDN requirements on the national public procurement portal. 3. \*Technical & Security Evaluation:\* Inspecting vendor samples for enterprise ECC memory, TPM 2.0 chips, and BSSN-compliant encryption support before awarding contracts. 4. \*Asset Registration & Disposal:\* Logging hardware assets into the state inventory system (\*SIMAN\*) and enforcing secure cryptographic data wiping (\*DoD 5220.22-M\* or physical destruction) upon equipment retirement.

- \*\*Nomenclature:\*\* Standar Pengadaan Teknis dan Perangkat Keras — \*Technical Procurement & Hardware Standard\*; Pengadaan Secara Elektronik Mahkamah Agung — \*Supreme Court E-Procurement\*.

` * *Issuing Body:* Mahkamah Agung RI (Office of the Secretary General)`  
` * *Instrument Reference:* Mandatory IT Supply-Chain Vetting & TKDN Compliance Circular`  
` * *Legal Principle:* All IT hardware acquisitions must include documented proof of secure supply-chain origin, compliance with BSSN hardware security directives, and verification of local maintenance support capacity.`  
` * *Why It Matters:* Eliminates untrusted hardware components and guarantees rapid repair response times for regional courts.`  
` * *Official Source:* Mahkamah Agung Administrative Bulletins.`

## Debates & Controversies

- Debate: Lowest-Cost Bidding vs. High-Security Enterprise Quality

` * *Position A (Pro-Lowest Cost Bidding):* Public procurement law requires fiscal discipline, meaning state agencies must award tenders to the lowest compliant bidder to protect public funds.`  
` * *Position B (Security-First Procurement):* Critical judicial infrastructure cannot be treated like ordinary office supplies; prioritizing lowest cost risks acquiring inferior hardware vulnerable to supply-chain backdoors.`  
` * *Evidence:* Historical challenges in balancing public procurement budget limits with advanced cybersecurity requirements.`  
` * *Current Status:* Resolved through "Two-Envelope" and weighted scoring systems: technical security compliance and TKDN thresholds must be fully satisfied before financial price bids are evaluated.`

## Rule-of-Law Connection

- \*Fiscal Accountability:\* Transparent e-procurement prevents corruption, bribery, and embezzlement in judicial budgeting.
- \*Supply-Chain Integrity:\* Vetting hardware origins ensures court networks remain secure against foreign or malicious hardware tampering.
- \*Equal Market Access:\* Transparent tenders allow qualified domestic technology providers fair opportunities to compete for state contracts.

## AI & Future of Law

- \*AI-Driven Fraud Detection in Bidding:\* Utilizing machine learning algorithms on LKPP tender logs to detect bid-rigging, collusive bidding rings, and abnormal pricing anomalies.
- \*Automated Specification Matching:\* AI tools cross-referencing vendor proposals against the Master Hardware & Infrastructure Specification to instantly flag non-compliant bids.
- \*Lifecycle Predictive Analytics:\* AI tracking of hardware failure rates and maintenance tickets to optimize future procurement budgeting cycles.

## Common Misconceptions

- \*Myth:\* A court unit can directly purchase computers and servers from any local electronics store using petty cash.

` * *Reality:* All state IT hardware acquisitions must comply with strict public procurement laws (LKPP), electronic catalog pricing, and centralized IT bureau technical approvals.`  
` * *Source:* State Financial Accountability Rules; LKPP Regulations.`

- \*Myth:\* TKDN domestic component rules apply only to physical furniture and construction, not to computer servers.

` * *Reality:* National TKDN industrial policies apply increasingly to electronic devices, telecommunication equipment, and assembling servers within domestic facilities.`  
` * *Source:* Ministry of Industry TKDN Guidelines.`

## Practical Example

- \*Scenario:\* A regional high court attempts to procure low-cost imported server racks from an unverified foreign vendor bypassing the national e-catalog.
- \*Question:\* How does the Technical Procurement & Hardware Standard handle the acquisition?
- \*Applicable Principle:\* Mandatory Vetting & Transparent Procurement (Pres. Reg. 12/2021; SPBE Standards).
- \*Analysis:\*

` 1. The Supreme Court's internal audit and procurement bureau reject the proposal for failing to use approved LKPP electronic catalog channels.`  
` 2. The hardware fails to provide certification for TPM 2.0 security chips and BSSN compliance.`  
` 3. The tender is cancelled and re-advertised through compliant e-procurement channels with verified domestic and security credentials.`

- \*Limitation:\* Compliance enforcement relies on active internal administrative audits and transparent reporting by court procurement committees.
