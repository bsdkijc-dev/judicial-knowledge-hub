# 'International Journal for Court Administration, Vol. 7, No. 1' (IACA, 2015)

### Wiki Entity Page

**International Journal for Court Administration (Vol. 7, No. 1, Source File: 18-83-PB.pdf)** is the 14th official peer-reviewed electronic periodical publication of the *International Association for Court Administration* (IACA) published on the official IACA website (www.iaca.ws). This issue features an executive editorial on Tunisia's democratic judicial transition, an editor's column on cultural diversity in courts, 1 professional case study on NextGen CM/ECF in the US, 6 comparative academic research articles, and 3 book reviews.

## Summary

This issue is led by Markus B. Zimmer (Executive Editor), Prof. Dr. Philip M. Langbroek (Managing Editor), Linda Wade-Bahr (Technical Editor), alongside Prof. Anne Wallace, Prof. Dr. Andreas Lienhard, Dr. Luis Maria Palma, and Leisha Lister (Journal Editors). The publication presents empirical and practical analyses regarding US federal e-filing modernization, sociological analysis of judges on Facebook, combining weighted caseload and organizational analysis in Switzerland, evaluating the impact of the Troika MoU on civil enforcement in Portugal, mental health courts in Illinois, the role of the EJTN in European judicial training, Romanian judges' attitudes toward vocational training, and book reviews on Asian courts and CEPEJ.

## Background & Key Article Summaries

### 1. Executive Editorial: Tunisian Judicial Transition (Markus B. Zimmer)

- Reviews the history of the Tunisian judiciary from the Ottoman era, the French protectorate (Directorate of Judicial Services 1896), the Bourguiba/Ben Ali era, the 2010 revolution (Mohammed Bouazizi incident), to the 2014 Constitution guaranteeing judicial independence \[7–9\].

### 2. Editor's Column: Cultural Diversity in Courts (Anne Wallace)

- Analyzes communication challenges, interpreter services, unconscious bias (e.g., eye contact), and the establishment of the *Judicial Council on Cultural Diversity* in Australia \[10–12\].

### 3. US Federal NextGen CM/ECF Case Study (John Brinkema & J. Michael Greenwood)

- Compares the legacy CM/ECF system (operating since 1996, managing 47 million cases and 600+ million PDF documents) with NextGen CM/ECF (10,000+ requirements, Central Sign-On / CSO via JENIE, internal Calendar module, Judge Review Packets, Workspace dashboard, NIEM XML standards, cloud computing, and SOA).

### 4. Judges on Facebook & Judicial Rituals (Paul van den Hoven)

- Uses Erving Goffman's theory (front stage/backstage), Actor-Network Theory (Latour), and Pierre Bourdieu (force du droit) to explain why posting judgments on Facebook clashes with the formal, unimodal, and punctualized official self-representation of civil law judges (*ritual of universalization*) \[68, 70, 75–78, 108–111\].

### 5. Weighted Caseload & Swiss Court Organizational Analysis (Lienhard et al.)

- An empirical study in the Canton of Basel-Stadt that combines quantitative weighted caseload time-measurements with qualitative organizational analysis (structure, potential, culture) based on CAF, ISO 9001, TCPS, CourTools, and CEPEJ standards.

### 6. Evaluation of the Troika MoU Experiment in Portugal (Correia & Videira)

- Empirical evaluation of 217 Portuguese county courts (78-month sample, 2008–2014). Mann-Whitney statistical tests proved an increase in the clearance rate from 71.2% to 116.4% (\$p=0.000\$), a reduction in disposition time by **743 days** (2+ years, \$p=0.000\$), and a decrease in the case backlog by more than **210,000 cases**.

### 7. Mental Health Courts / MHCs in Illinois (Arthur J. Lurigio et al.)

- Evaluation of 9 MHCs in Illinois based on 10 Essential Elements of MHCs, multi-disciplinary teamwork (judge, prosecutor, public defender, probation, TASC), evidence-based practices (CBT, motivational interviewing), and a 4-year recidivism analysis \[204, 219–222, 230, 232\].

### 8. The Role of the European Judicial Training Network / EJTN (Simone Benvenuti)

- Analyzes EJTN governance based on 4 pillars (cooperation, decentralization, complementarity, *European Added Value* / EAV), the Aiakos exchange program, and national court coordinators (Eurinfra, European Gaius).

### 9. Romanian Judges' Sentencing Learning (Diana Richards)

- Survey of 510 Romanian judges and prosecutors (National Institute of Magistracy - NIM). Found that 93% considered initial training useful, novice judges highly prioritized court internships, while senior judges needed legal interpretation discussions and Q&A sessions.

### 10. Book Reviews (Ingo Keilitz & Markus Zimmer)

- Reviews *The Solution Revolution* (Eggers & Macmillan; Liberia court monitoring example by Al Varney), the 2014 CEPEJ Report (45 Council of Europe countries), and *Asian Courts in Context* (14 Asian judicial systems).

## Policy Problems

1\. Limitations of legacy IT systems and the need for Single Sign-On across jurisdictions. 2. Conflicts between social media transparency and the necessity of formal judicial rituals. 3. The danger of weighted caseload studies that fail to verify internal organizational efficiency. 4. A crisis of civil debt enforcement backlogs due to economic crises. 5. The criminalization of individuals with severe mental illness due to a lack of community mental health facilities. 6. Resistance to the centralization of judicial training at the European Union level.

## Reform / Change

1\. Adoption of centralized IT architecture NextGen CM/ECF based on cloud and SOA. 2. Implementation of electronic bank account freezing by the Bank of Portugal under CPC 2013. 3. Adoption of problem-solving courts based on therapeutic jurisprudence. 4. Institutionalization of a European judicial training framework based on European Added Value.
