# Judicial-policy-multirational-court-management-2013

**The Management Responses to Multiple Rationalities in Courts Framework** is a policy framework and systematic literature review that analyzes the phenomenon of multirationality—the coexistence of various conflicting logics/rationalities (such as legal rationality, economic/managerial rationality, and political rationality) within the court environment. It classifies four forms of managerial responses (isolation, avoidance, tolerance, competence) based on the pillars of New Institutionalism and the *Basic Research into Court Management in Switzerland* study \[1, 3, 4, 15, 58–60\].

## Summary

Published by Angela Eicher and Prof. Dr. Kuno Schedler in the *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA), this policy framework dissects the complexity of court organizations that harbor multiple operational logics (multiple rationalities). Supported by the *Swiss National Science Foundation* (SNSF), this study bridges court administration literature with *New Institutionalism* theory. The framework identifies two main research streams (actor relationships and local legal culture) and formulates a matrix of four managerial practices to address the tensions between the legal rationality of judges and the economic rationality of court managers \[5, 6, 19–22, 35–37, 58–60\].

## Background

Today's courts are faced with a dual pressure: surging workloads and budget constraints on one side, and demands for public accountability on the other. Judicial reforms and New Public Management (NPM) movements since the 1990s introduced new non-legal actors into the courts, triggering conflicts due to differences in mindsets and objectives. Eicher and Schedler conducted a systematic literature review (1971–2012) to develop management strategies capable of integrating these diverse logics without disrupting the function of justice enforcement \[1, 8–10, 18\].

## Objectives

1\. Map the coexistence of various rationalities (legal, economic, political) within the judicial environment \[1, 4, 15, 35–37\]. 2. Integrate the concepts of *New Institutionalism* and *Institutional Logics* into court administration studies. 3. Provide a taxonomy of four managerial responses (isolation, avoidance, tolerance, competence) for court leadership \[58–60\]. 4. Overcome internal conflicts, communication barriers, and decision-making blockades in courts.

## Scope

This policy evaluates international court administration literature (focusing on comparative studies in the United States and Switzerland) covering the interactions between Chief Justices, Associate Judges, Court Administrators, Clerks, Judicial Secretaries, and ICT Staff \[8–10, 35–41, 45, 46, 66\].

## Key Provisions

- **Two Main Research Streams of Multirationality:**
  - *Stream 1 (Perceptions and Actor Relationships):* Analyzes the differences in attitudes, goals, and sources of conflict among judges, clerks, and court managers.
  - *Stream 2 (Local Legal Culture):* Analyzes the influence of four cultural archetypes (communal, autonomous, networked, hierarchical) and value incongruence across work areas \[6, 19, 21, 48–50\].
- **Clash of Legal vs. Economic Rationality:** Judges focus on impartial justice on a case-by-case basis (slow and detailed), whereas managers focus on system efficiency, cost reduction, and time management (fast and long-term) \[35–38\].
- **Matrix of Four Managerial Response Practices (Schedler 2012):**
  - *Isolation:* Conscious-monorational handling by restricting the authority of the new rationality \[58–60\].
  - *Avoidance:* Unconscious-monorational handling through the implicit dominance of a single logic \[58–60\].
  - *Tolerance:* Unconscious-multirational handling via intuitive mediation \[58–60\].
  - *Competence:* Conscious-multirational handling by strategically integrating a diversity of logics \[58–60\].

## Implementation

- **Application of Institutional Isomorphism (DiMaggio & Powell):** Explains how political pressure (coercive isomorphism) and professionalization (normative isomorphism) force courts to adopt modern managerial structures.
- **Cessation of Isolation Practices:** Encouraging leading judges to transition from *isolation* practices toward *competence* practices when managing professional court administrators \[58–60, 63, 68\].

## Policy Problems

- **Turf Struggles:** The tendency of judges to maintain full control over non-judicial tasks because they perceive professional managers as a threat to their autonomy \[40–43, 59\].
- **Clerk Skepticism:** The fear among traditional clerks of losing influence due to the presence of modern court managers.

## Reform / Change

- Institutionalization of multirational management training for Chief Judges and Court Managers.
- Adoption of successful mechanisms from hospital management, such as the clear separation between "juridical decisions" and "administrative decisions."
