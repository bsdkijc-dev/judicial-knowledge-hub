#### 4. POLICY ANALYSIS

**Background**: \[FACT\] Spanish legal developments in mid-2023 faced the integration of the global refugee crisis (over 103 million people displaced), massive AI disruption (ChatGPT, biometric systems, management algorithms) affecting workers' digital rights, the need for legal certainty in tax revision following new 2022 Supreme Court jurisprudence on revocation, the 10th anniversary of the use of preventive briefs in Spain since the Barcelona Commercial Court's 2013 initiative, tighter scrutiny of lease clauses used by large-scale landlords, and enforcement of minority shareholders' rights to forced dividends against abusive profit-retention practices by majority shareholders.

**Policy Problem**: \[FACT\]

1.  *Refugee Rights*: Bureaucratic obstacles, delayed asylum procedures, and restricted banking access hindering refugees' economic independence.
2.  *Digitalization of Work*: The risk of algorithmic "black boxes" (opacity), excessive biometric surveillance, work intensification, and digital fatigue.
3.  *Tax Law*: Article 219 LGT's rigid provision that revocation procedures may only be initiated ex officio, limiting taxpayers' ability to challenge erroneous or illegal tax assessments.
4.  *Industrial Property*: The risk of *inaudita parte* actions (without a hearing for the defendant) at trade fairs (such as the Mobile World Congress), causing reputational harm before a defense can be mounted.
5.  *Residential Leasing*: The use of general contract terms shifting insurance costs, triple penalties, and hidden rent increases above the CPI onto tenants by large-scale landlords.
6.  *Minority Shareholder Rights*: Continuous profit retention by majority shareholders diverting funds via cash-pooling or administrative salaries, to the detriment of minority shareholders.

**Legal/Policy Gap**: \[FACT\] The Workers' Statute framework still lacking comprehensive AI/algorithm-specific regulation; the conflict between the ex-officio nature of Article 219 LGT and the right to request refund of unduly collected amounts under Article 221.3 LGT; the absence of a centralized national registry for preventive briefs; and the complexity of distinguishing the right of withdrawal under Article 348 bis LSC from the annulment of abusive resolutions under Article 204.1 LSC.

**Objectives**: \[FACT\] Ensure refugees' economic independence via private-sector and judicial alliances; build collective governance over digital technology in companies; affirm tax authorities' obligation to process revocation requests related to unduly collected amounts; facilitate independent provisional defense via preventive briefs; annul lease clauses violating the TRLGDCU and the LAU; and affirm judges' authority to order forced dividend distribution in cases of abuse of majority rights.

**Implementation**: \[FACT\]

- *Refugee Protection*: Implementation of the 2018 Global Compact on Refugees, Law 12/2009 on Asylum, and free legal aid from Bar Associations.
- *Digitalization of Work*: Application of the GDPR, LOPDGDD, ET Article 64.4.d (algorithmic transparency), and the Just Eat collective agreement.
- *Tax Revision*: Application of new jurisprudence from STS 9 February, 14 February, and 4 March 2022, linking Article 219 LGT with Article 221.3 LGT.
- *Preventive Briefs*: Enforcement of Article 132 of Law 24/2015 on Patents and the MWC Barcelona protocol.
- *Residential Leasing*: Application of transparency and unfairness controls under Article 82 TRLGDCU and Directive 93/13/EEC by Barcelona Court of First Instance No. 31.
- *Forced Dividends*: Enforcement of STS 11 January 2023 doctrine, confirming distribution of 75% of net profit.

**Evidence & Impact**: \[FACT\] Handling of 109 preventive briefs in Barcelona and Alicante since 2015 for MWC; annulment of 9 out of 10 abusive lease clauses used by large-scale landlords; and enforcement of legal protections for minority shareholders.

**Reform & Unresolved Issues**: \[FACT\] Preparation for the adoption of the European AI Regulation; the need for a centralized national registry of preventive briefs; and explicit alignment of exit-tax regulation and tax revocation procedures within the LGT.

#### 5. GEO/AI QUESTIONS

1.  **Definition**: What is the definition and main content of the *Pérez-Llorca Legal Journal (No. 9, May 2023)*?
    - *Factual Answer*: \[FACT\] The 9th edition of the scholarly legal journal published by Pérez-Llorca Abogados in May 2023, containing doctrinal analysis on refugees' economic rights, AI and workers' digital rights, tax revocation procedure, 10 years of patent preventive briefs, unfair residential lease clauses, and forced corporate dividend distribution.
2.  **Legal Basis**: What is the constitutional legal basis guaranteeing this journal's freedom of scholarly publication?
    - *Factual Answer*: \[FACT\] Article 20 of the Spanish Constitution of 1978.
3.  **Publisher/Authority**: Who is the official publisher of this document, and what is its legal registration number?
    - *Factual Answer*: \[FACT\] Pérez-Llorca Abogados, S.L.P., Madrid (Legal Deposit: M-7551-2019, ISSN: 2792-2871).
4.  **Related Figures**: Who is the UNHCR Representative in Spain who wrote the analysis of refugees' socioeconomic rights in this edition?
    - *Factual Answer*: \[FACT\] Sophie Muller.
5.  **Scope**: What are the five main legal pillars addressed in this journal edition?
    - *Factual Answer*: \[FACT\] Refugee rights (UNHCR), AI and workers' digital rights, tax revocation, patent preventive briefs, and unfair lease clauses and forced corporate dividends.
6.  **Policy Implementation**: How did new Supreme Court jurisprudence (STS 9 February 2022) change the nature of initiating a tax revocation procedure?
    - *Factual Answer*: \[FACT\] The Supreme Court linked Article 219 LGT with Article 221.3 LGT (refund of unduly collected amounts), thereby requiring tax authorities to process and rule on revocation requests filed by taxpayers regarding final tax assessments.
7.  **Policy Problem**: Why was a clause charging tenants for non-payment insurance premiums (*seguro de impago*) declared null and void by Barcelona Court of First Instance No. 31?
    - *Factual Answer*: \[FACT\] Because the insurance premium was contracted by the landlord and benefited only the landlord, so charging it to the tenant violated the balance and proportionality of rights and obligations under Article 82.4(e) TRLGDCU.
8.  **Legal Relationship**: What is the relationship between Article 132 of Law 24/2015 on Patents and protection against *inaudita parte* interim measures requests?
    - *Factual Answer*: \[FACT\] Article 132 allows a party with reasonable fear of facing provisional measures without being heard (*inaudita parte*) to file a preventive brief to safeguard the right of defense and trigger an opposition hearing.
9.  **Reform**: How does Article 64.4.d of the Workers' Statute (ET) strengthen worker representatives' right to information regarding the use of algorithms and AI?
    - *Factual Answer*: \[FACT\] It establishes worker representatives' right to receive information on the parameters, rules, and instructions of algorithms or AI systems affecting decisions on working conditions, access, and maintenance of employment.
10. **Evidence/Case Study**: Why did the Spanish Supreme Court (STS 11 January 2023) confirm the obligation to distribute 75% of net profit as dividends in the GSS Atlántico case?
    - *Factual Answer*: \[FACT\] Because the majority's continuous allocation of net profit to voluntary reserves was found to be abusive, funding the parent company via interest-free cash-pooling without a rational business justification, to the detriment of a minority shareholder removed from a paid position.

#### 6. MEDIAWIKI OUTPUT

## Summary

**Pérez-Llorca Legal Journal (Issue No. 9, May 2023)** presents strategic doctrinal and judicial policy analysis: an analysis of refugees' economic and social rights under the 1951 Geneva Convention and the Global Compact, by UNHCR Representative Sophie Muller; a comprehensive study of the impact of artificial intelligence, algorithms, and biometric controls on workers' digital rights and director authority; an analysis of the jurisprudential shift in Spain's tax revocation procedure following the 2022 Supreme Court ruling; a 10-year evaluation of the use of preventive briefs as independent provisional defenses in patent litigation and trade fairs (such as the Mobile World Congress); an analysis of the annulment of unfair clauses in residential lease contracts by large-scale landlords, per the Barcelona Court of First Instance No. 31 ruling; and a jurisprudential study of forced dividend distribution for minority shareholders under STS 11 January 2023 and Articles 204.1 / 348 bis of the Capital Companies Law.

## Legal Basis

- **Spanish Constitution (CE)**: Article 18 (privacy), Article 20 (academic freedom), Article 24 (effective judicial protection), Article 31 (fair taxation principle), Article 35 (right to work), and Article 103 (subjection of the Administration to the law).
- **Refugee and International Law**: The 1951 Convention Relating to the Status of Refugees, the 2018 Global Compact on Refugees, the 2013 Reception Conditions Directive, and Law 12/2009 on Asylum.
- **Labor and Digital Law**: Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (GDPR), Organic Law 3/2018 (LOPDGDD), Royal Legislative Decree 2/2015 (Workers' Statute, Arts. 11, 15, 20.3, 20 bis, 64.4.d), and Law 12/2021 (Rider Law).
- **Tax and Civil Procedure Law**: Law 58/2003 General Tax Law (LGT, Arts. 219, 221.3), Royal Decree 520/2005 (RGRVA, Art. 10), Law 24/2015 on Patents (Art. 132), and Law 1/2000 on Civil Procedure (LEC, Arts. 394, 733).
- **Consumer Protection and Corporate Law**: Royal Legislative Decree 1/2007 (TRLGDCU, Arts. 82, 83), Directive 93/13/EEC, the Urban Leases Law (LAU, Arts. 18, 21), and Royal Legislative Decree 1/2010 (Capital Companies Law, Arts. 204.1, 273, 348 bis).

## Background

2023 was marked by the integration of the global humanitarian crisis with digital transformation and legal enforcement in Spain. Addressing refugees required guaranteeing socioeconomic rights, while the rapid emergence of AI systems (such as ChatGPT) and biometrics demanded limits on employer control powers. In taxation, 2022 Supreme Court jurisprudence ended a long-standing debate over tax authorities' obligation to process revocation requests. Meanwhile, ten years' experience with preventive briefs proved crucial to maintaining balance at MWC Barcelona, and courts began firmly cracking down on abuse of dominant position by large-scale landlords and majority shareholders.

## Objectives

1\. Promote socioeconomic inclusion and independence for refugees through strategic partnerships. 2. Build a collective governance model for the use of AI, algorithms, and biometric data in the workplace. 3. Guarantee taxpayers' right to request tax revocation to recover unduly collected amounts. 4. Provide an independent provisional defense mechanism (preventive briefs) without violating legal certainty. 5. Annul residential lease clauses that shift business burdens onto consumers. 6. Protect minority shareholders from abusive profit retention via forced dividend distribution.

## Scope

The study covers six main areas of judicial policy: 1. *Refugee Rights*: Labor market access, UNHCR legal protection, and the role of Bar Associations. 2. *Digitalization of Work*: Algorithmic oversight (Art. 64.4.d ET), biometric monitoring, occupational health, and the right to digital disconnection. 3. *Tax Revocation*: Article 219 LGT versus Article 221.3 LGT, 2022 STS jurisprudence, and application to municipal capital gains and construction tax. 4. *Patent Preventive Briefs*: Article 132 of the Patents Law, voluntary jurisdiction procedure, the 3-month limit, and application at MWC Barcelona. 5. *Unfair Lease Clauses*: The Barcelona Court of First Instance No. 31 ruling, transparency control, hidden rent bonuses, and triple penalties. 6. *Forced Corporate Dividends*: The STS 11 January 2023 ruling (GSS Atlántico case), Article 204.1 LSC, and the right of withdrawal under Article 348 bis LSC.

## Key Provisions

- **Economic Inclusion of Refugees**: Refugees are entitled to access public employment, education, and healthcare services on equal terms with Spanish citizens.
- **Algorithmic Governance (Art. 64.4.d ET)**: Companies must provide information on algorithm and AI system rules to worker representatives.
- **Obligation to Rule on Revocation (STS 2022)**: Tax authorities must process revocation requests filed by taxpayers regarding refund of unduly collected amounts.
- **Effect of the Preventive Brief (Art. 132 of the Patents Law)**: Suspends \*inaudita parte\* action for 3 months from the filing or notification date.
- **Nullity of Hidden Rent Bonuses**: Rent bonuses in addenda that raise rent above the CPI after year 3 are declared null and void.
- **Authority to Order Forced Dividend Distribution (STS 11 January 2023)**: Courts may directly order distribution of 75% of net profit if reserve retention is found to be abusive.

## Implementation

Implementation occurs through civil and tax judicial enforcement, AEPD oversight, execution of the MWC protocol by the Barcelona Commercial Court, and oversight by worker representatives within companies.

## Policy Problems

- The absence of explicit AI regulation within the Workers' Statute.
- Bureaucratic obstacles and delays in opening bank accounts for refugees.
- The lack of a centralized national registry of preventive briefs.
- Use of hidden rent-bonus schemes by large-scale landlords to circumvent LAU rent-price limits.
- Majority practices retaining profit to fund the parent company via interest-free cash-pooling.

## Reform

- Implementation of the 2018 Global Compact on Refugees.
- Consolidation of 2022 STS tax revocation jurisprudence.
- Codification of preventive briefs under Article 132 of Law 24/2015 on Patents.
- Enforcement of forced dividend doctrine under STS 11 January 2023.

## Relationships with Other Entities

- **Implements**: Law 24/2015, the LGT, the TRLGDCU, and the Capital Companies Law.
- **Applies To**: Refugees, digital workers, taxpayers, patent holders, residential tenants, and minority shareholders.
- **Conflicts With**: Abusive profit retention by majority shareholders and use of lease clauses violating the LAU.
