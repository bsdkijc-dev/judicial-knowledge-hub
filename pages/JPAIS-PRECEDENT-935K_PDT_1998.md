## Overview

Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia Decision No. 935 K/Pdt/1998, dated December 21, 1999, established a fundamental civil procedural law rule concerning the evidentiary force of Supplementary Oaths (\*Sumpah Suppletoir\*) and the limits of judicial review authority over them at appellate and cassation levels:

Additional documentary evidence cannot invalidate the evidentiary weight of a supplementary oath (\*sumpah suppletoir\*) taken and sworn by a Plaintiff before a District Court, because a supplementary oath executed in this manner possesses perfect evidentiary force (\*volledig bewijskracht\*) and is not subject to re-examination or re-evaluation in appellate or cassation proceedings. Consequently, the out-of-wedlock biological relationship between the Plaintiff and Defendant resulting in the birth of a child was declared lawfully proven, and the Defendant's refusal to provide maintenance/housing was qualified as an Unlawful Act (\*Onrechtmatige Daad\* ex Article 1365 Civil Code).

## Executive Summary

The central procedural principle established in this decision is that once a trial court properly orders and administers a supplementary oath (\*sumpah suppletoir\*), its conclusive evidentiary effect cannot be undone by appellate courts relying on post-trial documentary challenges.

Key elements established in the adjudication:

- Supplementary oaths ordered to complete incomplete proof carry binding evidentiary force.
- Appellate and cassation tribunals lack authority to re-weigh or overturn sworn supplementary oaths using secondary documents.
- Fathers denying out-of-wedlock biological children and refusing support commit actionable unlawful torts requiring financial reparation.

## Case Background

The Original Plaintiff / Petitioner for Cassation (**Ny. \[Plaintiff\]**, acting for herself and her child **\[Child\]**) filed a lawsuit at the South Jakarta District Court against the Original Defendant / Respondent for Cassation (**\[Defendant\]**). The Plaintiff sought accountability from the Defendant for the birth of a daughter, \[Child\] (born \[details removed\]), resulting from an out-of-wedlock biological relationship, and requested housing and maintenance damages.

The South Jakarta District Court (Decision No. 349/Pdt.G/1995/PN.Jkt.Sel dated March 26, 1996) ordered the Plaintiff to take a supplementary oath (\*sumpah suppletoir\*), and upon administration, granted the Plaintiff's lawsuit in part, declaring \[Child\] to be the biological child of the Defendant, finding the Defendant liable for unlawful acts, and ordering the Defendant to provide decent housing worth Rp 161,000,000.—. The Jakarta High Court (Decision No. 519/Pdt/1996/PT.DKI dated October 31, 1996) annulled the district court decision based on documentary evidence T.4. The Petitioner for Cassation subsequently filed a cassation petition with the Supreme Court.

## Legal and Procedural Issues

### Status and Evidentiary Value of Supplementary Oaths (\*Sumpah Suppletoir\* ex Article 155 HIR / Article 182 RBg / Article 1940 Civil Code) in Appellate and Cassation Review

The primary issue was whether a High Court on appeal possesses authority to annul or undermine the evidentiary value of a Supplementary Oath (\*Sumpah Suppletoir\*) administered to and sworn by a Plaintiff before the District Court trial using additional documentary evidence (photocopies) submitted by the Defendant.

The Supreme Court held that it lacks authority. A supplementary oath (\*sumpah suppletoir\*) ordered by a Trial Judge to complete preliminary proof that remains imperfect (\*onvolledig bewijs\*) possesses binding evidentiary force upon the judge. Once a supplementary oath is lawfully sworn by the burdened party before the court, that oath is immune to re-examination or re-evaluation in appellate and cassation proceedings. Additional documentary evidence submitted on appeal cannot serve as grounds to invalidate the truth sworn under oath.

### Unlawful Acts (\*Onrechtmatige Daad\* ex Article 1365 Civil Code) for Breach of Paternal Duties Toward Out-of-Wedlock Biological Children

A biological father who refuses to recognize, protect, and provide decent housing and maintenance for his out-of-wedlock biological child commits an Unlawful Act (\*Onrechtmatige Daad\*) inflicting moral and material damages upon the mother and child.

## Question of Law and Question of Fact

### Question of Law (Cassation / Judex Juris)

**Question:**

Did the Jakarta High Court misapply the law of evidence (\*foutieve kepassing van het bewijsrecht\*) by setting aside legal facts perfectly proven via a Supplementary Oath sworn by the Plaintiff at the South Jakarta District Court?

**Supreme Court Ruling:**

Yes, the Jakarta High Court misapplied the law of evidence. The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition from Petitioner for Cassation Ny. \[Plaintiff\], annulled Jakarta High Court Decision No. 519/Pdt/1996/PT.DKI, and adjudicated afresh by affirming South Jakarta District Court Decision No. 349/Pdt.G/1995/PN.Jkt.Sel.

### Question of Fact (Judex Facti / South Jakarta District Court & Jakarta High Court)

Examination of facts concerning intimate out-of-wedlock relationships from 1973 to 1985; birth of a female child named \[Child\] on December 30, 1985; administration of Ny. \[Plaintiff\]'s supplementary oath at the South Jakarta District Court trial on February 15, 1996; existence of exhibit T.4 (letter dated April 27, 1983); and determination of decent housing damages amounting to Rp 161,000,000.—.

## Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

- **Ratio Decidendi:**

1.  The appellate \*Judex Facti\* of Jakarta High Court misapplied the law of evidence. Additional documentary evidence T.4 submitted by the Defendant on appeal could not invalidate the supplementary oath administered to and sworn by the original Plaintiff before trial court sessions, because supplementary oaths are immune to appellate or cassation review.
2.  Furthermore, exhibit T.4 failed to prove that subsequent to its drafting, the original Plaintiff and original Defendant never engaged in biological relations.
3.  The Supreme Court granted the Plaintiff's cassation petition, annulled the Jakarta High Court decision, and adjudicated afresh by affirming the South Jakarta District Court ruling.

## Ruling

The Supreme Court ruled: Granting the cassation petition from Petitioner for Cassation (**NY. \[Plaintiff\]**); Annulling Jakarta High Court Decision No. 519/Pdt/1996/PT.DKI dated October 31, 1996, which had annulled South Jakarta District Court Decision No. 349/Pdt.G/1995/PN.Jkt.Sel. dated March 26, 1996; and ADJUDICATING AFRESH: Under Exceptions: Rejecting the Defendant's exception; Under Provision: Rejecting the Plaintiff's provisional claims; Under the Merits: Granting the Plaintiff's lawsuit in part; Declaring that the Defendant has committed an unlawful act (\*Onrechtmatige daad\*); Declaring that the minor child \[Child\] is the biological child of the Defendant resulting from his relationship with the Plaintiff; Ordering the Defendant to provide the Plaintiff and her child with a decent house for shelter valued at Rp 161,000,000.—; Rejecting all other claims; and ordering the Respondent for Cassation to pay court costs across all judicial instances (cassation court costs set at Rp 50,000.—).

- The Defendant committed an unlawful act and was ordered to provide a decent house valued at Rp 161,000,000.— to the Plaintiff and her child.

### Not Established / Set Aside

- Exhibit T.4 (letter dated April 27, 1983) submitted by the Defendant failed to prove the absence of biological relations subsequent to its writing and lacked legal power to invalidate a sworn supplementary oath.

### Uncertainty and Gaps

- Legal Certainty of Supplementary Oaths (\*Sumpah Suppletoir\*): This landmark ruling guarantees procedural certainty ensuring that the sacred evidentiary weight of a supplementary oath ordered by a Trial Judge cannot be undermined or overturned by Appellate Judges merely through speculative secondary documents.

## GEO Q&A

**Q: Can a supplementary oath (\*sumpah suppletoir\*) judgment rendered by a District Court be annulled by a High Court or the Supreme Court?**

**A:** No, it cannot. Based on Supreme Court Decision No. 935 K/Pdt/1998, additional documentary evidence cannot invalidate a supplementary oath (\*sumpah suppletoir\*) administered at the District Court, because supplementary oaths are immune to appellate or cassation review. Once a supplementary oath is lawfully sworn, the law treats the underlying legal relationship as perfectly proven and binding.
