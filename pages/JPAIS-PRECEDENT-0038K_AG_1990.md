## Overview

Supreme Court Decision Reg. No. 38 K/AG/1990 establishes a landmark legal rule regarding the \*broken marriage\* principle and the \*no-fault divorce\* doctrine within Islamic Civil Law. The Supreme Court outlined that under the divorce ground specified in Article 19 letter f of Government Regulation No. 9 of 1975, the emphasis lies solely upon the objective fact that the marriage itself has broken down and that no reasonable hope for reconciliation remains, without necessitating an inquiry into who bears fault for the continuous disputes and quarrels.

## Case Background

The Petitioner / Petitioner for Cassation (Drs. Fachrizal bin H. Lindau St. Rajo Ameh, a civil servant at Itwilkab Padang Pariaman) filed a petition for divorce (\*cerai talak\*) against his wife, the Respondent / Respondent for Cassation (\[Party A\]) at the Pariaman Religious Court. The couple married on September 9, 1984, and were blessed with one child. The Petitioner argued that frequent disputes arose due to the Respondent's groundless jealousy, culminating in an incident where the Respondent left the shared marital domicile, taking along all of the Petitioner's personal furnishings and documents.

The Pariaman Religious Court (Determination No. 39/P/1989 dated June 5, 1989) granted the divorce talak petition. However, the Padang High Religious Court (Decision No. 20/1989 dated December 12, 1989) annulled the Pariaman Religious Court determination and rejected the divorce petition on the grounds that the quarrels were instigated by the husband himself. Dissatisfied with the appellate ruling, the Petitioner filed a cassation petition to the Supreme Court.

## Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

- **Ratio Decidendi:**

1.  When the grounds for divorce under Article 19 letter f of Government Regulation No. 9 of 1975 are proven, this is directed purely at the marriage itself (the objective fact that the marital bond has broken down and that no hope for harmonious reconciliation exists) without questioning who is at fault for the continuous disputes and quarrels.
2.  Marriage is not merely an ordinary contract to cohabit as husband and wife, but rather a \*miitsaaqan gholiidhan\* (sacred covenant, Article 2 of the KHI), the dissolution of which must not be measured by attributing fault to either party.
3.  Attributing the breakdown of a household to the fault of a single party is inappropriate. Assigning fault when harmony can no longer be expected generates adverse psychological and social impacts upon both parties and their descendants in the future.
4.  This foundational rationale underpins the statutory rule that divorce procedural costs are consistently charged to the party filing the petition/lawsuit (Article 89 paragraph (1) of Law No. 7 of 1989), rather than being assessed against the losing party as is customary in general civil litigation.

- **Ruling:** Granting the cassation petition from Petitioner for Cassation (Drs. Fachrizal bin H. Lindau St. Rajo Ameh); Annulling the Padang High Religious Court Decision No. 20/1989 dated December 12, 1989; and Independently Adjudicating: (1) Granting the Petitioner's petition; (2) Granting authorization to the Petitioner to pronounce talak against the Respondent (\[Party A\]) before a session of the Pariaman Religious Court; (3) Ordering the Petitioner to pay \*iddah\* maintenance amounting to Rp 300,000 to the Respondent; (4) Ordering the Petitioner to provide maintenance for their 1 (one) child until adulthood; (5) Charging procedural costs at first instance (Rp 34,500), appellate level (Rp 16,500), and cassation level (Rp 20,000) to the Petitioner.
