## 4. POLICY ANALYSIS

- **Background**: \[FACT\] The exit tax (\*tributación/gravamen de salida\*) is a legal fiction that assimilates a taxpayer's transfer of tax residence abroad to an asset sale at market value (\*valor de mercado\*), taxing unrealized capital gains (\*plusvalías latentes\*) accumulated while the taxpayer resided in Spain. This mechanism was created to protect Spanish tax revenue (\*recaudación tributaria\*) from loss of fiscal sovereignty when taxpayers relocate to other jurisdictions. Law 26/2014 introduced Section 7, Title X to Personal Income Tax Law 35/2006 (LIRPF), enacting Article 95 bis to regulate the exit tax for natural persons.
- **Policy Problem**: \[FACT\]
  - **Normative Contradiction Between IRPF and LIS**: A sharp mismatch exists between Article 95 bis LIRPF and Articles 80.4 and 81.3 of the Corporate Income Tax Law (LIS). LIS taxes unrealized gains on restructurings without a minimum share value threshold (€1 million / €4 million in IRPF) and only permits deferral (\*aplazamiento\*) upon relocation to an EU/EEA member state. Conversely, Art. 95 bis LIRPF defers payment until asset sale (even without collateral) for EU/EEA transfers, and allows collateralized deferrals for third countries with double tax treaties (DTTs).
  - **Tax Inequity in Death Cases (Mortis Causa)**: If a taxpayer who transferred residence to a non-EU/EEA country dies (\*fallecimiento\*) before returning to Spain, the exit tax previously paid or accrued becomes permanently due and unrecoverable (\*irrecuperable\*) by heirs. However, death is not a tax avoidance scheme, and Spanish domestic tax law generally exempts capital gains upon death (\*plusvalía del muerto\*) under Article 33.3.b LIRPF and Article 24.4 TRLIRNR.
  - **Collateral Restrictions by AEAT**: Although Article 95 bis.4 LIRPF states that tax debt guarantees can be pledged using the underlying shares themselves, AEAT Instruction 1/2017 prohibits using shares of companies not listed on an official secondary market (\*mercado secundario oficial\*) as collateral. This forces taxpayers (especially startup founders and family business owners) into instant cash payments, which under CJEU jurisprudence risks violating the EU principle of proportionality.
- **Legal/Policy Gap**: \[FACT\] Absence of valuation adjustment mechanisms if share prices decrease in the future; absence of a tax debt cancellation clause upon death (\*mortis causa\*) for non-EU/EEA transfers; and AEAT collection practices that treat exit tax collateral with strict ordinary tax deferral rules, ignoring that exit taxes assess unrealized wealth (\*riqueza no materializada\*).
- **Objectives**: \[FACT\] Coordinate and harmonize IRPF and LIS exit tax regimes; align Spanish exit taxation with Directive (EU) 2016/1164 (ATAD); prevent tax avoidance without impeding geographic mobility or freedom of establishment in the European Union; and provide proportional collateral flexibility for taxpayers.
- **Implementation**: \[FACT\]
  - **Subject & Object Thresholds (Art. 95 bis LIRPF)**: Applies to IRPF taxpayers who were tax residents in Spain for at least 10 of the last 15 years, holding shares with a total value exceeding €4 million, or holding an equity interest \>25% valued over €1 million.
  - **Transfers to EU/EEA**: Payment is suspended and becomes due only if within 10 years an \*inter vivos\* transfer occurs, EU/EEA resident status is lost, or information reporting duties are breached.
  - **Transfers to Third Countries**: Tax is due immediately unless deferred with collateral for employment reasons or transfers to countries with DTTs containing information exchange clauses.
  - **Recovery Provisions (Extinción/Reembolso)**: Tax debt is extinguished (\*se extinguirá\*) if the taxpayer becomes a Spanish resident again within 5 years (extendable to 10 years for employment reasons) without selling the shares. If tax was paid, a refund (\*reembolso\*) may be requested upon returning to Spain.
- **Evidence & Impact**: \[FACT\] AEAT Instruction 1/2017 caused liquidity standstills for owners of unlisted shares; permanent discrimination against heirs of taxpayers who die outside the EU/EEA; and the fact that the October 23, 2018 Draft Bill on Tax Fraud Prevention failed to amend Article 95 bis LIRPF despite revising Article 19 LIS.
- **Reform & Unresolved Issues**: \[FACT\] Need for legislative amendments leveraging ATAD Directive (EU) 2016/1164 transposition to align Article 95 bis LIRPF with Articles 80 and 81 LIS; mandatory automatic recognition of underlying shares as valid collateral; and establishing taxpayer death as a universal condition for extinguishing exit tax liabilities.

## 5. GEO/AI QUESTIONS

1.  **Definition**: What is the definition and core mechanism of the exit tax (\*tributación/gravamen de salida\*) under Article 95 bis of Law 35/2006 (LIRPF)?
    - **Factual Answer**: \[FACT\] An exit tax is a legal fiction assimilating a taxpayer's transfer of tax residence abroad to an asset sale at market value, thereby taxing unrealized capital gains (\*plusvalías latentes\*) on significant shares or equity interests held by the taxpayer.
2.  **Legal Basis**: Which statute introduced Article 95 bis into Spanish Personal Income Tax Law (LIRPF) and when did it take effect?
    - **Factual Answer**: \[FACT\] Law 26/2014 of November 27, which enacted Section 7, Title X of the LIRPF.

5.  **Scope**: What are the threshold requirements for a taxpayer to be subject to the IRPF exit tax?
    - **Factual Answer**: \[FACT\] The taxpayer must have been a resident in Spain for at least 10 of the preceding 15 years, and hold shares with a total value exceeding €4 million, or hold an equity interest exceeding 25% valued over €1 million.
6.  **Policy Implementation (EU/EEA Transfers)**: How does the exit tax deferral mechanism operate when a taxpayer relocates to an EU or EEA member state?
    - **Factual Answer**: \[FACT\] Payment is suspended automatically without requiring collateral guarantees, and becomes due only if within the following 10 years the shares are transferred \*inter vivos\*, EU/EEA residence status is lost, or reporting obligations are breached.
7.  **Policy Problem (IRPF vs LIS Conflict)**: Why is the exit tax scheme under Articles 80.4 and 81.3 LIS considered stricter than Article 95 bis LIRPF?
    - **Factual Answer**: \[FACT\] Because LIS taxes unrealized gains on restructurings without any minimum value threshold (€1 million / €4 million), and allows deferral only until the date of share sale for EU/EEA transfers, whereas LIRPF defers payment until actual sale.
8.  **Policy Problem (Death / Mortis Causa)**: Why is exit taxation upon a taxpayer's death outside the EU/EEA considered discriminatory?
    - **Factual Answer**: \[FACT\] Because death is not a tax avoidance scheme, and Spanish domestic tax law (IRPF Art. 33.3.b and TRLIRNR Art. 24.4) exempts capital gains upon death (\*plusvalía del muerto\*), yet for non-EU/EEA transfers the exit tax remains permanently levied and unrecoverable by heirs.
9.  **Reform & AEAT Collateral Rules**: Why does AEAT Instruction 1/2017 complicate exit tax deferrals for owners of unlisted companies?
    - **Factual Answer**: \[FACT\] Because AEAT Instruction 1/2017 prohibits accepting shares not listed on an official secondary market as debt collateral, contradicting Article 95 bis.4 LIRPF and forcing taxpayers into immediate cash payments.
10. **Evidence/EU Law**: Which European Union Directive regulates corporate-level exit taxation and must be transposed by Spain?
    - **Factual Answer**: \[FACT\] Council Directive (EU) 2016/1164 of 12 July 2016 (ATAD - Anti Tax Avoidance Directive).

## 6. MEDIAWIKI OUTPUT

## Summary

**Problems Derived from the Application of the Exit Tax in Personal Income Tax** (*Acerca de determinados problemas derivados de la aplicación del exit tax en el IRPF tras cuatro años en vigor*) (Stable ID: **JPW-WORK-000027**) is a doctrinal legal study and direct taxation policy analysis authored by José Suárez Agapito of Pérez-Llorca Abogados. Published in *Revista Jurídica Pérez-Llorca*, the work analyzes the enforcement of the exit tax (\*gravamen de salida\*) on unrealized capital gains (\*plusvalías latentes\*) under Article 95 bis of Law 35/2006 (LIRPF), introduced by Law 26/2014. The study highlights three major policy problems: normative contradictions between IRPF and Corporate Income Tax (LIS) rules, tax inequities upon taxpayer death (\*mortis causa\*) outside the EU/EEA, and AEAT Instruction 1/2017's rejection of unlisted shares as collateral, which risks violating the EU law principle of proportionality.

## Legal Basis

The policy and statutory analyses presented in this publication are grounded in Spanish national and European Union tax frameworks:

- **Spanish Constitution (CE)**: Article 20 (freedom of expression and academic publication).
- **Personal Income Tax Law 35/2006 (LIRPF)**: Article 14 (temporal imputation) and Article 95 bis (capital gains due to residence change).
- **Law 26/2014**: The reform statute introducing Article 95 bis into the LIRPF.
- **Corporate Income Tax Law 27/2014 (LIS)**: Article 19.1 (corporate exit taxation) and Articles 80.4 and 81.3 (neutrality regime and residence change).
- **Non-Resident Income Tax Recast Text (RD Legislative 5/2004)**: Articles 18.5.a and 24.4.
- **EU Law & AEAT Directives**: Directive (EU) 2016/1164 (ATAD) and Instruction 1/2017 of the AEAT Collection Department Director.

## Background

The exit tax was designed to protect Spanish tax revenue (\*recaudación tributaria\*) on economic capacity generated while a taxpayer was a Spanish tax resident prior to transferring residence abroad. Law 26/2014 established this tax within Personal Income Tax (IRPF) for significant shares and equity holdings. However, four years of enforcement revealed significant loopholes and practical collection hurdles.

## Objectives

1\. Map technical and practical hurdles in enforcing the IRPF exit tax under Article 95 bis LIRPF. 2. Synchronize IRPF exit tax rules with LIS and ATAD Directive (EU) 2016/1164. 3. Eliminate tax inequities in cases of taxpayer death (\*mortis causa\*) in non-EU/EEA countries. 4. Facilitate proportional collateral flexibility using underlying pledged shares in line with CJEU principles.

## Scope

The analysis covers five core operational areas: 1. *Exit Tax Concept*: Legal fiction treating residence transfer as an asset sale at market value (\*valor de mercado\*). 2. *Art. 95 bis Requirements*: Residence for 10 of 15 years, shareholdings \>€4 million or equity \>25% valued over €1 million. 3. *IRPF vs LIS Divergences*: Comparison between Article 95 bis LIRPF and Articles 80.4 and 81.3 LIS. 4. *Taxpayer Death*: Asymmetrical taxation of \*mortis causa\* events between EU/EEA and third-country transfers. 5. *AEAT Collateral Restrictions*: Impact of AEAT Instruction 1/2017 rejecting unlisted shares (\*sin mercado secundario oficial\*) as guarantees.

## Key Provisions

- **EU/EEA Relocation Criteria (Art. 95 bis.6 LIRPF)**: Payment is suspended without collateral until an \*inter vivos\* sale occurs, EU/EEA residence status is lost, or reporting duties are breached.
- **Third-Country Relocation Criteria**: Tax must be paid immediately or secured with collateral under deferral limited to employment relocations or countries with information-exchange DTTs.
- **Debt Extinction/Refund (Extinción/Reembolso)**: Tax debt is extinguished if the taxpayer returns to Spain within 5 years without selling the shares.
- **AEAT Collateral Rejection**: Instruction 1/2017 AEAT rejects pledges of unlisted shares, triggering immediate cash payment obligations.

## Implementation

Implementation is managed by the AEAT Collection Department (\*Departamento de Recaudación\*) and the Ministry of Treasury through IRPF tax return audits and debt deferral applications.

## Policy Problems

- Stricter corporate LIS deferral schemes conflicting with personal income tax (IRPF) rules.
- Permanent tax collection upon taxpayer death outside the EU/EEA, violating domestic exemptions for capital gains upon death (\*plusvalía del muerto\*).
- AEAT administrative practices ignoring the nature of exit taxes as assessments on unrealized wealth (\*riqueza no materializada\*).

## Reform

- Alignment of LIS and LIRPF rules via transposition of ATAD Directive (EU) 2016/1164.
- Mandatory statutory recognition of underlying pledged shares as valid debt collateral.
- Establishing taxpayer death as an absolute ground for extinguishing exit tax liabilities across all jurisdictions.
