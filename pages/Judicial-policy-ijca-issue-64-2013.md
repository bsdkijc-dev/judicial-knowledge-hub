# Judicial-policy-ijca-issue-64-2013

# 'International Journal for Court Administration, Issue 64' (Zimmer & Langbroek, 2013)

### Wiki Entity Page

**International Journal for Court Administration (IJCA Issue 64)** is an official publication of the *International Association for Court Administration* (IACA) featuring an Executive Editorial by Markus Zimmer titled "Seven Billion Human Beings" regarding failed states, poverty, and global barriers to accessing justice. It also features a curated collection of judicial administration research articles covering judicial attire, Indonesian Islamic courts, court governance in Slovenia, court governance beyond independence, and an Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) primer.

## Summary

In his Executive Editorial, Markus Zimmer highlights UN data (October 31, 2011) noting that the global population reached 7 billion, with a quarter of humanity (around 2 billion people in 60 countries) living in fragile or failed states according to the *Failed States Index 2011*. In these nations, the judiciary often delivers "perverted justice" that serves regime privileges rather than the rule of law. On the other hand, this issue also features research on Indonesian Islamic court reform providing justice for the poor (Sumer & Lindsey), the constitutional relationship between the Ministry of Justice and the judiciary in Slovenia (Zoran Skubic), court governance beyond independence (Tin Bunjevac), an ADR primer for judges and administrators (Markus Zimmer), and a review of judicial gowns and the media (Philip M. Langbroek).

## Key Policy Issues & Executive Editorial

1\. **Failed States and Regime Justice:** Major obstacles for 2 billion people living in poverty and oppression, where freedom of expression is criminalized and the judiciary is controlled by the regime. 2. **Narrowing Access to Justice in Developed Nations:** Elite states face growing barriers to justice driven by rising criminal justice fee schedules, inhumane pre-trial detentions, and fear-driven legislation. 3. **Judicial Attire (The Judicial Gown Does Matter):** Philip Langbroek's study on the symbolism and authority of the judge's gown in court. 4. **Cover Artwork (String Theater):** A painting by Yana Payusova inspired by her experiences in a St. Petersburg youth prison post-Soviet collapse, critiquing a punitive criminal justice model that ignores core crime triggers like hunger and housing.
