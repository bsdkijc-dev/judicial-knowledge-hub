# Evidence

# Evidence

**Evidence** refers to information, material, testimony, or other admissible means used within a legal proceeding to establish or challenge facts.

The rules governing admissibility, relevance, burden, and evaluation vary by jurisdiction and proceeding.

**\[HUMAN VERIFICATION REQUIRED\]**

## Evidence Knowledge Model

Evidence may be represented through:

**SOURCE** → **FACT PROPOSITION** → **RELEVANCE** → **ADMISSIBILITY** → **WEIGHT** → **JUDICIAL FINDING**

This is an analytical model and not a statement of universal law.

## Evidence Types

Depending on the applicable legal system, evidence may include:

- Testimony
- Documents
- Physical evidence
- Digital evidence
- Expert evidence
- Electronic records
- Other legally recognized forms

## Evidence and AI

AI may assist with:

- Document classification
- Search
- Transcription
- Extraction
- Timeline construction
- Duplicate detection

AI-generated interpretation must not automatically be treated as evidence.

## Digital Evidence

Digital evidence may require consideration of:

- Authenticity
- Integrity
- Provenance
- Chain of custody
- Metadata
- Accessibility

Specific requirements must be verified against applicable law.
