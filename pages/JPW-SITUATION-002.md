# JPW-SITUATION-002

**Preliminary Examination of the Situation in Ukraine (ICC)** is an independent legal and factual evaluation conducted by the Office of the Prosecutor (OTP) of the International Criminal Court (ICC) since 25 April 2014 regarding alleged war crimes and crimes against humanity committed within the territory of Ukraine since 21 November 2013 \[1–4\].

## Summary

This preliminary examination encompasses three main event clusters: the Maidan protest events (21 November 2013 – 22 February 2014), the occupation of the Autonomous Republic of Crimea and the city of Sevastopol by the Russian Federation (since 20 February 2014), and the armed conflict in Eastern Ukraine (since April 2014) \[1–4, 6–13\].

## Legal Basis

- [Rome Statute](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Rome_Statute): Article 7 (Crimes Against Humanity), Article 8 (War Crimes), Article 12(3) (Declaration of Acceptance of Jurisdiction by a Non-State Party), Article 15 (Proprio Motu Investigations), and Article 53(1) (Initiation of Preliminary Examination) \[1–4\].
- Declarations submitted by the Government of Ukraine under Article 12(3) dated 17 April 2014 and 8 September 2015 \[1–4\].

## Background

Mass protests in Maidan arose from the decision of the government led by President [Viktor Yanukovych](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Viktor_Yanukovych) not to sign the Association Agreement with the European Union. Escalating violence led to a change of power, followed by the takeover of military control over Crimea by the Russian Federation and the outbreak of armed conflict in the Donetsk and Lugansk regions \[6–11\].

## Objectives

- Assess whether material jurisdiction, admissibility (complementarity and gravity), and the interests of justice are met pursuant to Article 53(1) of the Rome Statute.
- Determine whether there is a reasonable basis to request authorization to open a formal investigation under Article 15(3).

## Key Provisions

- \*\*Alleged Crimes in Crimea\*\*: Willful killing, torture, unlawful confinement, compelling protected persons to serve in enemy forces, transfer of detainees, and denial of fair trial rights \[20–23\].
- \*\*Alleged Crimes in Eastern Ukraine\*\*: Direct attacks against civilians and civilian objects, shelling of schools and hospitals, willful killing, torture, cruel treatment, and sexual violence.
- \*\*Conflict Classification\*\*: The conflict in Eastern Ukraine contains elements of both a non-international armed conflict and an international armed conflict.

## Implementation

In 2019, the OTP focused on admissibility assessments by mapping national judicial proceedings conducted by Ukrainian law enforcement agencies and conducting a mission to Ukraine in June 2019 \[36–44\].

## Policy Issues

- Lack of direct access for international organizations and assessment teams to occupied Crimea and non-government-controlled areas in Eastern Ukraine.
- The necessity of proving overall control by the Russian Federation over armed groups in Eastern Ukraine.
