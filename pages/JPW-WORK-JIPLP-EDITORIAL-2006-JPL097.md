# JPW-WORK-JIPLP-EDITORIAL-2006-JPL097

**Editorial: The art of the unsettlement** is a legal editorial authored by [Jeremy Phillips](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Jeremy_Phillips) and published in the [Journal of Intellectual Property Law & Practice](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Journal_of_Intellectual_Property_Law_%26_Practice) (Vol. 1, No. 9) by [Oxford University Press](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Oxford_University_Press) on July 11, 2006 [Editorial Publication](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Editorial_Publication) \[1–10\].

## Summary

This editorial analyzes the dynamics, drafting vulnerabilities, and judicial interpretation of settlement agreements in intellectual property (IP) disputes [IP Settlement Agreements](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/IP_Settlement_Agreements) \[1–10\]. The author uses the High Court decision in the dispute between [Apple Corps](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Apple_Corps) and [Apple Computer](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Apple_Computer) regarding the [iTunes](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/iTunes) music service as a primary case study [Apple iTunes Dispute](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Apple_iTunes_Dispute) \[1–10\].

## Legal Basis

- **Formal Legal Basis**: Not found in the available sources [Legal Basis](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Legal_Basis) \[1–10\].
- **Contractual Interpretation in IP**: Settlement provisions are tested against market evolutions and technological innovations unknown to the parties at the time of execution [Contract Interpretation](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Contract_Interpretation) \[1–10\].

## Background

IP disputes frequently arise between commercial entities tied by prior contractual relationships (such as licenses or distribution agreements) or businesses undergoing market convergence [Dispute Background](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Dispute_Background) \[1–10\].

## Objectives

1.  Highlight the economic, operational, and reputational benefits of resolving IP disputes out of court [Settlement Benefits](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Settlement_Benefits) \[1–10\].
2.  Identify drafting pitfalls and structural vulnerabilities within IP settlement clauses [Drafting Risks](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Drafting_Risks) \[1–10\].
3.  Evaluate how legacy settlement contracts withstand modern technological disruptions [Modern Technology](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Modern_Technology) \[1–10\].

## Key Provisions

- **Benefits of Settlement**: Avoiding trial exposure, spiraling litigation fees, and preserving commercial goodwill [Settlement Advantages](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Settlement_Advantages) \[1–10\].
- **Three Settlement Paradigms**: (i) An agreement is created to resolve a dispute, (ii) it is deemed conclusive, but (iii) it is later re-contested due to technological or market shifts [Three Paradigms](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Three_Paradigms) \[1–10\].
- **Apple Case Study (May 2006)**: Mr Justice Mann held that Apple Computer did not breach the 1991 settlement agreement by launching the iTunes store [Apple Ruling 2006](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Apple_Ruling_2006) \[1–10\].
- **Other Cited Disputes**: Prudential Insurance v Prudential Assurance, World Wide Fund v World Wrestling Federation, and Aston Barrett v recording companies handling Bob Marley tracks [Other Cases](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Other_Cases) \[1–10\].

## Policy Issues

- **Fudging the Issue**: Deliberately crafting ambiguous compromise language invites future re-litigation [Ambiguous Clauses](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Ambiguous_Clauses) \[1–10\].
- **Future Technological Windfalls**: Legacy contracts can grant unexpected commercial windfalls to one party when new technologies emerge [Future Tech](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Future_Tech) \[1–10\].
