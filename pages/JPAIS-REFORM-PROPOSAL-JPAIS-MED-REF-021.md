## Description / Deskripsi

Analyzes the codified governance of the global maritime domain under the 1982 United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS), the partition of ocean space into distinct jurisdictional zones (Internal Waters, Territorial Sea, Contiguous Zone, EEZ, Continental Shelf, and High Seas), typologies of maritime jurisdiction (Flag State, Coastal State, Port State, Universal, and Concurrent), and legal mechanisms for interstate criminal cooperation including the SUA Convention, \*aut dedere aut judicare\*, mutual legal assistance, and joint investigation frameworks.

## Problem / Masalah

- [JPAIS-MED-PROB-007](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/JPAIS-MED-PROB-007): Illicit actors exploit spatial boundaries and jurisdictional gaps between coastal State sovereignty and flag State high-seas exclusivity to evade interdiction.
- [JPAIS-MED-PROB-008](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/JPAIS-MED-PROB-008): Traditional definitions of piracy under UNCLOS Article 101 impose restrictive cumulative criteria—requiring private ends, a two-ship scenario, and location on the high seas—which exclude single-ship mutinies, political terrorism, and armed robbery in territorial waters.
- [JPAIS-MED-PROB-009](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/JPAIS-MED-PROB-009): Concurrent jurisdictional claims from multiple sovereign states create legal friction and require structured priority rules or extradition mechanisms.

## Current Indonesian Practice / Praktik Indonesia Saat Ini

- [JPAIS-MED-ID-021](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/JPAIS-MED-ID-021): Indonesia exercises absolute sovereignty over internal and archipelagic waters while navigating cooperative frameworks across regional straits for transboundary crime suppression.

## Japanese & Comparative Practice / Praktik Perbandingan

- [JPAIS-MED-JP-021](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/JPAIS-MED-JP-021): Global maritime nations utilize specialized bilateral shiprider agreements, port state control inspections under IMO conventions, and regional patrols to harmonize enforcement across boundaries.

## Gap / Kesenjangan

- [JPAIS-MED-GAP-004](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/JPAIS-MED-GAP-004): Disparities in domestic penal legislation, long-term detention capacity, and human rights safeguards (such as \*non-refoulement\* and prohibitions against sub-standard shipboard detention) complicate the prosecution and transfer of interdicted maritime offenders.

## Proposed Adaptation / Adaptasi Usulan

Adopt robust multilateral cooperation instruments, implement the 2005 SUA Protocols, streamline mutual legal assistance (MLA) workflows, and establish standardized regional prisoner transfer frameworks in alignment with international human rights standards.

## Action / Aksi

- [JPAIS-MED-ACT-007](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/JPAIS-MED-ACT-007): Operationalize bilateral shiprider agreements to enable seamless joint maritime law enforcement within overlapping EEZs.
- [JPAIS-MED-ACT-008](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/JPAIS-MED-ACT-008): Harmonize domestic statutory definitions of maritime terrorism and transnational organized crime with the SUA Convention and UNTOC.
- [JPAIS-MED-ACT-009](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/JPAIS-MED-ACT-009): Ensure all maritime interdictions and detentions strictly adhere to procedural due process and human rights obligations under international jurisprudence.

## Pilot / Pilot Proyek

- [JPAIS-MED-PILOT-021](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/JPAIS-MED-PILOT-021): Implementation of a regional joint operational protocol for fast-track evidence sharing and mutual legal assistance across strategic maritime chokepoints.

## KPI / Indikator Kinerja Utama

- [JPAIS-MED-KPI-021](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/JPAIS-MED-KPI-021): 100% compliance with flag state consultation protocols during high-seas boardings; zero procedural challenges in domestic courts regarding maritime chain-of-custody or evidentiary admissibility.

## Policy Implication / Implikasi Kebijakan

Integration of UNCLOS spatial zoning principles with targeted suppression conventions to eliminate legal safe havens for transnational maritime criminals.

## Regulatory Implication / Implikasi Regulasi

Enactment of comprehensive domestic legislation incorporating extraterritorial enforcement powers, port state control measures, and mandatory \*aut dedere aut judicare\* mechanisms.

## Evidence / Bukti Empiris

- UNCLOS spatial partitioning successfully reconciles coastal economic rights with traditional high seas freedoms across six distinct zones.
- The 2005 SUA Protocols successfully eliminate the two-ship and private ends requirements, providing an effective legal basis for prosecuting maritime terrorism.
