# JPW-REGULATION-GER-NETZENTGELT-2026

The **German Network Access Fee Subsidy Bill 2026** (German: *Entwurf eines Gesetzes zur Zuschussgewährung zur Dämpfung der Netzentgelte im Jahr 2026*) is a legislative proposal enacted by the [Federal Government of Germany](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Federal_Government_of_Germany) to cushion end-user electricity prices by providing a €6.5 billion subsidy for public transmission grid fees in 2026 [Network Subsidy](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Network_Subsidy).

## Overview

The Federal Government approved a draft bill allocating a direct €6.5 billion subsidy from the Climate and Transformation Fund (KTF) to Germany's four primary transmission system operators (TSOs) [Climate and Transformation Fund](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Climate_and_Transformation_Fund). This measure directly targets transmission network fees, which form a substantial component of total retail electricity bills in Germany [Electricity Subsidy](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Electricity_Subsidy).

## Legal Basis

- **Section 24c Energy Industry Act (EnWG)**: A new statutory provision inserted into the EnWG to establish the legal framework for TSOs to receive and account for the subsidy [EnWG](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/EnWG).
- **Electricity Network Access Fee Ordinance (Stromnetzentgeltverordnung)**: Framework governing non-discriminatory network access fee calculations [Stromnetzentgeltverordnung](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Stromnetzentgeltverordnung).
- **Incentive Regulation Ordinance (Anreizregulierung)**: Regulatory revenue cap framework from which TSOs must deduct received subsidies [Incentive Regulation](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Incentive_Regulation).

## Background

Persistently high energy costs in Germany continue to impact industrial competitiveness and consumer purchasing power [German Energy Costs](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/German_Energy_Costs). The federal intervention subsidizes upstream transmission network fees to lower electricity bills across all consumer tiers \[Energy Background\].

## Key Objectives

1.  Lower end-user power prices by dampening transmission network access fees [Tariff Reduction](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Tariff_Reduction).
2.  Distribute €6.5 billion in funding proportionally among the four national TSOs [TSO Allocation](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/TSO_Allocation).
3.  Complement relief through electricity tax (*Stromsteuer*) reductions for manufacturing, agriculture, and forestry sectors [Electricity Tax](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Electricity_Tax).

## Core Provisions

- **KTF Funding Allocation**: The €6.5 billion sum originates from the Climate and Transformation Fund (KTF) and is distributed based on each TSO's approved revenue cap [KTF Funds](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/KTF_Funds).
- **Eligible Recipients**: The four recipient TSOs are 50Hertz, Amprion, TenneT, and TransnetBW [German TSOs](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/German_TSOs).
- **Fee Deduction Obligation**: TSOs and downstream distribution operators must deduct the subsidy from their revenue caps when calculating net fee schedules [TSO Obligations](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/TSO_Obligations).
- **October 15 Deadline**: TSOs remain legally obligated to determine and publish next year's network access fees by October 15 annually [Publication Deadline](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Publication_Deadline).

## Implementation & Oversight

- **BNetzA Oversight**: The Federal Network Agency (*Bundesnetzagentur*) supervises compliance and proper accounting of the subsidy funds [Bundesnetzagentur](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Bundesnetzagentur).
- **Consumer Pass-Through**: Electricity suppliers must pass along lowered network access fees to end consumers upon final parliamentary enactment [Pass-Through](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Pass-Through).

## Policy Considerations

- **Tariff Relief Expectations**: The estimated reduction of 1.3 cents/kWh falls short of the government Coalition Agreement's broader target of 5 cents/kWh [Coalition Targets](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Coalition_Targets).
- **Parliamentary Delays**: Industry groups express concern over potential delays in parliamentary adoption beyond the October 15 publication cut-off [Parliamentary Risks](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Parliamentary_Risks).
- **Single-Year Limitation**: The policy applies exclusively to calendar year 2026, prompting calls for long-term structural relief such as an industrial power price cap (*Industriestrompreis*) [Industriestrompreis](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Industriestrompreis).
