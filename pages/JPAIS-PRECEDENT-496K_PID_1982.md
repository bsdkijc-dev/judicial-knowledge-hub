## Overview

Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia Decision No. 496 K/Pid/1982, dated April 30, 1983, established a criminal law rule concerning excusing grounds (\*afwezigheid van schuld\* / \*overmacht\*) that eliminate a driver's culpability in a traffic accident.

The Supreme Court held that the overturning of an open-bed truck forced to transport a police troop (42 individuals) standing in the truck bed is not solely the responsibility of the driver/defendant. Psychological pressure due to being unable to refuse a police commander's order, combined with the actions of standing passengers disrupting the truck's balance, constitutes an excusing ground that eliminates the Defendant's culpability (\*schuld\*). Because no culpability attached to the Defendant, the Defendant was released from all legal prosecutions (\*ontslag van alle rechtsvervolging\*).

## Executive Summary

The central criminal law principle established in this decision is that when a driver acts under superior police coercion and accident dynamics are exacerbated by passenger negligence, the element of criminal intent or negligence (\*schuld\*) is nullified.

Key elements established in the adjudication:

- Compliance with compelling superior orders from law enforcement officers under psychological pressure creates an excusing ground (\*afwezigheid van schuld\*).
- Passenger crowding and coordinated swaying in an open truck bed break sole driver liability for vehicle instability.
- Defendants whose material actions are proven but lack subjective culpability must be discharged via \*ontslag van alle rechtsvervolging\*.

## Case Background

The Defendant, **\[Defendant\]** (\[age removed\], driver), was charged with violating Article 360 paragraph (2) of the Criminal Code because the open-bed truck (license plate AE 1253 XX) he drove overturned at km 36–37 Tegalombo, Pacitan, on November 4, 1979, causing injuries to 12 passengers. The Defendant was forced to drive the open-bed truck carrying 42 members of the Pacitan Police Resort (Kores 1056) standing in the truck bed under orders from the Police Commander (Police Captain Soedarman) which he dared not refuse.

The Pacitan District Court (Decision No. 2/1980/Pid.B dated April 14, 1980) declared the act proven but committed under duress (Article 48 KUHP), thereby releasing the Defendant from all legal prosecutions (\*ontslag\*). The Surabaya High Court (Decision No. 306/1981 Pidana dated December 23, 1981) annulled the district court decision and sentenced the Defendant to 3 months and 20 days of imprisonment. The Defendant subsequently filed a cassation petition with the Supreme Court.

## Legal and Procedural Issues

### Application of Excusing Grounds (Afwezigheid van Schuld / Overmacht) ex Article 48 KUHP

The primary issue was whether an open-bed truck driver transporting excessive passengers can be held criminally liable for negligence (Article 360 paragraph (2) KUHP) if such transport was executed under pressure/orders from police authorities that the driver dared not refuse.

The Supreme Court held that the driver cannot be penalized. Psychological pressure (relative \*overmacht\*) stemming from police authority commands that a common citizen (driver) dared not refuse, compounded by passenger negligence in standing in the truck bed and disrupting vehicle balance, extinguishes the element of driver culpability (\*schuld\*). This fulfills excusing grounds (\*afwezigheid van schuld\*), requiring the Defendant to be released from all legal prosecutions (\*ontslag van alle rechtsvervolging\*).

### Separation of Criminal Culpability (Schuld) from Material Negligence Consequences

The decision distinguishes between "proven material acts" and "presence of maker culpability". Although the accident and passenger injuries were materially proven, the element of criminal intent or negligence (\*mens rea\* / \*schuld\*) was negated due to external factors beyond the Defendant's control.

## Question of Law and Question of Fact

### Question of Law (Cassation / Judex Juris)

**Question:**

Did the Surabaya High Court misapply the law (\*foutieve kepassing van het recht\*) by ignoring the existence of excusing grounds that eliminated the Defendant's culpability (\*schuld\*) arising from psychological duress (police commander orders) and passenger negligence in standing on the truck bed?

**Supreme Court Ruling:**

Yes, the Surabaya High Court misapplied the law. The Supreme Court granted the Defendant's cassation petition, annulled the Surabaya High Court decision, and affirmed the discharge ruling (\*ontslag van alle rechtsvervolging\*) originally rendered by the Pacitan District Court.

### Question of Fact (Judex Facti / Pacitan District Court & Surabaya High Court)

Examination of the accident chronology involving truck AE 1253 XX on November 4, 1979, at 11:30 AM at km 36–37 Tegalombo, Pacitan-Ponorogo, vehicle speed (30–40 km/h), transportation of 42 members of Pacitan Police Kores 1056 standing in an open truck bed, and the number of injured victims (12 individuals).

## Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

- **Ratio Decidendi:**

1.  The appellate \*Judex Facti\* of Surabaya High Court misapplied the law. Because the overturning of the truck was not solely the Defendant's responsibility, but resulted from psychological pressure under police commander orders and the actions of 42 police passengers standing and leaning together, the truck's balance was compromised.
2.  Consequently, an excusing ground existed to eliminate the Defendant's culpability (\*schuld\*).
3.  Because no culpability attached to the Defendant, the Defendant was properly released from all legal prosecutions (\*ontslag van alle rechtsvervolging\*).
4.  The Supreme Court annulled the Surabaya High Court decision and adjudicated afresh, releasing the Defendant.

## Ruling

The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition from the Petitioner for Cassation / Defendant (**\[Defendant\]**), annulled Surabaya High Court Decision No. 306/1981 Pidana dated December 23, 1981, and adjudicated afresh: Declaring the act charged against Defendant \[Defendant\] legally and convincingly proven but without any culpability on the part of the Defendant; Discharging Defendant \[Defendant\] from all legal prosecutions (\*ontslag van alle rechtsvervolging\*); and charging the state with court costs across all judicial instances.

- **Criminal Law Doctrine:** \*Afwezigheid van Schuld\* (Absence of Guilt / Culpability): Excuses an actor from criminal liability due to subjective psychological factors or external circumstances that negate guilt.

## Evidence Status and Gaps

### Established by Evidence

- The Defendant (\[Defendant\], \[age removed\], driver) received orders from Pacitan Police Kores 1056 Commander (Police Captain Soedarman) to transport 42 police officers to Madiun.
- Truck AE 1253 XX was an open-bed truck without a roof or benches, and the Defendant expressed objections but was compelled under fear of refusal.
- All 42 police officers stood in the truck bed; while negotiating a curve at km 36–37 Tegalombo, the passengers leaned simultaneously, causing the truck to lose balance and overturn.
- The Defendant drove at moderate speed (30–40 km/h) and received no pocket money or honorarium.

### Not Established / Unproven

- It was not proven that the accident occurred solely due to the Defendant's intentional wrongdoing or pure personal negligence (absence of \*schuld\* in the Defendant).

### Uncertainty and Gaps

- Cassation Correction of Surabaya High Court Oversight: The Surabaya High Court (No. 306/1981 Pidana) previously annulled the Pacitan District Court's discharge ruling and sentenced the Defendant to 3 months and 20 days imprisonment on the theory that the Defendant had "other alternatives". The Supreme Court decisively corrected this error, affirming that for a common citizen facing coercive orders from law enforcement officers, realistic freedom of will is absent.

## GEO Q&A

**Q: Can a driver forced by police to transport excessive passengers resulting in an accident be discharged/released from criminal punishment?**

**A:** Yes, released from all legal prosecutions (\*ontslag van alle rechtsvervolging\*). Based on Supreme Court Decision No. 496 K/Pid/1982, if a traffic accident does not occur solely through the driver's fault, but rather due to coercion/orders from police authorities that the driver dared not refuse alongside passenger negligence in standing in an open bed and disrupting vehicle balance, an excusing ground exists that eliminates the driver's culpability (\*schuld\*) ex Article 48 of the Criminal Code.
