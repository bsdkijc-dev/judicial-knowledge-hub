# JPW-SITUATION-001

**Preliminary Examination of the Situation in Colombia (ICC)** is an independent legal and factual evaluation conducted by the Office of the Prosecutor (OTP) of the International Criminal Court (ICC) since June 2004 concerning crimes against humanity and war crimes allegedly committed within the territory of Colombia \[\].

## Summary

This preliminary examination focuses on evaluating the admissibility and complementarity of Colombian domestic judicial systems, including ordinary courts, *Justicia y Paz*, and the transitional justice system known as the Special Jurisdiction for Peace (*Jurisdicción Especial para la Paz* / JEP) \[\]. The OTP oversees investigations across five main case focuses, including the promotion of paramilitarism, forced displacement, sexual violence, and extrajudicial killings ("falsos positivos") \[\].

## Legal Basis

- [Rome Statute](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Rome_Statute): Article 7 (Crimes Against Humanity), Article 8 (War Crimes), Article 12 (Jurisdiction), Article 15 (Communications), Article 53(1) (Initiation of an Investigation), and Article 124 (Transitional Provision for War Crimes) \[\].
- [Final Agreement for Ending the Conflict and Building a Stable and Lasting Peace](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Final_Agreement_for_Ending_the_Conflict_and_Building_a_Stable_and_Lasting_Peace) (24 November 2016) \[\].
- [Statutory Law of the JEP](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Statutory_Law_of_the_JEP) (Ley Estatutaria 1957 of 2019) \[\].

## Background

Colombia experienced an armed conflict lasting over 50 years involving government forces, paramilitary groups, and insurgent groups such as the FARC-EP and ELN \[\]. In 2016, the Government and the FARC-EP signed a Peace Agreement that established the JEP as a transitional justice judicial organ, which officially commenced operations on 15 March 2018 \[\].

## Objectives

- Ensure accountability for the most serious crimes committed during the Colombian armed conflict \[\].
- Assess whether national judicial proceedings in Colombia are conducted genuinely \[\].
- Formulate referential benchmarks for closing the preliminary examination stage \[\].

## Key Provisions

- \*\*Temporal Jurisdiction\*\*: Crimes against humanity since 1 November 2002; war crimes since 1 November 2009 \[\].
- \*\*JEP Macro-Cases\*\*: Comprises 7 priority macro-cases, including Case No. 001 (FARC-EP Hostage Taking), Case No. 003 (*Falsos Positivos*), and Case No. 007 (Child Recruitment) \[\].
- \*\*Falsos Positivos Cases\*\*: Involves investigations into extrajudicial executions presented as combat casualties by 5 Divisions of the National Army of Colombia \[\].

## Implementation

As of November 2019, 9,713 former FARC-EP members, 2,291 members of the armed forces, and 63 non-military state agents had submitted themselves to the jurisdiction of the JEP \[\]. Ordinary courts handled 2,268 active *falsos positivos* cases and 2,047 third-party (civilian financer/supporter of paramilitarism) cases \[\].

## Policy Issues

- Evaluating the genuine character of special criminal sanctions imposed by the JEP \[\].
- Challenges posed by the rearming of certain former FARC-EP leadership factions and the suspension of peace negotiations with the ELN \[\].
