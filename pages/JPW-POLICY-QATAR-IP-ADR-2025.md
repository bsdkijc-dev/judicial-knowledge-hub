# JPW-POLICY-QATAR-IP-ADR-2025

The **Arbitrability of Intellectual Property Disputes in Qatar** refers to the judicial policy and legal framework in the State of Qatar governing the resolution of intellectual property (IP) conflicts via arbitration and alternative dispute resolution (ADR) mechanisms [Qatar IP Policy](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Qatar_IP_Policy) \[1–81\].

## Overview

In transitioning toward a knowledge-based economy, Qatar utilizes alternative dispute resolution (ADR) and arbitration to resolve IP disputes efficiently [Knowledge Economy](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Knowledge_Economy) \[1–81\]. This framework aligns with UN Sustainable Development Goal 16.3 (SDG 16.3) to ensure equal access to justice and supports the Qatar Third National Development Strategy (2024–2030) [SDG 16.3](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/SDG_16.3) \[1–81\].

## Legal Basis

- **Law No. 2 of 2017** (Civil and Commercial Arbitration Law): Standalone arbitration legislation modeled on the UNCITRAL Model Law [Law No. 2 of 2017](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Law_No._2_of_2017) \[1–81\].
- **Law No. 21 of 2021**: Established the [Investment and Commerce Court](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Investment_and_Commerce_Court), conferring exclusive judicial jurisdiction over IP disputes [Investment Court](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Investment_Court) \[1–81\].
- **Emiri Decree No. 29 of 2003**: Accession to the 1958 New York Convention on the Recognition and Enforcement of Foreign Arbitral Awards [New York Convention](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/New_York_Convention) \[1–81\].
- **Qatari Court of Cassation Rulings**: Judgment of June 20, 2023 (Cassation No. 359/2023), holding that court jurisdiction does not preclude parties from agreeing to arbitrate IP disputes [Court of Cassation](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Court_of_Cassation) \[1–81\].

## Background

Rooted in Arab legal tradition, amicable dispute resolution incorporates principles of *sulh* (reconciliation), *wasta* (mediation), and *hakam* (arbitration) [Sulh Tradition](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Sulh_Tradition) \[1–81\]. Qatar integrates these historical concepts with modern digital procedures to build a secure, arbitration-friendly investment environment [Arbitration Modernization](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Arbitration_Modernization) \[1–81\].

## Key Objectives

1.  Facilitate effective access to justice in IP disputes under SDG 16.3 [Access to Justice](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Access_to_Justice) \[1–81\].
2.  Reduce domestic court caseloads and expedite dispute resolution for foreign investors [Dispute Efficiency](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Dispute_Efficiency) \[1–81\].
3.  Drive national innovation under the Qatar Third National Development Strategy (2024–2030) [Development Strategy](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Development_Strategy) \[1–81\].
4.  Establish Qatar as a regional commercial arbitration hub in the Middle East [Regional Hub](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Regional_Hub) \[1–81\].

## Core Provisions

- **Arbitrability Criteria**: Under Law No. 2 of 2017, arbitration is restricted to commercial matters capable of legal compromise (\*reconciliation\*) [Arbitration Criteria](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Arbitration_Criteria) \[1–81\].
- **Digital Access**: The 2024 QICCA Arbitration Rules enable electronic filings and expedited proceedings [QICCA 2024](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/QICCA_2024) \[1–81\].
- **Sports IP Enforcement**: Specialized legislation such as Law No. 27 of 2004 (2006 Asian Games) and Law Nos. 10 & 11 of 2021 (FIFA World Cup Qatar 2022™) [Sports IP](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Sports_IP) \[1–81\].

## Implementation & Administration

- **Role of QICCA**: The Qatar International Center for Conciliation and Arbitration (QICCA) handled cases valued at nearly QR 3 billion in 2023 [QICCA Caseload](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/QICCA_Caseload) \[1–81\].
- **Investment and Commerce Court**: Utilizes a paperless digital system capable of resolving urgent cases within 67 days [Digital Court](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Digital_Court) \[1–81\].
- **International Cooperation**: MoU signed between QICCA and the WIPO Arbitration and Mediation Center [WIPO MoU](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/WIPO_MoU) \[1–81\].

## Policy Issues & Grey Zones

- **Absence of Explicit Statutory Wording**: Law No. 2 of 2017 does not explicitly mention the term "intellectual property," causing public policy ambiguities [Statutory Grey Zone](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Statutory_Grey_Zone) \[1–81\].
- **Specialized Expertise**: Ongoing need to expand the pool of technical IP experts and arbitrators in Qatar [Expert Capacity](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Expert_Capacity) \[1–81\].
