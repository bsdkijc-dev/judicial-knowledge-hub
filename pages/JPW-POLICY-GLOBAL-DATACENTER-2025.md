# JPW-POLICY-GLOBAL-DATACENTER-2025

**Global Legal Risks for Data Centers** is a global legal and environmental risk guidance report published by [Eversheds Sutherland](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Eversheds_Sutherland) on September 9, 2025, designed to assist investors and operators in navigating environmental obligations, energy demands, water management, and international arbitration strategies \[1–13\].

## Summary

As data centers become the foundational infrastructure of the global digital economy, their legal and environmental footprints are drawing unprecedented scrutiny from governments, regulators, and international courts \[1–13\]. This publication outlines emerging international environmental duties and details investment mitigation strategies through bilateral investment treaties (BITs) and international arbitration \[1–13\].

## Legal Basis & International Jurisprudence

State environmental obligations under international law have been reshaped by landmark decisions and advisory opinions from international judicial bodies:

- **Inter-American Court of Human Rights (2025)**: Recognized the human right to a healthy climate, mandating ecosystem protection and equitable water access \[1–13\].
- **International Court of Justice / ICJ (2025)**: Reaffirmed the legal obligation of States under international law to prevent and repair climate-related damage \[1–13\].
- **International Tribunal for the Law of the Sea / ITLOS (2024)**: Classified greenhouse gas emissions as marine pollution under [UNCLOS](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/UNCLOS) \[1–13\].

## Background

There are currently over 8.4 million data centers globally \[1–13\]. The rapid expansion of high-density computing and liquid cooling technologies has driven an unprecedented surge in electricity and freshwater consumption, exposing operators to heightened regulatory and environmental liability \[1–13\].

## Objectives

1.  Provide a strategic compliance framework for global data center energy and environmental investments \[1–13\].
2.  Identify risks associated with the unilateral revocation of local tax incentives, utility subsidies, and zoning permits by host governments \[1–13\].
3.  Encourage the adoption of international arbitration clauses and Bilateral Investment Treaties (BITs) to shield foreign investors from uncompensated expropriation \[1–13\].

## Key Provisions & Technical Metrics

- **Hyperscale Infrastructure Metrics**: Hyperscale facilities (\>10,000 m², 500,000 servers, minimum capacity of 100 MW) are projected to require double their current power access by 2030 \[1–13\].
- **Water Consumption Estimates**: Hyperscale data centers consume approximately 2.1 million liters of water per day (760 million liters annually), whereas medium-to-large facilities utilize 68,000 liters daily (24.9 million liters annually) \[1–13\].
- **Contractual Flexibility Clauses**: Mandates the integration of robust force majeure clauses and regulatory adaptation provisions within cross-border operational contracts \[1–13\].

## Implementation Recommendations

- **Environmental Compliance Audits**: Conduct comprehensive environmental and energy compliance audits across all operating assets \[1–13\].
- **Dispute Resolution Selection**: Prioritize international arbitration mechanisms over local domestic courts to resolve host-state disputes \[1–13\].
- **Geopolitical Risk Monitoring**: Continuously track international judicial rulings and regional legislative updates \[1–13\].

## Policy Issues & Exposure

- **Unilateral Incentive Restructuring**: The risk of host nations unilaterally revoking tax breaks, utility discounts, or power allocation rights \[1–13\].
- **Cross-Border Stakeholder Disputes**: Mounting tension between cloud tenants, facility operators, utility providers, and local communities over water rights and transboundary data flows \[1–13\].
