## 4. Policy Analysis

Background  
South Korea transitioned from a career judge system (직업법관제) to a career unification system (법조일원화) requiring significant legal experience before judicial appointment. Following the 2011 amendment to the Court Organization Act, minimum career requirements stepped up incrementally (3 years in 2013, 5 years in 2018, 7 years in 2022, and 10 years by 2026). Concurrently, national judge demographics experienced severe aging: the average judge age rose from 38.8 in 2003 to 42.6 in 2017 (and is projected to exceed 50 by 2030).

<!-- -->

Policy Problem  
**Imminent Chronic Shortage of Qualified Judicial Candidates**: Requiring 10 years of prior legal practice severely restricts candidate pools, making recruitment difficult and creating chronic judicial vacancies.

**Accelerating Judge Resignations & Former Judge Privilege Controversies (전관예우)**: Most Korean judges resign long before reaching the statutory retirement age of 65 (only 6 judges reached statutory retirement between 2013 and 2017 out of hundreds of resignations) to open private law practices, triggering public distrust over judicial bias.

**Inadequacy of Current Judicial Pension & Placement Structures**: Judges joining at older ages receive lower public official pensions (공무원연금), while frequent nationwide mandatory transfers (전보인사) discourage senior judges from remaining on the bench.

**Limitations of Existing "Wonro-Beopgwan" (원로법관) Rules**: Introduced in Feb 2017 (Judicial Personnel Rules Art. 11-3) for judges with 30+ years experience, the rule merely assigned former court presidents to 1st-instance single-judge panels with standard full-time workloads without formal supernumerary status or workload reduction.

<!-- -->

Legal/Policy Gap  
ROK law lacks a statutory supernumerary (정원 외) part-time senior judicial framework capable of retaining veteran judges past age 60 without occupying standard judicial quotas, allowing flexible caseload reductions, and providing stable pension-integrated compensation.

<!-- -->

Objectives  
Mitigate judge shortages; reduce caseload pressures; abolish early resignation incentives; enhance judicial independence; and utilize veteran judicial expertise.

<!-- -->

Implementation & 4 Evaluated Reform Options  
**Option 1 (Creation of New Senior Judge Title)**: Establishing a new senior judge post (retiring at 68–70, part-time/reduced caseload, salary + pension).

**Option 2 (Expansion of Exclusive Dedicated Judges / 전담법관)**: Extending retirement age to 68–70 for dedicated judges (modeled on Japan's Summary Court Judges).

**Option 3 (Supernumerary Part-Time Judges)**: Adopting Canadian/UK supernumerary status with part-time schedules.

**Option 4 (German-Style Pre-Retirement Part-Time Work)**: Work-hour reductions for judges aged 60+.

**Final Recommended Framework (Author's Model)**:

- *Eligibility*: Judges aged 60+ who completed two 10-year reappointments (20+ years service).
- *Status & Quota*: Designated as Supernumerary Judges (정원 외 시니어판사), freeing up active judicial quota slots for new appointments.
- *Workload & Schedule*: Part-time schedule (e.g., 3–4 days per week or 60%–80% standard caseload).
- *Compensation & Pension*: Combined per diem/salary + pension drawing.

<!-- -->

Evidence & Empirical Data  
**US Federal Senior Judges**: Over 500 active senior judges handle 20%–25%+ of total federal civil and criminal cases.

**Canada Supernumerary Judges**: 89% of eligible Canadian federal judges choose supernumerary status at age 65 or Rule of 80, representing 20% of the federal judiciary.

**ROK Resignation Statistics**: Annual judge resignations averaged 50–60 judges (85 in 2011, 72 in 2012, 62 in 2013, 67 in 2014, 56 in 2015, 47 in 2016), whereas statutory 65-year-old retirements totaled only 6 judges between 2013 and 2017.

## 6. MediaWiki Output

**A Research on Senior Judge Program** is an official judicial policy research report published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of South Korea's Supreme Court.

## Summary

This document is an official judicial policy research report published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of South Korea's Supreme Court. It comprehensively analyzes the Senior Judge Program (시니어판사 제도) as a strategic solution to the judge shortage crisis and judge aging resulting from full career judicial unification (전면적 법조일원화, requiring 10 years of legal career experience by 2026); it addresses the early resignation of senior judges, which fuels controversy over former-judge favoritism (전관예우); evaluates senior/supernumerary/part-time judge schemes in 10 global jurisdictions (US, Canada, UK, Singapore, Hong Kong, Taiwan, Germany, Netherlands, Spain, Japan); and formulates concrete recommendations for enacting an out-of-quota part-time Senior Judge Program (정원 외 시간제 시니어판사) in South Korea.

## Legal Basis

The legal frameworks analyzed include:

- **Constitution of the Republic of Korea**: Article 105(3) (10-Year Judicial Term).
- **Court Organization Act of South Korea (법원조직법)**: Article 42 (Minimum 10-Year Legal Career Requirement for Career Unification).
- **Judicial Personnel Rules (법원인사규칙)**: Article 11-3 (Senior Judge System / 원로법관제, effective February 2017).
- **United States Federal Code**: 28 U.S.C. § 371 (Retirement in Senior Status / Rule of 80).
- **Canada Judges Act**: R.S.C. 1985, c. J-1, s. 28 & s. 42 (Supernumerary Judges).
- **UK Constitutional Reform Act 2005**: Sections 38 & 39 (Acting Judges & Supplementary Panel).
- **Constitution of Singapore**: Article 95 (Senior Judges of the Supreme Court).
- **Japan Court Act (재판소법)**: Articles 44, 45, 50 (Summary Court Judges / 간이재판소 판사).

## Background

The full implementation of career judicial unification in South Korea, requiring a minimum of 10 years of legal career experience by 2026, risks triggering a judge supply crisis, an increase in average judge age (projected to exceed 50 by 2030), and case backlogs. Meanwhile, most senior judges resign well before the statutory retirement age of 65 (only 6 judges retired naturally between 2013 and 2017, while 50–67 judges resigned annually) to open law practices, fueling accusations of *jeongwan-yeowu* (전관예우). The Senior Judge Program is needed to recruit and retain dedicated senior judges.

## Objectives

1.  Address the chronic judge shortage crisis caused by full career judicial unification.
2.  Prevent the early resignation of senior judges and curb *jeongwan-yeowu* controversies.
3.  Analyze comparative senior/part-time judge schemes across 10 countries.
4.  Design an out-of-quota part-time Senior Judge Program (정원 외 시간제 시니어판사) compatible with the Korean Constitution.

## Scope

- **Ten-Country Comparative Study**:
  - **United States**: 28 U.S.C. § 371 (Rule of 80), the California AJP, New York JHO, Indiana, Georgia.
  - **Common Law Systems**: Canada (Supernumerary Judges), UK (Recorders & Supplementary Panel), Singapore (Senior Judges), Hong Kong (Non-Permanent Judges).
  - **Civil Law Systems**: Taiwan (法官優遇制度), Germany (Altersteilzeit), the Netherlands (Rechter-plaatsvervanger), Spain, Japan (간이재판소 판사).
- **Evaluation of 4 Reform Options**: Option 1 (New Senior Judge Position), Option 2 (Expansion of Dedicated Judges), Option 3 (Supernumerary Part-Time Judges), Option 4 (German-Style Part-Time Work).

## Key Provisions

- **Eligibility Criteria**: Judges aged 60 and above who have completed two 10-year terms (20+ years of service).
- **Supernumerary Status**: Designated as "정원 외 시니어판사" (outside the active quota), freeing up new active judicial slots for recruitment.
- **Part-Time Workload**: A part-time schedule (3–4 days per week, or 60%–80% of the standard workload).
- **Compensation & Pension**: A combination of part-time per diem/salary and pension drawdown.

## Implementation

The Supreme Court adopts a pilot scheme based on the Judicial Personnel Rules, proposes an amendment to the Court Organization Act for out-of-quota status, and adjusts the civil servant pension system accordingly.

## Policy Issues

- A shortage of qualified judicial candidates due to the 10-year experience requirement.
- Early resignation of senior judges fueling *jeongwan-yeowu* accusations.
- Barriers posed by pension structure and mandatory regional transfer rotation (전보인사).

## Reform / Amendments

1.  Enactment of the out-of-quota part-time Senior Judge Program (정원 외 시간제 시니어판사).
2.  Freeing up active judge quota slots for new recruitment when a judge takes senior status.
3.  Establishing a 60%–80% part-time workload standard and case-assignment flexibility.
4.  Reform of the judicial pension system to ensure financial stability for senior judges.

## Relationship to Other Entities

- **Issuing Institution**: Judicial Policy Research Institute & Supreme Court of Korea.
- **Research Team**: Seongjun Mo (모성준), Yongsung Seo (서용성), Yuna Choi (최유나).
