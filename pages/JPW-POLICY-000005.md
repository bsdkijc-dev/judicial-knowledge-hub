# JPW-POLICY-000005

The **Indonesian Post-Soeharto Legal and Constitutional Reform Policy Framework** (Stable ID: `JPW-POLICY-000005`) is a national constitutional, statutory, and institutional reform initiative established following the May 1998 downfall of President Soeharto. Documented under the IDE Asian Law Series No. 12 by Prof. Dr. C.F.G. Sunaryati Hartono, Prof. Dr. phil. Astrid S. Susanto, and RM Surachman, the framework outlines the transition of the Indonesian state from an autocratic executive regime (*Machtsstaat*) to a constitutional democracy governed by the supremacy of law (*Rechtsstaat*). Note: Named entities *Cecep Mustafa* and *Reihan Abdan Syakur* are *Not found in the available sources*.

## Summary

The framework addresses the institutional collapse and systemic corruption (*KKN*) inherited from 32 years of New Order rule. It establishes four core pillars of legal reform: (1) Constitutional Amendments restoring legislative supremacy to Parliament (DPR) and inserting a Bill of Human Rights; (2) Judicial Independence, mandating the 5-year transfer of court administration from the Ministry of Justice to the Supreme Court under Act No. 35 of 1999; (3) Administrative Supervision, establishing the National Ombudsman Commission (*KON*) to combat judicial and bureaucratic maladministration; and (4) Decentralization, granting wide regional autonomy to regencies and cities (*Kabupaten/Kota*) under Acts No. 22 and 25 of 1999.

## Legal Basis

The policy framework derives authority from constitutional amendments, MPR resolutions, and statutory enactments:

- **Constitutional Amendments:** 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia (First Amendment, Second Amendment, Third Amendment \[2001/2002\]).
- **MPR Resolutions:** TAP MPR No. III/MPR/2000 (Hierarchy of Legal Sources); TAP MPR No. VI/MPR/2000 & VII/MPR/2000 (Separation and Roles of TNI and POLRI); TAP MPR No. VII/MPR/2001 (Indonesia's Vision 2020).
- **Judicial & Human Rights Acts:** Act No. 35 of 1999 (Judicial Power Amendment); Act No. 39 of 1999 (Human Rights); Act No. 26 of 2000 (Human Rights Courts).
- **Political & Autonomy Acts:** Acts No. 2, 3, and 4 of 1999 (Political Parties, Elections, Structure of DPR/MPR); Act No. 22 of 1999 (Regional Government); Act No. 25 of 1999 (Financial Balance).
- **Executive Decrees:** Presidential Decree No. 44 of 2000 (National Ombudsman Commission); Presidential Decree No. 127/1999 (KPKPN).

## Background & Policy Problems

### Background

The 1997 Asian Monetary Crisis triggered the economic and political collapse of Soeharto's New Order. Decades of executive overreach had rendered courts subservient to the Ministry of Justice, fostered widespread bribery, and suppressed civil liberties. Student demonstrations in May 1998 forced Soeharto's resignation, sparking urgent demands for constitutional democracy and rule of law.

### Policy Problems

- Subservient judiciary under dual executive-judicial control ("brains controlled by Supreme Court, stomach by Justice Department"), rendering judges vulnerable to executive pressure and corruption (Judicial Mafia).
- Absence of administrative accountability and independent avenues for citizens to complain against bureaucratic maladministration, procedural delays, and judicial forgery.
- Hyper-centralization and siphoning of natural resource revenues from regions outside Java by the Jakarta central government, sparking regional disaffection.
- Military overreach (*Dwifungsi*) where Armed Forces (ABRI) controlled civilian administration and suppressed civil liberties.

## Objectives

1\. Replace autocratic executive dominance with Parliamentary legislative supremacy. 2. Guarantee full judicial independence by removing executive control over court budgets and personnel. 3. Establish independent external supervision over judicial malpractice via the National Ombudsman Commission. 4. Institutionalize constitutional guarantees for human rights (Arts. 28A–28J). 5. Separate the Military (TNI) from law enforcement (POLRI). 6. Devolve political and financial power to regional regencies and cities.

## Scope & Implementation

### Scope

Applies across all constitutional, judicial, administrative, and local government institutions in the Republic of Indonesia.

### Key Provisions

- **Shift in Legislative Power:** Power to form laws transferred from the President to Parliament (DPR, Art. 20) with automatic 30-day enactment rules.
- **Judicial Unification:** 5-year administrative transfer of all lower courts to the Supreme Court under Act 35/1999; introduction of dissenting opinions and non-career judges.
- **Ombudsman Jurisdiction:** Independent investigation of public maladministration, court delays, and judicial forgery under Pres. Decree 44/2000; incorporation of 59 Gottehrer principles into Draft Bill.
- **TNI/POLRI Repositioning:** TNI limited to state defense under the President; POLRI assigned to public security under public court jurisdiction.
- **Regional Autonomy:** Direct autonomy granted to regencies/cities (*Kabupaten/Kota*); revitalization of customary village governance (*Adat / Marga / Nagari*).

### Implementation

- **Legislative Pipeline:** Formulation of 80+ reform bills by Parliament.
- **Ombudsman Case Processing:** Handling over 1,000 complaints in 2000–2001, with 45% targeting judicial maladministration.
- **Judicial Recruitment:** Fit and proper testing of non-career supreme court justices by DPR.

## Reform

- Enactment of standalone Acts for the Parliamentary Ombudsman, Constitutional Court, Anti-Corruption Commission (KPK), and State Treasury.
- Complete organizational, administrative, and financial transfer of lower courts to the Supreme Court by 2004.

## Related Key Figures & Institutions

### Key Figures

- **Prof. Dr. C.F.G. Sunaryati Hartono, SH:** Professor of Law; Vice Chairperson and Deputy Chief Ombudsman of the National Ombudsman Commission; former Head of BPHN (1988–1996); principal author.
- **Prof. Dr. phil. Astrid S. Susanto:** Professor of Political Sociology and Political Communication at Sahid University & UI; Member of Parliament (DPR/MPR) serving as Vice Chairperson of the First Commission; co-author.
- **RM Surachman, SH:** APU Research Professor eqv.; Deputy Ombudsman of the National Ombudsman Commission; co-author.
- **Dr. Naoyuki Sakumoto:** Senior Research Fellow at IDE-JETRO; coordinator of the Indonesian legal reform study.
- **Antonius Sujata, SH:** Chairman / Chief Ombudsman of the National Ombudsman Commission; former Deputy Attorney General.
- **Prof. Dr. Bagir Manan, SH, MCL:** Chief Justice of the Supreme Court of Indonesia; Professor of Constitutional Law at Padjadjaran University; non-career judge; co-drafter of Ombudsman Bill.
- **President Soeharto:** Second President of Indonesia (1966–1998); ruler of the "New Order" regime who resigned on May 21, 1998.
- **President B.J. Habibie:** Third President of Indonesia (1998–1999); enacted early political laws and local autonomy legislation.
- **President K.H. Abdurrahman Wahid:** Fourth President of Indonesia (1999–2001); established National Law Commission and National Ombudsman Commission.
- **President Megawati Soekarnoputri:** Fifth President of Indonesia (2001–2004).
- **Dean M. Gottehrer:** American International Ombudsman Consultant; author of 59 universal principles of ombudsmanship incorporated into Indonesia's Draft Ombudsman Bill.
