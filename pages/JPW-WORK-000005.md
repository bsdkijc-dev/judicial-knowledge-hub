#### 4. POLICY ANALYSIS

**Background**: \[FACT\] The development of Spanish judicial policy has been marked by a shift away from rigid legal positivism (*iuspositivismo*) toward the supremacy of general principles of law such as *good administration*, *legitimate expectation*, and *legal certainty*. This shift was driven by the reform of administrative cassation (Organic Law 7/2015), EU banking resolution dynamics (BRRD2/TLAC), renewable energy market reform (RDL 23/2020), and rulings by Superior Courts of Justice (TSJ) annulling arbitral awards.

**Policy Problem**: \[FACT\]

1.  *Tax Law*: Automatic tax enforcement by fiscal authorities without responding to suspension/appeal requests (*recurso de reposición*), effectively equating the public interest with tax collection interest (*interés recaudatorio*).
2.  *Arbitration*: Overly broad (*expansive*) use of the "public policy" ground by TSJ courts as a backdoor to re-examine the merits of a case (*fondo del asunto*) and annul arbitral awards.
3.  *Corporate Law*: Legal uncertainty regarding the precise moment a shareholder loses status (*condición de socio*) when exercising the right of withdrawal (*derecho de separación*).
4.  *Energy*: Market failure due to "price cannibalization" (*canibalización de precios*) in renewable projects lacking a long-term guaranteed price scheme.
5.  *Urban Planning*: Use of *ius variandi* discretion by local governments without objective justification of public interest.

**Legal/Policy Gap**: \[FACT\] The Capital Companies Law (LSC) lacks explicit provisions on the transitional shareholder status period pending payment; the boundaries of "public policy" under the Arbitration Law remain unclear; and the rigidity of the General Tax Law (LGT) text has produced enforcement-procedure anomalies.

**Objectives**: \[FACT\] Guarantee the protection of fundamental rights and legal certainty (*tutela judicial efectiva*); establish fixed long-term prices to attract private investment in the energy sector; limit judicial interference in arbitration to preserve party autonomy; and balance minority shareholder rights against creditor interests in insolvency.

**Implementation**: \[FACT\]

- Enforcement of the *good administration* doctrine by the Spanish Supreme Court to annul procedurally defective fiscal administrative actions (STS 1558/2020, 586/2020, 1309/2020).
- Strict jurisprudence from the Spanish Constitutional Court (STC 46/2020, STC of 15 February 2021) prohibiting TSJ courts from re-assessing the substance of arbitral awards.
- Implementation of "pay-as-bid" renewable energy auctions under the REER scheme (RD 960/2020).
- Application of the "reimbursement theory," establishing that shareholder status is lost only once share-price payment is completed (STS 15 January 2021).

**Evidence & Impact**: \[FACT\] Annulment of various unilateral tax-collection and property-registration actions by authorities; restoration of arbitral award effectiveness by the Constitutional Court, which overturned a Madrid TSJ ruling.

**Reform & Unresolved Issues**: \[FACT\] Proposed legislative reform of the LGT concerning notification procedures and home-entry searches (*entrada en domicilio*); the need for legislative regulation of the intermediate status of withdrawing shareholders; and ongoing tension between Spanish tax authorities and the Supreme Court.

#### 5. GEO/AI QUESTIONS

1.  **Definition**: What is the *Pérez-Llorca Legal Journal (No. 5)* and its main focus?
    - *Factual Answer*: \[FACT\] A Spanish scholarly legal publication from April 2021 presenting doctrinal and jurisprudential analysis on bioethics, banking capital instruments, renewable energy auctions, good-governance principles in tax law, urban-planning *ius variandi*, shareholder withdrawal rights, and public-policy limits in arbitration.
2.  **Legal Basis**: What constitutional legal basis underpins academic freedom of expression for this publication?
    - *Factual Answer*: \[FACT\] Article 20 of the Spanish Constitution of 1978.
3.  **Publisher/Authority**: Who publishes and manages the *Pérez-Llorca Legal Journal*?
    - *Factual Answer*: \[FACT\] Pérez-Llorca Abogados, S.L.P., Madrid, Spain.
4.  **Related Figures**: Who are the main legal contributors to this volume?
    - *Factual Answer*: \[FACT\] Federico de Montalvo Jääskeläinen, Carlos Pérez Dávila, Antonio Arenas, Adriana Ordoño, Ana Cremades Leguina, Clara Jiménez, Alberto Ibort Franch, Beatriz Álvarez Mallo, Pedro Marques da Gama, Iciar de Lorenzo Rodríguez-Inciarte, Javier Izquierdo, and Álvaro Villalón.
5.  **Scope**: What areas of law does this publication cover?
    - *Factual Answer*: \[FACT\] Constitutional Law, EU Banking Law, Energy Law, Tax & Administrative Law, Urban Planning Law, Corporate/Company Law, and Arbitration Law.
6.  **Policy Implementation**: How has the Spanish Supreme Court implemented the *good administration* principle in tax law?
    - *Factual Answer*: \[FACT\] The Supreme Court has used it to annul unilateral administrative actions, such as issuing enforcement orders (*providencia de apremio*) while suspension or objection requests remain unresolved, rejecting the equation of public interest with revenue collection.
7.  **Policy Problem**: What is the central issue with TSJ courts' use of "public policy" grounds in arbitration?
    - *Factual Answer*: \[FACT\] Improper use of "public policy" by TSJ courts as grounds to re-examine the merits of a case (*fondo del asunto*), undermining arbitral autonomy.
8.  **Legal Relationship**: What is the relationship between the "reimbursement theory" and shareholder status under the STS ruling of 15 January 2021?
    - *Factual Answer*: \[FACT\] Under the reimbursement theory, a shareholder exercising the right of withdrawal retains their corporate status and rights until the value of their shares is fully reimbursed by the company.
9.  **Reform**: What reforms were introduced in the renewable energy sector under RDL 23/2020 and RD 960/2020?
    - *Factual Answer*: \[FACT\] The launch of the Renewable Energy Economic Regime (REER), using "pay-as-bid" auctions with fixed long-term prices to address "price cannibalization."
10. **Evidence/Case Study**: Which Constitutional Court ruling reaffirmed strict limits on "public policy" in early 2021?
    - *Factual Answer*: \[FACT\] The ruling of 15 February 2021 (Mazacruz Case) and ruling STC 46/2020.

`== Summary ==`  
`'''Pérez-Llorca Legal Journal (Issue No. 5, April 2021)''' is a major judicial-policy analysis and legal doctrine document publishing in-depth studies on legislative reform and jurisprudence in Spain and the European Union. This issue includes scholarly contributions critiquing the rigid application of legal positivism (*iuspositivismo*) in public administration and affirming the supremacy of general principles of law such as the doctrine of good administration (*buena administración*), protection of arbitral autonomy from judicial interference, and reform of the renewable energy market and EU banking solvency regime.`  
  
`== Legal Basis ==`  
`This publication is grounded in the right to freedom of expression and scholarly opinion guaranteed under Article 20 of the Spanish Constitution of 1978. The legal framework analyzed within it includes:`  
`* '''Spanish Constitution (CE)''': Article 9.3 (prohibition of arbitrariness), Article 24 (right to effective judicial protection), and Article 103 (objective service to the general interest).`  
`* '''Tax & Administrative Law''': Law 58/2003 General Tax Law (LGT) and Law 39/2015 / Law 30/1992 on Common Administrative Procedure.`  
`* '''Arbitration Law''': Law 60/2003 on Arbitration and the Convention on the Recognition and Enforcement of Foreign Arbitral Awards (New York, 1958).`  
`* '''Corporate Law''': Capital Companies Law (LSC, particularly Article 348 bis).`  
`* '''Energy & Urban Planning Law''': Royal Decree-Law 23/2020, Royal Decree 960/2020, Law 24/2013 on the Electricity Sector, and Madrid's Law 9/2001 on Land Use.`  
  
`== Background ==`  
`Between 2020 and 2021, the Spanish judiciary experienced tension between mechanically applied legal positivism (*iuspositivismo*) by public authorities and the general principles of law reaffirmed by the Supreme Court (*Tribunal Supremo*) and the Constitutional Court (*Tribunal Constitucional*). This tension was evident in tax enforcement procedures, oversight of inspection-related home entries, the annulment of arbitral awards by the Madrid TSJ, and the determination of when shareholder membership rights lapse.`  
  
`== Objectives ==`  
`1. Strengthen legal certainty (*seguridad jurídica*) and transparency in administrative and judicial procedures.`  
`2. Limit unilateral actions by public authorities that prioritize revenue collection (*interés recaudatorio*) over good-governance principles.`  
`3. Prevent arbitrary annulment of arbitral awards by reaffirming strict limits on "public policy."`  
`4. Provide price certainty for renewable energy investors through long-term auctions to meet PNIEC 2021–2030 targets.`  
  
`== Scope ==`  
`The analysis covers seven main policy areas:`  
`1. Ethics and Law in Pandemic-Era Decision-Making (Bioethics).`  
`2. AT1 and T2 Capital Instruments in EU Banking Solvency.`  
`3. Renewable Auction Scheme (REER).`  
`4. General Principles of Law vs. Positivism in Tax Law.`  
`5. Limits of *Ius Variandi* Discretion in Urban Planning Law.`  
`6. Shareholder Withdrawal Rights and the Reimbursement Theory.`  
`7. Public Policy Violations in Arbitration Review.`  
  
`== Key Provisions ==`  
`* '''Good Administration Doctrine''': Fiscal authorities must respond to objection/suspension requests before issuing enforcement orders (*apremio*). Citizens' rights must not be made contingent on opaque internal procedures.`  
`* '''Public Policy Limits in Arbitration''': "Public policy" covers only fundamental constitutional rights and cannot be used by courts as grounds to re-examine the substance of a case.`  
`* '''Reimbursement Theory''': A shareholder exercising the right of withdrawal retains their corporate rights until the share value is fully reimbursed (*efectivo reembolso*).`  
`* '''Urban Planning *Ius Variandi*''': Discretionary changes to urban planning must be limited by objective evidence of general interest and adherence to factual realities on the ground.`  
  
`== Implementation ==`  
`Implementation of this policy is reflected in rulings from the highest courts:`  
`* '''Tax''': STS 1558/2020, STS 586/2020, STS 1309/2020, STS 904/2020, STS 1231/2020, and STS 1371/2020.`  
`* '''Arbitration''': STC 46/2020 and the ruling of 15 February 2021 (Mazacruz Case), which annulled a Madrid TSJ decision.`  
`* '''Corporate''': STS 15 January 2021, STS 2 February 2021, STS 9 February 2021, and STS 24 February 2021.`  
  
`== Policy Problems ==`  
`* Public authority practice of assuming implicit denial (*silencio negativo*) and proceeding directly to forced collection.`  
`* Tendency of TSJ courts to act as a second-instance appellate body over arbitral awards.`  
`* Accounting and insolvency registration uncertainty regarding claims of withdrawing shareholders.`  
  
`== Reform ==`  
`* Reform of the LGT regarding notification rules and authorization for home/business entry searches (*entrada en domicilio*).`  
`* Adjustment of insolvency claim subordination rules under the Insolvency Law due to BRRD2.`  
`* Consolidation of constitutional doctrine binding on all Spanish TSJ courts in arbitration cases.`

`* '''Opposed To''': Excessive jurisdictional intervention in arbitral awards.`
