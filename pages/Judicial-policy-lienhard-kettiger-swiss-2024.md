# Judicial-policy-lienhard-kettiger-swiss-2024

**Twenty Years of Research Into the Swiss Justice System** is a policy research article and scientific review authored by Prof. Dr. iur. Andreas Lienhard and Mag. rer. publ. Daniel Kettiger of the Center for Public Management (KPM), University of Bern, documenting 20 years of research into judicial governance in Switzerland.

## Summary

The article traces the paradigm shift in Swiss judicial research from purely procedural law analysis toward the scientific discipline of court management, New Public Management (NPM), e-justice, and judicial organization. It reviews the major projects supported by the Swiss National Science Foundation (SNSF), such as "Basic Research into Court Management in Switzerland" and "Open Justice v. Privacy," as well as the international integration of Swiss researchers within IACA, EGPA, and CEPEJ.

## Legal Basis

- **Swiss Criminal Procedure Code (CrimPC / SR 312.0):** In force since 1 January 2011, unifying cantonal criminal procedure and abolishing the position of investigative magistrate \[49–52\].
- **Swiss Federal Constitution (Article 30):** Governs guarantees of judicial independence, fair trial, and the composition of judicial panels (\*Spruchkörperbildung\*).
- **Cantonal Judicial Organization Acts:** Apply New Public Management (NPM) principles to courts in the cantons of Bern, Lucerne, Solothurn, and Zurich \[11, 19–20\].

## Background

Until the late 1990s, judicial research in Europe and Switzerland focused on procedural law and largely ignored court administration. Growing workloads, increasingly complex legal materials, and limited budgets created the need for efficient court management without compromising judicial independence \[5–9, 13–14\].

## Objectives

1\. Analyze the development of court management research in Switzerland over two decades \[2–5\]. 2. Provide an empirical foundation for court organization and efficiency \[25–30\]. 3. Analyze the challenges of digitalization, privacy (judgment anonymization), and artificial intelligence (AI) \[34–39\]. 4. Develop weighted caseload systems for determining court staffing needs \[40–45\].

## Scope

The paper covers analysis of internal court organization (resources, processes, structure, culture), the status of public prosecutors following the 2011 CrimPC reform, automation of judicial panel formation, representation of female judges, and Swiss institutions for judicial education and publication.

## Key Provisions

- **NPM as the Research Nucleus:** Integration of results-based management into cantonal courts \[3, 9, 19–20\].
- **Weighted Caseload Studies (PEBB§Y):** Application of weighted caseload methods at the Federal Administrative Court and cantonal courts \[40–48\].
- **Hybrid Status of Public Prosecutors:** The position of prosecutors as a \*compositum mixtum\* between the executive and judicial branches following the 2011 reform \[49–55\].
- **Open Justice vs. Privacy:** Research into re-identification risks in anonymized court judgments using Legal NLP and AI \[34–39\].
- **Automation of Judicial Panels:** Constitutional requirements for IT-based automated formation of judicial panels \[55–58\].

## Implementation

Implementation occurs through the interdisciplinary SNSF research project involving 6 universities (Bern, Lucerne, St. Gallen, Zurich, Lausanne, Utrecht) \[26–33\], the establishment of the Swiss Judicial Academy (CAS in Judiciary program) \[76–77\], eJustice.CH \[79–83\], and regular publication in the \*Justice - Justiz - Giustizia\* journal and the \*Schriftenreihe zur Justizforschung\* book series \[85–88\].

## Policy Problems

1\. The absence of a consolidated national empirical statistical database for the judiciary in Switzerland. 2. The risk that artificial intelligence (AI) could defeat the anonymization of court judgments published online \[35–39\]. 3. Tension between judicial autonomy and algorithmic automation of judicial panel formation \[55–58\]. 4. Uncertainty regarding the broader economic impact of court decisions \[97–98\].

## Reform / Change

- Abolition of the investigative magistrate position under the 2011 criminal procedure reform \[49–50\].
- A shift from manual to IT-automated judicial panel formation \[55–58\].
- Transition toward online (video) court hearings \[88–90\].
- Establishment of justice committees in various cantons \[56–59\].

## Relationships with Other Entities

- **KPM University of Bern:** Leading institution conducting judicial research in Switzerland \[26, 32, 60–62\].
- **International Association for Court Administration (IACA):** Publication platform via IJCA.
- **Swiss Judicial Academy:** Official training body for prospective judges and clerks \[76–77\].
