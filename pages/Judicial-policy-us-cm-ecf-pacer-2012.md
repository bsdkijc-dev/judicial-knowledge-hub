# Judicial-policy-us-cm-ecf-pacer-2012

The **U.S. Federal Courts Case Management/Electronic Case Files - CM/ECF and PACER Framework** is a policy framework, technical architecture, and implementation strategy for electronic case filing and management systems (*CM/ECF*) and public access to court electronic records (*PACER*) across the entire United States Federal Courts system \[1–3, 25\].

## Summary

Published by J. Michael Greenwood and Gary Bockweg in the *International Journal for Court Administration* (2012), this policy framework evaluates the key success factors of the national implementation of the CM/ECF and PACER systems. Developed since 1995 by the *Administrative Office of the U.S. Courts* (AOUSC), the system successfully eliminated conventional paper files and replaced them with standard PDF electronic documents as official court records (*official record*), processing tens of millions of docket entries and hundreds of millions of public accesses annually.

## Legal Basis

- **U.S. E-Government Act of 2002 & 28 U.S.C. § 1913:** Regulates the authority to collect PACER fees to fund court automation and expand public access.
- **Federal Rules of Civil, Criminal, and Bankruptcy Procedure (e.g., FRCP Rule 5):** Establishes technical provisions and the legal validity of electronic case filing (*e-filing*).
- **Bankruptcy Abuse Prevention and Consumer Protection Act of 2005:** Regulatory basis for handling mass bankruptcy cases.
- **Judicial Conference Policy on Public Access to Electronic Court Records:** Policy on the openness of court records and data privacy protection.

## Background

Prior to CM/ECF, the U.S. Federal Courts, consisting of 758 court facilities, relied on a traditional paper filing system that was expensive, slow, and prone to loss. The project was developed gradually, starting from an asbestos case prototype in Ohio (1995) and bankruptcy cases in New York (1996), until it eventually covered all first-instance and appellate courts in 2005.

## Objectives

1\. Integrate online filing (*e-filing*), file management (*e-files*), docket entries (*e-docket*), notifications (*e-noticing*), and public access (*PACER*) into a single unified database. 2. Establish PDF-based electronic documents as the official court record. 3. Ensure the operational reliability of the system 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, 365 days a year. 4. Facilitate transparent access to court records for advocates, the public, and the media.

## Scope

This policy applies nationally across all levels of the U.S. Federal Courts:

- 12 Courts of Appeals.
- 94 District Courts (Civil & Criminal).
- 90 Bankruptcy Courts.
- U.S. Court of International Trade.
- \*(Exception)\*: The U.S. Supreme Court manages a separate, independent ICT system.

## Key Provisions

- **Single PDF Mandatory Standard:** All documents must be uploaded in PDF/A format verified by a validation program.
- **Password & Hash Table Authentication:** Logins and passwords are legally recognized as valid electronic signatures (*legal signature*), protected by *hash table* security stamp encryption.
- **Notice of Electronic Filing (NEF):** Automated notifications sent via email within seconds of registration, functionally replacing the conventional legal process of service.
- **Self-Redaction Privacy System:** Filers are required to redact sensitive data (social security numbers, bank accounts) before files are made public on PACER.
- **Self-Sustaining PACER Fees:** Nominal query fees (\$0.08–\$0.10/page) are reinvested to fund the maintenance of judicial ICT systems.

## Implementation

- **Data Processing Functions (DPF):** Adoption of a PERL/Java-based mini-programming language that allows non-technical court staff to modify entry templates according to local rules.
- **Disaster Resilience:** The system proved to remain fully operational without loss of official records during the 9/11/2001 terrorist crisis in New York and Hurricane Katrina in 2005 in New Orleans.
- **U.S. Treasury Integration:** Filing fee payments are directly linked to the online payment systems of the U.S. Department of the Treasury.

## Policy Problems

- **2005 Bankruptcy Caseload Surge:** Amendments to the Bankruptcy Act triggered case filing increases of up to 1000% in some districts, which the system handled successfully without delays.
- **Data Security and Privacy:** The need for *layered protection* oversight to prevent hacking and ensure the protection of distributed/sealed documents.

## Reform / Change

- Establishment of *Court User Development Committees* that periodically evaluate system needs.
- Implementation of a mentoring program between senior courts and newly adopting courts.
- Adoption of the *NextGen CM/ECF* system, which includes full-text searching and judicial conflict checking features.

### 3 Most Important Improvements
