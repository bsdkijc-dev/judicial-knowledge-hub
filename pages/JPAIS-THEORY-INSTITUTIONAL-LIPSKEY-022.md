#### 1. Regulatory Framework, Administrative Screening, and Minimizing Formal Dismissals

**\[SOURCE EVIDENCE\]:** Under Japanese civil procedure presented by JICA Expert Yusuke Shima, complaint intake involves a multi-tier administrative screening mechanism:

- **Administrative Clerk Correction Advice (*Anjuran Koreksi Panitera* per Rule 56):** The court clerk (*panitera*) reviews the complaint for formal deficiencies (obscure *petitum*, incomplete descriptions) and requests informal corrections.
- **Judicial Correction Order (*Perintah Koreksi Hakim* per CCP Art. 137):** If formal errors persist, the judge issues a formal correction order.
- **Jurisdictional Transfer (Pasal 16 UU HAP):** If filed in an incompetent court, the case is flexibly transferred rather than dismissed.
- **Substantive Resolution Focus:** Crucially, Japanese civil procedure minimizes formal rejections (*Putusan Tidak Dapat Diterima* / NO). Formal requirements (*keabsahan gugatan* / *syarat beracara*: legal interest, party capacity, standing) are resolved during pre-trial issue structuring. Vague petitums or pleadings are clarified during pre-trial rather than resulting in formal dismissal.

**\[THEORETICAL INFERENCE\]:** In Institutional Theory terms, this administrative pre-screening prevents institutional decoupling—where courts accept flawed pleadings blindly, leading to multi-year litigation that ends in empty formal rejections (*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard* / NO). In Indonesia, civil courts under HIR/RBg frequently issue NO rulings after years of trial, forcing litigants to re-file from scratch. Adapting Japanese administrative correction notices (*Anjuran Koreksi*) and pre-trial issue clarification into Indonesia's e-Court system (RaPerMA) ensures that cases are decided on substantive merits rather than procedural traps.

#### 2. Judicial / Institutional Structure & Organizational Capacity (5M Framework)

Applying the 5M Framework to Yusuke Shima's presentation illuminates the structural mechanics of digital pre-trial proceedings:

Man (Human Capital & Administrative Synergy)  
**\[SOURCE EVIDENCE\]:** Case progress relies on administrative synergy between the Court Clerk (*Panitera*) (handling Rule 56 correction advice) and the Judge (*Hakim*) (issuing Article 137 orders, conducting pre-trial sessions, and giving *Wakai* advice).

**\[THEORETICAL INFERENCE\]:** In Organizational Sociology, empowering court clerks to handle routine formal screening frees judicial capacity for substantive issue evaluation and settlement guidance.

<!-- -->

Method (Procedural Methodology & Three-Phase Simulation)  
**\[SOURCE EVIDENCE\]:** Digital pre-trial proceedings follow a strict 3-phase methodology:

  
**Phase 1 (Initial Stage):** Utilizing Pleading Comparison Tables (*Tabel Penataan Dalil Para Pihak*) to map plaintiff and defendant claims side-by-side, clarifying vague assertions and evidence necessity.

**Phase 2 (Evidence Structuring):** Utilizing Evidence Description Lists (*Daftar Uraian Alat Bukti*) and Document Production Requests (*Permohonan Meminta Pengiriman Dokumen* per CCP Art. 226) to compel third parties holding documents to send official copies.

**Phase 3 (Pre-Trial Closing & Result Declaration):** Finalizing the issue map and formally declaring pre-trial results in open court under Article 173 Code of Civil Procedure, locking the scope of subsequent open-court witness examinations.

Material (Standardized Pleading Tables & Document Production Notices)  
**\[SOURCE EVIDENCE\]:** Pre-trial management uses standardized pleading comparison tables, evidence description lists, and Article 226 third-party document production forms.

Money (Trial Economy & Systemic Efficiency)  
**\[EMPIRICAL EVIDENCE\]:** Pre-trial sessions are conducted ~1 time per month, with verdicts delivered within 30 days post-trial. Conducting pre-trial remotely eliminates travel costs and dramatically speeds up dispute finality.

**\[SOURCE EVIDENCE\]:** Pre-trial sessions are conducted via remote online meetings (*rapat daring*) using video conferencing platforms (Webex, Zoom, Teams), allowing litigating parties and counsel to participate digitally without physical court attendance.

Legal Culture & Social Structure  
**\[SOURCE EVIDENCE\]:** Japanese civil justice prioritizes substantive dispute resolution over formalistic dismissal. By resolving formal defects during complaint intake and pre-trial, courts ensure that almost all cases end in substantive judgments (*dikabulkan* or *ditolak*) rather than formal rejections (*tidak dapat diterima*).

<!-- -->

Street-Level Bureaucracy vs. Judicial Discretion  
**\[THEORETICAL INFERENCE\]:** Lipsky's Street-Level Bureaucracy framework requires distinguishing court clerks from judges:

- **Court Clerks (*Panitera*):** Exercise frontline administrative discretion under Rule 56 to review complaints, issue informal correction advice (*Anjuran Koreksi*), assist in remote online meeting setups (*rapat daring*), and verify pleading table formatting.
- **Judges (*Hakim*):** Possess curial discretion and judicial independence. Judges exercise discretion when issuing Article 137 formal correction orders, leading digital pre-trial sessions, giving *Wakai* settlement advice, granting Article 226 third-party document production requests, and declaring pre-trial results in open court under Article 173 to lock trial scope.

#### 4. Implementation & Business Experience

**\[SOURCE EVIDENCE\]:** In commercial contract, debt recovery, and inheritance disputes (e.g., unauthorized bank account withdrawals by a common-law partner), unmanaged litigation characterized by vague pleadings and withheld documents creates severe business disruption.

**\[THEORETICAL INFERENCE\]:** By conducting digital pre-trial sessions via remote video meetings (*rapat daring*), utilizing pleading comparison tables, compelling third-party document discovery under Article 226 CCP, and declaring pre-trial results in open court under Article 173 CCP, the judicial system guarantees high regulatory predictability, low administrative burden, and rapid contract enforcement certainty.

### C. Synthesis of Evidence vs. Epistemological Uncertainty

#### What is Firmly Supported by Source Evidence

- Japanese civil procedure follows a 5-stage pipeline: Complaint Intake → Answer → Digital Pre-Trial → Intensive Evidence Exam → *Wakai*/Verdict within 30 days.
- Complaint intake utilizes *Panitera* correction advice (Rule 56) and Judicial correction orders (Art. 137 CCP).
- Jurisdictional errors are handled via flexible case transfer (Art. 16 CCP).
- Pre-trial proceedings are closed to the public, conducted ~1 time per month, utilize remote online video meetings (*rapat daring*), and include judicial *Wakai* advice.
- Formal lawsuit requirements (*keabsahan gugatan* / *syarat beracara*: legal interest, party capacity, standing) are resolved during pre-trial, ensuring most cases end on substantive merits rather than formal rejections (NO).
- Simulation case background: Heir child vs. common-law wife over unauthorized bank account withdrawals.
- Simulation operates in 3 phases: Phase 1 (Pleading comparison tables), Phase 2 (Evidence description lists & Art. 226 document production), Phase 3 (Pre-trial closing & Art. 173 open court result declaration).

#### Explicit Uncertainty / Source Limits (**\[UNCERTAIN\]**)

- **Specific Indonesian District Court Empirical Metrics:** Source D7.Simulasi_Prosedur_PreTrial_dengan_Pemanfaatan_Rapat_Daring.pdf provides Japanese civil procedure workflows, statutory articles, and simulation slides, but does not contain specific quantitative empirical disposition statistics for Indonesian District Courts (*Pengadilan Negeri*).
- **Non-Judicial Administrative Licensing:** The source focuses strictly on civil pre-trial procedure, digital video conferencing, administrative pleading correction, and document discovery; it contains no data on non-court business licensing software (e.g., OSS RBA) or regulatory agencies in Indonesia.

### D. Policy Recommendations for Indonesian Civil Procedure Reform (RaPerMA)

Based on JICA Expert Yusuke Shima's presentation on digital pre-trial proceedings (D7.Simulasi_Prosedur_PreTrial_dengan_Pemanfaatan_Rapat_Daring.pdf):

1.  **Institutionalize Digital Pre-Trial Meetings (*Rapat Daring*) in e-Court:** Amend Supreme Court Regulations (RaPerMA) to authorize remote video conferencing for pre-trial issue and evidence structuring, allowing counsel to participate digitally without physical travel.
2.  **Empower Court Clerks for Administrative Pleading Correction (*Anjuran Koreksi*):** Codify an equivalent to Rule 56, enabling court clerks during e-Court intake to issue informal correction advice and Q&A forms to clarify vague pleadings before the first trial hearing.
3.  **Adopt Standardized Pleading Comparison Tables (*Tabel Penataan Dalil*):** Mandate that litigating parties during e-Court pre-trial submit side-by-side pleading tables to map agreed vs. disputed facts clearly.
4.  **Codify Third-Party Document Production Orders (*Pasal 226 UU HAP*):** Amend civil procedure rules to establish a streamlined motion process authorizing courts to order third-party banks and document holders to produce official records.
5.  **Mandate Open Court Pre-Trial Result Declarations (*Pasal 173 UU HAP*):** Require judges to declare pre-trial result summaries in open court, creating a binding roadmap that restricts open-court witness examinations strictly to core disputed facts.
