## Overview

Supreme Court Decision No. 75 K/AG/2003 dated May 14, 2004, establishes two fundamental legal rules in Islamic Civil Procedure Law and Islamic Grant Law:

- **Scope of Application of Law No. 20 of 1947:** Law No. 20 of 1947 is a specialized statute governing appellate proceedings (\*peradilan ulangan\*); hence, the provisions of Article 7 paragraph (1) of Law No. 20 of 1947 cannot be applied to the formal requirements for drafting or signing statements of claim (\*surat gugat\*) at the first-instance court level.
- **Obligation to Detail Total Assets in Grant Lawsuits (Obscuur Libel):** Before applying the provisions of Article 210 paragraph (1) of the Compilation of Islamic Law (KHI), which restricts grants to a maximum of one-third of the grantor's estate, a plaintiff is legally obligated to describe and detail the total overall assets belonging to the grantor within the statement of claim. If the statement of claim fails to specify or itemize the grantor's total estate, leaving it unclear whether the grant exceeds the one-third estate limit, the lawsuit is deemed obscure (\*obscuur libel\*) and must be declared inadmissible (\*niet ontvankelijke verklaard\*).

## Case Background

The married couple H. Tuganal and \[Party B\] passed away in 2001 in Tembilahan, Indragiri Hilir. Each brought children from prior marriages: H. Tuganal was survived by \[Party A\] and Zaidah, while \[Party B\] was survived by Susiati and Yuniasih. During his lifetime, H. Tuganal granted a plot of land measuring 147.6 m² complete with a wooden house located at Jalan Jend. Sudirman, Tembilahan, to his wife (\[Party B\]) on May 17, 2001, pursuant to which Building Use Rights Certificate (\*HGB\*) No. 558 was issued. Following the deaths of both H. Tuganal and \[Party B\], the land and house were occupied by H. Tuganal's children (\[Party A\], Zaidah, et al.).

Susiati and Yuniasih (\[Party B\]'s children / Plaintiffs) filed a lawsuit at the Tembilahan Religious Court. The Tembilahan Religious Court (Decision No. 20/Pdt.G/2002/PA.Tbh dated May 16, 2002) granted the Plaintiffs' lawsuit in part and declared H. Tuganal's grant to \[Party B\] valid. However, the Riau High Religious Court (Decision No. 27/Pdt.G/2002/PTA.Pbr dated August 24, 2002) annulled the Tembilahan Religious Court decision and declared the Plaintiffs' lawsuit inadmissible on the grounds of formal power of attorney signing defects pursuant to Law No. 20 of 1947. Dissatisfied with the appellate ruling, the Plaintiffs (Susiati and Yuniasih) filed a cassation petition to the Supreme Court.

## Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

- **Ratio Decidendi:**

1.  Law No. 20 of 1947 is a statute enacted for appellate-level proceedings; therefore, Article 7 paragraph (1) thereof cannot be applied to the drafting of statements of claim at the first-instance level.
2.  Prior to applying Article 210 paragraph (1) of the KHI, the Plaintiff must first establish the total aggregate amount of the grantor's estate to determine whether the contested grant exceeds the one-third limit of the grantor's assets.
3.  Because the statement of claim fails to clarify whether the disputed property constituted the sole asset of the grantor or whether other assets existed, making it impossible to ascertain whether the grant exceeded one-third of the grantor's estate, the lawsuit is legally obscure (\*obscuur libel\*) and must be declared inadmissible (\*niet ontvankelijke verklaard\*).

- **Ruling:** Granting the cassation petition from Petitioners for Cassation SUSIATI and YUNIASIH; Annulling the Riau High Religious Court decision dated August 24, 2002, No. 27/Pdt.G/2002/PTA.Pbr and the Tembilahan Religious Court decision dated May 16, 2002, No. 20/Pdt.G/2002/PA.Tbh; and Independently Adjudicating: (1) Declaring that the Plaintiffs' lawsuit is inadmissible; (2) Declaring that the Confiscation Security (\*Sita Jaminan\*) executed by the Tembilahan Religious Court is invalid and devoid of legal force; (3) Ordering the Tembilahan Religious Court to lift the said Confiscation Security; (4) Ordering the Defendants to pay first-instance court costs amounting to Rp 1,623,500; (5) Ordering the Appellee to pay appellate-level court costs amounting to Rp 107,000; (6) Ordering the Petitioner for Cassation to pay cassation-level court costs amounting to Rp 500,000.
