# Judicial-policy-encj-retrenchment-2012

**Judiciary In Times Of Scarcity: Retrenchment And Reform** is a comparative policy research report prepared by Frans van Dijk and Horatius Dumbrava under the auspices of the European Network of Councils for the Judiciary (ENCJ), analyzing the impact of the economic crisis and budget cuts on judicial systems across Europe.

## Summary

The report documents the "double bind" phenomenon experienced by European judiciaries during the economic recession: a surge in financial and social disputes occurring simultaneously with operating budget cuts of up to 20%. The study evaluates various short-term austerity measures alongside structural reforms (digitalization, procedure simplification, judicial map consolidation, and output-based budgeting) against the core values of the judiciary.

## Legal Basis

- **Not found in the available sources:** The report operates as an independent comparative study endorsed by ENCJ and is not established under any specific statutory legislative instrument.
- **Article 6 of the European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR):** Guarantees the right to a fair trial and access to an independent and impartial tribunal.
- **Vilnius Declaration (2011):** An ENCJ declaration on protecting the judicial function and access to justice amid economic crises.

## Background

Banking crises and economic recessions in Europe triggered public finance crises. Governments across various European countries implemented drastic budget cuts, while caseloads for civil disputes, bankruptcies, and labor matters increased sharply.

## Objectives

1\. Identify and evaluate judicial reform measures across EU member states. 2. Analyze the causal relationship between the quality of legal/judicial infrastructure and national economic growth. 3. Provide ENCJ recommendations for preserving judicial independence and access to justice during periods of budget austerity.

## Scope

The study covers EU member states and ENCJ observer countries, including the Netherlands, Romania, Greece, Portugal, Ireland, the United Kingdom, Denmark, Lithuania, Latvia, Spain, Italy, Germany, Austria, Belgium, Poland, Turkey, and Sweden.

## Key Provisions

- **Economic Impact of Case Delay:** Inefficiency and delay in civil justice reduce annual GDP growth by up to 1.0% in Italy, while high-quality justice in the Netherlands contributes 0.8% to annual GDP.
- **Protection of Judicial Salaries:** Judicial salary cuts can only be justified under exceptional economic conditions if applied equally across the entire public sector, are time-limited, and are governed by law to prevent executive interference.
- **Access to Justice (Article 6 ECHR):** Increases in court fees must not obstruct citizens' right to bring legitimate cases before a court.
- **Output-Based Budgeting System:** Court funding allocation directly linked to workload volume and projected case output (as demonstrated by the model in the Netherlands).

## Implementation

Reform implementation focuses on:

- Digitalization and e-courts (e-filing, e-tracking, video recording of hearings in Sweden, remote hearings).
- Simplification of civil and commercial procedures (9–12 week procedures in Ireland).
- Redistribution of tasks from judges to qualified legal assistants (\*Rechtspfleger\* in Germany/Austria, judicial fellows in Ireland).
- Judicial map consolidation for operational efficiency.

## Policy Problems

1\. The "double bind" phenomenon: rising case volumes amid cuts to operating budgets. 2. Risk of erosion of judicial independence due to discretionary salary cuts imposed by the executive. 3. Closure of local courts potentially reducing geographical access to justice. 4. Judges' concerns regarding output-based budgeting systems perceived as overly "technocratic."

## Reform / Change

- A shift from destructive short-term cost-cutting toward long-term structural modernization.
- Use of ICT to replace physical court presence without reducing the quality of justice.
- Active involvement of judicial bodies in all stages of the budgeting and legal reform process.
