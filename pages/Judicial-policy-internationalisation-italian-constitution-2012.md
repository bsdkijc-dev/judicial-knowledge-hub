# Judicial-policy-internationalisation-italian-constitution-2012

The **Internationalisation of Constitutional Law and Multilevel Rights Protection Framework** (*Kerangka Kebijakan Internasionalisasi Hukum Konstitusi dan Perlindungan Hak Bertingkat*) is a constitutional law policy and theoretical framework examining the "de-provincialisation" of the 1948 Constitution of the Italian Republic, the integration of international and European Union norms into domestic law, and the multilevel protection of fundamental rights [^1].

## Summary

Authored by [Valerio Onida](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Valerio_Onida), Professor Emeritus of Constitutional Law at the [University of Milan](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/University_of_Milan), this framework posits that the 1948 Italian Constitution was not merely a local post-fascist political compromise, but a foundational document born out of the global experience of World War II and universal human rights movements [^2]. Drawing upon the ideas of [Giuseppe Dossetti](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Dossetti), Onida highlights that the Italian Constitution embodies universal human values that transcend local ideological boundaries [^3].

## Legal Basis

This framework rests upon national constitutional provisions and international legal instruments:

- **[Articles 10 & 11 of the Italian Constitution](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Articles_10_%26_11_of_the_Italian_Constitution)**: Mandates automatic compliance with customary international law and permits limitations of national sovereignty to promote international peace and justice [^4].
- **[Article 117 of the Italian Constitution](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Article_117_of_the_Italian_Constitution)**: Binds national and regional legislative power to EU law and international treaty obligations (as amended in 2001) [^5].
- **[UN Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948)](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Universal_Declaration_of_Human_Rights) & [ECHR (1950)](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/European_Convention_on_Human_Rights)**: Establishes international and European baseline standards for fundamental human rights protection [^6].
- **Jurisprudence of the Italian Constitutional Court**: \*Sentenza\* No. 170/1984 (primacy of Community law) and \*Sentenze\* No. 348 and 349 of 2007 (establishing the ECHR as a constitutional parameter) [^7].

## Background

Contemporary constitutionalism is defined by a universalist vocation: the recognition that every human being possesses inherent dignity and inalienable rights [^8]. Inspired by [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt)'s "Four Freedoms" speech (1941), the post-war order linked peace to international human rights protection [^9]. In Italy, cross-ideological dialogue between Catholic-Democrats ([Giorgio La Pira](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Giorgio_La_Pira), [Alcide De Gasperi](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Alcide_De_Gasperi)) and Marxist/Leftist factions ([Palmiro Togliatti](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Palmiro_Togliatti)) forged the "strong core" of the 1948 Constitution [^10].

## Objectives

- Advance a constitutional interpretation open to supranational human rights contributions [^11].
- Counter the erosion of social rights and the welfare state caused by unchecked market globalization [^12].
- Protect democratic institutions from populist erosion and power concentration disguised as executive "governability" [^13].

## Scope

The scope covers Italian constitutional law, EU supranational law, the European Court of Human Rights system, social rights within the welfare state, and comparative constitutional studies involving Germany, France, and the United States [^14].

## Key Provisions & Legal Implications

- **Multilevel Protection of Rights**: Harmonious integration of national constitutional rights, EU law, and ECHR case law without reducing higher domestic protection standards [^15].
- **Rejection of Rigid Originalism**: Human rights provisions must be interpreted dynamically rather than restricted to historical originalist perspectives [^16].
- **Parity of Social Rights**: Social rights (work, health, social security—encompassing *freedom from want*) share equal constitutional status with traditional civil and political liberties [^17].

## Implementation

Operationalized through Constitutional Court judgments, including the prohibition of extradition in capital punishment cases (\*Sentenze\* No. 54/1979 & No. 223/1996) and the enforcement of prisoners' fundamental rights (\*Sentenza\* No. 73/2001) [^18].

## Policy Issues

- **Threats of Free Market Globalization on Social Rights**: Critique of extreme fiscal austerity that undermines the social functions of the state [^19].
- **Democratic Depreciation via Populism**: The risk of power concentration and simplified consensus mechanisms that erode the separation of powers [^20].

## Reform & Proposed Changes

Promotes the adoption of "constitutional patriotism" and executive stability reforms based on the [Tommaso Perassi Agendum](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Tommaso_Perassi) (1946) while maintaining strict institutional checks and balances [^21].
