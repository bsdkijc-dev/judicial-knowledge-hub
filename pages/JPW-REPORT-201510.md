## 4. Policy Analysis

Background  
Globalization, expanding international trade, and complex commercial transactions have heightened the demand for rapid, specialized, and flexible alternative dispute resolution (ADR) mechanisms. While arbitration is traditionally conducted by private arbitrators (attorneys, academics, industry experts) or institutional bodies like KCAB, sitting judge-run arbitration (법관에 의한 중재) represents a unique hybrid where active judicial officers conduct confidential, binding arbitration inside court facilities. Common law jurisdictions exhibit divergent approaches: England & Wales (Sec. 93 Arbitration Act 1996) and Scotland (Sec. 25 Arbitration Act 2010) permit sitting High Court / Commercial Court / TCC judges to serve as sole arbitrators, whereas US judicial ethics rules generally forbid judges from acting as arbitrators.

<!-- -->

Policy Problem  
**Unconstitutionality of Confidential Judge-Run Adjudication**: In Delaware, the Court of Chancery established a confidential business arbitration program in 2009 (10 Del. C. § 349). In *Delaware Coalition for Open Government v. Strine* (2012–2013), federal courts struck down the program, ruling that conducting confidential, binding trials led by active judges using state facilities and taxpayer-funded salaries violates the First Amendment Right of Public Access.

**Violation of the Open Trial Principle in Korea**: ROK Constitution Article 109 guarantees that court trials and judgments shall be open to the public (재판공개의 원칙). Introducing confidential judge-arbitration would violate Article 109 and trigger fundamental constitutional challenges.

**Creation of a "Two-Tier Justice System" & Public Distrust**: Critics and legal scholars argue that judge-arbitration creates an elitist "special door to the courthouse" where wealthy corporations receive secret, expedited justice from active judges, while ordinary citizens endure public, delayed litigation, undermining public trust in judicial equality (법 앞의 평등).

**Ethical & "Current Official" Bias Risks (현관예우 / 現官禮遇)**: Allowing active judges to serve as private arbitrators or accept arbitration fees introduces severe conflicts of interest, perceived judicial bias, and risks of "current official favors."

**No Net Reduction in Court Caseload**: Empirical and comparative data show that judge-arbitration does not reduce general court litigation volume or appellate burdens because commercial parties choosing arbitration would otherwise use private arbitration rather than trial courts.

<!-- -->

Legal/Policy Gap  
ROK law contains no statutory authorization for sitting judges to act as arbitrators. Civil procedure and arbitration statutes strictly maintain the division between public judicial power and private arbitration.

<!-- -->

Objectives  
Evaluate international judge-arbitration models; confirm the constitutional and procedural infeasibility of sitting judge-arbitrators in Korea; and design alternative, constitutionally sound court-connected ADR reforms.

<!-- -->

Implementation & Proposed Alternative Reforms  
**Rejection of Sitting Judge-Arbitrators**: Conclude that South Korea should not introduce sitting judge-arbitrators due to insurmountable constitutional, ethical, and public trust barriers.

**Alternative 1: Promoting Retired Judges as Private Arbitrators (퇴직 법관에 의한 중재)**: Encourage retired senior judges with deep judicial expertise to register as private arbitrators with KCAB or conduct ad hoc arbitration, expanding private dispute resolution without compromising sitting judicial neutrality.

**Alternative 2: Establishing Specialized Court Arbitration Benches (중재전담법관 / 중재전문재판부)**: Designate specialized arbitration judges/benches at major courts (such as Seoul Central District Court and Seoul High Court) to handle court-related arbitration support (enforcement judgments, set-aside petitions, interim measures, arbitrator appointment support) with high expertise and speed, modeled after Australia (List G in Victoria Supreme Court), Ireland, and Hong Kong.

**Alternative 3: Court-Annexed Med-Arb utilizing Standing Mediators (상임조정위원을 활용한 조정-중재 프로그램)**:

- Establish a court-annexed Med-Arb program within Court Mediation Centers (법원조정센터) operating across 10 regional courts.
- Authorize 31 full-time Standing Mediators (상임조정위원 — senior legal professionals with 15+ years experience) to serve as arbitrators upon voluntary post-dispute consent by the parties (선 조정, 후 중재).
- Require a specific statutory amendment to the Arbitration Act authorizing courts to provide administrative and record-management support for Standing Mediator arbitration.

<!-- -->

Evidence & Empirical Data  
**Delaware Court of Chancery Usage**: Between 2009 and 2012, only 5 cases were submitted to Delaware Chancery judge-arbitration before the program was struck down in *Strine*.

**UK Judge-Arbitrator Caseload**: In England & Wales, High Court TCC and Commercial Court judges act as arbitrators in approximately 15 to 20 cases annually under Section 93.

**KCAB Arbitration Statistics (2013)**: KCAB handled 338 total arbitration cases (261 domestic, 77 international) with a total claim value of \$601 million USD, concentrated heavily in construction (128 cases / \$400M USD) and commercial transactions. Average domestic KCAB arbitration duration was 6 to 7 months, compared to 282 days for civil district court panel trials.

## 6. MediaWiki Output

**A Research on Judge-run Arbitration in the U.K., and the U.S., and the Possibility of Introducing Judge-Arbitrator** is an official judicial policy research report published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of South Korea's Supreme Court.

## Summary

This document is an official judicial policy research report published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of South Korea's Supreme Court. It comparatively analyzes the legal framework of judge-run arbitration (법관에 의한 중재) in England, Scotland, and the State of Delaware (US). It examines the landmark US ruling *Delaware Coalition for Open Government v. Strine* (3rd Cir. 2013), which held that confidential judge-run arbitration violates the First Amendment Right of Public Access; evaluates the constitutional barrier posed by Article 109 of the Korean Constitution (the Open Trial Principle); and formulates alternative policy recommendations, including promoting retired judges as private arbitrators (퇴직 법관에 의한 중재), establishing Specialized Arbitration Judges/Benches (중재전담법관 / 중재전문재판부), and an integrated Med-Arb scheme using Standing Mediators (상임조정위원) at Court Mediation Centers.

## Legal Basis

The legal frameworks analyzed include:

- **Court Organization Act of South Korea (법원조직법)**: The legal basis for establishing the Judicial Policy Research Institute under the Supreme Court of Korea.
- **Constitution of the Republic of Korea**: Article 27(3) (Right to a Fair and Speedy Trial) and Article 109 (Open Trial Principle / 재판공개의 원칙).
- **Arbitration Act of South Korea (중재법)**: Articles 3, 6, 7, 12, 13, 14, 20, 29, 31, 41.
- **UK Arbitration Act 1996**: Section 93 (Appointment of Judges as Arbitrators in the Commercial Court & TCC) & Schedule 2.
- **Arbitration (Scotland) Act 2010**: Section 25 & Rule 26.
- **Delaware Law (US)**: Former 10 Del. C. § 349 & Chancery Rules 96-98 (struck down); the Delaware Rapid Arbitration Act (DRAA 2015 / 10 Del. C. § 5801-5812) & Revised Chancery Rules 96-98.
- **Landmark US Jurisprudence**: *Delaware Coalition for Open Government v. Strine* (894 F.Supp. 2d 493 (D. Del. 2012), affirmed 733 F.3d 510 (3d Cir. 2013)).

## Background

The escalation of commercial disputes and the complexity of business transactions have driven demand for fast, expert Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) mechanisms. Common law countries take different approaches: England and Scotland allow sitting Commercial Court and TCC judges to act as sole arbitrators, while in the US, judicial ethics prohibit sitting judges from serving as arbitrators. Delaware's experiment with confidential judge-run arbitration (2009) sparked fierce debate and was ultimately struck down by US federal courts for violating the public's constitutional right to open access to justice.

## Objectives

1.  Diagnose the legal framework and effectiveness of judge-run arbitration in England, Scotland, and the US.
2.  Evaluate the implications of the *Strine* decision for confidential arbitration led by sitting judges.
3.  Assess the compatibility of sitting judge arbitration with the Constitution and civil procedure of South Korea.
4.  Formulate alternative judicial reforms to improve the effectiveness of commercial arbitration in Korea.

## Scope

- **Three-Jurisdiction Comparative Study**: England (Commercial Court & TCC Guide), Scotland (Court of Session & SAR Rule 26), and the US (Delaware Court of Chancery & DRAA 2015).
- **Constitutional & Ethical Issue Analysis**: The open trial principle (ROK Constitution Article 109 / US First Amendment), the right to equal justice, and the risk of "current official" bias (현관예우).
- **Alternative Policy Recommendations**: Retired judge arbitration, Specialized Judicial Arbitration Benches, and Standing Mediator Med-Arb.

## Key Provisions

- **Rejection of Sitting Judges as Arbitrators**: South Korea does not recommend appointing sitting judges as confidential arbitrators, as this would violate the open trial principle and create a "two-tier justice system."
- **Establishment of Specialized Arbitration Benches (중재전담법관 / 중재전문재판부)**: Allocates specialized judges at Seoul Central District Court and Seoul High Court to handle enforcement, set-aside, and arbitration support applications.
- **Standing Mediator Med-Arb Program (상임조정위원을 활용한 조정-중재)**:
  - Utilizes 31 Standing Mediators (상임조정위원) across 10 Court Mediation Centers.
  - Adopts a "mediation first, arbitration second" (선 조정, 후 중재) scheme based on the parties' voluntary agreement after a dispute arises.
  - Requires a specific amendment to the Korean Arbitration Act to provide the legal basis for court administrative support.

## Implementation

Courts encourage the registration of retired judges as arbitrators with KCAB (대한상사중재원), draft proposed amendments to the Arbitration Act for the role of Standing Mediators (상임조정위원), and strengthen mediation/arbitration courtroom infrastructure.

## Policy Issues

- The potential conflict between confidential judge-run arbitration and Article 109 of the ROK Constitution (the Open Trial Principle).
- Public perception of unfairness if large corporations gain access to a taxpayer-funded "special door" to confidential justice.

## Reform / Amendments

1.  Rejection of appointing sitting judges as confidential arbitrators.
2.  Establishment of Specialized Arbitration Judges/Benches (중재전담법관 / 중재전문재판부).
3.  Enactment of an integrated Med-Arb Program at Court Mediation Centers (상임조정위원 Med-Arb).
4.  Specific amendment to the Korean Arbitration Act for Med-Arb administrative support.
