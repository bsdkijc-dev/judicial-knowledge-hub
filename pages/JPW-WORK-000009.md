#### 4. POLICY ANALYSIS

**Background**: \[FACT\] Youth employment in Spain represents a long-standing structural challenge, further aggravated by the COVID-19 pandemic and the accelerating digital and ecological transformation of the labor market. Key obstacles include structural unemployment, early school leaving (13.7% in 2023 versus a 9.5% EU average), overqualification (*sobrecualificación*), and very high job turnover.

**Policy Problem**: \[FACT\]

1.  *Uncertainty over the Age Boundary of "Youth"*: A varying age range of 16 to 34 years appears across different policy analyses, even though Law 3/2023 restricts priority status to ages 16–29.
2.  *Precariousness and Involuntary Part-Time Work*: The rate of involuntary temporary contracts among youth up to age 24 reaches 44.2% (versus 15.8% in the Eurozone in 2023), while the rate of involuntary part-time work (*parcialidad involuntaria*) reaches 46.7% (versus 19.2% in the EU).
3.  *Digital and Sustainability Gap*: Digital-related employment in the EU grew by 57.8% over the past decade (seven times faster than overall employment growth of 7.9%), yet low-skilled youth face major barriers to accessing it.
4.  *Age Discrimination Above 30*: Restricting priority programs to ages 16–30 leaves youth aged 25–34 — who still earn 20% below the national average — without special protection, raising potential concerns under the constitutional equality principle (Art. 9 CE).

**Legal/Policy Gap**: \[FACT\] Law 3/2023 on Employment establishes a programmatic framework but does not include an explicit definition or judicially enforceable legal guarantee (*mecanismos concretos exigibles jurídicamente*) regarding the right to quality employment. Additionally, public employment services face capacity limitations in providing personalized support without private-sector collaboration.

**Objectives**: \[FACT\] Shift the legislative focus away from merely achieving "quantitative full employment" (*pleno empleo*) and toward fostering "decent, stable, and quality employment" (*empleo digno, estable y de calidad*), in line with Article 2.2 of the Employment Law and Articles 35 & 40 of the Spanish Constitution.

**Implementation**: \[FACT\]

- *RDL 32/2021 Labor Reform*: Elimination of project-based/service contracts (*obra y servicio*) and reorganization of training contracts (alternating training contracts and professional practice contracts under Art. 11 ET) to promote permanent contracts.
- *Youth Guarantee Plus Plan 2021–2027*: Development of personalized guidance oriented toward green and digital economic sectors.
- *Active Support Programs (Law 3/2023)*: Implementation of the TándEM Program (alternating training), the First Professional Experience Program (recruitment into public bodies), and the Investigo Program (research and data science) for youth aged 16–30.
- *Public-Private Cooperation Model*: Integration of the Spanish Employment Agency, regional (autonomous community) services, and private placement agencies, under Article 8 of the Employment Law and Component 23 of the PRTR.

**Evidence & Impact**: \[FACT\] A decline in youth unemployment from its pandemic peak, and an increase in the proportion of permanent contracts following RDL 32/2021 — though vulnerability remains significant among low-education youth and those over age 30.

**Reform & Unresolved Issues**: \[FACT\] The need to extend protection coverage beyond the 30-year age threshold, refine mechanisms for objectively evaluating the impact of active programs, and strengthen certainty around decent-work indicators across all productive sectors.

#### 6. MEDIAWIKI OUTPUT

## Summary

**Youth Employment Policies Facing the Transformation of Work: A Chronicle of Challenges and Contrasts** is a labor law study written by Prof. Dr. Estefanía González Cobaleda of Universidad de Málaga (UMA). This scholarly work provides an in-depth analysis of Spain's youth employment policy framework in the era of digital and ecological transformation. The study evaluates EU policy coordination (the European Youth Strategy 2019–2027 and Council Decision (EU) 2024/3134), the instruments of Law 3/2023 on Employment, the Youth Guarantee Plus Plan 2021–2027, the impact of RDL 32/2021 on restricting temporary contracts and renewing training contracts under Article 11 of the Workers' Statute, active support programs (TándEM, First Experience, Investigo), and the public-private collaboration model within Spain's National Employment System.

## Legal Basis

The youth employment policies and normative reforms analyzed are grounded in the following national and European legal framework:

- **Spanish Constitution (CE)**: Article 9.2 (substantive equality), Article 35.1 (right to work and remuneration guarantees), and Article 40 (mandate for full employment and vocational training).
- **Law 3/2023, of 28 February, on Employment**: Article 3(d) (definition of priority groups), Article 8 (National Employment System), Article 50 (priority vulnerable populations), and Article 53 (specific provisions for youth).
- **Royal Legislative Decree 2/2015 (Workers' Statute — ET)**: Article 11 (alternating training and professional practice contracts), Article 15, and Article 16 (permanent and fixed-discontinuous contracts).
- **Royal Decree-Law 32/2021, of 28 December**: Labor reform for employment stabilization.
- **Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU)**: Articles 2.3, 5.2, 145–150 (Employment Title), and Articles 165–166 (Education and Youth).
- **Council Decision (EU) 2024/3134**: Employment policy guidelines for EU Member States.

## Background

Spain's youth labor market has historically been marked by structural problems such as high unemployment, early school leaving (13.7% in 2023), excessive job turnover, and overqualification. This situation has grown more complex with the acceleration of automation, artificial intelligence, and the demands of ecological transition, which require new technical qualifications.

## Objectives

1\. Shift legal priorities from mere quantitative employment absorption toward guaranteeing dignified, stable, and quality employment (\*empleo digno, estable y de calidad\*). 2. Strengthen the effectiveness of the National Employment System through restructuring of the Youth Guarantee Plus scheme 2021–2027. 3. Minimize the digital and ecological skills gap among low-educated youth through alternating training programs. 4. Foster public-private synergy between the Spanish Employment Agency and private entities to optimize job placement.

## Scope

The study covers analysis across four main pillars: 1. *Quantitative Characterization of Youth*: Analysis of Eurostat/INE data on unemployment, involuntary temporary contracts (44.2%), and involuntary part-time work (46.7%). 2. *EU Coordination Framework*: The role of the Open Method of Coordination (OMC), the TFEU, and Council Decision (EU) 2024/3134. 3. *National Legal Instruments*: Implementation of Law 3/2023 on Employment, training-contract reform under RDL 32/2021, and the TándEM/First Experience/Investigo programs. 4. *Public-Private Cooperation*: Implementation of Article 8 of the Employment Law and Component 23 of the PRTR.

## Key Provisions

- **Redefinition of Employment Policy Objectives (Art. 2.2 of the Employment Law)**: The primary focus shifts toward employability development, job stability, and the right to decent work.
- **Priority Age Classification (Art. 50 of the Employment Law)**: Youth under 30, or participants in the Youth Guarantee, are explicitly classified as a priority attention group.
- **New Training Contract Model (Art. 11 ET)**: Establishment of two contract types — alternating training contracts and professional practice contracts — to limit precariousness.
- **Public-Private Partnership (Art. 8 of the Employment Law)**: Enforcement of coordination principles between the Spanish Employment Agency, regional services, and private placement agencies.

## Implementation

Implementation occurs through execution of the Youth Guarantee Plus Plan 2021–2027, allocation of Next Generation EU funds via PRTR Component 23, and recruitment incentive programs in the research sector and public administration.

## Policy Problems

- Exclusion of the 30–34 age segment from special priority schemes, despite this group still earning 20% below the national average.
- Absence of an explicit, judicially enforceable legal guarantee (\*exigibilidad jurídica\*) within the programmatic sections of the Employment Law.
- Risk of labor market segmentation between digitally skilled youth and low-educated youth.

## Reform

- Enactment of Law 3/2023 on Employment, replacing Royal Legislative Decree 3/2015.
- Labor reform under RDL 32/2021, eliminating project/service-based contracts (\*obra y servicio\*).
- Adoption of Council Decision (EU) 2024/3134 to align annual employment guidelines.

## Relationships with Other Entities

- **Implements**: Component 23 of the Recovery, Transformation and Resilience Plan (PRTR).
- **Applies To**: Articles 11, 15, and 16 of Royal Legislative Decree 2/2015 (Workers' Statute).
- **Regulates**: The National Youth Guarantee System & the Spanish Employment Agency.
