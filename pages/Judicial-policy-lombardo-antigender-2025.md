# Judicial-policy-lombardo-antigender-2025

**‘Stop torpedoing women's rights!’: Feminist institutional responses to anti-gender politics in Spain and Catalonia parliaments** is a comparative empirical policy research paper authored by Emanuela Lombardo, Paloma Caravantes, and Silvia Díaz Fernández of the Universidad Complutense de Madrid, published in the *European Journal of Political Research* (EJPR) in 2025.

## Summary

This study analyzes feminist institutional response strategies to anti-gender politics and radical right-wing opposition (particularly the Vox party) in the Spanish Parliament (2019–2023) and the Catalan Parliament (2021–2024). Based on content analysis of 21 parliamentary debates and 42 in-depth interviews, the study identifies four main response strategies (\*Knowledge\*, \*Coalition-Building\*, \*Rule-Making\*, and \*Everyday Pragmatic Engagement\*) and tests the enabling and constraining factors at the macro, meso, and micro levels.

## Legal Basis

- **Ley Orgánica 3/2007 (Spain):** The Law for the Effective Equality of Women and Men, mandating gender parity quotas on electoral lists.
- **Llei 19/2020 (Catalonia):** The Law on Equal Treatment and Non-Discrimination.
- **Ley Orgánica 10/2022 (Spain):** The Comprehensive Guarantee of Sexual Freedom Law (reforming the penal classification of sexual crimes).
- **Ley Orgánica 4/2023 (Spain):** The Law for Real and Effective Equality of Trans People ("Trans Law" / gender self-determination).
- **Protocol del Parlament de Catalunya (BOPC_207):** A protocol for the prevention and handling of sexual and gender-based harassment.

## Background

The entry of the far-right Vox party into the Spanish Parliament (14.8% of seats) and the Catalan Parliament (8.1% of seats) increased polarization and gender-based political violence. Anti-gender actors use hate speech, verbal harassment of MPs (such as calling them "witches" or "baby-killers"), and attempts to roll back equality policies.

## Objectives

1\. Identify the forms of feminist institutional response to anti-gender actors in parliament. 2. Analyze differences in response between the national level (Spain) and the regional level (Catalonia). 3. Test macro-level factors (democracy, historical legacy, decentralization), meso-level factors (equality institutionalization, party configuration), and micro-level factors (critical actors) \[22–27, 61–77\].

## Scope

This multi-level comparative study covers analysis of debates and interviews in the Spanish Parliament and the Catalan Parliament, conducted within the framework of the European CCINDLE research project.

## Key Provisions

- **Four Feminist Response Strategies:**

1.  *Knowledge Strategy:* Inviting civil society experts and academics to present scientific evidence to counter anti-gender disinformation \[15, 46–48\].
2.  *Coalition-Building Strategy:* Building cross-party alliances and extra-parliamentary coalitions, including implementation of a \*cordon sanitaire\* to isolate far-right proposals \[16, 49–53\].
3.  *Rule-Making Strategy:* Applying codes of conduct, Gender Equality Plans, and informal agreements to hold closed-door meetings to prevent media exploitation by Vox \[18, 30, 58–60\].
4.  *Everyday Pragmatic Engagement Strategy:* Direct, fact-based confrontation (such as the call "Stop torpedoing women's rights!"), cross-party solidarity, or non-interaction policies to limit far-right visibility \[19, 54–56\].

- **Catalonia vs. Spain Comparison:** Catalonia successfully implemented a formal \*cordon sanitaire\* ("anti-fascist pact") because the far-right's share of seats was small and PP representation minimal (2.2%). In Spain, by contrast, internal competition within the left-wing coalition and the larger share held by the right (PP 25.4% + Vox 14.8%) prevented a formal national \*cordon sanitaire\*.

## Implementation

The methodology was implemented through qualitative thematic analysis of 21 debates and 42 structured interviews with MPs, technical staff, and civil society organizations using Atlas.Ti software \[3, 39, 41–42\]. Data is stored under restricted access in the Radboud Data Repository for privacy and ethics reasons.

## Policy Problems

1\. The occurrence of gender-based political violence and intimidation against women, racial minority, and LGBTI\* MPs. 2. Deterioration in the quality of democratic deliberation, forcing equality discussions to be held outside official parliamentary chambers. 3. Limited capacity of the Parliamentary Bureau to sanction hate speech due to concerns about restricting political pluralism.

## Reform / Change

Promotes strengthening of gender equality institutionalization, the establishment of protective protocols for MPs against political violence, and recognition that feminist responses serve as a bulwark for democratic principles.
