# JPW-POL-ILJ-001

**The Italian Legal Imaginary and Journal Policy Framework** refers to the editorial, epistemological, and comparative policy structure developed by *[The Italian Law Journal](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/The_Italian_Law_Journal)* (ItALJ) to critically analyze, unpack, and disseminate the Italian legal tradition within global legal discourse [^1].

## Lead

Founded in 2014 by [Camilla Crea](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Camilla_Crea) and a group of Italian civil and comparative law scholars, *The Italian Law Journal* serves as an open-access, peer-reviewed e-journal using English as a lingua franca [^2]. Grounded in the post-WWII constitutionalization of private law under the [1947 Italian Constitution](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Italy) and the rise of comparative legal studies, the framework seeks to liberate legal thought from classic formalism and national narcissism [^3].

## Summary

The framework addresses two major epistemological turns in Italian legal history:

1.  **National Constitutionalization of Private Law**: Infusing the [1942 Civil Code](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Italian_Civil_Code) with constitutional values—notably human dignity, personalism, substantive equality (Art. 3(2)), and solidarity—transforming private law from economic concerns toward the human person [^4].
2.  **Rise of Comparative Legal Studies**: Breaking the isolation of domestic law through revolutionary methodologies, including [Gino Gorla](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Gino_Gorla)'s case method, [Mauro Cappelletti](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Mauro_Cappelletti)'s functionalism, and [Rodolfo Sacco](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Rodolfo_Sacco)'s theory of legal formants and cryptotypes [^5].

## Legal Basis

The policy framework operates at the intersection of:

- **[Italian Constitution (1947)](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Italy)**: Articles 2 (inviolable rights), 3 (formal and substantive equality), and 42 (social function of property) [^6].
- **[Civil Code of 1942](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Italian_Civil_Code)**: Re-evaluated through constitutionally-oriented interpretation and general clauses (good faith, social utility, injustice of harm) [^7].
- **Ministerial Decrees of 1999 and 2005**: Establishing comparative law as an autonomous and mandatory course in Italian law school curricula [^8].

## Background

Historically, Italian legal scholarship was constrained by a stereotype of rigid positivism and dogmatics, labeled by [John Henry Merryman](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/John_Henry_Merryman) as the "Italian Style" [^9]. Earlier benchmarks, such as Alessandro Pizzorusso's *Italian Studies in Law* (1992) and Giacinto della Cananea's *Italian Journal of Public Law* (2009), paved the way for ItALJ's focus on private law as a social engineering tool [^10].

## Objectives

- Make the Italian legal tradition visible and accessible to the international community in English [^11].
- Foster bidirectional critical dialogue without sacred texts or uncritical mythologies [^12].
- Promote interdisciplinary openness connecting private law with history, economics, sociology, and philosophy [^13].

## Scope

Applies to international legal scholarship, private law reform, comparative jurisprudence, legal education, and judicial interpretation across continental Europe, the Americas, and global legal systems [^14].

## Key Provisions & Formants

- **Theory of Legal Formants**: Dissecting legal systems into statutory, judicial, and doctrinal formants, along with unexpressed "cryptotypes" [^15].
- **Indirect Drittwirkung**: Applying constitutional fundamental rights directly to horizontal relationships between private parties [^16].
- **Alternative Use of Law**: Utilizing constitutional interpretation to advance social justice and protect disadvantaged social actors [^17].

## Implementation

Published bi-annually under the auspices of the Università degli Studi del Sannio and sponsored by the [Harvard Italian Law Association](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Harvard_Italian_Law_Association) (HILA), SISDIC, SIRD, and NIABA [^23].

## Policy Problems

- **Linguistic Periphery**: Esoteric Italian legal terminology and out-of-print, expensive English translations of the Italian Civil Code [^24].
- **Disciplinary Silos**: Rigid academic fragmentation between public, private, and comparative law departments [^25].

## Reform

Scholars advocate translating primary Italian judicial decisions into English and expanding trans-systemic legal education programs [^26].
