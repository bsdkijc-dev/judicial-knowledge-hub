## Overview

Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia Decision No. 363 K/Pdt/1984, dated July 30, 1985 (pronounced August 22, 1985), established jurisprudence and commercial/maritime transport legal principles concerning the exemption of a carrier from liability for cargo damage caused by force majeure or acts of God at sea.

The Supreme Court held that a carrier cannot be held liable for compensation for damage to cargo caused by an unavoidable disaster at sea, pursuant to Article 468 paragraph (2) of the Indonesian Commercial Code (KUHD).

## Executive Summary

The central legal principle established in this decision is that a maritime carrier is released from liability for cargo damage if the damage results from an inevitable sea disaster (\*force majeure\* / act of God) and the vessel is seaworthy.

Key elements established in the adjudication:

- The carrier cannot be burdened with indemnity for cargo damage caused by unavoidable perils of the sea ex Article 468 paragraph (2) KUHD.
- Proof of a severe storm (Beaufort scale 7–8) in the Atlantic Ocean for 4 days and 4 nights constitutes an act of God.
- Assessment of factual maritime disaster evidence is the authority of the \*judex facti\* and cannot be re-examined at the cassation level.

## Case Background

The Original Plaintiff / Petitioner for Cassation, **P.T. Asuransi Afia Indonesia**, filed a lawsuit claiming damages amounting to US \$ 104,317.00 against the Original Defendant / Respondent for Cassation, **P.T. Djakarta Lloyd**, concerning damage to cargo (contractors heavy equipment) belonging to Interimco Trading Co. Ltd. transported by the vessel \*M.V. Hadji Agus Salim\* from Canada to Indonesia.

The Plaintiff brought the lawsuit based on subrogation rights after paying an insurance claim under Subrogation Form No. 100568 dated July 19, 1978.

The Defendant contested the claim, arguing that the damage occurred due to a major storm of Beaufort scale 7–8 in the Atlantic Ocean lasting 4 days and 4 nights (\*force majeure\* / act of God) and that the vessel was seaworthy under Article 468 paragraph (2) KUHD.

Central Jakarta District Court Decision No. 64/1979 G and Jakarta High Court Decision No. 325/1983/PT.Perd rejected the Plaintiff's claim in its entirety. The Plaintiff subsequently filed a cassation petition with the Supreme Court.

## Legal and Procedural Issues

### Force Majeure / Act of God in Maritime Transport

The primary issue was whether a carrier is responsible for cargo damage caused by a Beaufort scale 7–8 ocean storm in the Atlantic that caused severe rolling and dislodgement of cargo from its lashings.

The legal rule established is that if the carrier proves that the damage arose from a maritime disaster that could not be prevented or avoided by the master and crew exercising maximum effort (\*good seamanship\*), the carrier is released from the obligation of indemnification.

### Seaworthiness vs. Carrier Negligence

The issue evaluated whether the carrier was negligent in safeguarding the cargo. A vessel equipped with official maritime safety documents (Certificate of Nationality, Certificate of Perfection, Construction Safety Certificate) and proper stowage is deemed seaworthy, rendering damage from extreme storm turbulence a force majeure event.

## Question of Law and Question of Fact

### Question of Law (Cassation / Judex Juris)

**Question:**

Did the \*Judex Facti\* (Central Jakarta District Court & Jakarta High Court) misapply Article 468 paragraph (2) KUHD and international maritime transport regulations (Hague Rules) in releasing the Defendant from liability?

**Supreme Court Ruling:**

No. The \*Judex Facti\* did not misapply the law. Exemption from compensation under Article 468 paragraph (2) KUHD was fully in accordance with applicable law.

### Question of Fact (Judex Facti / District & High Courts)

Evaluation of physical evidence including storm velocity and scale (Beaufort 7–8), vessel Logbook Extract (T-1), Marine Note of Protest (T-4), Survey Report by Toplis & Harding of Canada Ltd. (P-11), and vessel seaworthiness certificates (T-6, T-7, T-8, T-9).

## Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

- **Ratio Decidendi:**

1.  The \*Judex Facti\* did not misapply the law. Pursuant to Article 468 paragraph (2) KUHD, a carrier cannot be burdened with indemnity for cargo damage caused by unavoidable maritime disasters.
2.  It was proven that \*M.V. Hadji Agus Salim\* was in a seaworthy condition and that the damage was caused by extreme gale-force storms in the Atlantic Ocean constituting an act of God.
3.  Evaluation of evidentiary findings falls within the exclusive domain of the \*Judex Facti\* and cannot be examined in cassation proceedings.

## Ruling

The Supreme Court rejected the cassation petition from the Petitioner for Cassation (**P.T. Asuransi Afia Indonesia**) and ordered the Petitioner for Cassation to pay cassation court costs amounting to Rp 20,000.— (twenty thousand rupiahs).

## Related Knowledge and Jurisprudence

- **Commercial Code (KUHD) Article 468 paragraph (2):** The carrier is obliged to compensate for damage caused to cargo, unless the carrier proves that the damage was caused by an unavoidable or unpreventable disaster.
- **Commercial Code (KUHD) Article 506:** Definition and function of the Bill of Lading (\*Kognosemen\*) as proof of receipt of goods.
- **International Convention / Hague Rules (1924) Article IV Section 2:** Exemption of carriers from liability for perils, dangers, and accidents of the sea.

## Evidence Status and Gaps

### Established by Evidence

- P.T. Asuransi Afia Indonesia paid a claim of \$ 104,317.00 and received subrogation rights from Interimco Trading Co. Ltd. (P-1).
- Cargo transport (contractors heavy equipment) utilizing \*M.V. Hadji Agus Salim\* owned by P.T. Djakarta Lloyd (P-3/T-5).
- The vessel encountered strong gale storms of Beaufort scale 7–8 for 4 days and 4 nights (December 21–24, 1977) in the Atlantic Ocean, causing cargo to break loose and sustain damage (T-1, T-3, T-4).
- The vessel \*M.V. Hadji Agus Salim\* satisfied official seaworthiness requirements (T-6, T-7, T-8, T-9).

### Not Established / Unproven

- The Plaintiff failed to prove any negligence or intentional fault on the part of the Defendant or the ship's Master in safeguarding the cargo.

### Uncertainty and Gaps

- The Petitioner for Cassation attempted to argue in the cassation memorandum that a Beaufort scale 7–8 storm should have been anticipatable; however, the Supreme Court confirmed that technical evaluations concerning natural conditions and vessel maneuvers belong to the factual domain (\*Judex Facti\*) and cannot be re-examined during cassation review.

## GEO Q&A

**Q: Must a sea carrier compensate for losses if cargo is damaged due to severe storms at sea?**

**A:** No. Based on Supreme Court Decision No. 363 K/Pdt/1984 in conjunction with Article 468 paragraph (2) KUHD, the carrier is exempted from the obligation of compensation if it proves that cargo damage was caused by a maritime disaster (\*force majeure\* / act of God / perils of the sea) that could not be prevented or avoided, provided the vessel was seaworthy.
