# Judicial-policy-ijca-globalization-court-admin-2008

**The IJCA Editorial Framework on Globalization and Court Administration** (*From the Co-Managing Editors*) is an editorial policy framework and international judicial governance guideline that analyzes the impact of globalization on the function of national justice systems. It examines the risks of transferring or adopting foreign court organizational designs (such as *judicial councils*), the challenges of balancing international norms with local legal traditions, and the importance of precise translation services and globally oriented judicial training \[FACT\].

## Summary

Published by Co-Managing Editors Philip M. Langbroek and Barry Mahoney in the *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA), this policy framework outlines the impact of globalization on court administration. Based on discussions at the 3rd IACA International Conference in Dublin, the study highlights that adopting foreign judicial structures (like *Judicial Councils*) is a "risky business" if local legal traditions, religious norms, and social hierarchies are ignored. This framework emphasizes the importance of conflict mitigation strategies, international sensitivity training for judges, and the provision of precise translation services.

## Background

Globalization is transforming the role of national justice systems as direct economic intervention by the state diminishes. The emergence of international conventions and treaties has the potential to override domestic jurisdictional issues. In response to these dynamics, the IJCA published editorial guidelines to assist court administrators in integrating international best practices without causing friction with local norms.

## Objectives

1\. Strengthen the capacity of courts as mechanisms for the peaceful resolution of disputes. 2. Identify points of tension and mitigate conflicts when implementing imported reforms. 3. Ensure the process of adopting court organizational designs accounts for the local cultural context. 4. Improve the quality of translation services and internationally oriented training for judges.

## Scope

This policy encompasses a comparative discussion of global court administration, referencing experiences in judicial development in Iraq, Russia, Ireland, Switzerland, and Sweden.

## Key Provisions

- **The Risk of Borrowing Organizational Designs:** Adopting institutions like *Judicial Councils* risks drastically altering the relationships among judges, court management, and the Ministry of Justice.
- **Variations in Judicial Council Functions:** *Judicial Councils* have a very broad spectrum of roles, ranging from merely safeguarding judicial independence to acting as national administrative bodies.
- **The Importance of Precision Translation:** Courts are obligated to provide precise translations of international conventions and language services for increasingly diverse, non-local litigants.

## Implementation

- **IACA Dublin Conference:** A forum for exchanging innovations in court management, ICT, and delay reduction.
- **Judicial Sensitivity Training:** Training programs for judges to align their rulings with the international community without uprooting their understanding of local norms.

## Policy Problems

- **Normative Clashes:** Friction between international best practices and the values of regional tradition, religion, and law.
- **Potential for Political Conflict:** Adopting new institutions without mitigation risks triggering tensions between judicial leadership and the ministry of justice.

## Reform / Change

- Developing a risk analysis framework prior to adopting foreign judicial structures.
- Modernizing translation services and communication management for diverse litigants.
