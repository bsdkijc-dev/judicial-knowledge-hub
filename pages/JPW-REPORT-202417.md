## 4. Policy Analysis

Background  
Discussing the "privatization of justice" (사법의 민간화) triggers natural hesitation or apprehension due to historical societal conflicts associated with state asset sales or state enterprise privatizations. However, as state responsibilities expand and judicial caseloads grow, courts face severe budget and manpower constraints, leading to chronic trial delays (재판지연).

<!-- -->

Policy Problem  
**Resource Scarcity & Trial Delays**: The judiciary's budget comprises only 0.33% of South Korea's total national budget in 2024 (down from 0.43% in 2016), while judge quotas (법관 정원) have remained frozen at 3,214 since 2019, forcing Korean judges to handle up to 5.17 times the annual caseload of German judges.

**Conceptual Confusion**: Previous domestic discussions treated privatization fragmentationally or confused general public-sector enterprise privatization with judicial task delegation.

**Sectoral Disparities**: Different legal areas (civil, criminal, family, juvenile/child protection) possess distinct constitutional attributes, making uniform privatization models infeasible.

<!-- -->

Legal/Policy Gap  
**Distinction from General Privatization**: Unlike state enterprise privatization focused on transferring ownership and profit-maximization, judicial privatization retains ultimate state guarantee responsibility (*Gewährleistungsverantwortung*) and aims to enhance the fundamental right to a fair trial under Article 27 of the Constitution.

**Conceptual Typology**: Adopting and expanding Yale Law Professor Judith Resnik's model, the report establishes a 2-tier classification:

1.  *External Privatization (외부적 민간화 / Outsourcing)*: Delegating court tasks/functions to non-judicial, private, or external entities through functional entrustment or delegation (e.g., court-annexed mediation, external appraisal, private correctional facilities).
2.  *Internal Privatization (내부적 민간화 / Non-Publicity)*: Conducting court-administered procedures in non-public, closed settings to facilitate settlement or efficiency without delegating judicial power outside the court (e.g., court-administered in-house mediation, closed hearings).

<!-- -->

Objectives  
The ultimate objective (궁극적 목적) of judicial privatization is "thickly protecting the public's right to a trial through good adjudication" (좋은 재판을 통한 국민의 재판받을 권리의 두터운 보호). The realistic prerequisite (현실적 지향) is creating conditions that allow courts to concentrate on their core judicial function.

<!-- -->

Sectoral Analysis & Recommendations  
**Civil Sector (민사분야)**: Relatively easy to privatize conceptually due to private autonomy (사적자치원칙). Recommends expanding court-annexed ADR, improving appraisal fee structures (감정료), and reforming Expert Commissioner systems (전문심리위원제도).

**Criminal Sector (형사분야)**: Strictly limited due to the state monopoly on coercive force (국가의 물리적 강제력 독점). However, external participation exists in citizen jury trials (국민참여재판) and German-style criminal plea agreements (*Verständigung* § 257c StPO).

**Family, Juvenile & Child Protection Sector (가사, 소년·가정·아동보호분야)**: Possesses strong guardianship/supervisory attributes (후견적·복지적 성격). Offers vast potential for external functional delegation in post-trial counseling, probation, and care placements (e.g., Type 6 & 7 juvenile care facilities, domestic violence counseling), while preserving overall court oversight.

<!-- -->

Core Governance Principle — Dual Complementary Strategy (병행적·동시적 고려)  
The report strongly cautions that judicial privatization is NOT a substitute (대체적 관계) for increasing judicial budgets and judge quotas. Rather, judicial privatization and statutory budget/personnel expansion must be pursued parallely and simultaneously (병행적·동시적 고려) as complementary measures.

## 6. MediaWiki Output

**A Study on the Private Participation in Justice** is an official judicial policy research report published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of South Korea's Supreme Court.

## Summary

This document is an official judicial policy research report published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of South Korea's Supreme Court. It comparatively and theoretically analyzes the privatization of justice (사법의 민간화 / private participation in justice), distinguishing between internal privatization (내부적 민간화, based on non-publicity) and external privatization (외부적 민간화, based on outsourcing). It evaluates the civil, criminal, family, and juvenile/child protection sectors, and analyzes the US Rent-a-Judge system, Germany's *Verständigung*, and France's judge-free divorce procedure.

## Legal Basis

The legal frameworks analyzed include:

- **Constitution of the Republic of Korea**: Article 27 (Right to Trial) and Article 101(1) (Judicial Power).
- **Court Organization Act (법원조직법)**: Article 2(1).
- **Civil Procedure Act & Civil Mediation Act (민사소송법 / 민사조정법)**: Articles 1, 164-2~164-8 (Expert Commissioners), Articles 334~341 (Appraisal), and Articles 1~41 of the Civil Mediation Act.
- **Criminal Procedure Act & Act on Criminal Trials with Citizen Participation (형사소송법 / 국민참여재판법)**: Articles 1~3 of the Citizen Participation Act.
- **Family Litigation Act, Juvenile Act, and Domestic/Child Abuse Protection Acts (가사소송법 / 소년법 / 가정폭력처벌법 / 아동학대처벌법)**.

## Background

Growing judicial workloads combined with limited budgets and personnel (the Supreme Court's budget was only 0.33% of the national budget in 2024) have led to trial delays (재판지연). The report formulates judicial privatization as a governance strategy to allow courts to focus on their core functions.

## Objectives

1.  Formulate the concept of judicial privatization (사법의 민간화), distinguishing internal privatization (non-public) from external privatization (functional delegation).
2.  Establish the ultimate objective (궁극적 목적) as "thickly protecting the public's right to a trial through good adjudication" (좋은 재판을 통한 국민의 재판받을 권리의 두터운 보호).
3.  Affirm the principle of parallel and simultaneous consideration (병행적·동시적 고려) — that judicial privatization is NOT a substitute for increasing the number of judges and the judicial budget.

## Scope

- **Domestic Sectoral Study**: The civil sector (ADR, appraisal, expert commissioners), the criminal sector (citizen participation trials), the family sector (mediation, parent education, guardianship), and the juvenile/child sector (Type 6/7 juvenile protection facilities).
- **Foreign Comparative Study**:
  - **United States**: The Rent-a-Judge system (JAMS / Cal. Civ. Proc. Code §§ 638–645.2).
  - **Germany**: The criminal plea agreement (*Verständigung* § 257c StPO).
  - **France**: Consensual divorce without a judge (*divorce sans juge* / *acte déposé au rang des minutes d'un notaire*).

## Key Provisions

- **External Privatization**: Transferring or delegating court tasks to non-judicial institutions outside the court.
- **Internal Privatization**: Conducting proceedings within the court in a closed, non-public setting to expedite resolution without delegating authority externally.
- **State Responsibility Principle**: The state retains ultimate guarantee responsibility (*Gewährleistungsverantwortung*) for the quality and fairness of justice.

## Implementation

Courts implement a staged division of tasks: expanding civil mediation and referring child/juvenile protection matters to verified entrusted institutions (수탁기관), while tightening court oversight and supervision.

## Policy Issues

- A shrinking share of the judicial budget and a frozen judge quota (3,214 since 2019) that burdens Korean judges with 5.17 times the caseload of their German counterparts.
- Risk of declining public trust if privatization proceeds without accountability safeguards.

## Reform / Amendments

1.  Adoption of the Dual Strategy (병행적·동시적 고려): judicial privatization pursued alongside increases in judge quotas and budget.
2.  Reform of the civil sector (adjustment of appraisal fees and expert commissioner incentives).
3.  Strengthening of referral mechanisms in the family and child protection sectors (가사·소년보호 수탁기관).
