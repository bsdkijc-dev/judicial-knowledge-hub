## Policy Analysis

- **Background & Policy Problem**:
  - **FACT**: Formed in 1993 for international technical transfer, the Technical Intern Training Program (TITP) faced heavy criticism as some receiving organizations exploited trainees for cheap labor to address domestic shortages, causing labor breaches, human rights abuses, and high escape (\*absconding\*) rates.
  - **FACT**: Absconded trainees reached 8,796 in 2019 and 5,885 in 2020, with over 50% of illegal Vietnamese overstayers originating from runaway trainees.
- **Policy Objectives**:
  - **FACT**: Ensure fair execution of technical training while securing legal protection for trainees.
  - **FACT**: Advance international human resource development via technology transfer.
- **Key Provisions & Tools**:
  - **FACT**: **Plan Accreditation System**: Every training plan requires individual accreditation by OTIT before trainee entry.
  - **FACT**: **Supervising Organization Licensing**: Dual-tier licensing (\*General\* vs. \*Specified\*) issued jointly by the MOJ and MHLW.
  - **FACT**: **Native-Language Support & Protection**: OTIT provided 13,353 native-language consultations in FY2020 and facilitated workplace transfers for abused trainees.
- **Implementation & Statistics (2020)**:
  - **FACT**: Cumulative totals reached 3,376 supervising licenses and 1,075,523 accredited training plans through FY2020.
  - **FACT**: Total TIT status holders reached 378,200 by end of 2020 (TIT i: 75,681; TIT ii: 262.663; TIT iii: 39,856). Vietnamese nationals comprised ~55%.
- **Sanctions & Regulatory Reforms**:
  - **FACT**: In 2020, authorities revoked 66 training plan accreditations and 8 supervising organization licenses due to violations.
  - **FACT**: Statutory revisions enforced on April 1, 2020, mandated bank transfers for wage payments and froze new intake quotas for entities responsible for runaway trainees.
