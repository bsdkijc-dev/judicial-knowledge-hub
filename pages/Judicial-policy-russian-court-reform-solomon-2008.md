# Judicial-policy-russian-court-reform-solomon-2008

**The Assessing The Courts In Russia: Parameters Of Progress Under Putin Framework** is a policy framework and qualitative-comparative analysis of judicial administration in the Russian Federation. It evaluates the progress and challenges of court reform from the era of Boris Yeltsin through the Presidency of Vladimir Putin based on seven main criteria (judicial independence, fair procedural law, judicial power/jurisdiction, accountability, accessibility, workflow efficiency, and public perception) \[1, 2, 9, 15–17, 23–26\].

## Summary

Published by Prof. Peter H. Solomon, Jr. (University of Toronto) in the *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA), this policy framework dissects the journey of judicial reform in the Russian Federation. Addressing the Soviet legacy that left courts weak and dependent, Yeltsin-era reforms provided life tenure for judges and established the Judicial Department. Entering President Vladimir Putin's era, the government allocated tens of billions of rubles through Federal Targeted Programs to raise judicial salaries, expand the network of *Justices of the Peace*, enact new Criminal and Civil Procedure Codes, and increase public transparency via court websites \[15–21\]. However, the study underlines that the absolute power of *court chairmen* and *accusatorial bias* remain major challenges.

## Legal Basis

- **Constitution of the Russian Federation (1993):** Guarantees the principles of judicial independence and the separation of powers.
- **Conception of Judicial Reform 1991:** The foundational document for post-Soviet judicial reform.
- **Criminal Procedure Code of 2001 & Civil Procedure Code of 2002:** Established adversarial trial principles and shortened procedures (*plea bargaining*).
- **Federal Targeted Programs (2002–2006 & 2007–2011):** The budgetary foundation for modeling and modernizing national courts.

## Background

Following the collapse of the Soviet Union, the Russian judiciary faced severe budget constraints and the absence of modern procedural laws. During Putin's era, available state coffers were utilized to unblock stalled Yeltsin-era reforms, raising minimum judicial salaries to \$1,000/month, and building an accountability system without sacrificing judicial independence.

## Objectives

1\. Evaluate the achievements of Russian judicial reform using 7 qualitative criteria. 2. Address case delays by strengthening the role of the *Justices of the Peace*. 3. Enhance transparency and public accountability by publishing decisions on court websites. 4. Formulate 10 future improvement indicators to fully uphold the *Rule of Law* \[37–42\].

## Scope

This policy evaluates all pillars of the judiciary in Russia: Courts of General Jurisdiction, Arbitrazh (Commercial) Courts, the Constitutional Court, Justices of the Peace, and the Judicial Department.

## Key Provisions

- **Seven Judicial Evaluation Criteria (Solomon):** (1) Judicial independence; (2) Procedural law ensuring equality of parties; (3) Court power/jurisdiction; (4) Accountability systems; (5) Accessibility; (6) Performance efficiency; (7) Public attitudes and trust.
- **Absolute Power of Court Chairmen:** Court chairmen hold the authority to assign case files, control bonuses/facilities, and propose the dismissal of judges to the JQC, making them a potential channel for informal intervention.
- **Accusatorial Bias & Shortened Procedures:** Acquittal rates by single judges remain below 1%, while the *shortened procedure* is used in 37.5% of cases in District Courts and 47% in Justices of the Peace.

## Implementation

- **Strengthening the Justices of the Peace Network:** Handling 65% of civil cases and 30% of criminal cases nationwide to reduce the burden on District Courts.
- **Judicial Salary Increases & Computerization:** Setting a base judicial salary of at least \$1,000/month and launching a national court website system.

## Policy Problems

- **Low Salaries for Court Staff:** Wage disparity between judges and administrative staff triggers high staff turnover rates.
- **Obstacles to Enforcing Judgments:** The failure of bailiffs to execute civil/business judgments encourages the widespread use of private collection services.

## Reform / Change

- Recommendations to limit term lengths and introduce peer election for court chairmen.
- Abolition of rigid statistical metrics of "judicial error" in the performance evaluation of judges.

## Relationships with Other Entities

- **Supreme Court & High Arbitrazh Court:** The apex pillars of Russia's general and commercial judiciaries.
- **Ministry of Economic Development and Trade (MERT):** Initiator of the proposed judicial accountability law.

### 3 Most Important Improvements

1\. **Reforming the Appointment and Term Limits of Court Chairmen:** Alter the selection mechanism for court chairmen so they are elected on a rotating, short-term basis by their peers (*primus inter pares*) to eliminate informal interventions. 2. **Refining Judicial Performance Evaluation Indicators:** Abolish rigid statistical metrics of "judicial error" and "stability of sentences" to free judges from conformity pressures when issuing acquittals. 3. **Improving Welfare and Salaries for Court Administrative Staff:** Allocate Federal Targeted Program funds to increase the salaries of judicial assistants and clerks to reduce staff turnover and maintain the smooth operation of judicial computerization.
