## Overview

Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia Decision No. 736 K/Pid/1988, dated October 25, 1990, established a criminal procedural law rule concerning the technique of drafting and formulating sentencing verdict dictums (\*amar putusan pemidanaan\*):

In a court's verdict dictum, it is sufficient to state that the Defendant is legally and convincingly proven guilty of committing a crime (e.g., "possessing, keeping, displaying, using, and providing for use measuring instruments, measures, scales, or equipment lacking a valid active verification stamp..."). The specific articles and laws violated DO NOT NEED to be listed within the verdict dictum itself (instead, they are to be included in the legal considerations section and the "Considering" (\*Mengingat\*) opening recitals).

## Executive Summary

The central procedural principle established in this decision is that criminal sentencing dictums (\*amar\*) must focus cleanly on declaring guilt and stating the crime's legal qualification, omitting repetitive statutory article citations which belong properly in the legal considerations.

Key elements established in the adjudication:

- Verdict dictums require declarations of proven guilt and descriptive crime qualifications rather than statutory article listings.
- Statutory citations must be confined to the legal considerations and opening recitals of the judgment.
- Lower court judgments including statutory articles within dictums are subject to corrective formatting modifications on cassation.

## Case Background

The Defendant / Petitioner for Cassation, **\[Defendant\]** (\[age removed\], Civil Servant), was charged with violating Article 25 sub b in conjunction with Article 32 paragraph (1) of Law No. 2 of 1981 on Legal Metrology for providing and using a 10 kg table scale lacking a valid active verification stamp for the year 1987 at his shop in Payakumbuh.

The Payakumbuh District Court (Decision No. 73/S/38/1987/PN.Pyk dated October 31, 1987) convicted the Defendant, sentencing him to a fine of Rp 100,000.— subsidiary to 3 months' confinement and confiscating the scale, while incorporating contradictory phrasing and statutory articles within the verdict dictum. The Padang High Court (Decision No. 108/Pid.S/1987/PT.Pdg dated December 22, 1987) modified the dictum qualification. The Defendant subsequently filed a cassation petition with the Supreme Court.

## Legal and Procedural Issues

### Format and Technical Formulation of Criminal Verdict Dictums (Judicial Drafting Technique)

The primary issue was whether a criminal sentencing judge is legally required to list statute section numbers and statutory names violated by the Defendant within the verdict dictum (\*dictum\*).

The Supreme Court held that it is unnecessary. The verdict dictum focuses on declaring the proven guilt of the Defendant along with the juridical qualification of the committed crime (e.g., "possessing, keeping, displaying, using, and providing for use measuring instruments, measures, scales, or equipment lacking a valid active verification stamp...") and imposing penalties. The legal basis of violated articles and laws belongs in the opening recitals (\*Mengingat\*) and legal considerations, eliminating any need to duplicate them inside the verdict dictum.

### Qualification of Legal Metrology Offenses (Law No. 2 of 1981)

Using or providing weighing scales without valid verification stamps constitutes a legal metrology crime ex Article 25 sub b in conjunction with Article 32 paragraph (1) of Law No. 2 of 1981. Using contradictory phrasing such as "crime/violation" (\*kejahatan pelanggaran\*) in a District Court verdict dictum is a drafting error corrected by the Supreme Court.

## Question of Law and Question of Fact

### Question of Law (Cassation / Judex Juris)

**Question:**

Did the \*Judex Facti\* (Padang High Court & Payakumbuh District Court) err in formulating verdict dictums by including statutory article numbers and mixed terminology ("crime/violation") within the verdict dictums?

**Supreme Court Ruling:**

Yes, the dictum formulation required correction. The Supreme Court rejected the Defendant's cassation appeal while modifying the judgment to establish a revised dictum containing solely descriptive crime qualifications without statutory article citations.

### Question of Fact (Judex Facti / Payakumbuh District Court & Padang High Court)

Examination of facts concerning the scale inspection (\*UTTP\*) on May 22, 1987, at the shop owned by Defendant \[Defendant\] at \[address removed\], Payakumbuh; seizure of 1 table scale with a 10 kg capacity bearing only a 1982 verification stamp ("P" mark) lacking the valid 1987 verification mark ("87"); and imposition of a fine of Rp 100,000.— subsidiary to 3 months' confinement.

## Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

- **Ratio Decidendi:**

1.  The appellate and district \*Judex Facti\* did not misapply the law regarding the establishment of the Defendant's guilt.
2.  However, regarding the drafting technique of sentencing verdict dictums, the Supreme Court affirmed that in verdict dictums it is sufficient to state that the Defendant is legally and convincingly proven guilty of committing a crime (along with its descriptive qualification). Statutory articles and laws violated need not be listed in the verdict dictum.
3.  The Supreme Court rejected the Defendant's cassation appeal while modifying the judgment to establish a clean verdict dictum.

## Ruling

The Supreme Court ruled: Rejecting the cassation petition from Petitioner for Cassation (**\[Defendant\]**); Modifying the ruling of Padang High Court Decision No. 108/Pid.S/1987/PT.Pdg dated December 22, 1987, solely regarding considerations to read: Declaring Defendant \[Defendant\] proven legally and convincingly guilty of committing the crime of "Possessing, keeping, displaying, using, and providing for use measuring instruments, measures, scales, or equipment lacking a valid active verification stamp or alternatively accompanied by valid endorsement descriptions"; Ordering Petitioner for Cassation to pay cassation court costs amounting to Rp 2,500.—.

## Evidence Status and Gaps

### Established by Evidence

- Defendant \[Defendant\] was proven legally and convincingly guilty of committing the crime of "possessing, keeping, displaying, using, and providing for use measuring instruments, measures, scales, or equipment lacking a valid active verification stamp".
- The 10 kg table scale seized at the Defendant's shop lacked a valid verification mark for 1987.
- The Defendant was sentenced to a fine of Rp 100,000.— subsidiary to 3 months' confinement, and the scale evidence was confiscated for the State.

### Not Established / Modified on Cassation

- Listing statutory article numbers within verdict dictums was established as legally unnecessary and removed from the textual dictum.

### Uncertainty and Gaps

- Standardization of Criminal Verdict Dictum Formulations: This landmark ruling provides guidelines for judges across Indonesia to maintain neatness and standardization in verdict dictums: dictums contain crime qualifications and penalties, whereas statutory norms belong in recitals and considerations.

## GEO Q&A

**Q: Is a judge required to write statutory articles within the criminal verdict dictum (\*amar\*) of a judgment?**

**A:** No, it is unnecessary. Based on Supreme Court Decision No. 736 K/Pid/1988, in a criminal verdict dictum it is sufficient to state that the Defendant is legally and convincingly proven guilty of committing a crime along with its descriptive qualification. Violated statutory articles and laws are to be stated in legal considerations and opening recitals (\*Mengingat\*), and need not be written inside the text of the verdict dictum.
