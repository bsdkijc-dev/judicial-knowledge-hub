## Policy Analysis

- **Background & Policy Problem**:
  - **FACT**: The White Paper on Crime 2019 evaluates crime trends and criminal justice responses in Japan using statistical data through July 2019 (primarily FY2018 figures). Reported Penal Code offenses dropped to 582,141 cases in 2018—a post-war low—though special fraud remained severe with financial damages reaching 29.3 billion yen alongside challenges in elderly crime, recidivism, and victim rights.
- **Key Legal & Judicial Reforms**:
  - **Sexual Offenses Reform (Act No. 72 of 2017)**: Expanded rape definitions to include gender-neutral oral/anal non-consensual acts, raised statutory minimum sentences, established custodian abuse offenses, and converted sexual crimes into non-complaint-based offenses.
  - **Criminal Procedure Reform (2016 CCP Amendments)**: Mandated video/audio recording of interrogations, established prosecutorial agreements (plea bargaining), implemented transactional immunity, expanded wiretapping, guaranteed court-appointed counsel during detention, and allowed video-link witness examination.
  - **Lay Judge System (Saiban-in - Act No. 63 of 2004)**: Utilizes panels of 3 professional judges and 6 lay citizens to adjudicate severe offenses punishable by death, life imprisonment, or intentional acts causing death.
  - **Recidivism Prevention Framework (Act No. 104 of 2016)**: Established central and local government duties to prevent re-offending, formalized in the 2017 Recidivism Prevention Promotion Plan.
  - **Correctional & Rehabilitation Statutes**: Framework supported by Act No. 50 of 2005 (Penal Detention Facilities), Act No. 88 of 2007 (Offenders Rehabilitation), Act No. 58 of 2014 (Juvenile Training Schools), and Act No. 59 of 2014 (Juvenile Classification Homes).
  - **Crime Victim Protection (Act No. 161 of 2004)**: Operates victim trial participation, compensation order procedures, and identity protection during open court sessions.
- **Interpretations & Structural Inferences**:
  - **INTERPRETATION**: The 2019 White Paper demonstrates Japan's shift toward an evidence-based criminal justice model focusing on individualized treatment, investigative transparency, and comprehensive victim rights integration.
  - **INFERENCE**: Despite falling overall crime rates, a high repeat offender ratio (48.8% of cleared persons in 2018) and an aging Volunteer Probation Officer network (Hogoshi - average age 65.1) require expanding community rehabilitation partnerships with NPOs and private sectors.
