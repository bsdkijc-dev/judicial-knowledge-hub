==== 1. Regulatory Framework, Non-Commitment Rule, and Institutional Isomorphism ====
'''[SOURCE EVIDENCE]:''' In Japan's civil justice system, pre-trial proceedings (Proses Pra-Sidang) are governed by a dual regulatory hierarchy: primary statutes enacted by the Diet (Code of Civil Procedure / UU) regulating what actions can or must be taken, and procedural rules enacted by the Supreme Court (Rule of Civil Procedure / PerMA) regulating specific operational execution.<ref>Passages 1, 2</ref> The primary goal of pre-trial proceedings is Issue and Evidence Structuring (Penetapan/Penataan Isu dan Bukti).<ref>Passage 3</ref> Issues are defined as disputed facts or events alleged differently by plaintiff and defendant that require proof.<ref>Passage 3</ref> To prevent procedural rigidity where parties defensively maintain initial claims, Japanese pre-trial practice applies the Non-Commitment Rule (Aturan Tanpa Ikatan).<ref>Passage 4</ref> This unwritten rule allows litigating parties to make tentative factual statements or temporary concessions during pre-trial that can be withdrawn later without legal prejudice, fostering open negotiation and rapid issue narrowing.<ref>Passages 4, 5</ref>

'''[THEORETICAL INFERENCE]:''' In Institutional Theory terms, establishing the Non-Commitment Rule creates a safe institutional space that mitigates path dependence and defensive litigation behavior. In Indonesia, civil litigation under HIR/RBg often suffers from institutional decoupling—where written replies (Replik/Duplik) become formalistic, rigid exchanges that fail to clarify disputed facts prior to open trial. Adapting the Non-Commitment Rule into Indonesia's Supreme Court Regulation (RaPerMA) provides a normative bridge, enabling flexible pre-trial discussions without locking parties into premature procedural traps.

==== 2. Judicial / Institutional Structure & Organizational Capacity (5M Framework) ====
Applying the 5M Framework to Prof. IMAZU Ayako's presentation illuminates the structural mechanics of pre-trial case management:

'''Man (Human Capital & Judicial Leadership):'''
* '''[SOURCE EVIDENCE]:''' Pre-trial proceedings are led directly by the trial judge or an assigned panel member (''hakim pemutus perkara'').<ref>Passages 5, 6</ref> Japan previously experimented with assigning separate "preparation judges", but abandoned the model because delegated authority was unclear and assigning the same judge from preparation through trial proved far more efficient.<ref>Passage 6</ref>
* '''[THEORETICAL INFERENCE]:''' In Organizational Sociology, assigning continuous case responsibility to the same judge eliminates coordination overheads and prevents loss of contextual information during trial transitions.

'''Method (Procedural Methodology & Evidence Ordering):'''
* '''[SOURCE EVIDENCE]:''' Pre-trial proceedings enforce a strict 4-step analytical methodology:
*# '''Separation of Claims:''' Distinguishing facts concerning lawsuit validity/admissibility (''keabsahan gugatan / syarat gugatan'', e.g., legal standing, jurisdiction) from facts concerning the merits of the case (''pokok perkara'').<ref>Passages 7-10</ref> If validity is doubtful, judges prioritize examining validity first to avoid wasting resources on merit examinations for invalid suits.<ref>Passage 10</ref>
*# '''Issue Narrowing:''' Eliminating trivial factual differences and rejecting irrelevant evidence requests.<ref>Passage 11</ref>
*# '''Evidence Sequence Rule:''' Strictly enforcing the sequence Documentary Proof First → Witness Examination Second.<ref>Passages 12, 13</ref> Pre-trial proceedings permit examining documentary evidence (''bukti surat'') but strictly prohibit witness examination (''pemeriksaan saksi''), which is reserved exclusively for open court trials after issues are fully narrowed.<ref>Passages 13-16</ref>
*# '''Formal Open Statement:''' Upon pre-trial conclusion, results are formally stated in open court to lock the scope of subsequent trial hearings.<ref>Passage 17</ref>

'''Material (Electronic Minutes & Remote Infrastructure):'''
* '''[SOURCE EVIDENCE]:''' Pre-trial proceedings utilize electronic minutes (''berita acara elektronik'') recording written submissions and evidence requests.<ref>Passage 14</ref> Both parties can attend remotely via online video conference (''rapat daring''), provided the court verifies participant identity and location.<ref>Passage 14</ref>

'''Money (Trial Economy & Administrative Efficiency):'''
* '''[THEORETICAL INFERENCE]:''' Filtering out irrelevant evidence and reading documentary proof during closed pre-trial sessions drastically reduces overall court hearing hours, lowering litigation costs for both the judiciary and commercial litigants.

'''Machine / Technology (Digital Court Integration):'''
* '''[SOURCE EVIDENCE]:''' Hybrid pre-trial attendance (one party in-person, one online) or fully remote online meetings are legally recognized as valid court attendance (''kehadiran'').<ref>Passage 18</ref>
