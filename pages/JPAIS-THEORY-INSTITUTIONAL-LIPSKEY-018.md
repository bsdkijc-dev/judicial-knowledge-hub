## 1. EPISTEMOLOGICAL CLASSIFICATION TAXONOMY

To preserve strict provenance, eliminate ungrounded claims, and maintain empirical rigor, all analytical statements across this document are tagged according to the following taxonomy:

- **\[SOURCE EVIDENCE\]:** Direct factual data, statutory articles (Civil Conciliation Act / UU Chōtei Perdata Act No. 222/1951), procedural workflows, and structural definitions provided in D5.AM_Prosedur dan Kondisi Aktual Chotei Perdata di Jepang (Attorney Sonoo).pdf.
- **\[EMPIRICAL EVIDENCE\]:** Operational metrics and statistical data documented in Japanese civil conciliation practice for 2024 (e.g., 30,211 civil Chōtei petitions; 29,639 concluded cases with 23.97% agreed settlements and 36.29% Article 17 Orders in Lieu of Conciliation, achieving a combined 60.26% resolution rate; state honorarium of ~¥10,000/day for lay commissioners; 50% court filing fee discount; 2-week objection window).
- **\[THEORETICAL INFERENCE\]:** Deductive application of established social science paradigms (Institutional Isomorphism, Decoupling, Lipsky's Street-Level Bureaucracy, Weberian Bureaucracy, Curial Discretion, 5M Framework) onto the structural dispute resolution mechanisms detailed in the primary source.
- **\[ANALYTICAL SYNTHESIS\]:** Comparative cross-synthesis connecting Japanese civil conciliation (Minji Chōtei), lay expert panel composition, Article 17 orders, and Article 20(1) litigation remand to the Indonesian civil justice reform context (PERMA No. 1 of 2016, RaPerMA, Tripartite Conciliation Panels, e-Court/SIPP integration, JPAIS KCCP objectives).
- **\[UNCERTAIN\]:** Express identification of domains or empirical metrics where the primary source material lacks specific data (e.g., quantitative filing statistics for specific Indonesian District Courts or non-judicial administrative business licensing metrics in Indonesia).
