# Judicial-policy-harmening-localgeography-2025

**Geographic representation in local politics: Evidence from parliamentary questions in German city councils** is an empirical comparative political science research paper authored by Morten Harmening, Jona-Frederik Baumert, Sebastian Block, Martin Gross, Dominic Nyhuis, and Jan Velimsky of Leibniz University Hannover, LMU Munich, and Stuttgart University, published in the *European Journal of Political Research* (EJPR) in 2025 \[1–2\].

## Summary

This study analyzes patterns of geographic representation and local constituency appeals in 10,189 parliamentary questions submitted across 12 German city councils over a single legislative term \[1–2, 7\]. Using automated geospatial dictionaries built from Wikipedia and OpenStreetMap location data, the study finds that local politics is intensely territorial: \*\*43.5%\*\* of all questions contain a geographic reference \[2, 7–8, 41–43\]. Local political parties strategically prioritize areas that form their electoral strongholds, especially when their voter base is highly localized and when the electoral system employs electoral districts \[2, 8, 22–25, 58–61\].

## Legal Basis

- **Not found in the available sources:** This document is an academic political science research study and is not based on any specific statutory or judicial legislative instrument \[1–100\].
- **State Local Government Acts (\*Gemeindeordnungen\*):** German state-level statutes governing municipal council structures and guaranteeing councillors the right to submit questions to local public administrations.
- **Municipal Council Rules of Procedure (\*Geschäftsordnungen\*):** Internal council rules formally enshrining the parliamentary-question instrument.

## Background

In party-centered proportional representation systems, individual councillors rarely vote against party lines. Geographic constituency representation is therefore pursued through non-binding legislative instruments, primarily parliamentary questions \[9–12\]. Unlike national politics, which deals with abstract policy, local council politics addresses concrete physical infrastructure and spatial decisions tied to actual locations.

## Objectives

1\. Measure the level of geographic representation embedded in the parliamentary-question instrument at the municipal level \[2, 7–8\]. 2. Test whether political parties strategically focus more attention on their electoral strongholds (Hypothesis 1) \[22–23, 58\]. 3. Test the interaction effect between a localized voter base and the level of geographic questioning (Hypothesis 2) \[23–24, 58\]. 4. Analyze the effect of electoral system rules (the presence of electoral districts) on incentives for geographic representation (Hypothesis 3) \[24–25, 59–61\].

## Scope

The research covers 12 large German cities (population \> 100,000): Chemnitz, Düsseldorf, Erfurt, Frankfurt am Main, Halle/Saale, Karlsruhe, Leipzig, Magdeburg, Mainz, Munich, Potsdam, and Stuttgart, involving 6 national parties (AfD, CDU/CSU, FDP, Greens, SPD, The Left) \[1, 26, 28–34\].

## Key Provisions / Findings

- **High Overall Localness:** On average, 43.5% of city council questions contain a geographic reference, ranging from 24.4% in Mainz to 57.1% in Stuttgart \[41–43\].
- **Hypothesis 1 Supported (Electoral Strongholds):** A party's district vote share has a statistically significant positive association with the number of questions referencing that district (b = 0.023–0.032, p \< 0.05) \[52–54, 58\].
- **Hypothesis 2 Supported (Localized Electorate Interaction):** The effect of defending a stronghold area is strongest when a party's voter base is localized — i.e., low city-wide vote share combined with high district vote share (b = -0.002, p \< 0.05) \[52–54, 58\].
- **Hypothesis 3 Supported (Electoral District Incentives):** The use of electoral districts in city council elections significantly increases geographic questioning across the board (b = 0.807–0.848, p \< 0.05) \[52–54, 59–61\].
- **Construction of Geospatial Dictionaries:** The authors built 455 custom district dictionaries combining official shapefiles, physical landmark entries from German Wikipedia, and street network data from OpenStreetMap \[7, 36–39\].
- **Control Variables:** Larger dictionary size increases mentions (b = 0.005, p \< 0.05); distance from the city center decreases mentions (b = -0.132 to -0.140, p \< 0.05), confirming that central districts receive disproportionate attention \[52–54, 62\]. Over 30% of total variance is explained at the city level (ICC ≈ 0.30).

## Implementation

The methodology was implemented through multilevel negative binomial regression models with random intercepts at the city level and party fixed effects, controlling for dictionary size, distance to city center, term length, and city-wide vote share \[49–55\]. Replication data and code are published openly on the Open Science Framework (OSF) at \`https://osf.io/bhzc2/\`.

## Policy Issues

1\. A systematic imbalance in territorial representation, whereby areas outside a party's electoral base tend to be neglected in council deliberations. 2. The risk of socio-spatial segregation, where the concentration of questions on certain districts may deepen disparities in infrastructure and services between wealthier and poorer neighborhoods.

## Reform / Implications

The study demonstrates that local councillors' political behavior is shaped by institutional incentives similar to those found in national politics, and it offers a new, geospatial big-data-based method for measuring representation \[2, 7, 65–66\].
