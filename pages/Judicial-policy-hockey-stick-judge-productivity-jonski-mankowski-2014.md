# Judicial-policy-hockey-stick-judge-productivity-jonski-mankowski-2014

**The Hockey-Stick Judicial Production Function & Productivity Policy Framework** is a quantitative judicial efficiency and judge workload policy framework formulated by Kamil Joński and Daniel Mańkowski (Ministry of Justice of the Republic of Poland, 2014) based on empirical research across 45 first-instance criminal courts (\*Regional Courts / Sąd Okręgowy\*) in Poland. It revises the 'exogenous productivity of judges' hypothesis from the Law & Economics tradition (Beenstock & Haitovsky 2004), combines the weighted caseload model with a non-parametric LOWESS production function shaped like a 'hockey-stick' (featuring a breaking point/threshold of a judge's physical capacity at ~38 felony cases/year or ~77–102 hours per case), and evaluates the model based on a macroeconomic stylized facts approach \[1, 4–5, 8–10, 23–26, 38–41, 53–54\].

## Summary

Published by Kamil Joński and Daniel Mańkowski in the \*International Journal for Court Administration\* (IJCA), this policy framework revises the dramatic claims of Law & Economics (L&E) literature which asserted that judge productivity is exogenous and infinitely elastic \[1, 10–12, 53\]. Using data from 45 Regional Courts (\*Sąd Okręgowy\*) in Poland, the authors reconstructed the courts' production functions using the non-parametric LOWESS technique \[37–38\]. The results proved the existence of a \*hockey-stick\* shaped production function: at low to moderate workloads (\$\le 38\$ cases/judge), productivity increases linearly with the workload; however, once it surpasses the breaking point (\$\sim 38\$ felony cases per year), the judge's productivity hits a physical upper limit (\$\sim 28\$ resolutions/year) \[39–40, 66\]. These findings align perfectly with the calculation of judge working hours in the \*Weighted Caseload Study\* by the Polish Ministry of Justice.

## Legal Basis

- **Polish Code of Criminal Procedure (CCP / Kodeks postępowania karnego 1997):**
  - \*Article 25 § 1 CCP\*: Jurisdiction of the Regional Court (\*Sąd Okręgowy\*) over serious crimes (\*felonies\*) carrying a penalty of 3 years or more, narcotics offenses, and war crimes \[30–31\].
  - \*Article 28 CCP\*: Rules on the composition of the judicial panel (1 professional judge + 2 lay judges for general cases; 2 professional judges + 3 lay judges for life imprisonment cases) \[28–29\].
  - \*Article 335 & 387 CCP\*: Schemes involving parties' agreements for resolution without a full trial (\*conviction without trial\*) \[34–36\].
  - \*Principle of Objective Truth\*: The judge's obligation to actively collect evidence in the inquisitorial tradition.
- **Not found in the available sources:** Other specific formal statutory regulations are not mentioned in the source text \[FACT\].

## Background

Since Roscoe Pound (1906) and Zeisel et al. (1959), the allocation of judicial staff has been based on \*judge-year\* calculations and the \*weighted caseload\* methodology \[2, 4–5\]. However, L&E models based on the Cobb-Douglas production function (Beenstock & Haitovsky 2004) mistakenly glorified the exogenous productivity of judges and suggested that allowing case backlogs to accumulate would automatically force judges to work faster \[10–12, 15\].

## Objectives

1\. Reduce the methodological errors of the Cobb-Douglas econometric model in measuring court performance \[1, 18–19, 53\]. 2. Reconcile the econometric production function model with the \*weighted caseload\* methodology. 3. Test the model's validity based on a macroeconomic \*stylized facts\* approach \[8, 23–26\]. 4. Measure the impact of workload tension on the quality of procedural justice in the courtroom \[45, 48–50\].

## Scope

This policy encompasses an econometric analysis of 45 Regional Courts (\*Sąd Okręgowy\*) in Poland and integrates independent monitoring data of 378 criminal trials across 12 courts conducted by the \*Court Watch Poland Foundation\* \[20, 37–38, 47\].

## Key Provisions

- **Specifications of the 'Hockey-Stick' Production Function Model**:

1.  \*Steep Part (Workload \$\le 38\$ cases/judge)\*: Judge productivity adapts linearly to an increasing workload.
2.  \*Break Point / Threshold (Workload \$\approx 38\$ cases/judge)\*: The saturation threshold of a judge's physical capacity, equivalent to an accumulation of \$\sim 102\$ working hours per \*felony\* case \[33, 39–40, 42\].
3.  \*Flat Part (Workload \$\> 38\$ cases/judge)\*: Judge productivity hits a constant \*glass ceiling\* at \$\sim 28\$ resolutions/year \[39–40, 66\].

- **Rejection of the Cobb-Douglas Model**:
  - Historical simulation tests (2008–2012) prove that the Cobb-Douglas model falsely predicts a self-clearing backlog drop in large courts (like SO Warszawa), while the Hockey-Stick Model accurately replicates real-world backlog accumulation \[40–42, 44\].
- **Efficiency and Trial Quality Trade-Off**:
  - Workloads exceeding the breaking point are proven to trigger increased \*judicial lateness\* (\> 10%), \*judicial rudeness\*, and errors in drafting trial minutes \[48–51\].

## Implementation

- \*\*Non-Parametric LOWESS Method\*\*: Eliminates the bias of rigid econometric curve shape assumptions.
- \*\*Citizen Trial Monitoring Integration\*\*: Combines quantitative econometric data with voluntary field observations by Court Watch Poland \[47–50\].

## Policy Problems

- \*\*Upper Limit of Coping\*\*: The reality that a judge's working time is limited (24 hours a day), meaning endless workload increases actually damage the health of the judicial organization.
- \*\*The Fallacy of Judge Reduction Recommendations\*\*: Cutting the number of judges in overburdened courts has been empirically proven to accelerate backlog inflation \[12, 41–42, 54\].

## Reform / Change

- The absolute necessity of \*additional staffing\* or \*procedural streamlining\* when the workload surpasses the breaking point.
- The adoption of \*Bayesian estimation\* for the \*ex-ante impact assessment\* of judicial reforms.

### 3 Most Important Improvements

1\. **Cessation of the "Do-Nothing" Caseload Policy Without Adding Judges:** Stop using the Cobb-Douglas model in judicial budget planning and recognize that leaving courts overwhelmed does not increase productivity, but rather triggers an explosion of case backlogs \[12, 41–42, 54\]. 2. **Implementation of Capacity Threshold Staff Allocation:** Use the hockey-stick break point (\$\sim 38\$ felony cases/year per judge) as a mandatory trigger indicator to add judicial staff or redistribute workloads across regions \[39–40, 54\]. 3. **Adoption of Bayesian Estimation for Procedural Reforms:** Integrate quantitative Bayesian analysis that combines weighted caseload metrics with aggregate trial data to conduct ex-ante impact assessments before enacting criminal procedural streamlining.
