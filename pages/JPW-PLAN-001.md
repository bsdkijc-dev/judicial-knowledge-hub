# JPW-PLAN-001

**Strategic Plan of the International Criminal Court for 2023-2025** is the ICC's official strategic policy document for the 2023–2025 period, setting out 10 strategic goals across 3 pillars, supported by 27 measurable Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) and, for the first time, aligning the four principal components of the Court (the Court/Presidency, the OTP, the Registry, and the TFV) within a single strategic cycle.

## Summary

The document harmonizes four internal strategic plans — the Court as a whole, the Office of the Prosecutor (OTP), the Registry, and the Trust Fund for Victims (TFV) — into one coordinated cycle. The plan is anchored by 10 strategic goals across 3 pillars, complemented by 27 KPIs for objective measurement of results.

## Legal Basis

- [Rome Statute](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Rome_Statute): particularly the complementarity principle and Article 93(10) on information-sharing with national authorities.
- Mandate of the [Assembly of States Parties](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Assembly_of_States_Parties) (ASP).
- Recommendations of the [Independent Expert Review](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Independent_Expert_Review) (IER), 2020.
- The [One-Court Principle](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/One-Court_Principle) coordination framework, through the [Coordination Council](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/Coordination_Council).

## Background

The plan continues and expands on the achievements of the 2019–2021 Strategic Plan (extended through 2022). It responds to a sharp increase in judicial workload, the growing complexity of global conflicts, historical liquidity crises, and the need for internal governance reform following the Independent Expert Review. For the first time in the Court's history, it aligns four concurrent strategic plans in a single cycle and integrates 384 recommendations from the IER's Final Report of 30 September 2020.

## Objectives

- Provide a responsive, flexible, and resilient operational framework.
- Accelerate the expeditiousness of proceedings without compromising fairness or the highest legal standards.
- Integrate victims' rights, gender perspectives, and the handling of sexual and gender-based crimes (SGBC) and crimes against children (CAC).
- Strengthen international cooperation, complementarity, and resource-management efficiency.

## Key Provisions

The plan sets out 10 strategic goals across 3 pillars:

- **Pillar 1: Judicial and Prosecutorial Performance**
  - **Goal 1**: Improve the speed and efficiency of core judicial activities.
  - **Goal 2**: Develop the Court's approach to victims and reparations.
  - **Goal 3**: Mainstream gender perspectives across all areas of work.
- **Pillar 2: Cooperation and Complementarity**
  - **Goal 4**: Strengthen political support and operational cooperation modalities.
  - **Goal 5**: Advance complementarity and close gaps in impunity.
- **Pillar 3: Organizational Performance**
  - **Goal 6**: Strengthen professionalism, dedication, and integrity.
  - **Goal 7**: Ensure a safe, secure work environment focused on staff wellbeing.
  - **Goal 8**: Achieve fair geographical representation and gender balance (GRGB).
  - **Goal 9**: Manage resources effectively, transparently, and sustainably (environmentally responsible).
  - **Goal 10**: Develop and implement a completion strategy for situations under investigation.

## Implementation

Implementation is anchored in the One-Court principle and monitored via the Court-wide Values Project (launched 13 July 2022), advisory case-handling deadlines in the 5th edition of the Chambers' Practice Manual, expanded field presence, adoption of SGBC and CAC investigation policies, appointment of an Ombuds for informal dispute resolution, alignment with the 2023 annual budget, and regular risk monitoring by the Risk Management Committee (RMC).

## Evidence

The framework is monitored through 27 quantitative and qualitative KPIs developed via inter-organ consultation. Key indicators include:

- **Judicial Timeframes (KPI 1.1)**: Targets for investigation authorization (120 days), first appearance (48–96 hours), confirmation-of-charges decisions (60 days), Article 74 judgments (10 months), and appeal judgments (10 months).
- **Hearing Cancellation Rate (KPI 1.2)**: Baseline of 42.2%, with a target for significant reduction.
- **Geographical and Gender Representation (KPI 8.1–8.3)**: Target to reduce the number of non-represented/under-represented States Parties (baseline 25/54, target 24/53) and ensure at least 50% of senior staff recruitment (P-4 and above) are women.
- **Budget Compliance (KPI 9.1)**: Baseline budget execution rate of 97.4%, with a target of 100%.
