# JPAIS:REFORM-PROPOSAL:JPAIS-MED-REF-010

## Summary / Ringkasan

## Entity Ingestion / Ingesti Entitas

- Entity ID: JPAIS-MED-REF-010
- Knowledge Grapher: Cecep Mustafa
- Ingestion Status: Confirmed

## Description / Deskripsi

Evaluates alternative dispute resolution (ADR) architectures across six core domains between Indonesia and Japan, addressing structural bottlenecks such as Indonesia's low general civil mediation success rates (4.0%–4.08%) versus Japan's high conciliation efficiency (\>50% general civil, \>70% family/inheritance) through the Chōtei committee system, and proposes the Japan-Indonesia ADR Integration System (JPAIS) reform framework\[cite: 10\].

## Problem / Masalah

- [JPAIS-MED-PROB-001](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/JPAIS-MED-PROB-001): Court-connected mediation in Indonesia suffers from structural ritualization and negligible general civil settlement rates (4.0%–4.08%), driven by overloaded solo judge-mediators and uncompensated non-judge mediators\[cite: 10\].
- [JPAIS-MED-PROB-002](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/JPAIS-MED-PROB-002): Arbitration interfaces face post-award delays, dilatory annulment actions under Article 70 of Law No. 30 of 1999 following CC Decision No. 15/PUU-XII/2014, and the lack of direct executory power for provisional measures\[cite: 10\].
- [JPAIS-MED-PROB-003](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/JPAIS-MED-PROB-003): Small claims and electronic dispute resolution frameworks lack pre-litigation settlement ratification mechanisms and secure, integrated ODR virtual caucus portals, while mediated settlements encounter slow domestic execution and unratified cross-border enforcement (Singapore Convention)\[cite: 10\].

## Current Indonesian Practice / Praktik Indonesia Saat Ini

- [JPAIS-MED-ID-001](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/JPAIS-MED-ID-001): Governed by PERMA No. 1 of 2016, PERMA No. 4 of 2019 (Small Claims), PERMA No. 3 of 2022 (Electronic Mediation), PERMA No. 3 of 2023 (Arbitration Procedures), and Law No. 30 of 1999 on Arbitration and ADR\[cite: 10\].

## Japanese Practice / Praktik Jepang

- [JPAIS-MED-JP-001](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/JPAIS-MED-JP-001): Governed by the Civil Conciliation Act (1951), Domestic Relations Case Procedure Act (2011), Arbitration Act (2003), and ADR Act (2004), utilizing tripartite Conciliation Committees (Chōtei Iinkai) and ex-officio Orders in Lieu of Conciliation (Article 17)\[cite: 10\].

## Gap / Kesenjangan

- [JPAIS-MED-GAP-001](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/JPAIS-MED-GAP-001): Reliance on a solo mediator model without multidisciplinary expert panels, evaluative settlement authority, or enforced bad-faith cost sanctions\[cite: 10\].
- [JPAIS-MED-GAP-002](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/JPAIS-MED-GAP-002): Absence of summary screening for frivolous arbitral annulments, pre-action settlement ratification for small claims, mandatory default ODR portals, and statutory implementation for the Singapore Convention on Mediation\[cite: 10\].

## Proposed Adaptation / Adaptasi Usulan

Establish Tripartite Conciliation Panels (Majelis Konsiliasi Tripartit) with judicial Orders in Lieu of Settlement (Penetapan Pengganti Perdamaian), specialized commercial arbitration supervision benches with 7-day screening, pre-action settlement ratification for small claims, default SIPP-ODR portals with BSrE signatures, domestic fast-track execution, Singapore Convention ratification, and subsidized lay conciliator allowances\[cite: 10\].

## Action / Aksi

- [JPAIS-MED-ACT-001](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/JPAIS-MED-ACT-001): Issue Supreme Court Implementation Decree establishing the JPAIS pilot program across 12 designated courts, configure SIPP-ODR portal, and accredit 120 Non-Judge Expert Commissioners\[cite: 10\].
- [JPAIS-MED-ACT-002](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/JPAIS-MED-ACT-002): Deploy Tripartite Conciliation Panels, Rapid Small Claims Settlement Desks, and Specialized Commercial Arbitration Supervision Benches across pilot jurisdictions\[cite: 10\].
- [JPAIS-MED-ACT-003](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/JPAIS-MED-ACT-003): Conduct empirical performance audits, track settlement and execution timelines, finalize draft permanent PERMAs, and submit statutory reform proposals to DPR\[cite: 10\].

## Pilot / Pilot Proyek

- [JPAIS-MED-PILOT-010](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/JPAIS-MED-PILOT-010): 90-day multi-court pilot program across 12 target courts (PN Jakarta Pusat, PN Surabaya, PN Medan, PN Bandung, PN Semarang, PN Denpasar, PN Kupang, PN Jayapura, PA Jakarta Selatan, PA Surabaya, PA Malang, PA Depok)\[cite: 10\].

## KPI / Indikator Kinerja Utama

- [JPAIS-MED-KPI-010](https://judicialknowledge.miraheze.org/wiki/JPAIS-MED-KPI-010): General civil mediation success \$\ge 25\\\$; religious property/inheritance resolution \$\ge 60\\\$; mediation duration \$\le 21\$ calendar days; arbitral annulment disposal compliance \$100\\\$ (\$\>85\\\$ frivolous claims dismissed in 7 days); small claims ratification \$\le 3\$ days; ODR commercial processing \$\ge 50\\\$; settlement execution writ issuance \$\le 21\$ calendar days\[cite: 10\].

## Policy Implication / Implikasi Kebijakan

Promulgate joint ministerial decrees, Supreme Court circulars, and executive-legislative ratifications to establish sustainable funding for lay conciliators, ODR cryptographic standards, and international treaty enforcement\[cite: 10\].

## Regulatory Implication / Implikasi Regulasi

Promulgate revised PERMAs on Court-Connected Mediation and Conciliation, Small Claims Settlement Ratification, Electronic Mediation, and Arbitral Annulment Screening, alongside statutory revisions to Law No. 30 of 1999 and ratification of the Singapore Convention\[cite: 10\].

## Evidence / Bukti Empiris

- Indonesian general civil mediation success rates hover between 4.0% and 4.08%, while Japanese conciliation achieves \$\>50\\\$ in general civil disputes and \$71.8\\\$ in inheritance property cases\[cite: 10\].
- Japanese Article 17 orders resolve 29.9% of inheritance division cases alongside 42.0% consensual accords within two to three sessions spanning approximately three months\[cite: 10\].
