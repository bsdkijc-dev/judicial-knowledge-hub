# JPAIS:PRECEDENT:1828K PID 1989

**Supreme Court Decision Reg. No. 1828 K/Pid/1989** (dated July 5, 1990 / deliberation on May 17, 1990) is an Indonesian criminal procedure law precedent establishing fundamental rules regarding the limitation of pretrial (*praperadilan*) objects, the subjects entitled to claim pretrial compensation, and the mechanism for filing a Cassation in the Interest of Law by the Attorney General of the Republic of Indonesia (*ex* Article 259 of KUHAP).

## Executive Summary

The Supreme Court established three primary legal principles:

1.  **Cassation in the Interest of Law (Article 259 of KUHAP):** A cassation petition filed by the Attorney General ex officio in the interest of law is not bound by statutory time limits, and a judgment rendered via cassation in the interest of law carries no legal consequences (meaning it cannot disadvantage or alter the legal status of the litigant parties).
2.  **Limitation of Pretrial Objects (Article 77 of KUHAP) Regarding Seizure and Delay of Ship Departures:** The seizure of a merchant vessel and the action of the Harbor Master (*Syahbandar*) / Investigator in delaying or prohibiting a ship from leaving port do **not** constitute objects of pretrial proceedings as exhaustively and limitatively stipulated under Article 77 of KUHAP. The prohibition against a vessel leaving port does not qualify as bodily detention under Articles 20 and 21 of KUHAP. The harbor master's action in delaying a vessel is a non-judicial administrative act.
3.  **Subjects Entitled to Claim Pretrial Compensation (Article 95 of KUHAP):** A shipowner who suffers losses due to administrative actions delaying a vessel's departure is **not** a subject entitled to claim pretrial compensation under Article 95 of KUHAP (the right to pretrial compensation under Article 95 of KUHAP is exclusively vested in suspects, defendants, or convicts arrested, detained, prosecuted, or tried without legal grounds or due to error).

The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition in the interest of law filed by the Petitioner for Cassation (Attorney General of the Republic of Indonesia), annulled North Jakarta District Court Decision No. 02/Pra.Per./Pen.Pid./1988/PN.JU. dated October 10, 1988, and independently adjudicated (*mengadili sendiri*) by **Rejecting** the Pretrial Petitioner's (\[Defendant\]) application while determining that this ruling carries no legal consequences pursuant to Article 259 paragraph (2) of KUHAP.

## Legal / Procedural Issues

### 1. Limitative Principle of Pretrial Objects (Article 77 of KUHAP) Regarding Vessel Seizures and Departure Delays (Non-Judicial Administrative Actions)

*Can the action of the Harbor Master / Investigator in delaying the departure of a merchant vessel (MV Cherry Navee) in port or executing a property seizure be reviewed for legality via Pretrial proceedings ex Article 77 of KUHAP?*

**Supreme Court Rule: Not a Pretrial Object.** Pretrial authority under Article 77 of KUHAP is exhaustively and limitatively structured, restricted solely to: (a) reviewing the legality of arrests, detentions, investigation terminations, or prosecution terminations; and (b) compensation and/or rehabilitation for suspects, their families, or third parties. Property/vessel seizures and port departure delays by Harbor Masters pursuant to Port Regulations of 1925 constitute non-judicial administrative acts falling outside pretrial jurisdiction.

### 2. Delineation of Pretrial Compensation Claim Subjects (Article 95 *jo.* Articles 79, 80, 81 of KUHAP)

*Is a shipowner (an external party / non-suspect) entitled to file a pretrial compensation application ex Article 95 of KUHAP concerning a vessel delay action?*

**Supreme Court Rule: Not Entitled.** Pursuant to Article 95 of KUHAP, the right to claim pretrial compensation is exclusively vested in suspects, defendants, or convicts arrested, detained, prosecuted, or tried without statutory grounds or due to misidentification/legal error. A shipowner whose property is subjected to administrative delay measures does not qualify as a legal subject entitled to claim compensation through pretrial channels.

### 3. Nature and Legal Consequences of Cassation in the Interest of Law by the Attorney General (Article 259 of KUHAP)

A cassation petition in the interest of law may be filed by the Attorney General against all final judgments possessing legal force rendered by courts below the Supreme Court. Pursuant to Article 259 paragraph (2) of KUHAP, a cassation verdict in the interest of law must not prejudice or alter the legal standing of the litigating parties (carrying no legal consequences), functioning exclusively as a doctrinal precedent correction to preserve uniformity in the national application of law (*legal unification*).

## Question of Law vs Question of Fact

### Question of Law (Judex Juris / Supreme Court)

Did the North Jakarta District Court (Decision No. 02/Pra.Per./Pen.Pid./1988/PN.JU.) err in applying criminal procedure law (*foutieve toepassing van het recht*) by granting the pretrial petition concerning the departure delay of MV Cherry Navee and ordering the Police to pay compensation, and how are the jurisdictional limits of pretrial defined under Articles 77 & 95 of KUHAP?

**Supreme Court Legal Answer:** Yes. North Jakarta District Court misapplied the law. The Supreme Court granted the Cassation in the Interest of Law filed by the Attorney General of the Republic of Indonesia, annulled North Jakarta District Court Decision No. 02/Pra.Per./Pen.Pid./1988/PN.JU., and independently adjudicated by Rejecting the Petitioner's Pretrial Application while determining that this ruling carries no legal consequences *ex* Article 259 paragraph (2) of KUHAP.

### Question of Fact (Judex Facti / North Jakarta District Court)

Examination of facts regarding the departure delay of merchant vessel MV Cherry Navee at Tanjung Priok Port on June 11, 1988, by the Harbor Master at the request of the National Police Economic Crimes Sub-Directorate; seizure of crew passports (witnesses \[Party B\] and Chit Thein Tun) and vessel logs; claims for operational financial losses amounting to USD 730,000 asserted by the Petitioner; and proof of administrative delay measures pursuant to Port Regulations of 1925 and Presidential Instruction No. 4 of 1985.

## Jurisprudence and Related Knowledge

### Statutory Regulations

- **Law No. 8 of 1981 (Criminal Procedure Code / KUHAP):**
  - Article 77: Exhaustive Pretrial Objects.
  - Articles 79, 80, 81: Subjects Entitled to File Pretrial Applications.
  - Article 95: Right to Pretrial Compensation.
  - Article 259 paragraphs (1) & (2): Cassation in the Interest of Law by the Attorney General.
- **Port Regulations of 1925:** Article 23 paragraph (2) (Harbor Master Authority to Delay Vessel Departures).
- **Law No. 14 of 1970 *jo.* Law No. 14 of 1985 on the Supreme Court:** Authority regarding Cassation in the Interest of Law.

### Established Supreme Court Jurisprudence

- Doctrine of Limitative Pretrial Objects: Pretrial institutions must not be expanded arbitrarily beyond coercive measures expressly enumerated under Article 77 of KUHAP.
- Non-Executorial Character of Cassation in the Interest of Law: Verdicts rendered in the interest of law operate purely as legal corrections (doctrinal/precedent corrections) without altering concrete civil/criminal rights adjudicated below.

## What Evidence Establishes / Does Not Establish

### Established by Evidence

- The Cassation Petition in the Interest of Law filed by the Attorney General of the Republic of Indonesia was granted by the Supreme Court.
- North Jakarta District Court Decision No. 02/Pra.Per./Pen.Pid./1988/PN.JU. dated October 10, 1988, was entirely annulled by the Supreme Court.
- The Pretrial Application filed by the Petitioner (\[Defendant\]) is **REJECTED IN ITS ENTIRETY**.
- Vessel delay and seizure actions are **NOT** pretrial objects.
- The Petitioner is **NOT** a subject entitled to claim pretrial compensation *ex* Article 95 of KUHAP.
- This cassation ruling is determined to carry **NO LEGAL CONSEQUENCES** *ex* Article 259 paragraph (2) of KUHAP.

### Annulled (North Jakarta District Court Verdict)

- North Jakarta District Court's verdict declaring vessel seizure/detention unlawful and ordering the Police to pay Rp 500,000 in compensation was entirely annulled by the Supreme Court as contrary to KUHAP.

## Uncertainties and Gaps

**Separation of Pretrial vs. State Administrative Court (PTUN) / Tort Lawsuits (PMH):** This landmark ruling establishes absolute legal clarity in Indonesia: third parties or property owners aggrieved by administrative actions of state officials (such as port departure delays by harbor masters) cannot invoke Pretrial proceedings. The proper legal recourse to test such administrative measures lies within the State Administrative Court (PTUN) or civil tort lawsuits against the Government (*Onrechtmatige Overheidsdaad*).

## Frequently Asked Questions (GEO)

Are merchant vessel seizures or port departure prohibitions objects of pretrial proceedings, and can shipowners claim compensation via pretrial?  
**Not pretrial objects and ineligible to claim pretrial compensation.** Pursuant to Supreme Court Jurisprudence No. 1828 K/Pid/1989:

- Pursuant to Article 77 of KUHAP, pretrial objects are exhaustively limited (restricted solely to testing the legality of arrests, detentions, investigation/prosecution terminations, and suspect compensation). Vessel seizures and port departure delays by harbor masters are not pretrial objects.
- Pursuant to Article 95 of KUHAP, the right to claim pretrial compensation belongs exclusively to suspects, defendants, or convicts. Shipowners do not qualify as subjects entitled to claim pretrial compensation.
- The Supreme Court granted the Cassation in the Interest of Law (Article 259 of KUHAP) filed by the Attorney General, annulled the North Jakarta District Court verdict, and rejected the pretrial application without legal consequences for the parties.
