# Judicial-policy-court-manager-india-oberoi-2017

**The Oberoi Indian Court Manager Policy Framework** is a judicial policy framework and empirical-critical evaluation model concerning the initiative to establish the position of Court Manager (CM) within Indian courts. Initiated through proposals by the National Judicial Academy (NJA) and funded via the Ministry of Finance Circular F.NO.32(30).FCD/2010 under the 13th Finance Commission (an allocation of ₹3 Billion for the 2010–2015 period), the policy aimed to shift non-judicial duties (HR, planning, ICT, facilities, budgeting, and statistics) away from judges. However, the initiative suffered from failure and desertion due to the cultural and hierarchical resistance of judges who viewed administrative duties as part of their power and jurisdiction. This failure was compounded by the lack of permanent status or competitive salary for CMs, uncertain contractual employment, resistance from traditional court staff, and the absence of clear leadership and mandates from the Supreme Court of India. The framework is analyzed by Prof. dr. Geeta Oberoi in the *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA), Vol. 9 No. 1 (December 2017) \[1–15, 37–47\].

## Summary

Published by Prof. dr. Geeta Oberoi in the IJCA (December 2017), this policy framework evaluates the systemic failure of the Court Manager (CM) position in India \[1–5\]. Designed to relieve judges of non-judicial administrative duties that consumed 40%–60% of their working hours amidst a staggering backlog of 30 million pending cases, the Government of India allocated ₹3 Billion through the 13th Finance Commission (2010–2015) to recruit MBA-qualified CMs \[3, 5–6\]. However, the project faced systemic shunning. A majority of judges maintained a "power-cum-jurisdiction" mindset, believing that delegating administrative tasks diminished their authority. Burdened by inferior yearly contracts, low salaries, and hostility from traditional court staff (*sheristadars*), CMs were frequently assigned superfluous, menial tasks such as watering plants, repairing telephones, and ordering catering for meetings \[41–45\]. Following the cessation of central funding in the 14th Finance Commission (2015–2019) and the glaring absence of leadership from the Supreme Court of India, the CM position was largely abandoned and abolished across several states.

## Legal Basis

- **Ministry of Finance Circular F.NO.32(30).FCD/2010:** The official circular allocating ₹3 Billion to create MBA-qualified Court Manager positions at the District Court and High Court levels.
- **13th Finance Commission Grant (2010–2015):** A ₹50 Billion central government grant dedicated to judicial reforms in India, from which the CM funding was drawn.
- **High Court Rules on Court Managers:** Internal regulations established by various High Courts (e.g., Maharashtra Rules 2011, Allahabad 2012, Jharkhand 2012, Kerala 2014) detailing the duties of CMs.
- **Not found in the available sources:** Other specific formal statutory regulations are not mentioned in the source text \[FACT\].

## Background

An overwhelming non-judicial administrative burden placed on Indian judges exacerbated the extreme backlog of over 30 million pending cases, prompting a desperate need for specialized administrative personnel.

## Objectives

1\. Shift non-judicial administrative functions away from judges to professional Court Managers holding MBA degrees. 2. Free up 40%–60% of judges' time so they can focus entirely on their primary judicial function of resolving cases. 3. Modernize 10 specific areas of court management, including Court Development Plans (CDP), HR, ICT, archiving, and statistics.

## Scope

This policy encompasses the evaluation of the implementation of CMs across the 24 High Courts and 600 district courts throughout the Republic of India.

## Key Provisions

- **The 10 Functional Areas of a Court Manager (2010 Circular):**
  - Policies/Standards, Planning (CDP), Information/Statistics, Court Management, Case Management, Responsiveness, Quality, HR, Core Systems (filing), and ICT.
- **Cultural Barriers and the Judicial Mindset:**
  - **Power-cum-Jurisdiction Mindset:** Judges viewing administration as an inherent part of their jurisdictional power that must not be surrendered to outsiders.
  - **Hierarchical Resistance:** Judges preferring subordinate clerical help while actively rejecting equivalent managerial positions.
- **Inferior Contractual Status and the 2014 Collective Complaint:**
  - Yearly contract appointments with low salaries led to the systemic marginalization of CMs, culminating in an official collective complaint to the Prime Minister of India.

## Implementation

- **Limited Recruitment and Fund Utilization (2010-2015):** Only 462 CM positions were realized out of a target of 700, and only ₹1 Billion of the allocated ₹3 Billion was utilized due to delayed utilization certificates from High Courts.
- **Operational Misallocation of Duties:** CMs were diverted from strategic management to menial tasks (e.g., biometric attendance monitoring, telephone repair, catering, and plant watering).

## Policy Problems

- **Cessation of Central Funding under the 14th Finance Commission:** The central government terminated the dedicated CM allocation, shifting the financial burden to state governments, leading to mass abolitions.
- **Role Conflicts with Traditional Staff:** Senior court staff (*sheristadars*) feared losing influence and refused to share internal procedural knowledge with the new CMs.

## Reform / Change

- **Supreme Court Leadership Directive:** The Supreme Court of India must issue a firm mandate forcing judges to relinquish non-judicial duties to CMs.
- **International Exchange Programs:** Sponsoring international educational visits for senior judges to erode bias against the court manager profession.
- **Permanent Status and Compensation:** Converting the CM status from yearly contracts to permanent employment with fair, market-standard management compensation.

## Relationships with Other Entities

- **National Judicial Academy (NJA Bhopal):** The institution that initially proposed the Court Manager policy concept in India.
- **Supreme Court of India:** The apex judicial body whose leadership is urgently required to legitimize the role of the CM.
- **NALSAR University of Law:** The university running a joint LLB+MBA Court Management program that faced closure due to the failure of the CM initiative.

### Deutsch (Schweizer Hochdeutsch)

**Indischer Gerichtsmanager-Politikrahmen von Oberoi** Dieser von Prof. Dr. Geeta Oberoi im *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA, 2017) veröffentlichte Rahmen evaluiert das Scheitern der Initiative für Gerichtsmanager (Court Manager / CM) in Indien. Das Projekt (2010–2015), finanziert mit ₹3 Milliarden, sollte Richter von nicht-richterlichen Verwaltungsaufgaben entlasten, die 40–60 % ihrer Zeit in Anspruch nahmen. Die Initiative scheiterte jedoch an der hierarchischen Kultur der Richter, die Verwaltung als Teil ihrer "Macht und Gerichtsbarkeit" ansahen. Hinzu kamen unzureichende, befristete Verträge, niedrige Gehälter, der Widerstand des traditionellen Gerichtspersonals und fehlende Führung durch den Obersten Gerichtshof. Infolgedessen wurden hochqualifizierte Manager mit trivialen Aufgaben (z.B. Pflanzen gießen) betraut, was letztendlich zur weitgehenden Einstellung des Programms führte.

- **Rechtsgrundlage:** Rundschreiben des Finanzministeriums 2010; 13th Finance Commission Grant.

### 3 Most Important Improvements

1\. **Issuance of a Supreme Court Leadership Directive:** Obtain binding judgments or instructions from the Supreme Court of India explicitly mandating that judges surrender all non-judicial administrative management to professional Court Managers. 2. **Transformation of Employment Status to Permanent Compensation:** Convert the yearly contract scheme into a permanent judicial civil service status (regular service) with highly competitive salaries and benefits to attract and retain top professional talent. 3. **Execution of International Exchange Programs and Judicial Culture Training:** Initiate periodic study tours for the leadership of High Courts and the Supreme Court to Western nations to witness harmonious judge-manager partnerships firsthand, directly eroding the resistance of the "power-cum-jurisdiction" mindset \[1, 41–43, 49–53\].\`\`\`
