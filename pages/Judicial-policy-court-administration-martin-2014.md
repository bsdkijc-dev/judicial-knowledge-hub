# Judicial-policy-court-administration-martin-2014

**The Judicial-Administrative Partnership and Court Governance Policy Framework** is a judicial administration governance policy and judge-administrator partnership model formulated by Chief Justice Wayne Martin AC (Chief Justice of Western Australia, 2014) in the *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA). It rejects the executive model, refutes the existence of a strict demarcation (*bright line*) between judicial and administrative functions across 11 court operational areas, and combines the principles of Case Management and New Public Management (NPM) to guarantee institutional independence and judicial management efficiency \[1–2, 21, 44–46, 64–66\].

## Summary

Published by Chief Justice Wayne Martin AC in the *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA), this policy framework evaluates the failure of the executive model in court administration. Handing over budget, human resources, and court building matters to the Ministry of Justice/Executive has proven to threaten judicial independence and undermine public accountability, as the executive holds budgetary authority without bearing responsibility for case backlogs \[15, 20–21\]. Martin demonstrates that the assumption of a binary dichotomy between "judicial" and "administrative" functions is false (*the myth of the bright line*). Through an analysis of 11 court operational areas, Martin asserts that the administration of modern justice is an equal partnership between judges and professional court administrators \[44–46, 64–66\]. This framework rejects the commercial view that courts are businesses and adopts the *International Framework for Court Excellence* \[42–44, 62–63\].

## Legal Basis

- **Beijing Statement of Principles of the Independence of the Judiciary in the LAWASIA Region (1995):** International standards (Articles 35–37) mandating the transfer of court administration and budget management to the judiciary \[27–28\].
- **Montreal Declaration on the Independence of Justice (1983):** Article 2.40 which establishes that the primary responsibility for court administration lies with the judiciary.
- **UNODC Resource Guide on Strengthening Judicial Integrity and Capacity (2011):** UN guidelines regarding the crucial role of administrative personnel in maintaining judicial integrity and capacity.
- **High Court of Australia Act 1976:** The legislation granting administrative autonomy to the High Court of Australia.
- **Key Jurisprudence (*Byrnes v James 1889* & *Valente v The Queen 1985*):** The enforcement of judicial authority over court staff \[15–17, 26\].
- **Not found in the available sources:** Other specific formal statutory regulations are not mentioned in the source text \[FACT\].

## Background

19th-century British judicial administration reforms that abolished *sinecures* inadvertently transferred administrative authority to the executive. This executive model sparked prolonged budgetary and personnel tensions across various common law jurisdictions \[10–12, 15–20\].

## Objectives

1\. End the judiciary's administrative dependence on the executive through a transition toward a *judicial council model* or *autonomous court model* \[13, 25–27\]. 2. Build an equal strategic partnership between judges and professional court administrators \[2, 34–35\]. 3. Disprove the existence of a pure dividing line (*bright line*) across 11 court operational areas \[44–46, 64–66\]. 4. Reject the application of commercial *business case* doctrines to judicial appointments and the determination of judicial budgets \[42–44\].

## Scope

This policy encompasses the judicial governance models in Australia, the United States, Canada, Singapore, Ireland, Denmark, Norway, Sweden, the Netherlands, and international IACA/LAWASIA standards.

## Key Provisions

- **Rejection of the Executive Model & the Myth of the Bright Line:**
  - The assumption that court functions can be purely separated into judicial and administrative is incorrect.
  - There are 11 *shared operational areas* that represent joint responsibilities of judges and administrators: (1) Acceptance of Documents, (2) Case File Maintenance, (3) Administrative Disposition, (4) Allocation of Cases, (5) IT Utilization, (6) Data Analysis, (7) Budget Management, (8) Court Building Design, (9) Staff Recruitment, (10) Procedure Development, and (11) User Relations \[47–63\].
- **The "Courts Are Not Businesses" Principle:**
  - Courts are pillars for the enforcement of the Rule of Law, the value of which cannot be measured by profit-loss balance sheets or mere economic algorithms \[42–44\].
  - The use of New Public Management (NPM) techniques must be aligned with the goals of justice, utilizing the *International Framework for Court Excellence* \[36–41, 62–63\].

## Implementation

- **Adoption of the Judicial Council Model:** The implementation of independent judicial council models in South Australia, Victoria, Ireland, and Scandinavian countries \[13, 26–27\].
- **Case Management Integration:** Caseflow management and mediation (ADR) procedures that have successfully resolved \>97% of civil cases prior to trial \[31–33\].

## Policy Problems

- **Disconnection of Authority and Accountability:** The executive determines the budget and staff without bearing responsibility for case backlogs affecting the public.
- **The Danger of Narrow Quantitative Measurement:** The risk that judges are pushed to chase case resolution statistics rather than delivering substantial justice (*Spigelman: Not everything that counts can be counted*) \[41–43\].

## Reform / Change

- The complete transfer of budgetary and staff recruitment authority to an independent judicial management body.
- The recognition of the *court administrator* profession as a strategic partner in the formulation of court policies \[2, 34–35\].

## Multilingual Summaries (Swiss National Languages)

### Deutsch (Schweizer Hochdeutsch)

**Justiz-Administrative Partnerschaft und Court Governance Policy Framework** Dieser von Chief Justice Wayne Martin AC im *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA, 2014) veröffentlichte Rahmen bewertet das Scheitern des Exekutivmodells in der Gerichtsverwaltung. Die Verlagerung von Budget und Personal auf das Justizministerium gefährdet die richterliche Unabhängigkeit. Martin widerlegt die Annahme einer strikten Trennlinie („bright line“) zwischen juristischen und administrativen Funktionen anhand von 11 operativen Bereichen. Er fordert eine gleichberechtigte Partnerschaft zwischen Richtern und professionellen Gerichtsverwaltern, lehnt die kommerzielle Sichtweise ab („Gerichte sind keine Unternehmen“) und plädiert für die Einführung eines Justizrat-Modells.

- **Rechtsgrundlage:** Erklärung von Peking 1995; Erklärung von Montreal 1983; UNODC-Leitfaden 2011.

### Français

**Cadre de politique de partenariat judiciaire-administratif et de gouvernance des tribunaux** Publié par le juge en chef Wayne Martin AC dans l' *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA, 2014), ce cadre évalue l'échec du modèle exécutif dans l'administration des tribunaux. Confier le budget et les ressources humaines au ministère de la Justice menace l'indépendance de la justice. Martin réfute l'idée d'une ligne de démarcation stricte (« bright line ») entre les fonctions judiciaires et administratives à travers 11 domaines opérationnels. Il prône un partenariat égalitaire entre les juges et les administrateurs de tribunaux, rejette la vision commerciale (« les tribunaux ne sont pas des entreprises ») et préconise l'adoption d'un modèle de conseil de la magistrature.

- **Base Légale:** Déclaration de Pékin 1995 ; Déclaration de Montréal 1983 ; Guide de l'ONUDC 2011.

### Italiano

Surdar il budget ed il persunal al Ministeri da Giustia smanatscha l'independenza giudiziala. Martin refutescha l'existenza d'ina lingia da demarcaziun stricta ("bright line") tranter funcziuns giudizialas ed administrativas tras 11 secturs operativs. El pretenda in partenadi egual tranter derschaders ed administraturs da dretgiras professiunals, refusa la visiun commerziala ("dretgiras n'èn naginas interpresas") e propona l'introducziun d'in model da cussegl giudizial.

### 3 Most Important Improvements

1\. **Structural Shift from the Executive Model to the Judicial Council Model:** Enact legislative reforms that transfer full responsibility for court budgets, facilities, and staff to an independent judicial council led by judges. 2. **Operational Recognition of the 11 Shared Operational Areas:** Integrate formal cooperation protocols between chief judges and court administrators across the 11 operational areas to ensure efficiency without sacrificing judicial independence. 3. **Application of the International Framework for Court Excellence as an Evaluation Standard:** Replace narrow quantitative-commercial metrics ("courts are not businesses") with integrated public service quality standards that prioritize the enforcement of the Rule of Law and the satisfaction of justice seekers.
