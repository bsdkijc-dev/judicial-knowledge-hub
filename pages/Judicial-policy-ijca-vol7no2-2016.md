# Judicial-policy-ijca-vol7no2-2016

# **International Journal for Court Administration (IJCA) — Vol. 7 No. 2 (2016)**

### **Wiki Entity Page**

**International Journal for Court Administration (Vol. 7, No. 2, Source File: 19-85-PB.pdf)** is the official 15th electronic periodical issue of the \*International Association for Court Administration\* (IACA), published via IACA's official website (www.iaca.ws / www.iacajournal.org). This issue contains an executive editorial on rule of law on the high seas, 3 comparative academic research articles on social media in courts, judicial assistant/clerk delegation models, and the constitutional framework for Swiss law clerks, plus 3 academic book reviews.

## Summary

This issue is led by Markus B. Zimmer (Executive Editor), Prof. Dr. Philip M. Langbroek (Managing Editor), Linda Wade-Bahr (Technical Editor), and Prof. Dr. Andreas Lienhard, Dr. Luis Maria Palma, Prof. Anne Wallace, and Leisha Lister (Journal Editors). The issue presents empirical and practical analysis on maritime crime and human trafficking (Rohingya), court social media policy in Australia, a comparison of 4 judicial assistant models in the US, UK, and Netherlands, the issue of \*Gerichtsschreiberjustiz\* in Switzerland, and book reviews on Australian High Court politics, European Court of Justice (ECJ) compliance, and European Judicial Council (MEDEL) governance.

## Background & Summary of Main Articles

### 1. Executive Editorial: Rule of Law on the High Seas (Markus B. Zimmer)

- Reviews the history of piracy (\*Piracy Act 1698\*) through to the modern maritime crisis (5,200+ victims in 2014), human trafficking of ethnic Rohingya Muslims in Thailand/Myanmar, jurisdictional gaps, and the limitations of untrained private security forces operating without due process \[7–10\].

### 2. Social Media and the New Electronic World of Judges (Hon. Judith Gibson)

- Analyzes the adoption of social media in Australian courts (Victoria, NSW), plain-language judgment writing (\*R v Armitage\*), e-discovery of ESI, predictive coding, sanctions for spoliation of digital evidence (\*Palavi v Queensland Newspapers\*), and Susskind's concept of virtual hearings \[11, 14, 18, 20, 24–28\].

### 3. Judicial Assistant / Law Clerk Models and Roles (Nina Holvast)

- Compares 4 judicial assistant models based on 6 distinguishing factors:
  - \*US Law Clerks:\* Personal assistants to individual judges, high-achieving recent graduates, preparing cert/bench memos and drafting opinions.
  - \*Dutch Judicial Staff (griffiers):\* Career trial-court staff, recording hearings, present during deliberations, and drafting judgments.
  - \*UK Magistrates' Clerks:\* Career legal advisers giving legal advice to lay magistrates.
  - \*UK Judicial Assistants:\* 1-year contracts at the Court of Appeal and UK Supreme Court, preparing press summaries and research notes.

### 4. Law Clerks in Switzerland & Constitutional Limits (Peter Bieri)

- Examines the surge in the law clerk ratio (\*Gerichtsschreiber\*, 132 clerks for 38 judges at the FSC) and the risk of \*Gerichtsschreiberjustiz\* (a violation of Article 30(1) of the Swiss Constitution / ECHR Article 6). Emphasizes the need to preserve judges' non-delegable core duties and to establish quantitative performance targets (40–60 cases/year).

### 5. Book Reviews (Reinhardt & Porter; Di Federico; Zimmer)

- Reviews \*The High Court, the Constitution and Australian Politics\* (Dixon & Williams), \*International Courts and the Performance of International Agreements\* (Carrubba & Gabel — testing the ECJ political sensitivity hypothesis), and the MEDEL survey of European Judicial Councils (\*Judicial Governance in Europe\*).

## Policy Problems

1\. Gaps in maritime law enforcement and human trafficking crimes in international waters. 2. The clash between the interactive nature of social media and the legal certainty of the \*stare decisis\* doctrine. 3. Risk of "ghostwriting" and loss of judges' personal sense of responsibility due to excessive delegation (Posner, Kronman, Fiss). 4. The threat of \*Gerichtsschreiberjustiz\* to the right to a lawful and independent judge in Switzerland. 5. Obstacles to state compliance with international court rulings.

## Reform / Change

1\. Development of court social media policies and predictive-coding-based e-discovery standards. 2. Institutionalization of clear boundaries between assistant advisory tasks and judges' non-delegable adjudicative function. 3. Establishment of measurable performance targets and caps on clerk-to-judge ratios (maximum 1:1 in Lucerne). 4. Strengthening the independence of High Judicial Councils in Europe.
