## Overview

Supreme Court Decision No. 41 K/Pdt/1990 dated February 27, 1992, establishes a landmark legal rule regarding the protection and limits of civil liability for judicial officials (Judges, Registrars, Bailiffs, and Courts). The Supreme Court affirmed that judicial apparatus acting in the performance of technical judicial duties or judicial power cannot be sued civilly (as Defendants/Respondents). Actions by judicial officials that exceed boundaries or violate authority can only be subjected to administrative oversight and supervisory measures by higher judicial bodies (the High Court or the Supreme Court). Furthermore, regarding judicial administration actions tainted by legal defects, a civil lawsuit for nullification may only be brought by naming the party acquiring rights from such action as the Defendant, and not the Judge, Bailiff, Registrar, or Court concerned.

## Case Background

The Objector / Petitioner for Cassation (\[Party A\]) filed an auction execution third-party opposition (\*perlawanan eksekusi lelang\*) at the Ambon District Court against the Respondents: the State of the RI cq. Chairman of the Ambon District Court (Respondent I), Registrar of the Ambon District Court (Respondent II), Government of the RI cq. Minister of Finance cq. Director General of Taxation cq. Class II Auction Office of Ambon (Respondent III), and Sonny Waplau (Respondent IV / auction purchaser). The opposition was filed against the seizure and auction of Ownership Land Right No. 41 (Certificate No. 58/1973) in Ambon Municipality pursuant to Ambon District Court Chairman Determination No. 03B/1984-P/Deed No. 4/PN.AB/Eks. jo. No. 03C/1984-P/Deed No. 4/PN.AB/Eks. to execute a \*Grosse Akte\* of Debt Acknowledgement with Mortgage Power of Attorney No. 4 of 1981. The Objector argued that executing an auction of immovable property without first seizing movable property violated Article 208 of the RBg / Article 197 of the HIR.

The Ambon District Court (Decision No. 02/Pdt.G/Plw/1987/PN.AB dated April 9, 1988) rejected the Objector's opposition. The Maluku High Court in Ambon (Decision No. 2/Pdt/1989/PT.Mal. dated June 16, 1989) upheld the Ambon District Court decision. The Objector subsequently filed a cassation petition to the Supreme Court.

## Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

- **Ratio Decidendi:**

1.  Pursuant to Supreme Court Circular Letter (SEMA) No. 9 of 1976, jurisprudence, and public policy (\*public policy\*), any judicial apparatus acting in the execution of technical judicial duties or judicial power cannot be sued in a civil capacity.
2.  Actions by judicial apparatus that violate or exceed legally permitted authority may be brought before higher judicial institutions (the High Court or the Supreme Court) for supervisory action.
3.  Regarding judicial administration actions containing legal/juridical defects, a civil lawsuit for nullification may be filed, provided the party named as Defendant is the party deriving rights from such action, rather than the Judge, Bailiff, Registrar, or Court in question.

- **Ruling:** Rejecting the cassation petition from Petitioner for Cassation \[Party A\]; Ordering the Petitioner for Cassation / Objector to pay cassation-level court costs amounting to Rp 20,000 (twenty thousand rupiahs).
