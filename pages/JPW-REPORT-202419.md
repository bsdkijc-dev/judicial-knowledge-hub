## 4. Policy Analysis

Background  
Literacy (문해력) — the ability to read, interpret, and use written language — is essential for exercising rights and participating in judicial proceedings. According to the Ministry of Education's 2020 Adult Literacy Survey (2020년 성인문해능력조사), 8.9 million Korean adults aged 18+ have low literacy skills (Level 1 to 3), including 2.00 million adults at Level 1 (elementary 1st–2nd grade level / 4.5%), 1.85 million at Level 2 (elementary 3rd–6th grade level / 4.2%), and 5.04 million at Level 3 (middle school level / 11.4%). For marginalized groups — such as persons with intellectual or developmental disabilities (281,045 people in Korea), Deaf persons using Korean Sign Language (447,948 hearing-impaired people), children/adolescents (ages 8–15), North Korean defectors, and elderly citizens — traditionally drafted court judgments filled with archaic Sino-Korean legalese (한자어), complex multi-clause sentences, and passive voice create an insurmountable barrier to information accessibility and self-determination (자기결정권).

<!-- -->

Policy Problem  
**Substantive Inequality & Information Exclusion**: When courts deliver judgments in traditional legalese to low-literacy litigants, those litigants cannot independently understand whether they won or lost, why the decision was made, or whether to appeal. Providing identical, unreadable text to low-literacy citizens constitutes indirect discrimination (간접차별) and refusal of reasonable accommodation (정당한 편의 제공 거부) under Article 4 of the Disability Anti-Discrimination Act.

**First Easy-Read Precedent & Lack of Operational Standards**: On December 2, 2022, the Seoul Administrative Court (Presiding Judge Kang Woo-chan) delivered South Korea's first Easy-Read judgment (서울행정법원 2021구합89381), appending "안타깝지만 원고가 졌습니다" (unfortunately, the plaintiff lost) to Main Ruling Item 1 and inserting a summary box \[쉬운 말로 요약한 판결문의 내용\] with illustrations. Following UN CRPD Committee recommendations in September 2022 urging South Korea to develop Easy-Read formats across legal proceedings, the National Assembly introduced bill amendments. However, judges lacked concrete drafting standards, visual design templates, or legal guidelines, creating operational confusion and hesitation.

<!-- -->

Legal/Policy Gap  
Traditional judicial reform efforts focused on "judgment simplification" (판결서 간이화 / 적정화) for general citizens through plain language. However, plain language (평이한 언어) relies solely on text for moderately literate readers, omitting visual aids and structural design. In contrast, Easy-Read (이지리드) combines simplified text with matched visual illustrations (보조적 이미지), specific typography (12–14+ pt Gothic font, 180–200%+ line spacing, left alignment), and structured page layouts designed specifically for cognitive accessibility.

<!-- -->

Evaluation of Alternative Easy-Read Formatting Options  
**Option 1 — Single Easy-Read Judgment Only (판결서 원본을 이지리드 방식으로만 작성)**: Rejected. Omits essential legal reasoning, eroding the judgment's self-containment (자족성), appellate review function (상급법원 심리기초), and legal certainty regarding res judicata (기판력) and enforcement (집행력).

**Option 2 — Separate Easy-Read Document Post-Delivery (판결서 원본과 분리된 별도 이지리드 문서 제공)**: Rejected. Creates service timing confusion, risks missing strict appeal deadlines (상소기간), treats Easy-Read as an informal summary without legal standing, and reduces judicial oversight.

**Option 3 — Dual-Format Embedded Model (판결서 원본 내 이지리드 부분 병기)**: Selected as the Preferred Model. Inserts an Easy-Read text box (\[쉬운 말로 요약한 판결문의 내용\] / 이해하기 쉬운 판결) at the beginning of the Reason section, preceding the standard full legal text. Ensures zero service delay, binds Easy-Read directly to the official court record, preserves complete legal reasoning for appellate courts, and builds a searchable case law database (코트넷) for future judges.

<!-- -->

Drafting Guidelines & Methodological Standards  
**Text Standards**: Write in polite conversational tone (구어체 존댓말); limit sentences to 1 main idea (1–3 lines max); replace legalese with everyday vocabulary (e.g., replace 임대인/임차인 with 집주인/세입자, 대여 with 빌려주다, 변제 with 갚다, 상해진단서 with 진단서); state the core ruling in active voice at the very top; express dates clearly (2024년 2월 22일 instead of 2024. 2. 22.) and round large numbers (1억 5천만 원 instead of 150,000,000원); include an explicit disclaimer stating that standard legal text prevails in case of interpretation conflict.

**Visual Standards**: Place simple, clear illustrations or photos directly adjacent to corresponding text (left side preferred); maintain strict character visual consistency across scenes; utilize the 661 court-usable illustration assets developed under JPRI/Supreme Court research; restrict total Easy-Read box length to 5 pages (max 10 pages).

<!-- -->

Empirical Validation & Survey Results  
**Template Development**: Developed 14 model Easy-Read judgment templates: 7 Criminal (assault, theft, voice phishing fraud, sex crimes, child abuse), 3 Civil (building eviction, loan repayment, wage exploitation), 3 Family/Non-Contentious (divorce, child custody, parental rights termination), and 1 Administrative (disability rating rejection).

**Empirical Comprehension Testing**: Tested 40 participants (15 persons with developmental disabilities, 14 Deaf persons, 11 elementary school students aged 8–11) using 13 paired question sets comparing standard vs Easy-Read text.

**Key Statistical Findings**: Overall comprehension gain score (차이값) averaged +3.35 points. 80% of participants (32 out of 40) demonstrated measurable comprehension gains (Elementary students: +4.82, Deaf persons: +2.93, Persons with developmental disabilities: +2.67). 82.5% of participants (33 out of 40) explicitly rated the Easy-Read judgment format as easier to understand.

## 6. MediaWiki Output

**A Guide to Producing Easy-Read Judgments for Persons with Disabilities** is an official judicial policy research report published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of South Korea's Supreme Court.

## Summary

This document is an official judicial policy research report published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of South Korea's Supreme Court. It formulates practical guidelines, linguistic standards, visual layout rules, and draft Supreme Court regulations for judges to produce Easy-Read judgments (이지리드 판결서) for persons with intellectual/developmental disabilities, Deaf persons, children, and low-literacy citizens. It reviews comparative practice from 8 countries, develops 14 draft model judgments (7 criminal, 3 civil, 3 family, 1 administrative), and demonstrates their effectiveness through empirical testing on 40 participants.

## Legal Basis

The legal frameworks analyzed include:

- **Constitution of the Republic of Korea**: Article 10 (Human Dignity & General Personality Rights), Article 11(1) (Equality Principle), Article 27(1) (Right to a Fair Trial), and Article 34(5) (State Protection for Persons with Disabilities).
- **Disability Anti-Discrimination Act (장애인차별금지법)**: Article 4(1) (Direct/Indirect Discrimination & Refusal of Reasonable Accommodation), Article 7 (Right to Self-Determination), Article 21(1) (Access to Information), and Article 26(1), (4), (5), (8) (Reasonable Accommodation in Judicial Proceedings).
- **Act on Guarantee of Rights of Persons with Developmental Disabilities (발달장애인법)**: Article 8(1) and Article 10(1) (Communication Support in Easy-to-Understand Formats).
- **Mental Health Welfare Act (정신건강복지법)**: Article 6(1).
- **International Conventions**: UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (UN CRPD Articles 2, 5, 9, 13, 21 & Concluding Observations CRPD/C/KOR/CO/2-3, September 2022); UN Convention on the Rights of the Child (CRC Articles 3, 12); UN International Principles and Guidelines on Access to Justice for Persons with Disabilities (2020).

## Background

According to the 2020 Adult Literacy Survey (2020년 성인문해능력조사), 8.9 million South Korean adults have low literacy levels (Level 1–3). For 281,045 persons with developmental disabilities and 447,948 Deaf/hearing-impaired persons, the complex sentence structure and Sino-Korean legal terminology (법률용어) of court judgments has been a major barrier to the right to information access and self-determination (자기결정권). The first Easy-Read judgment, issued by the Seoul Administrative Court on December 2, 2022 (Case 2021Guhap89381), together with UN CRPD recommendations, spurred the need for official drafting guidelines for judges.

## Objectives

1.  Remove communication barriers in the judicial process and ensure substantive equality for vulnerable groups.
2.  Develop draft Supreme Court Regulations (대법원예규) and an Easy-Read judgment drafting guide.
3.  Provide 14 draft Easy-Read judgment models and 661 visual illustration assets to ease judges' drafting workload.

## Scope

- **Comparative Study of 8 Countries & International Bodies**: United Kingdom (Supreme Court *A Local Authority v JB* & the "Dear Sam" decision), Germany (*Leichte Sprache* & BGG § 11), France (FALC & Conseil d'Etat), Spain (Supreme Court Decision STS 339/2023), Colombia (Decision T-573/16), Australia (Style Manual), the United States (Plain Writing Act 2010), Canada, as well as IFLA, the UN, and the EU.
- **Empirical Testing**: A survey of 40 participants (15 persons with developmental disabilities, 14 Deaf persons, 11 elementary school students) using 13 paired comparison question sets (standard text vs. Easy-Read).

## Key Provisions

- **Option 3 Drafting Model (Dual-Format Embedded Model)**: The Easy-Read section is placed in a text box labeled "\[쉬운 말로 요약한 판결문의 내용\]" at the beginning of the Reason (이유) section within the same official judgment document.
- **Language Standards**: Uses polite conversational language (구어체 존댓말), short active sentences (1 sentence = 1 idea, max 1–3 lines), replaces legal terms with everyday language (e.g., 임대인/임차인 becomes 집주인/세입자, 변제 becomes 갚다), and includes a disclaimer that the standard legal text prevails in case of interpretive conflict.
- **Visual Standards**: Places relevant illustrations/photos to the left of the corresponding text, maintains visual character consistency, uses 12–14+ pt Gothic font with 180–200%+ line spacing, and limits the Easy-Read section to a maximum of 5–10 pages.

## Implementation

Judges draft the Easy-Read section independently or with the assistance of an Expert Commissioner (전문심리위원). The Easy-Read judgment is printed double-sided and bound like a booklet for direct delivery to the parties at the time the judgment is read.

## Policy Issues

- Judges' concerns about increased workload and the risk of legal inaccuracy.
- A lack of verified illustration assets for specialized legal cases.

## Reform / Amendments

1.  Enactment of the Supreme Court Regulation on the Production and Provision of Easy-Read Judgments for Persons with Disabilities (장애인 등을 위한 이해하기 쉬운 판결서 작성 및 제공에 관한 예규(안)).
2.  Enactment of the Supreme Court Rule on Non-Discrimination and Access to Justice for Persons with Disabilities (장애인 차별금지 및 사법접근에 관한 규칙(안)).
3.  Integration of an Easy-Read judgment training module into the Judicial Research and Training Institute (사법연수원) curriculum.
4.  Appointment of Easy-Read Expert Commissioners and establishment of Disability Access to Justice Centers (장애인 사법접근센터).
