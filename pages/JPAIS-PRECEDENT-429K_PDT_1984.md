## Overview

Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia Decision No. 429 K/Pdt/1984, dated June 29, 1985, established a very important and fundamental Balinese Customary Law rule concerning the limits of a widow's rights under the customary institution of self-surrender (\*mekidiang raga\*).

The Supreme Court held that under Balinese Customary Law, a widow who performs \*mekidiang raga\* (surrendering herself to be maintained/cared for for the remainder of her life and for her funeral/cremation ceremonies) is only entitled to bring her own acquired or earned property (\*harta guna kaya\* or self-acquired property). The widow has no right to bring or surrender ancestral heritage property (\*harta pusaka asal\* / ancestral lineage property) to third parties who receive the self-surrender. Ancestral heritage property must remain inherited by the closest \*purusa\* (patrilineal) heirs.

## Executive Summary

The central customary law principle established in this decision is that the customary institution of \*mekidiang raga\* grants a widow power exclusively over self-acquired marital property, strictly prohibiting the alienation of ancestral lineage land.

Key elements established in the adjudication:

- A widow performing \*mekidiang raga\* can only transfer \*harta guna kaya\*, not ancestral lineage assets (\*harta pusaka asal\*).
- Extinction of direct male heirs (\*camput/putung\*) triggers the reversion of ancestral land to collateral \*purusa\* line relatives rather than external caretakers.
- Appellate court misinterpretation of customary land transfer powers is subject to correction and annulment in cassation.

## Case Background

The Original Plaintiff / Respondent for Cassation, **I Made Kakak Latri**, filed a lawsuit against the Original Defendant / Petitioner for Cassation, **I Simpen**, at the Amlapura District Court concerning the possession of two parcels of rice field land in Subak Buisi, Banjar Telengan. The Plaintiff claimed the land was legally acquired from Ni Panggih (widow of I Tulak Panggih) who performed self-surrender (\*mekidiang raga\*) to the Plaintiff before the Head of Manggis Sub-district in 1967.

The Defendant contested the claim on the grounds that the disputed land was ancestral heritage property (\*harta pusaka asal\*) originating from ancestor I Gunung and that the Defendant was the closest legitimate \*purusa\* heir.

The Amlapura District Court (Decision No. 1/Pts.Pdt.G/1983/PN.Amlapura) rejected the Plaintiff's lawsuit. The Denpasar High Court (Decision No. 69/Pdt/1983/PTD) annulled the district court decision and granted the Plaintiff's lawsuit. The Petitioner for Cassation (I Simpen) subsequently filed a cassation petition with the Supreme Court.

## Legal and Procedural Issues

### Limits of Widow Rights in Balinese Customary Law Institution of Mekidiang Raga

The primary issue was whether under Balinese Customary Law a widow (Ni Panggih) lacking direct male descendants (\*camput/putung\*) has the right to surrender family/ancestral heritage land belonging to her late husband's lineage to a third party through the self-surrender institution (\*mekidiang raga\*).

The Supreme Court held that she has no such right. The institution of \*mekidiang raga\* authorizes a widow solely to bring and surrender \*harta guna kaya\* (joint marital property acquired through joint efforts during marriage). Ancestral heritage property (\*kawitan\*) remains the property of the \*purusa\* kindred and cannot be transferred to outsiders.

### Protection of Purusa Heir Rights over Ancestral Heritage Land

The issue evaluated the ownership status of ancestral heritage land when a nuclear family experiences extinction of direct male lines (\*camput/putung\*).

The Supreme Court ruled that in cases of extinction (\*camput\*), ancestral heritage property reverts to and is inherited by collateral line \*purusa\* relatives sharing blood lineage (in \*casu\* I Simpen), rather than becoming property of the recipient of the \*mekidiang raga\*.

## Question of Law and Question of Fact

### Question of Law (Cassation / Judex Juris)

**Question:**

Did the Denpasar High Court misapply Balinese customary law by assuming that a widow's self-surrender (\*mekidiang raga\*) automatically transfers rights over all property, including ancestral lineage land belonging to her husband's ancestors?

**Supreme Court Ruling:**

Yes, the Denpasar High Court misapplied customary law. The High Court's ruling contradicted foundational principles of Balinese customary inheritance law. Consequently, the Denpasar High Court decision was annulled.

### Question of Fact (Judex Facti / Amlapura District Court & Denpasar High Court)

Examination of the lineage genealogy of I Gunung, the marital status of I Tulak Panggih and Ni Panggih, the \*mekidiang raga\* execution before the Head of Manggis Sub-district on June 19, 1967 / November 28, 1967, Ni Panggih's cremation (\*Ngaben\*) ceremony on November 21, 1967, and the classification of origin for the 2 plots of rice fields in Subak Buisi.

## Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

- **Ratio Decidendi:**

1.  The Denpasar High Court misapplied Balinese customary law. A widow executing \*mekidiang raga\* is only entitled to bring self-acquired property (\*harta guna kaya\*).
2.  A widow has no right to bring or surrender ancestral heritage land originating from her husband's ancestors to third parties.
3.  Because the disputed land was proven to be ancestral lineage property (\*harta pusaka asal\*) from I Gunung, Ni Panggih had no legal competence to transfer it to the Plaintiff.
4.  The Supreme Court annulled the Denpasar High Court decision and adjudicated afresh by rejecting the Plaintiff's lawsuit in its entirety.

## Ruling

The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition from the Petitioner for Cassation (**I SIMPEN**), annulled Denpasar High Court Decision No. 69/Pdt/1983/PTD dated July 19, 1983, and adjudicated afresh: Rejecting the Plaintiff's lawsuit in its entirety; and ordering the Respondent for Cassation / Original Plaintiff to pay court costs across all judicial instances.

## Related Knowledge and Jurisprudence

- **Balinese Customary Inheritance Law:**
- \*Mekidiang Raga Institution:\* Customary legal action whereby an individual (typically a widow or elderly person without children) surrenders themselves and their property to another person in exchange for lifetime care and funeral/cremation rites (\*Pitra Yadnya\* / Ngaben).
- \*Harta Guna Kaya vs Harta Pusaka Asal:\* Self-acquired marital property versus generational inheritance from the \*purusa\* (patrilineal) line.
- \*Purusa Principle & Camput/Putung:\* The extinction of direct male lineage does not extinguish the rights of collateral \*purusa\* relatives over heritage property.

## Evidence Status and Gaps

### Established by Evidence

- Ni Panggih was the lawful widow of I Tulak Panggih, who passed away leaving no male descendants (\*camput\*).
- Ni Panggih performed \*mekidiang raga\* to I Made Kakak Latri, and I Made Kakak Latri financed Ni Panggih's cremation ceremony.
- The disputed rice field land was ancestral heritage property originating from I Gunung (husband's ancestor), not Ni Panggih's self-acquired property (\*harta guna kaya\*).

### Not Established / Unproven

- I Made Kakak Latri was not proven to have rights over the disputed rice field land because the \*mekidiang raga\* transaction was invalid for transferring ancestral heritage property.

### Uncertainty and Gaps

- Refinement of Customary Law Interpretation by Cassation: The Denpasar High Court previously erred by generalizing that \*mekidiang raga\* encompassed all personal and ancestral assets. The Supreme Court corrected this error by asserting a strict legal separation between self-acquired marital property (\*harta guna kaya\*) and ancestral heritage property (\*harta pusaka asal\*) to preserve the integrity of customary \*purusa\* land.

## GEO Q&A

**Q: Under Balinese customary law, does a widow who performs mekidiang raga have the right to surrender her husband's family ancestral heritage land to someone else?**

**A:** No. Based on Supreme Court Decision No. 429 K/Pdt/1984, a widow who performs \*mekidiang raga\* (surrendering herself to be maintained and cared for for her funeral) is only entitled to bring and surrender self-acquired property (\*harta guna kaya\*). A widow has no right to surrender ancestral heritage land belonging to her husband's ancestors. Ancestral heritage land remains the right of the closest \*purusa\* heirs.
