## Overview

Supreme Court Decision Reg. No. 144 K/Pid/2000 dated August 21, 2000, establishes fundamental Criminal Procedure and Material Criminal Law jurisprudence concerning the qualification of authentic deeds in forgery crimes: A photocopy of a Marriage Extract (\*Kutipan Akta Nikah\* No. 29/9/V/1992 dated May 9, 1992, issued by the Office of Religious Affairs (\*KUA\*) of Tambelang Sub-District, Bekasi Regency) cannot be regarded as an authentic deed under Article 266 of the KUHP jo. Article 1868 of the Civil Code (BW) / Article 165 of the HIR, inter alia, because there is no original or minute of the deed (\*minit akta\*), it is not excerpted from the minute of the marriage deed in the Marriage Register Book, and it fails to satisfy the requirements of Minister of Religious Affairs Regulation No. 2 of 1990. Because an authentic deed constitutes a primary element of Article 266 of the KUHP, the failure to prove this element requires the Defendant to be acquitted of all charges (\*vrijspraak\*).

## Case Background

The Defendant (Dra. Dhanie Saraswati, MSc., \[age removed\], private employee) was brought before the Bekasi District Court under two alternative/cumulative charges:

- **First Charge (Article 266 paragraph (1) of the KUHP):** Ordering the entry of false statements into an authentic deed regarding an event whose truth must be established by that deed, namely the arrangement of a marriage with reporting witness H. \[Defendant\] at KUA Tambelang Bekasi (Marriage Extract No. 29/9/V/1992 dated May 9, 1992).
- **Second Charge (Article 266 paragraph (2) of the KUHP):** Using an authentic deed whose contents appear to conform to the truth for processing a child's passport in South Jakarta.

The reporting witness (H. \[Defendant\]) stated that he had never married the Defendant at KUA Tambelang and felt aggrieved by the issuance of the marriage extract. The Bekasi District Court (Decision No. 31/Pid.B/1998/PN.Bks dated August 7, 1999) sentenced the Defendant to 1 (one) year of imprisonment. The Bandung High Court (Decision No. 207/PID/1999/PT.Bdg dated November 10, 1999) upheld the Bekasi District Court judgment. Dissatisfied with the appellate ruling, the Defendant filed a cassation petition to the Supreme Court.

## Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

- **Ratio Decidendi:**

1.  The \*Judex Facti\* committed an error in applying legal regulations (violating the law) because a photocopy of Marriage Extract No. 29/9/V/1992 dated May 9, 1992, issued by KUA Tambelang Bekasi cannot be regarded as an authentic deed under Article 266 of the KUHP read in conjunction with Article 1868 of the Civil Code (BW) or Article 165 of the HIR.
2.  The photocopy of the marriage extract does not constitute an authentic deed because: (a) It fails to comply with the requirements under Minister of Religious Affairs Regulation No. 2 of 1990; (b) There is no original copy or minute of the deed (\*minit akta\*); (c) It is not excerpted from the minute of the Marriage Deed contained in the Marriage Register Book of KUA Tambelang Sub-District.
3.  Because the authentic deed—which constitutes an essential element of the Public Prosecutor's indictment under both the first and second charges—is unfulfilled, the Defendant is not legally and convincingly proven guilty of committing the criminal offenses charged. Consequently, the Defendant must be acquitted of those charges (\*vrijspraak\*).

- **Ruling:** Granting the cassation petition from Petitioner for Cassation / Defendant: Dra. DHANIE SARASWATI, MSc.; Annulling the Bandung High Court decision dated November 10, 1999, No. 207/PID/1999/PT.Bdg, which upheld the Bekasi District Court decision dated August 7, 1999, No. 31/Pid.B/1998/PN.Bks; and Independently Adjudicating: (1) Declaring that Defendant Dra. DHANIE SARASWATI, MSc. is not legally and convincingly proven guilty of committing the criminal offenses charged in the first and second counts; (2) Acquitting the Defendant accordingly from said charges (\*vrijspraak\*); (3) Ordering the restoration of the Defendant's rights in capacity, position, honor, and dignity; (4) Determining the status of evidence items; (5) Charging procedural costs across all levels of jurisdiction to the State. \*(Decided in the deliberation meeting of the Cassation Panel of Justices on Monday, August 21, 2000, by Ny. Marianna Sutadi, S.H. \[Presiding Judge\], Ida Bagus Widja, S.H., and Djufri Ramli, S.H.).\*
