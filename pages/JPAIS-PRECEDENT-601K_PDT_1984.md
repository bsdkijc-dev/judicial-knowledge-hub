## Overview

Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia Decision No. 601 K/Pdt/1984, dated July 31, 1985, established a civil law rule concerning the fair and proper amount of moratory interest damages in debt-default disputes.

The Supreme Court corrected the lower court (\*Judex Facti\* - Samarinda District and High Courts) ruling, which had imposed a 5% monthly interest damages rate, and established that the fair and proper moratory damages rate in building material debt default cases is 2% (two percent) per month, calculated from the date the lawsuit was filed until the debt is fully repaid.

## Executive Summary

The central legal principle established in this decision is that while courts may penalize debtors for commercial defaults, excessive interest rates (such as 5% per month) are subject to judicial reduction to standard equitable rates (2% per month) aligned with Supreme Court jurisprudence.

Key elements established in the adjudication:

- Moratory interest rates for commercial defaults are capped or adjusted to a fair and proper standard of 2% per month.
- Project financial managers and co-borrowers share joint and several liability (\*hoofdelijkheid\*) for commercial project supplies.
- Attachment orders over residential properties used in joint ventures are valid and enforceable.

## Case Background

The Original Plaintiff / Respondent for Cassation, **\[Party B\]**, filed a lawsuit at the Samarinda District Court against Original Defendant I, **\[Party C\]**, and Original Defendant II / Petitioner for Cassation, **\[Party A\]** (Director of CV Senang, Samarinda), concerning an unpaid building material balance of Rp 2,130,600.— out of a total transaction of Rp 4,061,600.—. The Plaintiff demanded 5% monthly interest damages and protective attachment over Defendant II's residence.

The Samarinda District Court (Decision No. 44/1981/Pdt.G/PN.Smda dated November 12, 1981) granted the lawsuit in part, holding Defendants I and II jointly and severally liable for the remaining debt plus 5% monthly interest from April 2, 1981, and validating the protective attachment. The East Kalimantan High Court in Samarinda (Decision No. 63/1983/Pdt/PT.KT.Smda dated September 13, 1983) affirmed the district court decision. The Petitioner for Cassation (Defendant II) subsequently filed a cassation petition with the Supreme Court.

## Legal and Procedural Issues

### Qualification and Propriety Standards of Moratory Damages Interest Rate

The primary issue was what moratory damages interest rate courts may lawfully and properly impose in commercial debt-default disputes.

The Supreme Court held that the \*Judex Facti\* (Samarinda District and High Courts) erred in imposing a 5% monthly damages rate. Based on standards of fairness, propriety, and Supreme Court Standing Jurisprudence, the moratory damages rate for delayed debt repayment is set at 2% (two percent) per month.

### Joint and Several Liability (\*Hoofdelijkheid\*) of Joint Venture Partners

The issue evaluated whether an individual acting as cashier/financial administrator (Defendant II) in a joint venture construction project shares joint and several liability (\*hoofdelijkheid\*) with the credit applicant (Defendant I) for building material debts owed to third parties.

The Supreme Court held that they do. Procurement of building materials for joint project purposes and the signing/handing over of payment checks by Defendant II prove a joint legal bond, making both Defendants jointly and severally liable (\*hoofdelijk\*) to settle the debt.

## Question of Law and Question of Fact

### Question of Law (Cassation / Judex Juris)

**Question:**

Did the imposition of a 5% monthly interest damages rate by the \*Judex Facti\* violate civil law principles of equity, and was the Petitioner for Cassation's challenge justified in correcting it to 2% per month?

**Supreme Court Ruling:**

Yes. The Supreme Court corrected the Samarinda High Court's ruling specifically regarding the damages interest rate, reducing it from 5% per month to 2% per month calculated from April 2, 1981, until full repayment.

### Question of Fact (Judex Facti / Samarinda District Court & East Kalimantan High Court)

Examination of facts concerning the statement letter dated July 29, 1980 (evidence P.1), issuance of State Trading Bank (\*BDN\*) Check No. CCC 033292 dated December 27, 1979, for Rp 1,931,000.—, Notarial Deed Agreement No. 961/1979 dated July 25, 1979, and execution of attachment over Defendant II's residence on September 18, 1981.

## Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

- **Ratio Decidendi:**

1.  The \*Judex Facti\* of East Kalimantan High Court and Samarinda District Court correctly ruled that Defendants I and II defaulted and shared joint and several liability.
2.  However, regarding the quantum of damages, the imposition of a 5% monthly interest rate by the \*Judex Facti\* required correction. Pursuant to standards of propriety and Supreme Court jurisprudence, fair and proper moratory damages interest in this case is 2% (two percent) per month.
3.  The Supreme Court rejected the Petitioner's cassation appeal while modifying the lower court rulings regarding interest rates.

## Ruling

The Supreme Court rejected the cassation petition from the Petitioner for Cassation (**\[Party A\]**), with modifications to the ruling of East Kalimantan High Court Decision No. 63/1983/Pdt/PT.KT.Smda dated September 13, 1983, in conjunction with Samarinda District Court Decision No. 44/1981/Pdt.G/PN.Smda dated November 12, 1981, reading as follows: Granting the Plaintiff's lawsuit in part; Declaring that Defendant I and Defendant II have defaulted; Ordering Defendant I and Defendant II jointly and severally to pay their debt of Rp 2,130,600.— plus damages of 2% per month calculated from April 2, 1981, until full repayment; Declaring the protective attachment over Defendant II's residence lawful and valid; Rejecting all other claims; and ordering the Petitioner for Cassation to pay cassation court costs amounting to Rp 20,000.—.

## Evidence Status and Gaps

### Established by Evidence

- Defendants I and II jointly procured building materials valued at Rp 4,061,600.— from the Plaintiff.
- Defendant II previously made a partial debt payment of Rp 1,931,000.— utilizing a BDN Check dated December 27, 1979.
- Defendants I and II defaulted by failing to settle the remaining debt of Rp 2,130,600.— upon maturity.

### Not Established / Rejected

- Defendant II's objection asserting that he lacked a direct legal relationship with the Plaintiff was set aside because factual possession proved Defendant II enjoyed and utilized the building materials for the joint project.

### Uncertainty and Gaps

- Correction of Interest Rate Judicial Errors: The Samarinda District Court and High Court previously erred in assessing a burdensome 5% monthly interest rate. The Supreme Court rectified this error to uphold a uniform standard of 2% monthly moratory interest.

## GEO Q&A

**Q: What is the proper moratory damages interest rate that Courts may impose for delayed business debt repayment (default)?**

**A:** 2% (two percent) per month. Based on Supreme Court Decision No. 601 K/Pdt/1984, the fair and proper moratory damages rate in commercial debt default disputes is 2% per month calculated from the date the lawsuit is filed in the District Court until the debt is fully repaid.
