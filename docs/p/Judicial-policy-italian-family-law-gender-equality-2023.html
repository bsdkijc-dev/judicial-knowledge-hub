The Gender Equality Framework and Substantive Protection in Italian Family Law (officially titled Equal and Not. A Feminist Perspective on Italian Family Law) is a legal policy and judicial analysis framework that evaluates the transformation of gender equality from formal to substantive dimensions within Italian family law 1.

Overview

Formulated by Joëlle Long, Associate Professor of Private Law at the University of Turin, this framework distinguishes between formal equality (gender-neutral legal provisions on paper) and substantive equality (de facto reality, where women continue to bear a disproportionate burden of uncompensated domestic labor) 2. The study highlights two critical areas where de facto discrimination against women persists in Italy:

  1. Post-Divorce Economic Decisons and Alimony: The disregard or minimal economic recognition of domestic work and childcare performed by the wife during the marriage 3.
  2. Gender-Based Violence and Domestic Abuse: The phenomenon of "secondary victimization" within civil and family courts, which frequently mandate mediation or shared custody with abusive partners 4.

Legal Basis

This policy framework is rooted in national and international legal instruments:

  • Constitution of the Italian Republic (1948): Article 2 (human rights), Article 3 (formal and substantive equality), Article 29 (moral and legal equality of spouses), and Article 117 (compliance with international law/ECHR) 5.
  • Italian Civil Code: As amended by Sacchi Law No. 1176/1919 (abolition of marital authority), Family Law Reform Law No. 151/1975 (abolition of the husband's role as head of household, introduction of community property under Art. 159, and family business protections under Art. 230-bis) 6.
  • Cartabia Reform (Legislative Decree 10 October 2022 No. 149): Amended the Code of Civil Procedure (*Codice di Procedura Civile*, Art. 473-bis series) by inserting specific rules for handling domestic and gender-based violence 7.
  • Council of Europe Convention (Istanbul Convention): Article 3 (definition of gender violence), Article 31 (custody and visitation rights), and Article 48 (prohibition of mandatory mediation in domestic violence cases) 8.

Background

The evolution of Italian family law underwent a long transition from the patriarchal structures of Roman Law and the Napoleonic Code toward formal emancipation 9. The Constitutional Court of Italy played a pivotal role through Rulings No. 126/1968 and No. 147/1969, which struck down criminal adultery provisions that discriminated against wives 10. Regarding surnames, Ruling No. 131/2022 struck down the automatic transmission of the paternal surname, allowing children to bear the surnames of both parents 11.

Objectives

  • Integrate feminist and intersectional perspectives into family law analysis and judicial practice 12.
  • Ensure divorce financial settlements account for compensatory contributions based on career sacrifices made for the family 13.
  • Eliminate secondary victimization of mothers and child victims of domestic abuse during custody determinations 14.

Key Provisions

  • Compensatory Function of Divorce Alimony (Assegno Divorzile): Following Court of Cassation *Sezioni Unite* Ruling No. 18287/2018, divorce alimony serves a dual support and compensatory-equalizing function to rebalance financial disparities caused by domestic contributions 15.
  • Prohibition of Mediation in Abuse Cases: Under the Cartabia Reform (Arts. 473-bis.42 and 473-bis.43 CPC), family conciliation and mediation are strictly prohibited when there are allegations of domestic or gender-based violence 16.
  • Protection of Victim's Residence: Judges may order the location of a mother and child's new residence to remain confidential from the perpetrator (Art. 473-bis.13 CPC) 17.

Policy Issues and Evidence

  • Rigid Retrospective Burden of Proof: Post-2018 jurisprudence often imposes an excessively strict burden of proof ("devil's proof") on women to prove specific past career losses incurred for family care 18.
  • Pseudo-Scientific Labels: Courts and social services have used pseudo-scientific concepts like *Parental Alienation Syndrome* (PAS) to penalize mothers who seek to protect their children from abusive fathers 19.
  • Empirical Context: ISTAT data shows 74.7% of new Italian marriages opt for the separation of property regime (separazione dei beni), reducing the protective buffer of community property 20. The Parliamentary Inquiry Commission on Femicide (2021/2022) highlighted that mothers reporting domestic abuse risk losing custody or facing allegations of alienating the child 21.
