Policy Analysis

  • Background & Policy Problem:
    • FACT: Micro, small, and medium-sized enterprises (MSMEs) and various industrial sectors faced severe labor shortages, where productivity improvements and domestic recruitment were insufficient.
    • FACT: Japan required an official framework to accept work-ready foreign nationals possessing specific technical skills and expertise.
    • FACT: The regime sought to eliminate exploitation by malicious intermediary brokers who extract heavy deposits from prospective foreign workers or their families.
  • Policy Objectives:
    • FACT: Meet labor demands across 14 designated industrial fields experiencing acute labor shortages.
    • FACT: Eliminate illegal brokers through intergovernmental cooperation frameworks and bilateral Memorandums of Cooperation (MOC).
    • FACT: Build an inclusive society of harmonious coexistence where Japanese and foreign residents live safely together.
  • Key Provisions & Reforms:
    • FACT: Two Residency Categories:
      • Specified Skilled Worker (i): Requires substantial knowledge/skills confirmed via exams, max 5-year cumulative stay, daily conversational Japanese proficiency, and generally no family accompaniment.
      • Specified Skilled Worker (ii): Requires proficient skills confirmed via exams, renewable stay without the 5-year limit, and family accompaniment permitted (spouse and children).
    • FACT: 14 Designated Industrial Fields: Care worker, Building cleaning management, Machine parts & tooling, Industrial machinery, Electric/electronics/information, Construction, Shipbuilding & ship machinery, Automobile repair & maintenance, Aviation, Accommodation, Agriculture, Fishery & aquaculture, Food & beverage manufacturing, and Food service.
    • FACT: Quota Caps: 5-year acceptance caps are established within each sector's operational policy.
    • FACT: Support Mechanism for Category (i): Mandates life orientation, Japanese language acquisition support, and consultation/complaint handling mechanisms.
  • Implementation Schedule & Indicators:
    • FACT: Enacted Dec 8, 2018; Cabinet Basic Policy issued Dec 25, 2018; fully enforced Apr 1, 2019.
    • FACT: As of August 31, 2020, SSW holders reached 7,538 individuals (led by food & beverage manufacturing at 2,674, agriculture at 1,130, and food service at 794).
    • FACT: Japan signed MOCs with 12 sending countries by August 31, 2020 (including Philippines, Cambodia, Nepal, Myanmar, Mongolia, Sri Lanka, Indonesia, Vietnam, Bangladesh, Uzbekistan, Pakistan, and Thailand).
  • Identified Issues & Gap Analysis:
    • FACT: COVID-19 caused skill exam cancellations domestically and overseas from March to May 2020, with gradual resumptions starting August 2020.
    • FACT: Permanent Residence guidelines were revised on May 31, 2019; stay periods under SSW (i) and Technical Intern Training are explicitly excluded from the 5-year work-qualification requirement for permanent residence.
