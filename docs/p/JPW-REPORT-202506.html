Definition

An official judicial policy research publication issued by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of the Supreme Court of Korea that comprehensively analyzes accessibility rights (접근권) and facility standards (편의시설 기준) for persons with disabilities (장애인 등, encompassing persons with physical/sensory/mental disabilities, older adults, pregnant women, and mobility-impaired individuals) across Korean court buildings (법원청사) and specialized courtrooms (법정); diagnoses the statutory supervision failures and absence of substantive court-specific facility standards under current legislation; evaluates international models (US ADA/ABA & California Trial Court Facilities Standards, Germany BGG/DIN 18040-1, Japan Barrier-Free Act); conducts nationwide court surveys and physical courtroom site inspections; and formulates short-, medium-, and long-term reform proposals — including mandatory 5-year full court surveys (전수조사), Court Building Design Guidelines (법원청사 설계지침서) amendments, an 18-item/55-point courtroom inspection checklist, and statutory amendments to the Act on Convenience Promotion for Persons with Disabilities, Older Persons, Pregnant Women, Etc., the Building Act Enforcement Decree, and the Court Organization Act.

4. Policy Analysis

Background
Physical access to court facilities is a foundational gateway right (수단적 권리) necessary to exercise the constitutional right to trial (재판청구권) and achieve genuine access to justice (사법접근성). In South Korea, the mobility-disadvantaged population (이동약자, including registered persons with disabilities, older adults aged 65+, pregnant women, infants, and young children) comprised 29.64% of the total population in 2021 (15.33 million individuals), with demographic projections estimating that seniors aged 65+ alone will constitute 29.9% of the population by 2035. Meanwhile, Korean courts handle over 16.4 million cases annually (including 1.26 million main merit cases). However, out of 205 total court facilities nationwide (68 general court buildings and 137 registry offices), over 68.3% (140 facilities) are over 20 years old (15 general court buildings are 40+ years old), causing severe physical accessibility barriers.

Policy Problem
Statutory Supervision System Failure & Ineffective Corrective Enforcement: Under the Act on Convenience Promotion for Persons with Disabilities, local municipal authorities possess discretionary power (재량행위) to issue correction orders (시정명령) for facility non-compliance. Nationwide data (2018–2022) reveals that out of 2.28 million facility violations in Korea, local governments issued correction orders for only 0.03% (8,211 cases), and imposed enforcement fines (이행강제금) in only 6 cases. For court buildings, a 2023 Ministry of Health and Welfare survey of 58 court buildings revealed that only 1 court building met 100% appropriate legal installation standards, yet zero correction orders or follow-up notices were issued.

Absence of Substantive Court-Specific Facility Standards: Existing convenience legislation treats court buildings identically to general public administrative buildings (공공업무시설 under 업무시설), omitting technical standards for specialized judicial spaces such as courtrooms (법정), party waiting halls (당사자 대기홀), witness support rooms (증인지원관실), and holding cells (구치감).

Abstract Internal Guidelines: The judiciary's internal Court Building Design Guidelines (법원청사 설계지침서) contain only narrative, abstract principles (e.g., "court facilities shall provide convenient facilities") without precise numeric dimensions, turning radiuses, or standardized inspection checklists.

Severe Budget Stagnation: Court facility maintenance budgets (~149.8 billion KRW in 2021) met only 52.5%–60.4% of requested maintenance needs. Dedicated accessibility improvement budgets were cut and frozen at 526 million KRW for 2025.

Legal/Policy Gap
The legal framework imposes generic public building rules on courts while failing to enforce judicial accessibility, resulting in an administrative legislative omission (행정입법 부작위) recognized as unconstitutional and state-tort liable by Supreme Court En Banc Decision 2022Da289051.

Objectives
Establish substantive court accessibility standards (실체적인 법원 시설 기준); institute mandatory 5-year full court surveys (전수조사) and annual maintenance planning; amend the Act on Convenience Promotion for Persons with Disabilities, the Building Act Enforcement Decree, and Court Building Design Guidelines; and implement standardized courtroom inspection checklists.

Implementation & Proposed Statutory & Administrative Reforms
Nationwide 5-Year Full Accessibility Survey (전수조사) & Database: Conduct a mandatory 5-year full inspection across all 205 court facilities (including branch courts and registry offices), converting findings into a centralized database to drive annual phased budget allocation and sequential repairs.

Statutory Amendments to Convenience Act (장애인등편의법 개정안):

  • Amend Building Act Enforcement Decree Art. 3-5 (Table 1) to create a separate building category for "법원시설" (Court Facilities).
  • Amend Convenience Act Enforcement Decree Art. 2, Art. 3 (Table 1), Art. 4 (Table 2), and Art. 5-2 (Table 2-2) to establish "법원시설" as a mandatory target facility and BF-certification facility.
  • Amend Convenience Act Enforcement Rules Art. 2 (Table 1) to add Section 30 (장애인등의 이용이 가능한 법정), establishing explicit courtroom standards: 1.5m hallway width for party waiting halls (당사자 대기홀), 1.4m x 1.4m wheelchair turning space in courtroom seats, 0.7m–0.9m desk/witness stand height with 0.65m knee/0.45m foot clearance, max 1/12 ramp slopes, non-slip flooring, 1%+ wheelchair spectator seats with companion seating, and assistive listening devices.

Court Building Design Guidelines Revision (법원청사 설계지침서 개정안): Amend Chapter 5 Section 8 (법정 일반) to incorporate concrete technical measurements: ground-floor courtroom placement recommendations, 1.5m waiting hall widths, 1%+ wheelchair waiting seats, 1.4m x 1.4m courtroom seat turning radiuses, 0.7m–0.9m desk heights with 0.65m/0.45m clearances, fixed 1/12 ramp rules for bench platforms (법단), non-slip impact-absorbing flooring, and companion seating alignment.

Courtroom Inspection Matrix & Checklist (법정 장애인 등 편의시설 현황 조사표): Implement an 18-item, 55-point evaluation checklist covering location, waiting hall width/slopes/flooring/seating, door thresholds/widths/clearances/handles/braille plates, courtroom seating turning radiuses, table/witness stand clearances, floor slopes/finishes, and spectator seating ratios/structures.

Integrated Web Accessibility Portal (사법접근성 통합 안내 페이지): Establish a prominent "Judicial Accessibility" portal on court websites providing location-based pictogram guides, 3D/photo route maps, and integrated disability support application forms.

Evidence & Empirical Data
2023 Government Convenience Survey of 58 Court Buildings: Only 1 out of 58 surveyed court buildings met 100% appropriate legal installation standards. 18 court buildings (31%) fell below the national public agency average (80.7%), and all 4 court buildings below 70% compliance were 20+ years old (e.g., Hapcheon Registry Office 63.46%, Yangsan Registry Office 67.21%, Gokseong Registry Office 69.33%).

Internal Survey of 20 High/District Court Main Buildings (2024): Overall installation rate was 92.7% with an appropriate compliance rate (적정설치율) of 80.3%. Compliance varied sharply by age: courts built after 2014 achieved 95%+ appropriate installation (e.g., Seoul Eastern District Court 100%, Suwon Court Complex 93.75%, Suwon Family Court 97.5%), whereas buildings over 20 years old scored 58%–75% (e.g., B Court 60.2%, C Court 58.6%, J Court 60.6%, M Court 61.0%).

Sub-Item Compliance Failures:

  • Courtroom/Approach Ramps: Only 65% complied with legal slope limits (1/18 or 1/12).
  • Parking Walkways: Only 35% had proper physical separation between walkways and vehicle lanes.
  • Braille Plates & Tactile Blocks: Only 50% placed courtroom entrance braille plates; only 55% placed proper entrance tactile blocks.
  • Emergency Warning Systems: Only 35% had visual/auditory fire evacuation lights/voice alerts.
  • Nursing & Lactation Rooms: Only 35% provided required sink/changing table facilities.

6. MediaWiki Output

Research on Enhancing Accessibility Rights for Persons with Disabilities in Court Facilities is an official judicial policy research report published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of South Korea's Supreme Court.

Summary

This document is an official judicial policy research report published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of South Korea's Supreme Court. It comprehensively, empirically, and comparatively analyzes accessibility rights (접근권) and facility convenience standards (편의시설 기준) for persons with disabilities, the elderly, pregnant women, and the mobility-impaired (장애인 등) across court buildings (법원청사) and courtrooms (법정). It diagnoses failures in the current administrative supervisory system; analyzes comparative law in the US (ADA/ABA & California Standards), Germany (BGG & DIN 18040-1), and Japan (Barrier-Free Act); conducts surveys of 200+ court buildings and on-site inspections; and formulates reforms including a 5-year comprehensive survey (전수조사), amendment of the Act on Convenience Promotion for Persons with Disabilities (장애인등편의법), revision of the Court Building Design Guidelines (법원청사 설계지침서), and an 18-item / 55-point courtroom inspection matrix.

Legal Basis

The legal frameworks analyzed include:

  • Constitution of the Republic of Korea: Article 10 (Human Dignity & the Right to Pursue Happiness), Article 11(1) (Substantive Equality Principle), Article 27 (Right to Trial / Access to Justice), Article 34(1) & (5) (Right to a Decent Life & State Duty to Protect Persons with Disabilities/the Elderly), and Article 37(1).
  • Act on Convenience Promotion for Persons with Disabilities, Older Persons, Pregnant Women, Etc. (장애인등편의법): Articles 2, 3, 4 (Right of Access / 접근권), 5, 7, 8, 9, 9-2, 9-3, 10, 10-2 (BF Certification / BF인증), 10-3, 10-5, 10-6, 11 (Field Surveys), 14, 16, 22, 23 (Correction Orders), 24, 25.
  • Disability Anti-Discrimination Act (장애인차별금지법): Article 1, Article 4(1) Item 3 & (2) (Reasonable Accommodation / 정당한 편의), Articles 8, 11, 14, 18, 24, 25, Article 26(4) (Access to Justice & Public Services), Articles 46, 48.
  • Act on Promotion of Transportation Convenience for the Mobility Disadvantaged (교통약자법): Articles 2, 3, 9, 12.
  • Building Act (건축법): Enforcement Decree Article 3-5 (Public Business Facilities / 공공업무시설) and Article 87.
  • Internal Judicial Regulations: Regulations on Court Facility Standards (사법시설기준에 관한 예규, Articles 2, 4, 5); Court Building Design Guidelines (법원청사 설계지침서); Regulations on Courtroom Seating (법정 좌석에 관한 규칙).
  • Supreme Court Jurisprudence: Supreme Court En Banc Decision 2022Da289051 (December 19, 2024 — recognizing accessibility rights (접근권) as a constitutional fundamental right and establishing state-tort liability for administrative legislative omission spanning 14+ years).
  • International UN Conventions: UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD Articles 3, 5, 9, 20, 21; General Comment No. 2; Concluding Observations for Korea 2014 & 2022); the 2020 UN International Principles and Guidelines on Access to Justice for Persons with Disabilities.

Background

The right to physical accessibility of court buildings is a gateway right (수단적 권리) essential to exercising the right to trial. In South Korea, the mobility-disadvantaged population (이동약자) reached 29.64% of the total population in 2021 (15.33 million people), and Korea entered a super-aged society in December 2024 (20%+ elderly). Meanwhile, Korean courts handle more than 16.4 million cases annually. However, more than 68.3% of court buildings nationwide (140 of 205 facilities) are over 20 years old, creating severe physical barriers for court users.

Objectives

  1. Guarantee the right to accessibility (접근권) as a constitutional fundamental right in accordance with Supreme Court En Banc Decision 2022Da289051.
  2. Enact a mandatory 5-year comprehensive survey (전수조사) covering all 205 court buildings and registry offices.
  3. Amend the enforcement decree and ministerial rules of the Act on Convenience Promotion for Persons with Disabilities (장애인등편의법) to establish a dedicated "법원시설" (Court Facilities) category and technical courtroom standards.
  4. Revise the Court Building Design Guidelines (법원청사 설계지침서) and implement an 18-item / 55-point courtroom inspection matrix.

Scope

  • Empirical Study & Field Audit: Analysis of a 2023 government survey of 58 court buildings (only 1 met 100% of the standard); an internal 2024 survey of 20 main court buildings (an appropriate installation rate of 80.3%); and physical inspections of courtrooms at Court D and Court Q.
  • Three-Country Comparative Study:
    • United States: US ADA/ABA 2010 Standards Section 808 (Courtrooms), the California Trial Court Facilities Standards (CTCFS, 2023 edition), and recommendations from the Courthouse Access Advisory Committee (CAAC).
    • Germany: The Disability Equality Act (BGG § 8), the Model Building Code (§ 50), DIN 18040-1 (Public Buildings), and the NRW Guidelines.
    • Japan: The 2006 Barrier-Free Act (バリアフリー法) and courtroom accessibility pictograms.

Key Provisions

  • Dedicated Building Category (법원시설): Amends the Building Act and Convenience Act enforcement decrees to separate court buildings from ordinary general office buildings.
  • Technical Courtroom Standards (Ministerial Rule Section 30):
    • Minimum 1.5-meter corridor width for the party waiting hall (당사자 대기홀).
    • Minimum 1.4m x 1.4m wheelchair turning space in each courtroom seating area.
    • Bench/witness stand height of 0.7m–0.9m with minimum 0.65m knee clearance and 0.45m foot clearance.
    • Maximum ramp slope of 1/12 (or 1/18).
    • At least 1%+ wheelchair spectator seats with adjacent companion seating.
  • Web Accessibility Portal (사법접근성): Opens an integrated judicial accessibility guide page on court websites, equipped with 3D photo route maps and pictograms.

Implementation

Courts adopt the "법정 장애인 등 편의시설 현황 조사표" (18-item / 55-point Courtroom Inspection Matrix) to conduct periodic oversight and formulate phased annual repair budgets.

Policy Issues

  • The discretionary nature of local government correction orders (시정명령), resulting in 99.97% of facility violations in Korea going unaddressed.
  • The court accessibility facility repair budget frozen at 526 million KRW for 2025.

Reform / Amendments

  1. Amendment of the Act on Convenience Promotion for Persons with Disabilities (장애인등편의법) & the Building Act (establishing the '법원시설' category & Section 30 courtroom standards).
  2. Implementation of the 5-Year Comprehensive Survey (전수조사) & a centralized digital database.
  3. Revision of the Court Building Design Guidelines (법원청사 설계지침서).
  4. Adoption of the Courtroom Inspection Matrix (18 items / 55 points).
  5. Establishment of an integrated 사법접근성 web portal.
