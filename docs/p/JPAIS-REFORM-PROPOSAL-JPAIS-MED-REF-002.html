JPAIS:REFORM-PROPOSAL:JPAIS-MED-REF-002

Description / Deskripsi

Evaluates judicial mediation through a comparative framework between Indonesia and Japan, addressing structural frictions such as Indonesia's low general civil settlement rates (<10%) versus Japan's high conciliation success (>50%) through the Chōtei system, and proposes actionable structural reforms[cite: 2].

Problem / Masalah

  • JPAIS-MED-PROB-001: General civil court litigation mediation in Indonesia suffers from structural ritualization, with nationwide settlement rates remaining under 10%[cite: 2].
  • JPAIS-MED-PROB-002: Indonesia relies on a single mediator operating within a strict short procedural window without public budget line items for non-judge compensation[cite: 2].
  • JPAIS-MED-PROB-003: Lack of institutional integration between preliminary evaluation, issue sorting, and trial resolution compared to the Japanese model[cite: 2].

Current Indonesian Practice / Praktik Indonesia Saat Ini

  • JPAIS-MED-ID-001: Mandatory court-annexed mediation under PERMA No. 1 of 2016 and PERMA No. 3 of 2022 serves as an obligatory procedural step for civil filings[cite: 2].

Japanese Practice / Praktik Jepang

  • JPAIS-MED-JP-001: Japan's Civil Conciliation Act of 1951 utilizes specialized Conciliation Committees (Chōtei Iinkai) comprising a presiding judge and lay experts to resolve over 50% of petitions[cite: 2].

Gap / Kesenjangan

  • JPAIS-MED-GAP-001: Procedural formalism where litigants treat mediation as a routine delay rather than a substantive resolution space[cite: 2].
  • JPAIS-MED-GAP-002: Absence of state-funded compensation for external non-judge mediators and limited evaluative training[cite: 2].

Proposed Adaptation / Adaptasi Usulan

Implement tripartite conciliation panels, codify evaluative-facilitative hybrid protocols with a 10-day issue-sorting phase, institutionalize state budget allocations for non-judge compensation, and upgrade digital mediation infrastructure[cite: 2].

Action / Aksi

  • JPAIS-MED-ACT-001: Issue SEMA authorizing pilot procedures across four target courts (PN Jakarta Selatan, PA Jakarta Selatan, PN Surabaya, PA Sleman)[cite: 2].
  • JPAIS-MED-ACT-002: Deploy Tripartite Mediation Panels and mandatory 10-day issue-sorting protocols[cite: 2].
  • JPAIS-MED-ACT-003: Conduct mid-term empirical audits and finalize permanent PERMA revisions[cite: 2].

Pilot / Pilot Proyek

  • JPAIS-MED-PILOT-002: 90-day multi-court pilot program across PN Jakarta Selatan, PA Jakarta Selatan, PN Surabaya, and PA Sleman[cite: 2].

KPI / Indikator Kinerja Utama

  • JPAIS-MED-KPI-002: District court settlement rate $\ge 25.0\$; religious court settlement rate $\ge 55.0\$; settlement duration $\le 18$ calendar days[cite: 2].

Policy Implication / Implikasi Kebijakan

Promulgate joint ministerial decrees and Supreme Court circulars to establish sustainable funding and operational guidelines for tripartite panels[cite: 2].

Regulatory Implication / Implikasi Regulasi

Amend PERMA No. 1 of 2016 and PERMA No. 3 of 2022 to incorporate hybrid evaluative-facilitative protocols and digital infrastructure standards[cite: 2].

Evidence / Bukti Empiris

  • National religious court settlement rate stands at 39.85% while general district court civil settlement rate ranges between 6.8% and 7.4%[cite: 2].
  • Japanese civil conciliation achieves >50% settlement rates across summary and district courts[cite: 2].
