Supreme Court Decision No. 1205 K/Pid/1985 is an Indonesian criminal-law precedent concerning the offence of written defamation (pencemaran nama baik / Article 310(2) of the Criminal Code) and, in particular, the requirement that the statement be made "with the intention that it be made known to the public".

The decision establishes that statements containing sharp words or attacks on a person's reputation, when included in a Counter-Memorandum of Appeal (Kontra Memori Banding) addressed officially to the panel of judges of a Religious High Court as a self-defence document in judicial proceedings, do not satisfy the publicity element of Article 310(2) KUHP. Where the publicity element is not proven, the defendant cannot be convicted and must be acquitted (Vrijspraak, Article 191(1) KUHAP).

Executive Summary

Supreme Court Decision Reg. No. 1205 K/Pid/1985 (pronounced 23 June 1987; deliberated 21 April 1987) sets out a fundamental rule of substantive criminal law on the inapplicability of the offence of written defamation (Article 310(2) KUHP) to official documents filed in judicial remedy proceedings.

Writings or statements containing sharp words or attacks on a person's good name that appear in a Counter-Memorandum of Appeal addressed officially to the panel of judges of the Religious High Court (Pengadilan Tinggi Agama, a self-defence document in judicial proceedings) do not satisfy the element "with the intention that it be made known to the public" under Article 310(2) KUHP. Because the element of dissemination or publicity to the public was not proven, the Defendants could not be convicted and had to be acquitted of all charges (Vrijspraak, Article 191(1) KUHAP).

The Supreme Court granted the cassation petitions of the Defendants [Defendant II] and [Defendant I], annulled Banda Aceh High Court Decision No. 30/Pid.S/1985/PT.BNA and Banda Aceh District Court Decision No. 21/Pid.S/1985/PN.BNA, adjudicated the case itself, and acquitted the Defendants of all charges.

Case Background

In a Counter-Memorandum of Appeal (Kontra Memori Banding) dated 17 August 1984, addressed to the Religious High Court (Pengadilan Tinggi Agama, PTA) of Banda Aceh, the Defendants wrote statements attacking the morals and reputation of two witnesses:

  • witness H.M. Nanyan was described as immoral and as dividing property according to the "law of his stomach" (hukum perut); and
  • [Witness] was described as known as a gambling kingpin and criminal.

The document was prepared for the Defendants' self-defence in a legal dispute before the Religious High Court. The Defendants were prosecuted for defamation, and the lower courts convicted them.

Procedural History

First Instance: Banda Aceh District Court

The Banda Aceh District Court decided Case No. 21/Pid.S/1985/PN.BNA. It convicted the Defendants under Article 311 KUHP (calumny) and imposed a three-month sentence with probation (percobaan).

Appeal: Banda Aceh High Court

The Banda Aceh High Court decided Case No. 30/Pid.S/1985/PT.BNA. It modified the sentence to two months' imprisonment without probation (penjara murni).

Cassation: Supreme Court

The Defendants filed a cassation petition. The Supreme Court granted the petition, annulled both lower-court decisions, adjudicated the case itself, and acquitted the Defendants.

Procedural timeline

Legal Issues

The case raised the following principal legal issues:

  1. Whether words attacking a person's honour, contained in an official judicial document (a Counter-Memorandum of Appeal) addressed to the panel of judges of the Religious High Court, satisfy the element "with the intention that it be made known to the public" under Article 310(2) KUHP.
  2. Whether written self-defence arguments submitted in an official file to the judges hearing the case may be criminalised as defamation.
  3. Whether the Judex Facti (the Banda Aceh District Court and High Court) erred in applying the law by convicting the Defendants without the publicity element being established.

Supreme Court's Reasoning

1. The Publicity Element of Article 310(2) KUHP

The Supreme Court examined the element "with the intention that it be made known to the public" (dengan maksud supaya diketahui umum) in the offence of written defamation under Article 310(2) KUHP.

2. The Statements Were Made in an Official Judicial Document

The words were written in a Counter-Memorandum of Appeal addressed officially to the panel of judges of the Religious High Court. The document was submitted through the official channel of judicial proceedings, in a closed manner, for the purpose of the Defendants' self-defence (due process of law).

3. The Publicity Element Was Not Proven

Because the document was addressed solely to the Religious High Court and was not disseminated to the general public, the element of intentionally disseminating the statements so that they would be known to the public was not legally proven.

4. Protection of the Right of Self-Defence in Judicial Proceedings

Every citizen or litigant is entitled to present arguments, responses, and defences before the court or through official judicial documents. The courts should not hastily criminalise written defence arguments as defamation, provided the arguments are submitted in official files addressed to the panel of judges hearing the case.

5. The Lower Courts Misapplied the Law

The Banda Aceh District Court and High Court convicted the Defendants without examining whether the element of dissemination to the public was fulfilled. The Supreme Court held that this was an erroneous application of the law (foutieve toepassing van het recht).

6. Consequence: Acquittal

Since an essential element of the offence was not proven, the Defendants could not be punished and were acquitted of all charges (Vrijspraak, Article 191(1) KUHAP).

Ratio Decidendi

The core ratio decidendi may be stated as follows:

Statements containing sharp words or attacks on a person's reputation that appear in a Counter-Memorandum of Appeal addressed officially to the panel of judges of the Religious High Court, as a self-defence document in judicial proceedings, do not satisfy the element "with the intention that it be made known to the public" under Article 310(2) KUHP. Where the publicity element is not proven, the defendant cannot be convicted and must be acquitted (Vrijspraak, Article 191(1) KUHAP).

The Supreme Court's considerations (p. 13), as recorded in the source, read in the original Indonesian:

"Terdakwa tidak terbukti melakukan tindak pidana dimaksud dalam pasal 310 (2) KUHP, karena kata-kata tersebut terdakwa tulis dalam surat kontra memori banding yang ditujukan kepada Pengadilan Tinggi Agama, tanpa maksud untuk diketahui oleh umum."

In English: The defendant has not been proven to have committed the offence referred to in Article 310(2) KUHP, because the defendant wrote those words in a counter-memorandum of appeal addressed to the Religious High Court, without the intention that they be made known to the public.

Holding

  2. Annulled Banda Aceh High Court Decision No. 30/Pid.S/1985/PT.BNA.

Question of Law vs Question of Fact

Question of Law (Judex Juris / Supreme Court)

Whether the Judex Facti (Banda Aceh High Court and District Court) erred in applying the law (foutieve toepassing van het recht) by convicting the Defendants of defamation/calumny (Article 310(2) / Article 311 KUHP), although the statements were written in an official Counter-Memorandum of Appeal addressed to the Religious High Court without the intention that they be made known to the public.

Supreme Court's answer: Yes, the Judex Facti erred in applying the law. The Supreme Court granted the cassation petitions, annulled Banda Aceh High Court Decision No. 30/Pid.S/1985/PT.BNA and Banda Aceh District Court Decision No. 21/Pid.S/1985/PN.BNA, adjudicated the case itself, and acquitted the Defendants.

Question of Fact (Judex Facti)

The factual matters underlying the case, as recorded in the Claim-Evidence Matrix, were:

  • The document was addressed to the Religious High Court of Banda Aceh for the Defendants' self-defence in a legal dispute, and was not disseminated to the general public.
  • The Defendants were [Defendant II] (farmer) and [Defendant I] (farmer), both of Meunasah Tuha, Aceh Besar.

Legal Significance

This precedent is significant for Indonesian criminal law because it clarifies the publicity element of written defamation and protects self-defence in judicial proceedings.

The decision illustrates several principles:

  • Defamatory content alone is insufficient for conviction under Article 310(2) KUHP; the element "with the intention that it be made known to the public" must also be proven.
  • Statements made in an official judicial document addressed to the judges hearing the case, for the purpose of self-defence, are not made with the intention that they be made known to the public.
  • Courts should not hastily criminalise written self-defence arguments submitted through official judicial channels.
  • The Supreme Court may annul Judex Facti convictions where the publicity element was not examined, and may adjudicate the case itself and acquit the defendants.
  • The failure of an essential element leads to acquittal (Vrijspraak) rather than a conviction with a reduced sentence.
