Description / Deskripsi

Provides an exhaustive comparative assessment of evidence-based judicial policy frameworks between Indonesia and Japan, examining micro-data fragmentation, de-identification standards (SK KMA 2-144/2022), institutional research think-tanks (Pustrajak Kumdil vs. LTRI/Chōsakan), biennial trial expedition verifications, regulatory sandboxes (IJPL), and algorithmic governance for artificial intelligence[cite: 9].

Problem / Masalah

  • JPAIS-MED-PROB-001: Indonesian judicial data remains functionally fragmented across distributed SIPP databases, focusing on mechanical caseload accounting rather than structured longitudinal micro-data modeling[cite: 9].
  • JPAIS-MED-PROB-002: Manual anonymization under SK KMA 2-144/2022 creates inconsistent redaction standards, exposing protected identifiers or stripping out non-identifying substantive variables[cite: 9].
  • JPAIS-MED-PROB-003: Policy interventions lack institutionalized ex-post empirical evaluations, formal research judge corps, or controlled regulatory sandboxes prior to nationwide promulgation[cite: 9].

Current Indonesian Practice / Praktik Indonesia Saat Ini

  • JPAIS-MED-ID-001: Institutionalized technological infrastructure (SIPP, e-Court, e-Berpadu, Direktori Putusan, Smart Majelis) paired with the restructured Center for Law and Judicial Policy Strategy (Pustrajak Kumdil under BSDK)[cite: 9].

Japanese Practice / Praktik Jepang

  • JPAIS-MED-JP-001: Centralized empirical governance operated by the General Secretariat and LTRI, supported by statutory biennial verification reports under the Act on Expediting Trials, Judicial Research Officials (Saibansho Chōsakan), and phased IT transformations[cite: 9].

Gap / Kesenjangan

  • JPAIS-MED-GAP-001: Absence of a centralized, queryable National Judicial Data Repository (NJDR), explicit statutory mandates for periodic empirical reviews, and formal operational integration of Pustrajak Kumdil research into Supreme Court chambers[cite: 9].
  • JPAIS-MED-GAP-002: Lack of automated algorithmic de-identification pipelines, formal judicial policy testing laboratories, and explicit ethical audit charters for artificial intelligence systems[cite: 9].

Proposed Adaptation / Adaptasi Usulan

Establish the National Judicial Data Repository (NJDR), deploy automated AI-driven de-identification pipelines, transform Pustrajak Kumdil into an empirical think-tank with Research Judges, institutionalize Biennial Trial Expedition Verification Reports, create the Indonesian Judicial Policy Laboratory (IJPL) sandboxes, and adopt an Algorithmic Governance Framework[cite: 9].

Action / Aksi

  • JPAIS-MED-ACT-001: Promulgate Supreme Court Regulation on Judicial Data Governance, execute a 90-day data cleaning and micro-data extraction pilot, and establish the Pustrajak Kumdil Research Fellowship[cite: 9].
  • JPAIS-MED-ACT-002: Deploy the IJPL sandbox in Surabaya for pre-trial disclosure protocols, roll out the MD-JPD dashboard, and integrate the advisory Simetris Precedent Analytics Engine in pilot criminal chambers[cite: 9].
  • JPAIS-MED-ACT-003: Conduct independent algorithmic audits, compile the Prototype Biennial Judicial Expedition Report, and submit policy recommendations to Supreme Court Chamber Plenaries[cite: 9].

Pilot / Pilot Proyek

  • JPAIS-MED-PILOT-009: 90-day multi-court pilot program across PN Jakarta Pusat, PN Surabaya, PA Surabaya, PN Sleman, and PN Kisaran[cite: 9].

KPI / Indikator Kinerja Utama

  • JPAIS-MED-KPI-009: Anonymization accuracy $\ge 99.5\$; micro-data structuring latency $< 24$ hours; policy brief adoption $\ge 75\$; trial postponement reduction $\ge 25\$; off-hours data batching reduced by $50\$; pre-trial settlements increased by $25\$; 100% algorithmic audit compliance[cite: 9].

Policy Implication / Implikasi Kebijakan

Issue Supreme Court Executive Orders and Regulations requiring empirical data briefs for all proposed Perma and SEMA regulations and institutionalizing Pustrajak Kumdil research integration[cite: 9].

Regulatory Implication / Implikasi Regulasi

Promulgate comprehensive Supreme Court Regulations on Judicial Data Governance, Biennial Trial Expedition Verification, Experimental Judicial Sandboxes, and Algorithmic AI Governance[cite: 9].

Evidence / Bukti Empiris

  • Indonesian Supreme Court achieved 99.54% clearance in 2025 across 37,973 resolved cases, with first-instance courts handling over 2.8 million filings[cite: 9].
  • Japan's statutory biennial verification reports (such as the 11th report in 2025) empirically track civil trial durations, showing 7.6% of civil first-instance cases exceeding two years[cite: 9].
