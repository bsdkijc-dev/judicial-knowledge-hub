The IDE Asian Law Research and Legal Information Framework (Stable ID: JPW-POLICY-000017) is a multi-jurisdictional legal research, judicial documentation, and legal education policy framework evaluating primary statutory/case law sources, official gazettes, digital databases, court hierarchies, and research methodologies across six Asian nations (China, India, Malaysia, Philippines, Thailand, and Vietnam). Published under IDE Asian Law Series No. 23 by IDE-JETRO in collaboration with leading legal scholars (including Prof. Xin Chunying, Dr. S.K. Verma, Mercy Selvaratnam, Prof. Antonio M. Santos, Judge Vichai Ariyanuntaka, and Prof. Dr. Dao Tri Uc), the policy outlines legislative publication structures, digital legal research tools (CALR), judicial training institutions, and comparative legal systems analysis. Note: Named entities Cecep Mustafa and Reihan Abdan Syakur are Not found in the available sources.

Summary

The framework synthesizes three years of comparative research conducted by IDE-JETRO domestic research committees on law, economic development, and judicial reform in Asia. It covers six national legal research guides: (1) China, evaluating NPC legislation, State Council regulations, Ministry Gazettes, CASS law compilations, and digital databases (*Chinalawinfo*, *Law-Star*); (2) India, analyzing constitutional grundnorms, Parliament/State statutes, law reporting (*AIR*, *SCC*, *SCR*), *SCC Online*, and *JUDIS*; (3) Malaysia, mapping Straits Settlements, FMS, Unfederated States, Federal Gazettes, *Laws of Malaysia* (Acts 1–615), *Mallal's Digest*, and *LawNet*; (4) Philippines, details on 1987 Constitution, Republic Acts, Presidential Decrees, ONAR administrative register, *SCRA*, and CD Asia *Lex Libris*; (5) Thailand, examining Prince Rapee's 1897 Law School, civil codification, bar examinations, Judicial Training Institute, and university LL.B./LL.M. curricula; and (6) Vietnam, documenting *Cong Bao*, *Hong Duc* and *Gia Long* codes, 1992 Constitution, ONAR-equivalent CDS/ISIS systems, village regulations (*Huong uoc*), and the Institute of State and Law library.

Legal Basis

Authority and execution derive from constitutional charters and procedural statutes across six jurisdictions:

  • People's Republic of China: 1982 Constitution of the PRC; Legislation Law of the PRC (Arts. 52, 71).
  • Republic of India: Constitution of India (1950, Arts. 141, 245–253, 372); Code of Civil Procedure (1999/2002).
  • Malaysia: Federal Constitution of Malaysia (1957/1963, Art. 4(1), Ninth Schedule); Revision of Laws Act 1968 (Act 1); Interpretation Act 1948/1967 (Act 388).
  • Republic of the Philippines: 1987 Constitution of the Philippines (Art. VIII); Administrative Code of 1987 (Exec. Order 292); Judiciary Reorganization Act of 1980.
  • Kingdom of Thailand: Constitution of the Kingdom of Thailand; Civil and Criminal Codes; Judicial Service Act B.E. 2543 (2000); Advocate Act B.E. 2528.
  • Socialist Republic of Vietnam: 1992 Constitution of Vietnam; Law on Organization of People's Courts; 1995 Civil Code; Commercial Law 1997.

Background & Policy Problems

Background

Market liberalization and booming intra-Asian investment in the late 1990s highlighted severe legal information deficits among Asian scholars, practitioners, and judiciaries. IDE-JETRO established comparative committees to systematically map primary legal materials, gazette structures, and judicial training systems.

Policy Problems

  • Heterogeneity & Complexity of Legal Traditions: Comparative legal analysis complicated across civil law, common law, mixed systems, and socialist law.
  • Accessibility Bottlenecks: Official legal gazettes historically restricted to paper formats or internal government distribution without robust indices.
  • Severe Court Backlogs: Massive docket congestion delaying resolution across trial and appellate courts in major Asian jurisdictions.
  • Lack of Standardized Citators: Inconsistent legal citation standards and absence of automated case treatment tools in Southeast Asia.

Objectives

1. Provide standard research guides for primary legislative and judicial sources in Asia. 2. Standardize legal citations and document retrieval methods. 3. Promote computer-assisted legal research (CALR) and official online legal databases. 4. Comparative analysis of court hierarchies and ADR mechanisms. 5. Support legal education and judicial training reform.

Scope & Implementation

Scope

Applies across legal libraries, universities, judicial academies, court registries, and bar associations in China, India, Malaysia, Philippines, Thailand, and Vietnam.

Key Provisions

  • Official Gazette Mandates: Statutory requirement for publication of laws in standard gazettes (*State Council Gazette*, *Gazette of India*, *Federal Gazette*, *Official Gazette*, *Cong Bao*).
  • Administrative Rule Registries: Requirement to deposit agency regulations in centralized registers (e.g., ONAR at UP Law Center).
  • Judicial Training Frameworks: Mandatory pre-service and in-service training for judges (e.g., Thai Judicial Training Institute 1-year course).
  • Village Customary Law Integration: Preservation of customary rules (e.g., *Adat* in Malaysia, *Barangay* in Philippines, *Huong uoc* in Vietnam) alongside formal statutory codes.

Implementation

  • Digital Legal Publishing: Widespread adoption of CD-ROM and online databases (*Chinalawinfo*, *SCC Online*, *LawNet*, *Lex Libris*, *vietlaw.gov.vn*).
  • Judicial Output & Education: Operationalizing judicial academies and expanding law faculties across state and private universities.

Policy Problems

  • Publication and Accessibility Barriers: Incomplete public access to trial court judgments in civil/socialist law jurisdictions.
  • Lack of Standardized Citators: Absence of shepardized case treatment tools in Southeast Asia.
  • Judicial Backlogs: Severe case delays in India, Thailand, and the Philippines.

Reform

  • Integrating open-access national electronic legal databases.
  • Adopting neutral electronic citation formats for judicial opinions.
  • Strengthening specialized judicial training academies.
