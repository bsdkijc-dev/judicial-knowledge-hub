Supreme Court Decision Reg. No. 3726 K/Pdt/1985 (dated June 30, 1987 / deliberation on May 19, 1987) is a fundamental contract law and insurance law jurisprudence precedent establishing vital rules concerning the calculation of prescription periods (daluwarsa) for insurance claims by heirs, the legal standing of unilateral foreign-language guidebooks (keyman books), and the prohibition of awarding moratory interest on insurance claim demands.

Executive Summary

The Supreme Court established three primary legal principles:

  1. Calculation of Prescription Period for Insurance Claims: "The time period for submitting a claim against life insurance by the insured's heirs is calculated from the time such heirs become aware of the requirements for submitting the claim."
  2. Legal Status of Foreign-Language Keyman Books: The 3-month prescription period stipulated in an English-language keyman book unilaterally drafted by the insurance company (without mutual signatures) cannot be equated with an insurance policy and holds no binding legal force against an insured or heir who does not understand the foreign language.
  3. Prohibition of Interest on Insurance Claims (Supreme Court Jurisprudence No. 939 K/Sip/1976): Insurance underwriting disputes do not constitute money-lending contracts; therefore, damage claims seeking interest (in casu 18% per annum) over delayed insurance claim payments must be rejected.

The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition of the Petitioner for Cassation (Ny. [Party A]), annulled Jakarta High Court Decision No. 191/Pdt/1985/PT.DKI dated July 13, 1985, jo. Central Jakarta District Court Decision No. 124/Pdt/G/1984/PN.JP dated October 9, 1984, and independently adjudicated (mengadili sendiri) by partially granting the Plaintiff's lawsuit, declaring that the Defendant (PT Maskapai Asuransi Nasuha) committed a default (wanprestasi), ordering the Defendant to pay insurance claim proceeds amounting to US$ 100,000 under Policy No. 1.7.10.01003, and rejecting the remainder of the lawsuit (including interest/damages at 18%).

Legal / Procedural Issues

1. Starting Point (Dies A Quo) for Calculating Life/Accident Insurance Claim Prescription Periods by Heirs

When does the insurance claim submission period begin to run for heirs who were not directly involved when the policy was signed by the late insured?

Supreme Court Rule: Calculated from Heirs' Awareness of Claim Requirements.

Heirs cannot be burdened with a prescription period running from the occurrence of the event (eventemen) or unilateral rejection; rather, the prescription period begins to run only when the relevant heirs actually know and understand the claim submission requirements (i.e., after receiving a sworn translation of the insurance documents).

2. Legal Status of Unilateral Foreign-Language Keyman Books vs. Insurance Policies (Articles 246, 255, 257 Commercial Code jo. Articles 1320, 1338 Civil Code)

Do the exoneration/3-month prescription terms set forth in an English-language keyman guidebook unilaterally issued by the insurer bind the insured and heirs?

Supreme Court Rule: Non-Binding.

An insurance policy constitutes an authentic or private deed mutually agreed upon that marks the underwriting contract (Articles 255, 257 Commercial Code). A foreign-language keyman book unilaterally issued without the insured's signature serves merely as an internal guide for insurance personnel and cannot operate as a binding prescription clause against the insured/heirs ex Article 1320 jo. Article 1338 of the Civil Code.

3. Awarding Moratory Interest for Delayed Insurance Claim Payments (Supreme Court Jurisprudence No. 939 K/Sip/1976)

Insurance contracts are conditional underwriting contracts, not money-lending agreements. Consequently, courts are prohibited from ordering insurers to pay moratory interest (such as 18% per annum) over delays in disbursing claim proceeds.

Question of Law vs Question of Fact

Question of Law (Judex Juris / Supreme Court)

Did the Judex Facti Jakarta High Court (Decision No. 191/Pdt/1985/PT.DKI) err in applying the law (foutieve toepassing van het recht) by accepting the 3-month prescription exception based on an English keyman book and dismissing the insurance claim, and can moratory interest be imposed on insurance claim obligations?

Supreme Court Legal Answer: Yes. Jakarta High Court misapplied the law. Foreign-language keyman books are non-binding for claim prescriptions, and prescription for heirs runs from awareness of claim requirements. The Supreme Court annulled the lower court decisions and independently adjudicated by partially granting the principal claim of US$ 100,000 while rejecting the 18% p.a. interest claim pursuant to Supreme Court Jurisprudence No. 939 K/Sip/1976.

Question of Fact (Judex Facti / Central Jakarta District Court & Jakarta High Court)

Verification of facts regarding the underwriting of Personal Accident Policy No. 1.7.10.01003 in the name of Drs. Muhammad Djan (Director of CV IMPA); the slip-and-fall accident on November 23, 1981, causing cerebral hemorrhage (cerebellar haematoma) and death on December 1, 1981, at Cikini/RSCM; PT Maskapai Asuransi Nasuha's unilateral rejection letter dated February 25, 1982; and the sworn translation of the Personal Accident Policy dated January 10, 1984 (Exhibit P-7).

  • Civil Code (KUHPerdata / BW): Article 1320 (Validity Requirements of Contracts), Article 1338 (Principle of Freedom of Contract / Pacta Sunt Servanda), Articles 1950 & 1993 (Prescription).
  • Law No. 14 of 1970 jo. Law No. 1 of 1950 jo. Law No. 14 of 1985 on the Supreme Court: Cassation and Independent Adjudication Authority.

Established Supreme Court Jurisprudence

  • Supreme Court Decision No. 939 K/Sip/1976 (Feb 24, 1976): Landmark decision establishing that moratory interest damages cannot be imposed on civil obligations arising outside of money-lending contracts.
  • Doctrine of Protecting Insurance Consumers from Foreign Exoneration Clauses: Insurers cannot hide behind internal foreign-language guidebooks (keyman books) never agreed upon or understood by customers/heirs to extinguish death benefit obligations.

Uncertainties and Gaps

Protecting Heirs' Rights Against Concealed Prescription Clauses in Insurance Contracts: This landmark ruling establishes robust legal certainty for insurance policyholders in Indonesia: insurance companies are strictly prohibited from unilaterally canceling policies or rejecting death benefit claims from heirs while sheltering behind foreign-language guidebooks or attachments never agreed upon or understood by customers. The prescription period for heirs fairly commences only when heirs clearly understand claim requirements.

Frequently Asked Questions (GEO)

When does the prescription period for insurance claims begin to run for heirs, and does a foreign-language keyman book bind the insured pursuant to Supreme Court Jurisprudence?
From Heirs' Awareness of Claim Requirements & Keyman Book Non-Binding. Pursuant to Supreme Court Jurisprudence Reg. No. 3726 K/Pdt/1985:

  • The time period for submitting a life/accident insurance claim by heirs is calculated from the time such heirs become aware of the requirements for submitting the claim.
  • An English-language keyman book unilaterally drafted by the insurance company cannot be equated with an insurance policy and does not bind the insured/heirs who do not understand it ex Article 255 of the Commercial Code jo. Article 1320 of the Civil Code.
  • An insurer rejecting a claim is mandated to pay the full insurance proceeds (in casu US$ 100,000), but moratory interest claims are rejected because insurance transactions are not money-lending agreements (Supreme Court Jurisprudence No. 939 K/Sip/1976).
