How do parties react to defections? Electoral strategies after a valence loss is an empirical comparative political science and public policy research paper authored by Andrea Ceron (University of Milan) and Elisa Volpi (University of Geneva), published in the European Journal of Political Research (EJPR) in 2022.

Summary

This study analyzes how political parties across 14 Western European democracies respond to parliamentary defections in shaping their election manifestos. Treating defections as a form of "valence loss" that damages leadership reputation, credibility, and party brand, the study tests changes in manifesto strategy across 135 political parties through an analysis of 1,131 manifestos spanning 1945 to 2015. The findings show that parties experiencing defections respond by strengthening their emphasis on competence, increasing programmatic clarity, reducing issue diversity, and focusing on the core issues they already own [7, 42, 45, 53–54].

Background

Internal cohesion is a crucial asset for political parties in delivering on campaign promises and maintaining electoral accountability. When MPs switch parties or form new groupings, voters and prospective coalition partners face greater uncertainty about the party's true positions and commitments. Classic spatial competition literature generally examined only shifts along the left-right ideological spectrum. This research fills that gap by examining the non-positional manifesto strategies parties use to repair damaged valence reputation.

Objectives

1. Test Hypothesis 1: the greater the extent of defections a party suffers, the higher the emphasis on competence in its next manifesto. 2. Test Hypothesis 2: the greater the extent of defections a party suffers, the greater the programmatic clarity of its next manifesto. 3. Test Hypotheses 3A & 3B: defections reduce issue diversity and increase emphasis on the party's core issues [28–29, 38].

Scope

The research covers a cross-national database of 2,053 legislators involved in 611 annual defection episodes across 14 Western European countries (Austria, Belgium, Denmark, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Ireland, Italy, Netherlands, Norway, Spain, Switzerland, and the United Kingdom) between 1945 and 2015.

Key Provisions / Findings

  • Hypothesis 1 Supported (Competence Emphasis): Past defections significantly increase the share of manifesto content devoted to competence to govern (β = +3.18, p < 0.05; IV model p < 0.01).
  • Hypothesis 2 Supported (Programmatic Clarity): Defections increase programmatic clarity (β = +4.70, p < 0.10; IV model p < 0.05), showing that damaged parties abandon ambiguity in favor of clear commitments.
  • Hypotheses 3A & 3B Supported (Streamlining & Issue Specialization): Defections significantly reduce issue diversity (β = -2.30, p < 0.05) and increase focus on the party's owned core issues (β = +5.79, p < 0.05).
  • Latent Reputation Index: Defections have a strong positive effect on the composite reputation index (β = +0.73, p < 0.001).

Implementation

The methodology was implemented through three-level linear random-intercept models (manifestos, parties, countries) with control variables including RILE placement, government status, party size, party age, and electoral volatility [41–42, 45–46]. Endogeneity was addressed using an Instrumental Variable approach (`Switches IV`) based on structural defection determinants. Manifesto data were sourced from MARPOR/CMP (WZB Berlin).

Policy Issues

1. Damage to party brand and label caused by leadership's inability to maintain parliamentary cohesion. 2. The risk of ambiguous "broad-appeal" strategies failing for fractured parties, as illustrated by the UK Labour Party's 2019 election on Brexit [22–23, 56–58].

Reform / Implications

1. Encouraging parties that experience defections to adopt streamlined, clear campaign messaging focused on governing competence. 2. Strengthening specialization in owned core issues to rebuild trust among voters and prospective coalition partners.
