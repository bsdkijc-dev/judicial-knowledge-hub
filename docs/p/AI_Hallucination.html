AI Hallucination

AI hallucination refers to an AI system producing content that appears plausible but is unsupported, inaccurate, fabricated, or otherwise inconsistent with reliable evidence.

Judicial Risks

Potential examples include:

  • Fabricated cases
  • Incorrect legal provisions
  • Invented quotations
  • Wrong case numbers
  • Misstated holdings
  • Outdated law
  • False attribution

Why It Matters

Judicial and legal environments require a high level of traceability because inaccurate information can affect:

  • Legal research
  • Drafting
  • Education
  • Policy
  • Administration
  • Rights

Detection

Potential detection methods include:

  • Source verification
  • Citation checking
  • Retrieval from authoritative databases
  • Cross-checking
  • Expert review
  • Automated consistency checks

Prevention

Potential safeguards include:

  • Retrieval-augmented generation
  • Grounded prompts
  • Source restrictions
  • Citation requirements
  • Structured outputs
  • Human review

No safeguard guarantees zero hallucination.

Core Rule

Never treat fluent AI output as proof of legal accuracy.
