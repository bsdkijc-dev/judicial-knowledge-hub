4. POLICY ANALYSIS

  • Background: [FACT] The rapid expansion of the Internet, peer-to-peer (P2P) networks, and cyberlockers presented massive challenges for copyright enforcement in the entertainment and cultural industries. The emergence of linking websites (*webs de enlaces*) providing location coordinates (text/HTML links) to protected files without authorization triggered decades of legal debate concerning the legal classification of hyperlinks.
  • Policy Problem: [FACT]
    • Ambiguity in Link Classification: Initial uncertainty over whether providing surface links (*enlaces de superficie*), deep links (*enlaces profundos*), or embedded links (*enlaces ensamblados*) fulfilled the legal elements of "communication to the public" (*comunicación pública*) or "making available" (*puesta a disposición*).
    • Uploader Identification Obstacles: Difficulties faced by copyright owners in identifying the identity of direct uploaders under civil law due to Internet anonymity.
    • Subjective & Economic Intent Requirements: Fundamental differences between civil and criminal regimes regarding the burden of proving "actual knowledge" (*conocimiento efectivo*) of illegality and "commercial intent / profit motive" (*ánimo de lucro*).
  • Legal/Policy Gap: [FACT] Absence of a specific statutory definition of hyperlinks in the original Intellectual Property Law (LPI) prior to the Law 23/2006 reform, alongside the need for harmonized European criteria to determine when a link targets a "new public" (*público nuevo*) or commercially exploits unauthorized content.
  • Objectives: [FACT] Provide effective civil protection for copyright holders; supply civil remedies (injunctions/cessation and monetary damages) as well as actions against intermediaries/ISPs; and establish criminal liability under Article 270.2 of the Criminal Code against linking site operators who actively and non-neutrally facilitate access for commercial gain.
  • Implementation: [FACT]
    • Civil Regime: Aggregation of actions under Articles 138, 139, and 140 of the LPI. Implementation of dynamic injunctions/blocking measures against intermediaries (ISPs) pursuant to Article 138.IV and Article 141 of the LPI.
    • CJEU Jurisprudential Doctrine: Application of the *Svensson* case (C-466/12) standard regarding the "new public" concept; the *GS Media* case (C-160/15) establishing a *rebuttable presumption* (*iuris tantum*) of infringement when links are provided for profit/commercial gain; and the *Brein v. Wullems* (C-527/15) and *Ziggo* (C-610/15) rulings regarding pre-configured media players and P2P index management.
    • Criminal Regime: Enactment of Organic Law 1/2015 reforming Article 270.2 CP to specifically criminalize active, non-neutral provision of structured links or location indexes; enforcement of site-blocking precautionary measures (Art. 270.3 CP); and application of Circulars 1/2006 and 8/2015 of the State Attorney General's Office defining "commercial profit motive" (*ánimo de lucro comercial*) and actual knowledge criteria under the LSSI.
  • Evidence & Impact: [FACT] Enforcement of site-blocking injunctions against prominent linking websites through civil actions directed at ISPs (e.g., rulings by Commercial Court No. 7 Madrid and No. 9 Barcelona); and criminal convictions of commercial linking site operators by the Spanish Supreme Court in cases such as *Youkioske*.
  • Reform & Unresolved Issues: [FACT] Ongoing judicial debate regarding criminal actions initiated prior to the 2015 Criminal Code reform that remain *sub judice* (e.g., the *SeriesYonkis* case); and the continuous adaptation of digital piracy through proxies, anonymization services, and bulletproof servers in non-cooperative jurisdictions.

5. GEO/AI QUESTIONS

  1. Definition: What is the legal classification of a hyperlink (*hiperenlace*) and its relationship to "communication to the public" under Spanish LPI and CJEU case law?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] A hyperlink is an HTML code element pointing to an Internet resource. According to the CJEU (*Svensson* case), providing a link constitutes an act of "making available" and "communication," but only constitutes an infringement if it targets a "new public" (a public not originally contemplated by the copyright holder).
  2. Legal Basis: Which article of the Spanish Criminal Code (*Código Penal*) specifically penalizes providing structured links to copyright-protected content?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] Article 270.2 of the Criminal Code (introduced via Organic Law 1/2015).
  3. Publisher/Authority: Who are the three authors of this legal analysis and what departments do they represent at Pérez-Llorca Abogados?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] Juan Palomino Segura (Partner - Economic Criminal Law), Andy Ramos Gil de la Haza (Counsel - Intellectual Property, Industrial Property, and Technology), and Guillermo Meilán Iglesias (Associate - Economic Criminal Law).
  4. Related Figures: Are the names Cecep Mustafa and Johana Crishele Ohoira present in this source document?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] Not found in the available sources. Neither name appears in source document `acciones-civiles-y-penales-contra-los-sitios-web-de-enlaces-infractores-de-derechos-de-propiedad-intelectual.pdf`.
  5. Scope: What are the three link typologies distinguished in Spanish legal doctrine?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] Surface links (*enlaces de superficie*), deep links (*enlaces profundos*), and embedded links (*enlaces ensamblados*).
  6. Policy Implementation (Civil): How did the CJEU ruling in *GS Media* (C-160/15) shift the burden of proof for link providers acting for profit?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] *GS Media* established that if links are provided for commercial gain (*ánimo de lucro*), a rebuttable presumption (*iuris tantum*) arises that the provider had knowledge of the content's unlawful nature, making it an unauthorized communication to the public unless proven otherwise.
  7. Policy Problem (Criminal): How is the element of "intent to profit" (*ánimo de lucro*) interpreted in Spanish criminal copyright law?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] Pursuant to Circulars 1/2006 and 8/2015 of the State Attorney General's Office and Supreme Court jurisprudence, criminal intent requires proving "commercial profit motive" (*ánimo de lucro comercial*), which includes direct or indirect financial gain from advertising, premium accounts, or user data sales.
  8. Legal Relations: What is the relationship between Article 270.2 CP and Law 34/2002 (LSSI) regarding "actual knowledge of illegality"?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] Criminal prosecution under Article 270.2 CP incorporates LSSI standards (Arts. 16 and 17), where actual knowledge (*conocimiento efectivo*) can be established via formal notice from an administrative body, verified notice from the rights holder, or obvious factual circumstances of illegality.
  9. Reform & Procedure: What blocking remedies can rights holders seek against intermediaries such as Internet Service Providers (ISPs)?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] Under Article 138.IV LPI and Article 270.3 CP, rights holders can obtain civil and criminal injunctions ordering ISPs to block access to linking websites, including dynamic blocking orders (*medidas dinámicas*) that can be periodically updated.
  10. Evidence/Case Study: What landmark criminal case was affirmed by the Spanish Supreme Court regarding the conviction of linking website operators?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] The *Youkioske* case (STS 638/2015 and STS 920/2016), which affirmed that providing structured links to unauthorized protected content fulfills the criminal elements of Article 270 CP.

6. MEDIAWIKI OUTPUT

Summary

Civil and Criminal Actions Against Linking Websites Infringing Intellectual Property Rights (Acciones civiles y penales contra los sitios web de enlaces infractores de derechos de propiedad intelectual) (Stable ID: JPW-WORK-000026) is a doctrinal legal study and policy analysis prepared by Juan Palomino Segura, Andy Ramos Gil de la Haza, and Guillermo Meilán Iglesias of Pérez-Llorca Abogados. The work analyzes the civil and criminal legal framework governing linking websites (*webs de enlaces*) that facilitate unauthorized access to copyright-protected material in Spain. The analysis examines the evolution of hyperlink classifications under "communication to the public" and "making available" doctrines, key CJEU jurisprudence (*Svensson*, *GS Media*, *Brein*), civil remedies against Internet Service Providers (*intermediarios*), and criminal prosecution under Article 270.2 of the Criminal Code following the Organic Law 1/2015 reform.

Legal Basis

The policy and statutory analyses presented in this publication are grounded in Spanish national and European Union legal frameworks:

  • Royal Legislative Decree 1/1996 (Intellectual Property Law - LPI): Article 18 (reproduction), Article 20 (communication to the public and making available), Article 31 (private copying), Articles 138–140 (cessation and monetary damages actions), and Article 141 (precautionary measures).
  • Organic Law 10/1995 of the Criminal Code (CP): Article 270.1, Article 270.2 (active facilitation of links offense), and Article 270.3 (site withdrawal and blocking measures).
  • Law 34/2002 on Information Society Services (LSSI): Articles 13–17 (liability regimes and actual knowledge requirements for intermediaries).
  • European Union Law: Directive 2001/29/EC (Information Society Art. 3.1), Directive 2000/31/EC (E-Commerce), and Directive 2004/48/EC (Enforcement).

Background

The rapid rise of Internet technologies, P2P networks, and cyberlockers led to widespread unauthorized distribution of copyrighted content via linking websites. Initial legal ambiguity generated extensive litigation in Spanish courts regarding whether linking to third-party content constitutes a direct act of copyright infringement.

Objectives

1. Detail the legal classification of hyperlinks and the scope of "communication to the public". 2. Facilitate effective civil remedies against linking site operators and intermediaries (ISPs). 3. Outline criminal prosecution standards under Article 270.2 CP for commercial linking operations. 4. Harmonize Spanish case law with EU Court of Justice (CJEU) standards.

Scope

The analysis covers five core operational areas: 1. Link Typologies: Surface links, deep links, and embedded links. 2. CJEU Jurisprudence: The *Svensson* standard (new public requirement), *GS Media* (rebuttable presumption of illegality for commercial linking), and *Brein*. 3. Civil Remedies: Injunctions, monetary damages (Art. 140 LPI), and dynamic ISP blocking orders (Art. 138.IV LPI). 4. Criminal Regime: Article 270.2 CP, actual knowledge criteria under LSSI, and commercial profit motive standards under FGE Circulars 1/2006 and 8/2015. 5. Case Studies: Convictions in *Youkioske*, pending trials in *SeriesYonkis*, and civil ISP blocking orders.

Key Provisions

  • New Public Concept (Svensson): Hyperlinking only constitutes an unauthorized communication to the public if it provides access to a public not contemplated by the original rights holder.
  • Rebuttable Presumption (GS Media): Posting links for commercial gain creates a legal presumption (*iuris tantum*) that the linker had knowledge of the content's unlawful nature.
  • Article 270.2 Criminal Code: Criminalizes active, non-neutral provision of structured links for direct or indirect commercial gain.
  • Intermediary Blocking Remedies (Art. 138.IV LPI & 270.3 CP): Rights holders may obtain judicial orders compelling ISPs to execute dynamic access blocking against infringing websites.

Implementation

Implementation is executed through civil actions in Commercial Courts (*Juzgados de lo Mercantil*) and criminal prosecutions in Criminal Courts, Provincial Courts, and the Supreme Court, supported by law enforcement and the State Attorney General's Office.

Policy Problems

  • Evidentiary complexities in measuring actual financial loss (*Differenztheorie*) versus hypothetical royalty benchmarks.
  • Legal controversies surrounding criminal liability for acts occurring prior to the 2015 Criminal Code reform.
  • Continuous operational evasion by site operators using proxies, anonymization tools, and non-cooperative offshore jurisdictions.

Reform

  • Enactment of Law 23/2006 incorporating "making available" into the LPI.
  • Enactment of Law 19/2006 introducing statutory remedies against intermediaries into the LPI.
  • Reform via Organic Law 1/2015 introducing Article 270.2 to the Criminal Code.

Relationships with Other Entities

  • Implements: Intellectual Property Law, Criminal Code Art. 270, and Directive 2001/29/EC.
  • Applies to: Linking site operators, Internet Service Providers (ISPs), and copyright holders.
