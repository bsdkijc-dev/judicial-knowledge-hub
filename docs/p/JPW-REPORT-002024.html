Digitalization of South Korea's Justice System: Enhancing Access and Efficiency is an official policy research and assessment report published by the World Bank Group (Law Reforms and Judicial Support Unit, Legal Vice Presidency), in partnership with South Korea's Ministry of Economy and Finance through the Korea-World Bank Partnership Facility (KWPF).1 It evaluates the digital transformation of South Korea's civil justice system (E-Litigation/ECFS) and criminal justice system (KICS), assessing improvements to access to justice and efficiency in line with Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) 16.3.

Legal basis

South Korea's judicial digital transformation rests on:

  • Constitution, Article 11: the principle of equality before the law
  • Constitution, Article 27: the right to a fair and speedy trial by a qualified judge
  • Act on the Use of Electronic Documents in Civil Litigations, etc. (2010): legal basis for civil E-Litigation
  • Act on Promotion of the Digitalization of the Criminal Justice Process (2010): legal basis for KICS
  • Act on Special Cases Concerning Remote Video Trials (1995, amended 2016/2021)
  • Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA, 2011)
  • Legal Aid Act (1987)

Background

South Korea transformed its judicial system from paper-based to an integrated digital ecosystem, driven by a vision of national modernization, elimination of case backlogs, and alignment with SDG 16.3 on equal access to justice.2 Before digitalization, the courts faced case backlogs, high costs of handling physical documents, and inefficient inter-agency file transmission; the government leveraged national IT strength to build transparent, publicly accessible digital judicial infrastructure.

Objectives

  1. Promote fast, transparent and affordable justice for all citizens.
  2. Increase automation of minor criminal cases (such as traffic violations and DUI) through Electronic Summary Proceedings, cutting average processing time from 47 to 25 days.
  3. Develop next-generation platforms, Smart Court 4.0 and Next-Gen KICS, using artificial intelligence and big-data analytics, targeted for 2024.

Scope

  • E-Litigation / ECFS: public portal, e-filing, e-case management, e-courtroom and remote work (Smart Work).
  • KICS: federated server architecture, Electronic Summary Proceedings, the public criminal portal www.kics.go.kr.
  • Virtual trials: remote hearings by interactive video and their security standards.
  • Smart Court 4.0: application of AI, big data and intelligent case search.

Policy problems

  • Judicial cultural resistance. In the civil sector, early resistance came from judges' preference for in-person hearings and physical files.
  • Multi-agency friction. In the criminal sector, merging data across police, prosecution, courts and the Ministry of Justice met resistance from concerns over eroding institutional autonomy and potential human-rights violations.
  • Digital divide. Risk of excluding vulnerable groups (the elderly, people with disabilities, and those unfamiliar with technology) from E-Litigation services.

Legal and policy gaps

  • Cross-examination right. A 2021 Constitutional Court ruling (2018Hun-ba524) held that using video-recorded testimony of a child victim without guaranteeing the defendant's right to cross-examination violates the right to a fair trial.
  • Need for a federated architecture. A centralized server model was rejected over cybersecurity risk and threats to institutional autonomy, leading Korea to adopt a federated server system instead.

Implementation and evidence

Sector Evidence
Civil (E-Litigation) Began at the Patent Court in 2010, reaching over 99.9% adoption by 2013; extended to general civil cases in 2011, reaching 91% adoption by 2020.
Criminal (KICS) National portal www.kics.go.kr launched in 2010; the system recorded more than 399 million portal connections, 129 million uses of search services, and processes 2.3 million cases per day.
Virtual trials Legal basis dating to 1995, amended in 2016 and 2021; includes virtual courtroom backgrounds, SRTP encryption and multi-factor authentication to preserve the dignity of proceedings.

Data protection: Korea's PIPA has been recognized as adequate by the European Commission (EU GDPR Adequacy Decision, 2021).

Reform proposals

  1. Dual-pathway approach: keep a paper-based track and in-person court assistance for citizens facing technological limitations, to prevent access bottlenecks.
  2. **Strengthen personal data protection,**maintaining PIPA standards recognized as adequate under the EU GDPR.
  3. User-oriented AI adoption: deploy AI chatbots and intelligent case search to assist self-represented litigants without replacing judicial discretion.
  4. Roll out Smart Court 4.0 and Next-Gen KICS (2021–2024) and strengthen Mobile KICS for on-scene investigation.

Frequently asked questions

What is the report's title, publisher and focus?
Digitalization of South Korea's Justice System: Enhancing Access and Efficiency, published by the World Bank Group and KWPF, evaluating the modernization of civil (E-Litigation) and criminal (KICS) justice systems in Korea.

What constitutional basis supports judicial digitalization in Korea?
Article 11 (equality before the law) and Article 27 (the right to a speedy, fair trial by a qualified judge).

Why did Korea choose a federated server architecture for KICS?
To preserve the constitutional autonomy of each agency (police, prosecution, courts, Ministry of Justice) without concentrating control in a single centralized database vulnerable to hacking.

How did Korea address judges' early resistance to E-Litigation?
Through judicial autonomy, Supreme Court leadership, overseas training programs, grassroots initiatives by judges (such as self-built legal databases), and phased piloting starting at the Patent Court.

What was the E-Litigation adoption rate in civil cases by 2020?
91% by the end of 2020.

What was the effect of Electronic Summary Proceedings on DUI and traffic cases?
It cut average processing time for minor criminal/DUI cases from 47 to 25 days.

What did the Constitutional Court decide on 23 December 2021 about virtual trials?
Ruling 2018Hun-ba524 held that using video recordings of a child victim without adequate cross-examination for the defendant violates the right to a fair trial.

What is the name of Korea's next-generation e-court modernization project?
Smart Court 4.0 (Next Generation Electronic Litigation System) and Next-Generation KICS.

How does E-Litigation support judges through "Smart Work"?
It lets judges securely access digital case files remotely through Smart Work Centers (SWC) located across major regions.

배경

대한민국은 국가 정보화 비전, 사건 적체 해소, 사법 접근의 평등을 규정한 SDG 16.3과의 부합을 동력으로 삼아 종이 기반 사법 체계를 통합 디지털 생태계로 전환하였다.3 디지털화 이전에는 사건 적체, 종이 문서 처리 비용, 기관 간 비효율적인 서류 전달이 문제였으며, 정부는 국가의 IT 강점을 활용하여 투명하고 어디서나 접근 가능한 디지털 사법 인프라를 구축하였다.

목적

  1. 모든 국민에게 신속하고 투명하며 저렴한 재판을 제공한다.
  2. 전자약식절차를 통해 교통위반·음주운전 등 경미 형사사건의 자동화를 확대하여 평균 처리 기간을 47일에서 25일로 단축한다.
  3. 인공지능과 빅데이터 분석을 활용하는 차세대 플랫폼 스마트법원 4.0과 차세대 KICS를 2024년까지 개발한다.

범위

  • 전자소송/ECFS: 공공 포털, 전자접수, 전자사건관리, 전자법정, 원격근무(스마트워크).
  • KICS: 연합형 서버 구조, 전자약식절차, 형사 공공 포털 www.kics.go.kr.
  • 영상재판: 대화형 화상 원격 심리와 그 보안 기준.
  • 스마트법원 4.0: 인공지능, 빅데이터, 지능형 사건 검색의 적용.

정책 문제

  • 사법 문화의 저항: 민사 분야에서는 대면 심리와 종이 서류를 선호하는 법관들의 관행에서 초기 저항이 생겼다.
  • 다기관 마찰: 형사 분야에서는 경찰·검찰·법원·법무부 간 데이터 통합이 기관 자율성 침해 우려와 인권 침해 가능성으로 저항에 부딪혔다.
  • 디지털 격차: 노인, 장애인, 기술에 익숙하지 않은 국민이 전자소송 서비스에서 소외될 위험.

법제 공백

  • 반대신문권: 2021년 헌법재판소 결정(2018헌바524)은 피고인에게 반대신문 기회를 충분히 보장하지 않은 채 아동 피해자의 영상진술을 증거로 사용하는 것은 공정한 재판을 받을 권리를 침해한다고 판시하였다.
  • 연합형 구조의 필요성: 중앙집중형 서버 모델은 사이버 보안 위험과 기관 자율성 침해 우려로 배제되어, 한국은 연합형 서버 체계를 채택하였다.

개선 제안

  1. 이중 경로 접근: 기술적 제약이 있는 국민을 위해 종이 기반 경로와 법원 현장 지원을 유지하여 접근 병목을 방지한다.
  2. 개인정보 보호 강화: EU GDPR 적정성 인정을 받은 PIPA 기준을 유지한다.
  3. 이용자 중심 AI 도입: AI 챗봇과 지능형 사건 검색을 도입하여 법관의 재량을 대체하지 않으면서 나홀로 소송인을 지원한다.
  4. 스마트법원 4.0과 차세대 KICS 전개(2021~2024)와 현장 수사를 위한 모바일 KICS 강화.
