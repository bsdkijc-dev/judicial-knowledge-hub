The IJCA Journal Professionalization and Visibility Framework is a publication management policy framework designed by the Editorial Board of the International Journal for Court Administration (IJCA). It aims to transform a volunteer-based governance structure into a professionally managed organization, enhance the global visibility of judicial research through the Utrecht University Library web-shell platform, and maintain a balance between reports on court improvement projects and academic research.

Summary

Based on the progress report "From the Managing Editor: Our Journal’s Progress" authored by Philip Langbroek, this policy framework outlines the development strategy for the IJCA. Since its establishment in 2006, the journal has grown through the voluntary contributions of academics and practitioners. This policy formulates steps for the professional management of volunteers, the adoption of a web-shell platform facilitated by the Utrecht University Library to connect the journal with global library databases, and the strengthening of relationships with sponsors and advertisers.

Legal Basis

  • Not found in the available sources: This policy framework functions as an internal operational planning document and an organizational report of the IJCA Editorial Board, rather than a statutory law or government regulation.

Background

The production of the IJCA relies entirely on the time and effort of volunteers acting as authors, reviewers, proofreaders, and production managers. As the volume of research manuscripts and reports on court improvement projects grows, the organization requires a more professional structure and a broader scientific distribution system.

Objectives

1. Promote the professionalization of IJCA's governance and the organization of its volunteer team. 2. Expand the journal's visibility and audience reach by adopting a professional university library web-shell. 3. Connect IJCA publications with worldwide library databases. 4. Maintain a publishing balance between practitioner experience reports and academic research. 5. Build and maintain long-term collaborative relationships with reviewers, editors, sponsors, and advertisers.

Scope

This policy applies internally to all IJCA publication management processes, its international network of volunteers (including editing teams in the Solomon Islands and the US), and its ICT infrastructure cooperation with the Utrecht University Library.

Key Provisions

  • Structured Volunteer Management: Reorganizing the roles of publishing volunteers to optimize journal production efficiency.
  • Library Web-Shell Hosting: Adopting a digital platform facilitated by the Utrecht University Library to improve global indexing and traceability.
  • Sustainable Funding: Raising funds from sponsors and advertisers to cover the costs of maintaining and developing the publishing platform.

Implementation

  • Language Correction for Non-Native Authors: Engaging international volunteer editors (such as Helen Child in Honiara, Solomon Islands) to refine the grammar of manuscripts submitted by non-native English speakers.
  • Production Management Support: Involving court administrators (such as Linda Wade-Bahr from the U.S. District Court - Middle District of Florida) in the technical production process of the journal.
  • Strategic Plan Presentation: Delivering a work plan presentation on June 15 at 15:30 at the Peace Palace, The Hague.

Policy Problems

  • Limited Journal Traceability: Without being connected to a university library web-shell, judicial administration research publications are difficult for the general public to access outside the internal IACA network.
  • Volunteer Workload: The need to organize volunteers so that journal production is not hindered by limitations in individual time allocation.

Reform / Change

  • Moving or linking the journal to a professional web-shell platform integrated with global academic libraries.
  • Institutionalizing reward mechanisms and long-term relationships with manuscript reviewers and proofreaders.
