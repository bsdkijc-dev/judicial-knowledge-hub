The Rodolfo Sacco's Comparative Law Method and Legal Formants Framework (officially titled Rodolfo Sacco’s Conception of The Comparative Law Method) is a comparative legal science methodology framework that establishes procedures for analyzing comparative legal systems, the doctrine of legal formants (*elementi costitutivi del diritto*), and interdisciplinary comparative research principles.

Overview

Authored by Katharina Boele-Woelki, President of Bucerius Law School and Chair of the Commission on European Family Law, this review evaluates the scholarship of prominent Italian comparative legal scholars Rodolfo Sacco and Piercarlo Rossi in their treatise Einführung in die Rechtsvergleichung. The framework emphasizes that comparative law is not merely a single "pure method," but rather a structured combination of methodological approaches designed to identify similarities and differences across jurisdictions, explain their underlying causes, and evaluate normative solutions.

Legal Basis

This methodological framework is rooted in foundational scholarly works and harmonization instruments:

  • Einführung in die Rechtsvergleichung (2017): The 3rd edition comparative methodology textbook authored by Rodolfo Sacco and Piercarlo Rossi, published by Nomos Verlagsgesellschaft.
  • Legal Formants: A Dynamic Approach to Comparative Law (1991): The landmark article by Rodolfo Sacco published in the *American Journal of Comparative Law* detailing the theory of legal formants.
  • Principles of European Family Law: Harmonization instruments drafted by the Commission on European Family Law (CEFL) utilizing a combined comparative methodology.

Background

Rodolfo Sacco made a fundamental contribution to comparative legal science by dismantling the myth of national legal homogeneity. Sacco demonstrated that a legal system does not consist of a single, uniform rule, but rather a multiplicity of coexisting and often conflicting elements known as "legal formants" (*formanti del diritto*)—such as statutory provisions, judicial decisions, and scholarly doctrine. Katharina Boele-Woelki's review synthesizes Sacco's core methodological precepts for future generations of comparatists.

Objectives

  • Prevent comparative research from predetermining its results (*ex-ante conclusions*).
  • Affirm the comparability of legal rules and systems across jurisdictions with different economic foundations.
  • Promote integrated interdisciplinary comparative research (*synchronized comparative research*).
  • Provide evaluative guidance for European legal harmonization by balancing the search for a common core (*Common Core*) against selecting the best solution (*Better Law*).

Scope

The scope encompasses comparative private law methodology, family law, commercial transactions, interdisciplinary legal studies, and comparative legal translation studies.

Key Provisions & Five Methodological Principles

  1. Methodological Pluralism: Comparative law is not a single pure method, but a scientific process comprising four distinct stages: description, analysis, explanation, and evaluation.
  2. Absence of Predetermination: Comparative research is a "journey of discovery" where researchers must not predetermine findings or outcomes prior to conducting the inquiry.
  3. Comparability Across Economic Systems: Different legal systems remain comparable through establishing a *tertium comparationis* based on structure, function, and consequences, while strictly avoiding superficial quick scans.
  4. Synchronized Interdisciplinary Research: Promotes *synchronized comparative research*, in which legal scholars and experts from other social sciences conduct simultaneous research in the same jurisdiction.
  5. Balancing Common Core and Better Law: Harmonization projects should neither blindly chase a common core nor seek unneeded novelty, but must justify adopting a "better law" approach through explicit value-oriented argumentation.

Implementation

Implemented across comparative law curricula at Bucerius Law School, scholarly activities of the International Academy of Comparative Law, and drafting practices for family law principles by the Commission on European Family Law (CEFL).

Policy Issues

  • Risk of Superficial Quick Scans: The tendency to scan multiple legal systems rapidly without deep context risks misinterpreting how rules function within local legal cultures.
  • Challenges of Synchronized Research: Significant time and resource constraints make fully integrated, multi-jurisdictional *synchronized comparative research* difficult compared to single-jurisdiction *restricted comparative research*.

Proposed Reforms

Encouraging the formation of cross-disciplinary, international research teams to carry out synchronized comparative studies, alongside enforcing transparency in value justifications whenever selecting a *Better Law* standard.
