Overview

Supreme Court Decision Reg. No. 83 K/AG/1999 dated February 24, 2000, establishes essential jurisprudence and legal principles in Islamic Civil Procedure Law regarding the evidentiary standing of family/parental witnesses in divorce proceedings: In divorce lawsuits based on disputes and persistent quarrels (*syiqaq* / Article 19 letter f of Government Regulation No. 9 of 1975 jo. Article 116 letter f of the KHI), the father and mother of the Plaintiff or Defendant may be appointed and heard as witnesses pursuant to Article 76 paragraph (1) of Law No. 7 of 1989, which constitutes a *lex specialis* overriding the general restriction under Article 145 paragraph (1) of the HIR (*unus testis nullus testis*), when corroborated and synchronized with other supporting witness testimonies.

Case Background

The Original Plaintiff / Petitioner for Cassation ([Plaintiff], Civil Servant/Teacher) filed a divorce suit against her husband, the Original Defendant / Respondent for Cassation ([Defendant], Private Sector Employee), at the Semarang Religious Court. The couple married on June 26, 1983 (Marriage Certificate No. 103/24-VI/1983) and were blessed with 3 children ([Child], [Child], and [Child]). Their household experienced frequent disputes beginning from the pregnancy of their first child due to the Defendant's womanizing habits, heavy drinking, emotional volatility/physical abuse, and separation of domicile since May 1996, with the Plaintiff and children residing at [address removed].

The Semarang Religious Court (Decision No. 506/Pdt.G/1996/PA.Smg dated March 24, 1997) granted the Plaintiff's lawsuit in part, pronounced a single *ba'in sughro* divorce, and awarded child custody (*hadhanah*) to the Plaintiff. However, the Semarang High Religious Court (Decision No. 57/Pdt.G/1997/PTA.Semarang dated January 12, 1998) annulled the Semarang Religious Court decision and dismissed the Plaintiff's lawsuit on the grounds that the testimony of the Plaintiff's father and mother was prohibited under Article 145 of the HIR and constituted only a single external witness (*unus testis nullus testis*). Dissatisfied with the appellate ruling, the Plaintiff filed a cassation petition to the Supreme Court.

Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

  • Ratio Decidendi:
  1. In divorce lawsuits grounded on disputes and quarrels (*syiqaq* ex Article 19 letter f of Government Regulation No. 9 of 1975), the father and mother may be appointed as witnesses and have their testimonies heard pursuant to Article 76 paragraph (1) of Law No. 7 of 1989, which acts as a *lex specialis* provision superseding the general rule of Article 145 paragraph (1) of the HIR.
  2. The Semarang High Religious Court committed an error of law by setting aside Article 76 paragraph (1) of Law No. 7 of 1989 and treating parental testimony as legally inadmissible. The testimony of the Plaintiff's parents, corroborated by another witness ([Witness]) and consistent documentary evidence, clearly demonstrated that the parties' marriage had irretrievably broken down (*broken marriage*) and could no longer be maintained.
  3. Regarding child custody (*hadhanah*), the Semarang Religious Court's ruling granting custody and upbringing of children who have not yet reached the age of discernment (*mumaiyiz*) to the mother is accurate and correct under Islamic law (Article 105 of the KHI).
  • Ruling: Granting the cassation petition from Petitioner for Cassation [Plaintiff]; Annulling the Semarang High Religious Court decision dated January 12, 1998, No. 57/Pdt.G/1997/PTA.Semarang; and Independently Adjudicating: (1) Granting the Plaintiff's lawsuit in part; (2) Pronouncing a single *ba'in sughro* divorce of Defendant [Defendant] against Plaintiff [Plaintiff]; (3) Awarding child custody (*hadhanah*) of the children named [Child] and [Child] to the Plaintiff; (4) Declaring the remainder of the Plaintiff's lawsuit inadmissible; (5) Ordering the Plaintiff to pay first-instance court costs (Rp 91,500); (6) Ordering the Defendant/Appellant to pay appellate-level court costs (Rp 48,000); (7) Ordering the Respondent for Cassation to pay cassation-level court costs (Rp 100,000).
