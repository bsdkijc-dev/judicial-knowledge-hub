Core Definition

    • Article 24 of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia** is the supreme constitutional provision establishing the foundation, independence, and structural division of judicial power (*Kekuasaan Kehakiman*) within the Indonesian state, serving as the ultimate constitutional grundnorm for all courts and judicial administration.
  • **Simple Definition:** The highest constitutional article in Indonesia that guarantees the courts are completely independent and sets up the foundation for the entire legal and judicial system.
  • **Legal / Technical Definition:** The foundational constitutional provision (as amended during the third and fourth amendments, 2001–2002) declaring that judicial power is an independent state authority administered by a Supreme Court and a Constitutional Court to uphold law and justice across specialized judicial branches.
  • **Plain-Language Explanation: It acts as the ultimate constitutional shield protecting the independence of judges, ensuring that the justice system operates separately from political, executive, and legislative interference.

Why It Matters

  • **Establishes Constitutional Separation of Powers:** Formally insulates the judiciary as an independent co-equal branch of government alongside the executive and legislature.
  • **Creates the Dual Apex Court Architecture:** Mandates the division of supreme judicial authority between the Supreme Court (*Mahkamah Agung*) and the Constitutional Court (*Mahkamah Konstitusi*).
  • **Guarantees Judicial Independence as a Fundamental Right:** Protects citizens by ensuring that disputes are resolved by neutral, impartial judges bound solely by law and justice.
  • **Serves as the Apex Grundnorm for Court Administration:** Provides the constitutional legitimacy underlying the One-Roof administrative system (*Satu Atap*) and all subordinate judicial statutes.

Key Principles

Principle of Independent Judicial Authority (Asas Kekuasaan Kehakiman yang Merdeka)

  • **Meaning:** Judicial power is an independent state power possessed by courts to administer justice free from any external political, executive, or legislative interference.
  • **Why It Matters:** Absolute constitutional safeguard for the rule of law and the protection of civil liberties.
  • **Legal Basis / Source:** 1945 Constitution Art. 24(1); Law No. 48/2009 Art. 1.
  • **Related Concepts:** Separation of powers, judicial independence, non-interference.

Principle of Dual Apex Adjudication (Asas Peradilan Puncak Ganda)

  • **Meaning:** Supreme judicial power is distributed between two specialized apex tribunals: the Supreme Court (overseeing ordinary, religious, administrative, and military cases) and the Constitutional Court (reviewing statutory constitutionality).
  • **Why It Matters:** Divides ordinary case appellate review from fundamental constitutional disputes, enhancing specialized judicial competence.
  • **Legal Basis / Source:** 1945 Constitution Art. 24(2); Law No. 48/2009 Art. 18.
  • **Related Concepts:** Supreme Court, Constitutional Court, dual apex model.

Principle of Subordinate Specialized Jurisdictions (Asas Lingkungan Peradilan Khusus di Bawah Mahkamah Agung)

  • **Meaning:** All lower judicial branches must operate under the ultimate administrative and appellate authority of a single Supreme Court to ensure legal unity.
  • **Why It Matters:** Prevents jurisdictional fragmentation and conflicting legal interpretations across regional courts.
  • **Legal Basis / Source:** 1945 Constitution Art. 24(2); Law No. 48/2009 Art. 25.
  • **Related Concepts:** Four judicial jurisdictions (*Empat Lingkungan Peradilan*), legal certainty.

Indonesia Section

  • **Constitutional Evolution (Post-1998 Reforms):** Prior to the constitutional amendments (1999–2002), Article 24 was a brief, single-paragraph provision. The third and fourth amendments expanded Article 24 into a robust structural framework, establishing the Constitutional Court (Article 24C), formalizing Supreme Court authority (Article 24A), and creating the Judicial Commission (Article 24B).
  • **Textual Breakdown of Article 24:**

1. *Paragraph (1):* Declares the independence of judicial power. 2. *Paragraph (2):* Establishes the Supreme Court, lower judicial bodies (General, Religious, State Administrative, Military courts), and the Constitutional Court as joint holders of judicial power. 3. *Paragraph (3):* Authorizes the establishment of other specialized tribunals meeting statutory requirements.

  • **Nomenclature:** Pasal 24 Undang-Undang Dasar Negara Republik Indonesia Tahun 1945 — *Article 24 of the 1945 Constitution*; Kekuasaan Kehakiman yang Merdeka — *Independent Judicial Power*.

Debates & Controversies

  • Debate: Broad Constitutional Judicial Review vs. Parliamentary Supremacy

 * *Position A (Constitutional Judicial Supremacy):* Article 24 empowers courts (especially the Constitutional Court) to strike down parliamentary statutes that violate fundamental constitutional rights.
 * *Position B (Democratic Parliamentary Primacy):* Unduly aggressive constitutional invalidation of laws passed by elected representatives can undermine democratic policy choices.
 * *Evidence:* Ongoing debates surrounding constitutional review of major economic and social legislation.
 * *Current Status:* Stabilized through structured constitutional review standards: courts strike down laws strictly when they violate explicit constitutional text or rights.

Rule-of-Law Connection

  • *Constitutional Supremacy:* Article 24 anchors the rule of law by ensuring state power is exercised under independent judicial oversight.
  • *Separation of Powers:* Separates judicial adjudication permanently from executive and legislative control.
  • *Protection of Rights:* Guarantees citizens an independent forum to challenge unlawful state actions and protect fundamental rights.

Common Misconceptions

  • *Myth:* Article 24 places all courts and legal institutions under the direct control of the executive government.

 * *Reality:* Article 24 explicitly mandates that judicial power is *independent* (*merdeka*), completely separating courts from executive and legislative control.
 * *Source:* 1945 Constitution Art. 24(1); Law No. 48/2009.

  • *Myth:* The Supreme Court is the only court mentioned in Article 24.

 * *Reality:* Article 24(2) explicitly establishes both the Supreme Court (and its lower judicial branches) and the Constitutional Court as joint holders of judicial power.
 * *Source:* 1945 Constitution Art. 24(2).

Practical Example

  • *Scenario:* Parliament passes a statute attempting to grant a government ministry the authority to overturn final district court rulings in land disputes.
  • *Question:* How does Article 24 apply to resolve the constitutional crisis?
  • *Applicable Principle:* Independent Judicial Authority & Separation of Powers (1945 Constitution Art. 24(1)).
  • *Analysis:*

 1. The statute violates Article 24(1) of the 1945 Constitution, which guarantees that judicial power is independent and administered exclusively by state courts.
 2. Citizens or legal associations file a constitutional review petition with the Constitutional Court (*MKRI*).
 3. The Constitutional Court reviews the statute against Article 24 and formally declares the executive interference provision unconstitutional and void (*null and void*).

  • *Limitation:* Constitutional annulment requires a formal petition and adjudication by the Constitutional Court; executive agencies cannot be checked without judicial intervention.
