Policy Analysis

Background & Context

Commercial maritime trade depends fundamentally on vessel operations, requiring specialized employment agreements between shipowners/outfitters, ship masters, and crew members. Because work aboard ships entails distinct geographic, environmental, and occupational risks, the Saudi legislature enacted a dedicated chapter (Chapter 11, Articles 168–184) in the Saudi Labor Law (Royal Decree M/51 of 1426H) tailored specifically to maritime employment contracts.

Core Problems & Policy Gaps

Standard land-based labor contracts fail to account for shipboard conditions, voyage delays, vessel perils, shipboard living requirements, and international navigation hazards. Additionally, maritime contracts involve dual roles—such as the Ship Master who acts simultaneously as an employee of the outfitter and as the outfitter’s representative commanding all crew members.

Statutory terminology in Article 168 uses the phrase "operating contract" () rather than "agreement" (), which risks narrowly restricting maritime contracts to technical crew while excluding medical, administrative, or service personnel working on board. Furthermore, statutory seaman qualification conditions under Article 173 apply textually to seamen but omit explicit statutory mention of masters.

Legal Basis & Statutory Baseline

  • KSA Labor Law (Royal Decree M/51 of 1426H):
    • Article 168: Defines maritime employment contract as an agreement for pay between a shipowner/outfitter and a seaman.
    • Article 169: Establishes the Ship Master as the employer's agent holding statutory authority over all crew members.
    • Article 171: Mandates specific contract terms including work type, wages, and duration.
    • Article 173: Sets minimum seaman qualification conditions: age 18, maritime certificate, and medical fitness.
    • Article 174: Regulates currency of wage payment (Saudi Riyal or agreed foreign currency outside territorial waters).
    • Article 178: Mandates food and lodging at outfitter expense.
    • Article 179: Limits work hours to 14 hours/day or 72 hours/week.
    • Article 180: Entitles seamen to a share in vessel salvage rewards.
    • Article 181: Regulates wage entitlement upon shipwreck, seaman death, or profit-sharing.
    • Article 182: Regulates contract termination due to shipwreck, capture, or unseaworthiness.
    • Article 184: Details mandatory outfitter repatriation obligations.
    • Article 222: Establishes a 12-month limitation period for labor lawsuits.
  • Sharia Civil Procedure Law 1435H (Royal Decree M/1): Transfers labor dispute jurisdiction to newly established Labor Courts ().

Implementation & Enforceability

Maritime contracts must be executed in writing across three original copies (outfitter, master, seaman) and recorded in official ship logs. Employers failing to issue written contracts or record seamen face statutory fines of 5,000 SAR per worker.

Reforms & Unresolved Issues

Legal scholarship recommends key statutory amendments:

  1. Amending Article 168 to define the contract as an "agreement" () rather than an "operating contract" ().
  2. Explicitly extending Article 173 qualification conditions to ship masters.
  3. Rewording Article 182 to state automatic termination upon vessel loss rather than optional employer termination.
  4. Establishing specialized maritime labor judicial panels within the Labor Courts.

Unresolved issues include arbitrary statutory compensation formulas under Article 77 during premature fixed-term contract termination and strict enforcement of the 12-month limitation period () under Article 222.

What primary statute and chapter govern maritime employment contracts in Saudi Arabia?
KSA Labor Law (Royal Decree M/51 of 1426H), specifically Chapter 11 (), Articles 168 through 184.

Which judicial/administrative bodies hold jurisdiction over maritime labor disputes in Saudi Arabia?
Historically, Primary and High Commissions for Settlement of Labor Disputes (), transitioning to Labor Courts () under Sharia Civil Procedure Law Royal Decree M/1 of 1435H.

Who authored the source study on maritime employment contracts under Saudi Labor Law?
Dr. Shawakh Muhammad Al-Ahmad (), Associate Professor of Law at Dar Al Uloom University Faculty of Law.

What vessel criteria determine the application of Chapter 11 of the Saudi Labor Law?
The vessel must be a commercial floating structure registered in Saudi Arabia with a capacity of 500 tons or more. Military and non-commercial vessels are excluded.

What minimum age and medical conditions are required for a person to work as a seaman on a Saudi vessel?
The person must be at least 18 years old (an exception to the general youth labor age of 15), hold a maritime service certificate, and be medically fit as verified by a medical certificate.

What maximum work hour limits are imposed on seamen aboard ships at sea under Saudi law?
Article 179 limits work hours at sea to a maximum of 14 hours in any 24-hour period, or a maximum of 72 hours in any 7-day period.

What unique legal role does a Ship Master (ربان) hold in relation to the employer and crew?
The Master is simultaneously an employee of the shipowner/outfitter and an agent/representative () of the employer holding statutory authority over all crew members on board.

Under what statutory circumstances is a ship outfitter legally obligated to repatriate a seaman at outfitter expense?
Article 184 mandates repatriation if: (1) voyage is cancelled by outfitter after departure; (2) trade with destination is prohibited after departure; (3) seaman is removed due to illness/injury; (4) vessel is sold in a foreign port; (5) seaman is unlawfully dismissed during voyage; or (6) contract ends at a non-agreed port.

What is the statutory limitation period for filing lawsuits arising from a maritime employment contract?
Article 222(1) establishes that lawsuits claiming rights under a labor contract are inadmissible if filed more than 12 months () after the termination of the employment relationship.
