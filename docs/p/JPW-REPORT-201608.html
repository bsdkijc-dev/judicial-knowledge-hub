Definition

An official judicial policy research publication issued by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of the Supreme Court of Korea that analyzes the operational status and systemic problems of motions for a formal trial following summary orders, conducts comparative legal reviews across five foreign jurisdictions (US, UK, Germany, France, Japan), and proposes comprehensive policy and statutory reform measures — including the abolition of the prohibition of reformatio in peius (불이익변경금지원칙) — to ensure due process and optimize judicial resource allocation. 1

4. Policy Analysis

Background
Under South Korea's criminal justice system, summary procedures allow courts to impose fines, minor fines, or confiscations via written examination without formal public trials. In 1997, Article 457-2 was inserted into the Criminal Procedure Code, establishing the prohibition of reformatio in peius (불이익변경금지원칙) for formal trials requested by defendants following summary orders, meaning the court in a formal trial could not impose a heavier penalty than that in the summary order.

Policy Problem
The introduction of Article 457-2 caused a massive surge in formal trial requests — increasing from 1.8% of summary orders in 1997 to 11.5% in 2014 (and over 14% in 2012). Defendants filed formal trials with a "nothing to lose" (밑져야 본전) mindset solely to delay fine payments, delay administrative license suspensions (especially in business offenses/영업범), or attempt to obtain a suspended sentence of imprisonment rather than a fine.

Legal/Policy Gap
Formal trial cases represent over 30% of all single-judge criminal trial caseloads (고정사건). Because full trial proceedings with strict rules of evidence are applied to minor summary cases without the possibility of increasing the fine, judicial resources are severely distorted, causing trial delays and impairing the court's ability to conduct thorough trials for serious and complex criminal cases.

Objectives
Reform the formal trial system to ensure that the constitutional right to trial is upheld without being abused for improper collateral purposes, restore the proper filtering function of summary orders, and establish an efficient, two-tiered criminal justice procedure.

Implementation & Proposed Reforms
Abolition or Modification of Article 457-2: Abolish the prohibition of reformatio in peius for formal trials, or restrict its scope. To protect defendants' rights upon abolition, implement safeguards: advance explanation/notice of summary procedures, pre-trial confirmation of intent, written decision procedure for fine-only appeals (similar to German StPO § 411(1)), restriction on pre-trial detention during formal trial, and mandatory written reasons when increasing sentence.

Summary Stage Reforms: Expand summary order targets to include judgments of acquittal (무죄), dismissal of public prosecution (공소기각), and exemption of punishment (면소/형면제); enforce verified service locations to reduce public notice service (공시송달) rates which exceed 30%.

Trial & Fine Efficiency: Introduce simplified evidence examination rules and simplified judgment drafting for formal trials; operationalize suspended fines (벌금형의 집행유예), installment/deferred fine payments, and community service in lieu of fine detention (노역장 유치).

Long-term Structural Reform: Establish specialized summary courts (간이법원) or expand Rechtspfleger (사법보좌관) duties to handle minor criminal cases, expand electronic summary filing (전자약식절차), and introduce plea bargaining / summary disposal models.

Evidence & Empirical Data
A nationwide survey of 328 district court judges revealed that 99.4% agreed on the need for formal trial procedure reform, with 93% supporting the abolition of Article 457-2. Judicial statistics showed that in 2014, out of 84,102 formal trial dispositions, 92% resulted in the same fine or withdrawal, with actual acquittal/dismissal rates at only 4.8%.

6. MediaWiki Output

Research on Improvement Measures of the System of Motion for a Formal Trial is an official judicial policy research report published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of South Korea's Supreme Court.

Summary

This document is an official judicial policy research report published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of South Korea's Supreme Court. It comparatively and systematically analyzes problems within the system of motions for a formal trial against summary orders (약식명령), and formulates legal and practical reforms to prevent abuse of the right to trial and optimize judicial resource allocation.

Legal Basis

The legal frameworks analyzed include:

  • Constitution of the Republic of Korea: Article 27(1) (Right to a Fair Trial) and Article 27(3) (Right to a Speedy Trial).
  • Criminal Procedure Code of the Republic of Korea (형사소송법): Article 448 (Summary Order Requirements), Article 449 (Request for Summary Order), Article 450 (Referral to Public Trial), Article 453 (Motion for Formal Trial), Article 455 (Dismissal Decision), Article 457 (Effectiveness of Summary Order), and Article 457-2 (Prohibition of Reformatio in Peius / 불이익변경금지원칙).
  • Act on Special Cases Concerning the Promotion, etc. of Legal Proceedings (소송촉진 등에 관한 특례법): Article 22.
  • Act on Use of Electronic Documents in Summary Procedure (약식전자문서법).

Background

The summary order procedure (약식절차) was designed to quickly resolve minor offenses through written examination without an open trial. However, since Article 457-2 of the Criminal Procedure Code took effect in 1997 — prohibiting the imposition of a heavier sentence in a formal trial requested by the defendant — the rate of formal trial motions surged from 1.8% (1997) to 11.5% (2014).

Objectives

  1. Restore the filtering function of the summary order procedure and prevent abuse of formal trial motions.
  2. Ensure rational allocation of judicial resources so that serious and complex cases receive thorough examination (공판중심주의, trial-centered principle).

Scope

  • Five-Country Comparative Study: United States (Fed. R. Crim. P. 58 & Magistrate system), United Kingdom (Summary Trial & CJS Common Platform), Germany (StPO §§ 407–412 & Beschleunigtes Verfahren), France (Procédure simplifiée), and Japan (Summary Court & 在庁略式).
  • Empirical Survey: A survey of 328 criminal judges across district courts in South Korea.

Key Provisions

  • Reformatio in Peius Principle: A defendant who files for a formal trial cannot be given a heavier fine than the original summary order (Article 457-2 of the Criminal Procedure Code).
  • Practical Impact: Defendants exploit this procedure to delay payment of fines or administrative sanctions (영업범) without risk of a heavier penalty.

Implementation

The report proposes a four-stage reform:

  1. Abolition or scope restriction of Article 457-2 of the Criminal Procedure Code.
  2. Expansion of summary order eligibility to include acquittals (무죄), dismissal of prosecution (공소기각), and exemption from punishment (면소).
  3. Simplification of judgment drafting and evidence examination for minor formal trial cases.
  4. Optimization of suspended fines (벌금형의 집행유예), installment fine payments, and electronic summary filing (전자약식).

Policy Issues

  • Single-judge caseloads (고정사건) exceed 30% of all first-instance criminal cases.
  • Of 84,102 formal trial cases in 2014, only 4.8% resulted in a substantive change (acquittal or exemption), indicating a high rate of non-substantive motions.

Reform / Amendments

  • Abolition of the prohibition of reformatio in peius, paired with procedural safeguards (advance explanation, written procedures for fine challenges, and mandatory justification when increasing a sentence).
  • Establishment of specialized summary courts (간이법원) or expansion of Rechtspfleger (사법보좌관) authority.

  • Research Team: Kim Hyung-jin, Ha Min-kyung.
  • Related Institutions: National Assembly of the Republic of Korea & Constitutional Court of Korea.
