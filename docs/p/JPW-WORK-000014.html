4. POLICY ANALYSIS

Background: [FACT] EU and Spanish legal developments in mid-2022 were marked by the 70th anniversary of the CJEU's consolidation; the transition to a new consumer collective representation system under Directive (EU) 2020/1828; a surge in renewable energy investment and PPA dynamics; a 12-year review of business corruption offenses following Organic Law 1/2015; jurisprudential and registral conflict over mortgage auctions without bidders under Article 671 LEC; and uncertainty over standards for assessing consumer lawyer fees in banking litigation.

Policy Problem: [FACT]

  1. Consumer Protection: Fragmentation of Spain's collective action system and the need to harmonize compensation mechanisms without triggering abusive claims resembling US-style class actions.
  2. Energy Sector: Energy price volatility and structural risk in negotiating physical and financial PPAs for M&A transactions.
  3. Business Criminal Law: Ambiguity over the protected legal interest (whether purely "fair competition" or multi-faceted) and inconsistency in treating business owners as active offenders.
  4. Mortgage Enforcement: Application of the "cash-out rule" by the DGRN/DGSJFP, requiring creditors to deposit funds if debt falls below 50%/60% of auction value, conflicting with ordinary court jurisdiction.
  5. Cost Assessment: Divergence between courts setting costs based on "petrification of undetermined value" versus "effective work" and matter simplicity.

Legal/Policy Gap: [FACT] The absence of a specific civil procedure for collective representative actions within the LEC; the lack of regulation excluding business owners from being classified as active offenders in business corruption; misalignment between the text of Article 671 LEC and the registral qualification function under Article 132.4 of the Mortgage Law; and uncertainty over concrete criteria for a "reasonable and proportionate" fee figure following the Caixabank CJEU ruling.

Objectives: [FACT] Provide an efficient and fair collective representation framework; ensure financial certainty for energy M&A transactions via PPAs; clarify the scope of private corruption criminal liability; resolve uncertainty in mortgage auction enforcement; and align Spanish cost regulations with European effectiveness principles without permitting unjust enrichment.

Implementation: [FACT]

  • Collective Actions: Transposition of Directive (EU) 2020/1828 (deadline 25 December 2022).
  • Energy PPAs: Use of physical PPA structures (on-site/off-site) and financial structures (swaps, floors, collars), plus registration of Guarantees of Origin (GdO) with the CNMC.
  • Criminal Law: Application of Criminal Code Articles 286 bis and ter following Organic Law 1/2015 & Organic Law 1/2019.
  • Mortgage Auctions: Enforcement of STS 866/2021 of 15 December 2021, limiting registral qualification authority under Article 100 of the Mortgage Regulation.
  • Cost Assessment: Application of the CJEU ruling of 7 April 2022 (Caixabank, C-385/20), confirming the limits under Article 394.3 LEC (maximum one-third of the claim value) and judges' authority to assess effective work performed.

Evidence & Impact: [FACT] CJEU jurisprudence (Van Gend en Loos, Costa/ENEL, Simmenthal, Francovich, Cassis de Dijon, Caixabank) affirmed the CJEU's role as a pillar of European law; Spain reached 56.6% installed renewable energy capacity in 2021; and the Spanish Supreme Court enforced limits on registral qualification authority.

Reform & Unresolved Issues: [FACT] Projected reform of Article 671 LEC through the draft Law on Procedural Efficiency Measures; challenges in drafting specific collective civil procedure legislation in Spain; adjustment of qualification rules under Article 132.4 of the Mortgage Law; and establishing fee figures considered proportionate by Spanish courts.

  6. Policy Implementation: How did the CJEU ruling of 7 April 2022 (Caixabank, C-385/20) govern limits on assessing consumer lawyer fees?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] The CJEU permitted setting a maximum limit on lawyer fees recoverable by consumers, provided that limit still allows the consumer to obtain a "reasonable and proportionate" reimbursement corresponding to the objective cost of the procedure.
  7. Policy Problem: Why did conflict arise between registrars (registradores) and courts over the "cash-out rule" in mortgage auctions without bidders?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] Because the DGRN linked Articles 671 and 670.4 LEC to require creditors to deposit funds when the debt fell below 50%/60% of the auction value, while courts considered such registral qualification to exceed authority under Article 100 of the Mortgage Regulation.
  8. Legal Relationship: What is the relationship between M&A transactions and Power Purchase Agreements (PPAs) in Spain's renewable energy sector?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] PPAs act as a key instrument for setting long-term (8–15 year) pricing and revenue, determining the valuation and financing visibility (bankability) of the acquired SPV in M&A transactions.
  9. Reform: How did Directive (EU) 2020/1828 change the consumer collective representative action system in Spain and the EU?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] It introduced collective compensatory actions alongside cease-and-desist actions, tightened qualification criteria for "qualified entities," and restricted third-party funding to prevent abusive litigation.
  10. Evidence/Case Study: How does Article 286 bis of the Criminal Code (Organic Law 1/2015) distinguish between active and passive corruption in business?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] Passive corruption (Art. 286 bis.1) criminalizes directors/employees who request or accept an unjustified benefit, while active corruption (Art. 286 bis.2) criminalizes those who promise or give such a benefit to obtain preferential treatment.

6. MEDIAWIKI OUTPUT

Summary

Pérez-Llorca Legal Journal (Issue No. 7, May 2022) presents strategic doctrinal and judicial policy analysis: a reflection on 70 years of the history and jurisprudence of the Court of Justice of the European Union (CJEU) by former Vice-President Rosario Silva de Lapuerta; reform of the consumer collective representative action system under Directive (EU) 2020/1828; the structure and risks of Power Purchase Agreements (PPAs) in Spanish renewable energy M&A transactions; analysis of private business corruption offenses following Organic Law 1/2015; evaluation of award value in mortgage foreclosure auctions without bidders under STS 15 December 2021 and Article 671 LEC; and limits on consumer lawyer fees in unfair-clause litigation under the CJEU ruling of 7 April 2022 (Caixabank, C-385/20).

Legal Basis

  • Spanish Constitution (CE): Article 20 (freedom of expression and academic freedom).
  • Consumer and Civil Procedure Law: Directive (EU) 2020/1828, Law 1/2000 on Civil Procedure (LEC, Arts. 11, 15, 251, 394, 654.3, 670.4, 671), and Directive 93/13/EEC.
  • Energy and Corporate Law: Law 24/2013 on the Electricity Sector (LSE), Royal Decree-Law 24/2020, Directive (EU) 2018/2001 & Directive (EU) 2019/944.
  • Criminal and Mortgage Law: Organic Law 1/2015 (Criminal Code Arts. 286 bis, ter, quater), Mortgage Law (Art. 132.4), and the Mortgage Regulation (Art. 100).

Background

2022 was marked by a phase of European and Spanish legal transformation. The 70th anniversary of the CJEU underscored the importance of the principles of primacy and direct effect of EU law. In consumer affairs, the issuance of Directive (EU) 2020/1828 required Member States to provide a collective compensation mechanism. In the energy sector, the transition to renewable energy drove growth in PPA-linked M&A. Meanwhile, the Spanish judiciary faced uncertainty over registral qualification in mortgage auctions, and a wave of mass banking litigation triggered disputes over cost assessment.

Objectives

1. Consolidate the CJEU's role as a pillar of the EU's Community of Law. 2. Provide a collective representation procedural framework that prevents abusive litigation. 3. Provide structural certainty for M&A and PPA transactions in energy investment. 4. Clarify the criminal boundaries of private corruption and protection of fair competition. 5. Resolve the limits of registral qualification authority in mortgage auctions. 6. Affirm reasonable and proportionate lawyer-fee limits per CJEU Caixabank doctrine.

Scope

1. History & CJEU Jurisprudence: EU legal autonomy, the principles of primacy, effectiveness, and judicial independence. 2. Collective Representative Action: Qualified entities, opt-in/opt-out, third-party funding, and cross-border actions. 3. Energy Transactions (PPAs): Physical PPAs (on-site/off-site) versus financial PPAs (swaps, floors, collars), change-of-law clauses, and CNMC Guarantees of Origin. 4. Private Business Corruption: Active/passive corruption under Art. 286 bis of the Criminal Code, the protected legal interest, and corporate liability. 5. Mortgage Enforcement: Limits under Article 671 LEC, rejection of the "cash-out rule" by STS 866/2021, and the scope of Article 132.4 of the Mortgage Law. 6. Cost Assessment: CJEU Caixabank doctrine (C-385/20), Article 394.3 LEC, and criteria for assessing lawyers' effective work.

Key Provisions

  • Independence of EU Judicial Bodies: Requires full autonomy without hierarchical subordination and guaranteed judicial irremovability.
  • Qualified Entities Filter: Only registered non-profit organizations meeting representativeness requirements may file collective actions.
  • PPA Price Balance: Physical PPAs transfer delivery risk to the supply point, while financial PPAs are differential derivative instruments.
  • Nature of the Business Corruption Offense: Classified as an abstract-danger offense, completed upon agreement of the promise/benefit, without requiring proof of actual market harm.
  • Limits on Registral Qualification (STS 866/2021): Registrars may not deny mortgage auction enforcement under Article 100 of the Mortgage Regulation, except regarding the rights of subsequent-ranking creditors.
  • Proportionality Principle for Costs (CJEU Caixabank): National courts may limit lawyer fees charged to the losing party, provided the consumer still receives a proportionate reimbursement.

Implementation

Implementation occurs through enforcement of CJEU jurisprudence, oversight by Spanish civil courts, energy market registration by the CNMC and OMIE, and registral qualification oversight by the Property Registry.

Policy Problems

  • The absence of specific civil procedure legislation for collective actions, causing fragmentation of rules within the LEC.
  • The exclusion of business owners as active offenders in business corruption, blurring the boundaries of fair competition.
  • Ongoing discrepancy between registrars and courts over the scope of Article 132.4 of the Mortgage Law and Article 671 LEC.
  • Divergent criteria among courts in determining a "proportionate" absolute fee figure.

Reform

  • Enactment of Directive (EU) 2020/1828.
  • Projected reform of Article 671 LEC via the draft Law on Procedural Efficiency Measures.
  • Adjustment of national cost-assessment criteria in line with 2022 CJEU jurisprudence.

Relationships with Other Entities

  • Implements: Directive (EU) 2020/1828, Law 24/2013 (LSE), Organic Law 1/2015, and Directive 93/13/EEC.
  • Applies To: EU courts, consumer organizations, the renewable energy market, banking, and mortgage foreclosure enforcement.
  • Conflicts With: The DGRN's "cash-out rule" practice, which exceeds Article 100 of the Mortgage Regulation.
