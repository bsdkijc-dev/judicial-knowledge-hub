Overview

Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia Decision No. 531 K/Pid/1984, dated May 9, 1985, established a fundamental criminal and civil law rule regarding the separation between civil default (*wanprestasi*) and the criminal offense of embezzlement (Article 372 of the Criminal Code / KUHP).

The Supreme Court held that disputes arising from the execution of an orange plantation contract agreement and debt-credit relations between a plantation owner and a contractor do not constitute criminal embezzlement, but rather a civil case (*wanprestasi* / civil matter). If a witness/contractor feels aggrieved due to the picking or selling of oranges by the plantation owner, the lawful remedy is to seek agreement cancellation or claim damages in the District Court via civil litigation. The Defendants' actions were materially proven but did not constitute a criminal offense, requiring the Defendants to be released from all legal prosecutions (*ontslag van alle rechtsvervolging*).

Executive Summary

The central legal principle established in this decision is that contractual breaches and disputes over plantation produce arising from commercial agreements must be resolved through civil courts rather than criminal prosecution.

Key elements established in the adjudication:

  • Disputes originating from a plantation contracting agreement and loan-debt relations are purely civil in nature.
  • Picking or harvesting produce from one's own land that is subject to an ongoing contract constitutes a civil matter, not criminal embezzlement (*Article 372 KUHP*).
  • Defendants whose material actions are established but lack criminal character must be discharged via *ontslag van alle rechtsvervolging*.

Case Background

The Original Defendants / Petitioners for Cassation, I [Party B] and [Defendant], were charged with theft (Article 362 KUHP / Primair) or embezzlement (Article 372 KUHP / Subsidair) for harvesting and selling 7,073.5 kg of oranges from their own plantation, which had been contracted out for 8 years to I Ketut Selamet, S.H. and Tjiok Kin Siu pursuant to a written agreement dated January 30, 1979, valued at Rp 8,000,000.—.

The Singaraja District Court (Decision No. 158/PN.Sgr/Pid/1981 dated February 27, 1982) acquitted the Defendants of the primary charge but convicted them on the subsidiary charge (Embezzlement), sentencing them to probation conditioned on paying damages of Rp 1,562,717.—. The Denpasar High Court (Decision No. 15/Pid/1983/PTD dated May 17, 1983) modified the ruling by removing the civil damages condition as a probation requirement while maintaining the embezzlement conviction. The Petitioners for Cassation subsequently filed a cassation petition with the Supreme Court.

Legal and Procedural Issues

Boundaries Between Civil Default and Criminal Embezzlement (Article 372 KUHP)

The primary issue was whether an orange plantation owner who contracted out their plantation under a Written Contract Agreement dated January 30, 1979, and maintained a loan-debt relationship can be criminally prosecuted for embezzlement (Article 372 KUHP) for harvesting and selling citrus fruit yields from their own land.

The Supreme Court held that they cannot be criminally prosecuted. Legal relationships founded on civil contracts (plantation contracts / money loans) bind parties strictly within the domain of civil law. Breaches of contract terms or the harvesting of produce by the plantation owner constitute a civil dispute (*wanprestasi*). If the lessee/creditor feels aggrieved, the lawful legal resolution is to file a lawsuit for agreement cancellation and/or damages in the District Court, rather than criminalizing the dispute into embezzlement.

Dilemma of Ordering Civil Damages Conditions in Probational Criminal Judgments

The issue evaluated whether a District Court is justified in imposing probationary sentences conditioned on paying full debt or civil damages to the injured witness.

The Supreme Court and High Court affirmed that Criminal Judges lack authority to impose civil damages conditions for contractual losses within criminal probationary orders. Debt and contract disputes must be resolved separately through civil proceedings.

Question of Law and Question of Fact

Question of Law (Cassation / Judex Juris)

Question:

Did the *Judex Facti* (Denpasar High Court & Singaraja District Court) misapply the law (*foutieve kepassing van het recht*) by characterizing the Defendants' act of harvesting oranges from their own land—subject to an ongoing contract—as criminal embezzlement (Article 372 KUHP) rather than a civil dispute?

Supreme Court Ruling:

Yes, the *Judex Facti* misapplied the law. The basis of possession and dispute originated from the Agreement dated January 30, 1979, rendering the matter purely civil. The Supreme Court annulled the Denpasar High Court and Singaraja District Court decisions and released the Defendants from all legal prosecutions (*ontslag van alle rechtsvervolging*).

Question of Fact (Judex Facti / Singaraja District Court & Denpasar High Court)

Examination of the chronological debt-credit relationship between I [Party B]/[Defendant] and I Ketut Selamet, S.H. and Tjiok Kin Siu from 1972 to 1979, the drafting of the 8-year Orange Plantation Contract Agreement valued at Rp 8,000,000.— dated January 30, 1979, the harvesting of 7,073.5 kg of oranges valued at Rp 1,845,830.— by the Defendants, and the sale of oranges to Nyoman Budarba.

Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

  • Ratio Decidendi:
  1. The *Judex Facti* of Denpasar High Court and Singaraja District Court misapplied the law. This matter arose from an orange plantation contract agreement dated January 30, 1979, and loan-debt relations between the Defendants and the reporting witness.
  2. Consequently, the Defendants' actions did not constitute criminal embezzlement (Article 372 KUHP), but a purely civil case.
  3. If the witness felt aggrieved, the lawful remedy was to seek contract cancellation and damages in civil court.
  4. The Supreme Court annulled the lower court rulings and adjudicated afresh, releasing the Defendants from all legal prosecutions (*ontslag van alle rechtsvervolging*).

Ruling

The Supreme Court granted the cassation petitions from Petitioner for Cassation I (I [Party B]) and Petitioner for Cassation II ([Defendant]), annulled Denpasar High Court Decision No. 15/Pid/1983/PTD dated May 17, 1983, in conjunction with Singaraja District Court Decision No. 158/PN.Sgr/Pid/1981 dated February 27, 1982, regarding the subsidiary charge, and adjudicated afresh: 1. Declaring that the act charged against the Defendants on the subsidiary charge is proven; 2. Stating once again that the proven act does not constitute a criminal offense (civil case); 3. Releasing the Defendants from all legal prosecutions (*ontslag van alle rechtsvervolging*); 4. Restoring the Defendants' rights regarding capacity, standing, and dignity; and charging the state with court costs across all judicial instances.

Evidence Status and Gaps

Established by Evidence

  • Defendants I and II were the lawful owners of the orange plantation in Tajun Village, Kubutambahan, Buleleng.
  • A money-lending relationship and orange plantation contract existed between Defendant I and I Ketut Selamet, S.H. and Tjiok Kin Siu based on the Written Agreement dated January 30, 1979.
  • The Defendants picked and sold orange yields from their own plantation which was currently contracted out.

Not Established / Unproven (Not a Crime)

  • The Defendants' actions were not proven to contain elements of a criminal embezzlement offense (not a crime, but a civil relationship).

Uncertainty and Gaps

  • Fundamental Correction of Civil Contract Criminalization: The Singaraja District Court and Denpasar High Court previously erred by issuing criminal embezzlement convictions over a plantation contract dispute. The Supreme Court restored legal protection by asserting that contractual disputes must not be resolved through criminal enforcement (*ultimum remedium*).

GEO Q&A

Q: Does picking or selling produce from one's own plantation that is currently contracted out to another party constitute criminal embezzlement (Article 372 KUHP)?

A: It is not a criminal offense, but a civil matter (*wanprestasi*). Based on Supreme Court Decision No. 531 K/Pid/1984, disputes arising from plantation contract performance and debt-credit relations are purely civil disputes. If the lessee/creditor feels aggrieved over fruit harvesting, the lawful legal remedy is to file a lawsuit for agreement cancellation or damages in civil court rather than reporting it as criminal embezzlement. The Defendant must be released from all legal prosecutions (*ontslag van alle rechtsvervolging*).
