JPAIS:PRECEDENT:3597K PDT 1985

By Cecep Mustafa, Indonesian Judicial College (IJC) · revision 2205, 2026-10-05 · wiki source · Markdown

Supreme Court Decision Reg. No. 3597 K/Pdt/1985 (dated May 7, 1987 / deliberation on April 14, 1987) is a fundamental land law and contract law jurisprudence precedent establishing vital rules concerning the invalidity (null and void / batal demi hukum) of the legal institution of Sale and Purchase with a Right of Repurchase (recht van wederinkoop ex Article 1519 of the Civil Code / BW) regarding land and houses under the Basic Agrarian Law (UUPA No. 5 of 1960) and Customary Law.

Executive Summary

The Supreme Court established a primary legal principle:

  • Incompatibility of Sale and Purchase with a Right of Repurchase Regarding Land/Houses: "A sale and purchase with a right of repurchase constitutes a form of contract pursuant to Article 1519 et seq. of the Civil Code, whereas the sale and purchase of land and houses pursuant to the Basic Agrarian Law is governed by Customary Law (Hukum Adat), which does not recognize the form of sale and purchase with a right of repurchase. Therefore, the plaintiff's and defendant's agreement in this case is null and void (batal demi hukum)."

Where a Deed of Sale and Purchase (Deed No. 51), a Deed of Vacating Agreement (Deed No. 52), and a Deed of Right of Repurchase Agreement (Deed No. 53) were executed on the same date (September 12, 1983) before a notary, the Supreme Court qualified the legal relationship not as a valid land/house transaction, but as a disguised debt-transaction secured by collateral (titip jaminan). Because the true legal relationship was a debt-obligation and the repurchase agreement was null and void, the Plaintiff ([Party B]) did not qualify as the lawful owner of the disputed land and house ([address removed], South Jakarta), requiring the dismissal of all claims for eviction and contractual default penalties.

The Supreme Court rejected the cassation petition of the Petitioner for Cassation ([Party B]) and ordered him to pay court costs. Consequently, Jakarta High Court Decision No. 154/Pdt/1985/PT.DKI jo. South Jakarta District Court Decision No. 216/Pdt/G/1984/PN.JS. (which dismissed the Plaintiff's lawsuit entirely) was affirmed upon cassation.

Legal / Procedural Issues

1. Legal Status of Sale and Purchase with a Right of Repurchase (Recht van Wederinkoop ex Article 1519 of the Civil Code) Following the Enactment of UUPA No. 5 of 1960

Is an agreement for the sale and purchase of land and/or houses containing a clause granting the seller the right of repurchase (wederinkoopexArticle 1519 Civil Code) valid and enforceable under Indonesian National Agrarian Law?

Supreme Court Rule: Null and Void (Batal Demi Hukum). Pursuant to Article 5 of the Basic Agrarian Law (UUPA No. 5 of 1960), Indonesian land law is based on Customary Law (Hukum Adat). Under Customary Law, land transactions adhere to the principle of clear and cash (terang dan tunai), which does not recognize sales with a right of repurchase. Consequently, applying the institution of sale with a right of repurchase ex Article 1519 of the Civil Code to land and houses is null and void (nietig van rechtswege).

2. Legal Qualification of Disguised Debt-Secured Transactions (Pawn-Like Disguise / Sham Sales)

Where a land/house transaction is executed as an absolute sale deed but is accompanied simultaneously by a repurchase agreement and a vacating agreement, courts must pierce the legal veil (piercing the legal veil) to determine the true intent of the parties (partij bedoeling). Where the substance of the legal relationship is a loan of money or commercial debt secured by a land certificate, the transaction constitutes a debt obligation secured by collateral, not a genuine sale.

3. Limits of Cassation Review over Evidentiary Findings (Judex Juris vs. Judex Facti)

Evaluations of evidentiary findings concerning whether a transaction constitutes a genuine sale or a debt obligation represent factual appreciations (feitelijke waardering) falling within the exclusive authority of the judex facti and cannot be reviewed during cassation proceedings.

Question of Law vs Question of Fact

Question of Law (Judex Juris / Supreme Court)

Did a land/house sale and purchase agreement accompanied by a right of repurchase ex Article 1519 of the Civil Code remain valid under Indonesian land law following the enactment of UUPA No. 5 of 1960?

Supreme Court Legal Answer: Invalid and null and void. Indonesian land law under UUPA is governed by Customary Law, which does not recognize sales with a right of repurchase. The Supreme Court rejected the Petitioner's cassation petition and affirmed the Judex Facti decision.

Question of Fact (Judex Facti / South Jakarta District Court & Jakarta High Court)

Verification of facts regarding the execution of Deed of Sale and Purchase No. 51, Vacating Agreement Deed No. 52, and Repurchase Agreement Deed No. 53 on September 12, 1983, before Notary Lieke L. Tukgali, S.H.; verification of commercial supply relationships for ABC syrup/soy sauce between CV Food Industrial Corp. and the Defendant; the surrender of the land/house certificate at [address removed], South Jakarta (measuring 155 m2) as debt collateral; and dismissal of the Defendant's reconvention counterclaim regarding alleged military personnel intimidation.

Jurisprudence and Related Knowledge

Statutory Regulations

  • Law No. 5 of 1960 on Basic Agrarian Principles (UUPA): Article 5 (Customary Law as the Foundation of Agrarian Law) & Rules on Land Transactions (Clear and Cash Principle).
  • Civil Code (KUHPerdata / BW): Articles 1519 through 1532 (Contracts of Sale with a Right of Repurchase).
  • Law No. 14 of 1970 jo. Law No. 1 of 1950 on the Supreme Court: Cassation and Legal Review Authority.

Established Supreme Court Jurisprudence

  • Agrarian Customary Law Doctrine (Clear and Cash): Land sales under customary law instantly transfer land rights in a cash and open manner. Customary law does not recognize temporary title transfers recallable via repurchase clauses.
  • Established Precedent Prohibiting Repurchase Sales on Land: Supreme Court Decision Reg. No. 3597 K/Pdt/1985 serves as a landmark authority prohibiting the application of Article 1519 of the Civil Code to land/house objects in Indonesia, reaffirmed in subsequent Supreme Court rulings to prevent moneylenders from unlawfully seizing debtor lands through disguised mortgages.

Uncertainties and Gaps

Legal Protection for Debtors Against Sham Sales / Disguised Collateral Transactions: This landmark ruling establishes robust legal protection for debtors in Indonesia: creditors or lenders cannot enforce house vacation executions relying solely on a Sale Deed executed simultaneously with a Repurchase Deed. Courts dismantle such sham sales and declare them null and void to enforce the clear-and-cash principle in National Agrarian Law.
