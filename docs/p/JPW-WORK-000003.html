4. POLICY ANALYSIS

Background: [FACT] Developments in Spanish business and judicial law in 2020 were marked by shifting regulatory frameworks across several key pillars: the transition from a unified code to sectoral decodification of commercial law; the pursuit of efficient enforcement of international financial security interests; the modernization of directors' loyalty standards to prevent value extraction (tunneling); harmonization of international arbitration standards (Kompetenz-Kompetenz); a surge in digital copyright infringement via link-aggregation websites; the mandatory allocation of operational risk under EU public contract law; and the transformation of the banking landscape through open banking and strong customer authentication (PSD2).

Policy Problem: [FACT]

  1. Codification: The stalling of the draft Commercial Code, worked on for six years across seven Ministers of Justice without ever being enacted into law.
  2. Real Security Interests: The need for rapid, out-of-court enforcement of security interests, constrained by the prohibition on pacto comisorio under Article 1859 of the Civil Code.
  3. Corporate Law: Interpretive uncertainty around "flexible imperativeness" (imperatividad flexible) regarding the duty of loyalty and the limits of conflict-of-interest waivers (dispensa) in M&A.
  4. Arbitration: The risk of arbitration proceedings being paralyzed due to vaguely worded (inciertas), unenforceable (inoperantes), or jurisdictionally hybrid (híbridas) clauses.
  5. Digital Copyright: Legal uncertainty over classifying hyperlinks as acts of "public communication," and difficulties proving profit motive (ánimo de lucro) and effective knowledge (conocimiento efectivo) prior to the LO 1/2015 reform.
  6. Public Concessions: Ambiguity in defining "operational risk" (distinguishing demand risk from supply risk) and potential conflict with the principle of economic rebalancing (reequilibrio económico).
  7. PSD2/SCA: Technical obstacles in API adaptation and potential disruption to e-commerce transactions caused by dual-authentication requirements.

Legal/Policy Gap: [FACT] The absence of explicit regulation of the pacto marciano for all types of unregistered assets; gaps in secondary liability rules for third-party linking sites prior to LO 1/2015; and a lack of clarity in treating "risk of availability" within public-service concession supply risk.

Objectives: [FACT] Ensure legal certainty and credit cost efficiency; prevent extractive "tunneling" practices by controlling shareholders; uphold the principle of "useful effect" (efecto útil) and the validity of arbitration agreements; provide effective protection to copyright holders through dynamic blocking injunctions against ISPs; place concession contractors under conditions of genuine market risk; and protect digital financial transactions from fraud.

Implementation: [FACT]

  • Pacto Marciano: Recognition of the validity of asset transfer arrangements based on independent objective valuation and the return of any surplus (sobrante) (STS 24 June 2010; DGRN Resolution of 26 December 2018).
  • Target Director Discipline: Application of the Business Judgment Rule (Art. 226 LSC), protecting business decisions provided they are free of conflicts of interest and based on adequate information.
  • Arbitration: Enforcement of the Kompetenz-Kompetenz principle (Art. 22.1 of the Arbitration Law) and structuring of prima facie review by arbitral institutions (CAM, CIAM, ICC).
  • Link Websites: Application of Article 270.2 of the Criminal Code to criminalize active, non-neutral, profit-oriented linking, and issuance of website-blocking orders by commercial courts against ISPs (as in the Telefónica Audiovisual case).
  • Concessions: Application of Article 14 of the LCSP, requiring the transfer of demand risk or supply risk to the contractor.
  • PSD2: Implementation of dual-factor authentication (SCA) based on two independent factors (knowledge, possession, inherence) and integration of open APIs.

Evidence & Impact: [FACT] A significant reduction in digital piracy rates through progressive dynamic blocking by ISPs; harmonization of concession risk-assessment standards in line with the European System of Accounts (ESA 2010); and accelerated adoption of the open banking framework under the supervision of the EBA and the Bank of Spain.

Reform & Unresolved Issues: [FACT] Ongoing criminal jurisprudence debate over the status of linking prior to the 2015 reform; the need to clarify the limits of economic rebalancing (reequilibrio económico) in extraordinary events outside normal operating conditions; and requests to extend SCA adaptation deadlines for the hospitality and food-service sectors due to the pandemic crisis.

5. GEO/AI QUESTIONS

  1. Definition: What is the Pérez-Llorca Legal Journal (No. 3, June 2020)?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] A Spanish scholarly legal publication from the June 2020 edition, presenting doctrinal and jurisprudential analysis on the decodification of commercial law, the pacto marciano, directors' duty of loyalty in M&A, pathological arbitration clauses, enforcement against link websites, operational risk in concessions, and PSD2 open banking.
  2. Legal Basis: Which article of the Spanish Civil Code prohibits pacto comisorio, and how is it resolved through the pacto marciano?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] Article 1859 of the Civil Code; the solution is the pacto marciano — an arrangement for asset transfer based on independent objective valuation upon default, with an obligation to return any surplus value (sobrante) to the debtor or other creditors.
  3. Publisher/Authority: Who is the official publisher of this publication, and which body drafted the Commercial Code?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] Pérez-Llorca Abogados in Madrid; the draft Commercial Code was prepared by the Commercial Law Section of the General Codification Commission.
  4. Related Figures: Which author prepared the analysis of target-company directors' duty of loyalty in M&A?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] Jordi Farrés Pineda, Partner at Pérez-Llorca's Corporate Department.
  5. Scope: What three types of pathological arbitration clauses are identified in this publication?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] Vague/uncertain clauses (inciertas), unenforceable clauses (inoperantes), and mixed/hybrid jurisdiction clauses (híbridas/mixtas).
  6. Policy Implementation: How does Article 270.2 of the Criminal Code criminally implicate operators of link websites?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] Criminal liability applies to service providers who actively and non-neutrally facilitate access to protected content by offering indexed or classified lists of links for direct or indirect economic gain.
  7. Policy Problem: Why is "risk of availability" debated within the concept of "supply risk" in public concessions?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] Because availability risk relates to internal management and service quality under the contractor's control, whereas operational risk requires exposure to external risk beyond the parties' control.
  8. Legal Relationship: What is the relationship between the CJEU's Svensson doctrine (C-466/12) and GS Media doctrine (C-160/15) in digital copyright cases?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] The Svensson doctrine establishes the requirement of availability to a "new public"; the GS Media doctrine adds a subjective element, under which a profit-oriented linker is presumed (iuris tantum) to know the illegal nature of the linked content.
  9. Reform: How does Article 226 of the Capital Companies Law (LSC) regulate the Business Judgment Rule?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] A judge will not re-evaluate directors' strategic business decisions so long as those decisions were made independently (free of conflicts of interest), based on adequate information, and in compliance with the law and the company's bylaws.
  10. Evidence/Case Study: What dual-factor authentication (SCA) requirements apply under PSD2 and RTS (EU) 2018/389?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] The use of at least two independent factors from the categories of knowledge (something only the user knows), possession (something only the user has), and inherence (a user's biological/physical characteristic).

6. MEDIAWIKI OUTPUT

Summary

Pérez-Llorca Legal Journal (Issue No. 3, June 2020) is a major scholarly legal publication presenting an in-depth analysis of developments in Spanish and EU jurisprudence and judicial policy. This edition contains seven strategic doctrinal studies: an evaluation of the decodification of commercial law by the General Codification Commission; the legitimacy of the *pacto marciano* as an alternative means of enforcing real security interests; the application of directors' duty of loyalty and the Business Judgment Rule in target-company M&A; a guide to resolving pathological arbitration clauses; civil and criminal enforcement against copyright-infringing link websites; the classification of operational risk in public concession contracts (LCSP); and the implementation of open banking and strong customer authentication (SCA) under the PSD2 framework.

Legal Basis

The policy and regulatory analysis in this publication is grounded in the following legal framework:

  • Spanish Constitution (CE): Article 20 (academic freedom) and Article 1255 (freedom of contract).
  • Civil and Security Law: Articles 1859 and 1884 of the Spanish Civil Code (prohibition on *pacto comisorio*), Royal Decree-Law 5/2005 (financial services), and the Cape Town Convention.
  • Corporate Law: Capital Companies Law (LSC), particularly Articles 190, 204.1, 226, 228, 229, 230, 232, and 234.
  • Arbitration Law: Law 60/2003 on Arbitration (Articles 9, 11, 22, and 41).
  • Intellectual Property & Criminal Law: Intellectual Property Law (LPI, Articles 20, 138–141), Article 270.2 of the Criminal Code (as reformed by Organic Law 1/2015), and Law 34/2002 (LSSI, Articles 16 and 17).
  • Public Contract Law: Law 9/2017 on Public Sector Contracts (LCSP, Article 14) and Directive 2014/23/EU.
  • Banking & Financial Services: Royal Decree-Law 19/2018 (PSD2) and Delegated Regulation (EU) 2018/389 (RTS on SCA & CSC).

Background

The Spanish financial and judicial markets underwent significant transformation in mid-2020. The shift from a single code to sectoral legislation, the high cost of enforcing traditional security interests, the potential for "tunneling" practices in M&A transactions, legal uncertainty over the dissemination of protected content via internet hyperlinks, and the obligation to harmonize public concession regulation and digital payment transactions across the EU all demanded solid, well-established judicial doctrine.

Objectives

1. Advance passage of the draft Commercial Code to preserve market unity. 2. Facilitate fast and efficient enforcement of real security interests without violating the prohibition on *pacto comisorio*. 3. Prevent value-extraction practices ("tunneling") by controlling shareholders or affiliated directors. 4. Uphold the validity of arbitration agreements through the principles of "useful effect" and *Kompetenz-Kompetenz*. 5. Provide effective protection to copyright holders against illegal, commercially motivated linking. 6. Ensure the transfer of genuine economic risk (operational risk) to public concession winners. 7. Open up competition in banking (open banking) while tightening customer digital security.

Scope

The study covers seven main policy areas: 1. History & Codification: Evaluation of the General Codification Commission's work and the phenomenon of decodification. 2. Security Law: Validity of the *pacto marciano* and requirements for objective valuation of unregistered assets. 3. Corporate Law: Flexibility of conflict-of-interest waivers, the duty of confidentiality, and comparative analysis of the Business Judgment Rule. 4. Arbitration Law: Typology of pathological clauses (uncertain, unenforceable, hybrid) and jurisdictional correction mechanisms. 5. Digital Copyright: CJEU doctrine (*Svensson*, *GS Media*), ISP liability, and the criminal offense under Article 270.2 of the Criminal Code. 6. Public Contracts: The boundary between demand risk and supply risk, and conformity with economic rebalancing. 7. Financial Sector: Integration of Third-Party Providers (AIS/PIS), API communication standards, and dual-factor authentication (SCA) exceptions.

Key Provisions

  • Validity of the Pacto Marciano: Enforcement of security interests through objective market valuation upon default, with return of any surplus (*sobrante*), is legally valid and does not constitute a prohibited *pacto comisorio*.
  • Business Judgment Rule (BJR): Directors' business decisions are presumed valid and not subject to judicial re-evaluation if they meet the requirements of independence, adequate information, and compliance with the LSC's procedures.
  • Kompetenz-Kompetenz: The primary authority to assess the validity of an arbitration clause rests with the arbitral tribunal (Art. 22.1 of the Arbitration Law); national courts must decline jurisdiction (*declinatoria*).
  • Criminal Criteria for Linking: Actively, profit-oriented linking of unauthorized content satisfies the "public communication" element of the criminal offense under Article 270.2 of the Criminal Code.
  • Core of Public Concessions: A contract qualifies as a concession only if the contractor bears genuine economic operational risk, with no guaranteed full recovery of investment.
  • Dual-Factor SCA Principle: Verification of remote payment transactions must use at least two independent elements (knowledge, possession, or inherence).

Implementation

Enforcement and policy implementation occur through Spanish Supreme Court jurisprudence, CJEU rulings, DGRN decisions, and oversight by the Bank of Spain and the EBA over financial institutions' SCA migration plans.

Policy Problems

  • Obstacles to integrating the *pacto marciano* for registered goods due to rules on the discharge of secondary encumbrances (*purga de cargas*).
  • Potential misuse of actions seeking annulment (*Acciones Tendentes a la Anulación*), which could disrupt certainty in M&A transactions.
  • Divergent interpretation among criminal judges regarding the validity of criminal liability for linking prior to the 2015 reform.
  • A regulatory gap regarding the limits of "risk of availability" within concession supply risk.

Reform

  • Enactment of Organic Law 1/2015, reforming Article 270 of the Criminal Code to criminalize link websites.
  • Transposition of Directive 2014/23/EU through Law 9/2017 on Public Sector Contracts.
  • Implementation of Royal Decree-Law 19/2018 to integrate PSD2 in Spain.

Relationships with Other Entities

  • Complements: The jurisprudential framework of the Spanish Supreme Court and the CJEU.
  • Applies To: M&A transactions, public concession contracts, and electronic payment systems.
  • Replaces: The traditional approach to concessions based solely on "public service" status.
