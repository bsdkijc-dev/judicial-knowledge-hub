Overview

Supreme Court Decision No. 111 K/AG/1998 dated September 13, 1999, establishes important jurisprudence and legal principles regarding the qualification of ownership disputes in Islamic inheritance law and the formal requirements of inheritance lawsuits:

  • Estate Disputes Among Family Members: In inheritance matters (*waris mal waris*), disputes concerning estate properties among heirs who share family ties do not constitute pure property ownership disputes, and thus fall under the absolute competence of the Religious Courts.
  • Formal Defects in Inheritance Lawsuits: If, in an inheritance lawsuit, the Plaintiff merely requests to be declared as an heir, whereas witness testimonies at trial reveal that there are other entitled heirs (as also acknowledged by the Plaintiff), the Plaintiff's lawsuit must be declared inadmissible (*niet ontvankelijke verklaard*).

Case Background

The Original Plaintiff / Petitioner for Cassation ([Party B], represented by his attorney Jawite) filed a lawsuit against the Original Defendant / Respondent for Cassation ([Party C]) and 9 Co-Defendants at the Praya Religious Court. The object of the dispute consisted of the estate left by his late father ([Party A], who passed away around 1947), comprising wet rice fields measuring approximately 1.000 Ha and garden land measuring 0.0768 Ha in Banyu Urip Village, West Praya, Central Lombok. The Plaintiff claimed that in 1996, the Defendant (child of Amaq Gande / sibling of [Party A]) unilaterally took over control of the land.

The Praya Religious Court (Decision No. 45/Pdt.G/1997/PA.PRA dated May 21, 1997) granted the Plaintiff's lawsuit and determined the distribution of inheritance. However, the Mataram High Religious Court (Decision No. 72/Pdt.G/1997/PTA.MTR dated October 13, 1997) annulled the Praya Religious Court decision, declared the lawsuit inadmissible, and ruled that the Praya Religious Court lacked jurisdiction on the grounds that the case constituted a property ownership dispute. Dissatisfied with the Mataram High Religious Court ruling, the Plaintiff ([Party B]) filed a cassation petition to the Supreme Court.

Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

  • Ratio Decidendi:
  1. The Mataram High Religious Court erred in applying the law because disputes concerning estate properties among persons sharing family relations do not constitute property ownership disputes, but rather pure inheritance disputes (*waris mal waris*) falling within the absolute competence of the Religious Courts.
  2. Although this dispute falls within the competence of the Religious Courts, the Plaintiff in this lawsuit merely requested a declaration of his status as an heir, whereas witness testimonies indicated that there were other entitled heirs (a fact also acknowledged by the Plaintiff). Because all entitled heirs were not joined or accounted for in the proceedings, the Plaintiff's lawsuit must be declared inadmissible (*niet ontvankelijke verklaard*).
  3. Consequently, the cassation petition from the Petitioner for Cassation is rejected with a modification to the phrasing of the Mataram High Religious Court's ruling.
  • Ruling: Rejecting the cassation petition from Petitioner for Cassation [Party B] with a modification to the phrasing of the Mataram High Religious Court decision dated October 13, 1997, No. 72/Pdt.G/1997/PTA.MTR jo. the Praya Religious Court decision dated May 21, 1997, No. 45/Pdt.G/1997/PA.PRA; and Independently Adjudicating: (1) Accepting the Appellant's appeal; (2) Annulling the Praya Religious Court decision No. 45/Pdt.G/1997/PA.PRA; (3) Declaring the Plaintiff's lawsuit inadmissible (*niet ontvankelijke verklaard*); (4) Ordering the Plaintiff / Petitioner for Cassation to pay court costs across all levels of jurisdiction (assessed at Rp 216,000 at the first instance, Rp 48,000 at the appellate level, and Rp 50,000 at the cassation level). *(Decided in the deliberation meeting of the Cassation Panel of Justices: Drs. H. Taufiq, SH., MH. [Presiding], H. Chabib Syarbini, SH., and H. Zainal Abidin Abubakar, SH.)*.
