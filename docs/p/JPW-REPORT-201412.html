Definition

An official judicial policy research publication issued by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of the Supreme Court of Korea that comprehensively analyzes South Korea's dual real estate public disclosure system (이원적 부동산 공시제도) — wherein the Judiciary manages the real estate registration system (부동산 등기제도) via court register books (부동산등기부) and the Executive branch manages the cadastral/registration system (부동산 등록제도) via public cadastres (대장); evaluates comparative foreign models and unification debates in Germany and Japan; diagnoses the constitutional, legal, administrative, and practical flaws of proposals to unify real estate records or incorporate registration data into the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport's (MOLIT) comprehensive real estate register (부동산종합공부 under the Act on Surveying, Hydrographic Survey and Cadastre / 측수지법 Enforcement Decree Art. 62-2); and proposes realistic, practical improvements within the current dual framework — including amending the Building Act (건축법) to mandate court registration commission by local government heads (지방자치단체장의 등기촉탁 의무화), real-time electronic linkage/notification between court registration systems and cadastral databases, and automated updates across systems.

4. Policy Analysis

Background
Real estate constitutes the foundation of individual living and the core object of private property rights under Article 23(1) of the ROK Constitution, forming the core of property law (물권법). To protect property rights and guarantee transaction safety, public disclosure systems (공시제도) have evolved. In South Korea, a dual public disclosure system (이원적 부동산 공시제도) operates: the Judiciary (Supreme Court) manages the real estate registration system (부동산 등기제도) via court register books (부동산등기부) to reflect legal rights (권리관계), while the Executive branch (MOLIT & local governments) manages the cadastral/registration system (부동산 등록제도) via public cadastres (대장) to reflect physical property status (현황) and taxation. In 2013, Korean court registry offices (192 locations with 1,768 staff) processed over 10.69 million real estate registration cases (averaging 84.8 cases per registrar daily).

Policy Problem
Potential Discrepancies Between Register Books and Cadastres: Because court register books and executive cadastres are managed separately by different branches of government, discrepancies in physical descriptions or owner records frequently occur, reducing public disclosure credibility, creating unexpected transaction losses, and increasing litigation costs.

Dual Procedural Burden on Citizens: Property owners must complete double administrative procedures (e.g., applying for use approval with local government heads and then separately filing building modification registrations with court registry offices within 1 month), incurring double fees and inconvenience.

Administrative Inefficiency: Civil servants in both branches perform redundant manual double-entry and notification processing.

Executive Overreach & Unconstitutional Delegated Legislation: The Executive branch launched a project (부동산 행정정보 일원화 사업) to merge 18 real estate records (15 MOLIT cadastral records + 3 court registration records) into a single Comprehensive Real Estate Register (부동산종합공부). The Executive promulgated Article 62-2 of the Enforcement Decree of the Act on Surveying, Hydrographic Survey and Cadastre (측수지법 시행령 제62조의2), attempting to force the Judiciary to hand over court rights registration data to executive cadastral offices.

Legal/Policy Gap
Unconstitutionality & Legal Invalidity of 측수지법 Enforcement Decree Article 62-2: The parent statute (측수지법 Art. 76-3 No. 5) delegated only "other necessary matters" (ancillary details), NOT core judicial property rights data. By expanding this via executive decree to include court registration rights data, the decree exceeded delegated statutory authority (위임명령의 한계 일탈), rendering it legally void (무효) under Supreme Court En Banc precedent (2011Du30878). Furthermore, it directly conflicts with the Court Organization Act (Art. 9(3)) and Real Estate Registration Act (Art. 109(2)), which grant exclusive authority over registration data management and certificate issuance to the Judiciary.

Inherent Flaws of Record Unification: Real estate registration rights data (갑구, 을구) contains highly complex legal relationships (trusts, provisional registrations, multiple attachments, mortgages) requiring quasi-judicial review by independent registrars (등기관). Summarizing or mixing complex rights data into an executive administrative certificate (부동산종합증명서) misleads the public, fails to replace legal register certificates (등기사항증명서), wastes budget, and violates the Separation of Powers (삼권분립).

Comparative Evidence Against Unification:

  • Germany: In 2004 and 2006, the German Bundestag rejected agency unification bills (BT-Drucksache 15/3148 & 16/510) to merge Grundbuch (Courts) and Liegenschaftskataster (Executive) following unanimous opposition from the Federal Government, Federal Notaries Association (BNotK), and Judiciary, which cited the need to preserve independent judicial review, constitutive effect (konstitutive Wirkung), and public trust (공신력).
  • Japan: Japan's 1960 unification of register books and cadastres occurred because the Ministry of Justice's registry offices had already managed BOTH systems since 1950 (meaning there was no Judiciary vs. Executive division). After unification, Japanese registry offices suffered severe operational backlogs (taking 2+ weeks per registration), and local municipalities still had to maintain separate tax registers (고정자산과세대장).

Objectives
Preserve the structural advantages and judicial independence of Korea's dual public disclosure system while resolving administrative discrepancies and double procedural burdens through practical, e-government technical linkage.

Implementation & Proposed Reforms
Building Act Amendment (건축법 제39조 개정): Amend Article 39 of the Building Act to transform local government heads' registration commission from discretionary (등기촉탁을 할 수 있다) to MANDATORY (지방자치단체장의 등기촉탁 의무화) whenever building cadastre details change upon use approval. Simultaneously, abolish the civil fine penalty under Real Estate Registration Act Article 112 (과태료 규정 삭제).

Direct E-System Linkage (등기기록과 대장 전산시스템 연계): Create direct, seamless electronic cross-viewing links between the Court Internet Registry (대법원 인터넷등기소) and Executive Minwon24 (민원24) / cadastral systems, allowing citizens and officials to view and cross-check both records with a single click (one-click).

Real-Time Notification & Commission (실시간 통지와 촉탁): Upgrade court and executive computer networks to enable instant, automated transmission of registration and cadastral modifications between court registry offices and local government cadastral departments.

Automated System Updates (관련 정보의 자동 변경): Implement automated data synchronization so that when a change is entered in the primary source system (e.g., physical changes in cadastres or ownership changes in registers), the corresponding fields in the secondary system update automatically without manual re-entry.

Restriction of MOLIT's Project Scope: Confine MOLIT's 부동산 행정정보 일원화 사업 strictly to the 15 executive cadastral records under MOLIT jurisdiction, excluding court registration rights records.

6. MediaWiki Output

A Research on Methods for Improving Korea's Real Estate Records System is an official judicial policy research report published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of South Korea's Supreme Court.

Summary

This document is an official judicial policy research report published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of South Korea's Supreme Court. It comprehensively analyzes South Korea's dual real estate public disclosure system (이원적 부동산 공시제도) — under which the Judiciary (Supreme Court) manages the real estate registration system (부동산 등기제도) through court register books (등기부), while the Executive branch (Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport / MOLIT and local governments) manages the cadastral system (부동산 등록제도) through cadastral books (대장). It evaluates comparative studies and unification debates in Germany and Japan; diagnoses the legal defects, unconstitutionality, and technical limitations of the executive-led project to unify property records (부동산 행정정보 일원화 사업 / 부동산종합공부); and formulates practical improvement recommendations within the current dual framework through amendment of the Building Act (건축법), integration of e-government computer systems, and automated cross-system data updates.

Legal Basis

The legal frameworks analyzed include:

  • Constitution of the Republic of Korea: Article 23(1) (Guarantee of Property Rights & the Private Property System).
  • Civil Act of South Korea (민법): Article 186 (Constitutive Principle of Property Rights Registration / 성립요건주의 / 형식주의), Article 268, Article 303(2), and Article 361.
  • Real Estate Registration Act (부동산등기법): Articles 7, 11, 12, 15, 19, 22, 29, 35, 36, 38, 41, 43, 48, 58, 62, 65, 66, 100, 107, 109(2), and 112.
  • Building Act (건축법): Article 22 (Use Approval), Article 38 (Building Cadastre / 건축물대장), and Article 39 (Registration Commission / 등기촉탁).
  • Act on Surveying, Hydrographic Survey and Cadastre (측수지법): Articles 64, 71, 76-2, 76-3, 84, 88, 89, and Enforcement Decree Article 62-2.
  • Court Organization Act (법원조직법): Article 2(3), Article 9(3), and Article 19.
  • Supreme Court Jurisprudence: En Banc Decision No. 2011Du30878 (Dec 20, 2012) & No. 2006Du19693 (Mar 19, 2009) on the limits of executive delegation orders (위임명령의 한계 일탈).

Background

Real estate is the object of property rights underlying citizens' livelihoods and the national economy. South Korea operates a dual disclosure system in which the courts manage property rights (권리관계) and the executive government manages the physical status of property (현황) and taxation. In 2013, 192 court registry offices with 1,768 staff handled more than 10.69 million property registration cases. However, the existence of two separate systems has led to potential data discrepancies between the register book (등기부) and the cadastre (대장), dual procedures for citizens, and administrative inefficiency.

Objectives

  1. Diagnose the strengths and operational challenges of South Korea's dual property disclosure system.
  2. Evaluate comparative studies and unification debates in Germany (Grundbuch vs. Liegenschaftskataster) and Japan (1960 Amendment).
  3. Examine the unconstitutionality and legal defects of Article 62-2 of the Enforcement Decree of the 측수지법, which forces the merger of judicial registration data into the executive's 부동산종합공부.
  4. Formulate practical and technical improvement recommendations within the dual framework without altering the judiciary's institutional structure.

Scope

  • Ten-Country Comparative Study: Germany, Japan, the Netherlands, France, Belgium, Austria, Slovenia, Greece, the US, and the UK.
  • Evaluation of the Executive Project: Analysis of the 부동산 행정정보 일원화 사업 (15 MOLIT cadastral records + 3 court registration records).
  • Practical Reform Recommendations: Amendment of the 건축법, e-government system linkage (대법원 인터넷등기소 & 민원24), real-time notification, and automated data updates.

Key Provisions

  • Advantages of the Dual System & Separation of Powers: Maintains the separation of authority between the Judiciary and the Executive to prevent centralization of property data in a single institution and to protect private property rights.
  • Unconstitutionality of Article 62-2 of the Enforcement Decree of the 측수지법: Confirms that the Enforcement Decree Article 62-2 is null and void (무효) for exceeding the scope of parent-statute delegation (위임명령의 한계 일탈) and violating the Court Organization Act and the Real Estate Registration Act.
  • Mandatory Building Registration Commission (지방자치단체장의 등기촉탁 의무화): Changes Article 39 of the Building Act from discretionary (촉탁할 수 있다) to mandatory (촉탁하여야 한다) for local government heads whenever building cadastre changes occur.
  • Computer System Linkage & Automated Updates: Integrates the e-Court portal and Minwon24, and implements automatic cross-system data updates without manual re-registration.

Implementation

The government confines the 부동산 행정정보 일원화 사업 project to the 15 cadastral records under MOLIT's authority, while the Supreme Court strengthens the e-government data network linkage between registry offices and local governments.

Policy Issues

  • Data discrepancies between the court register book (등기부) and the executive cadastre (대장).
  • The dual procedural burden placed on citizens when making physical changes to buildings.

Reform / Amendments

  1. Amendment of Article 39 of the Building Act (건축법 제39조 개정 — Mandatory Registration Commission).
  2. Abolition of the administrative fine under Article 112 of the Real Estate Registration Act (과태료 규정 삭제).
  3. Integration of the 인터넷등기소 and 민원24 computer systems.
  4. Digitalization of real-time notification and automated cross-system data updates.
