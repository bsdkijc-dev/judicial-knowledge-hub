JPW-POLICY-000013

By Cecep Mustafa, Indonesian Judicial College (IJC) · revision 1345, 2026-09-17 · wiki source · Markdown

The Thai Court-Annexed Alternative Dispute Resolution Policy Framework (Stable ID: JPW-POLICY-000013) is a national statutory, judicial, and out-of-court dispute resolution policy framework evaluating court-annexed mediation, commercial arbitration, specialized court procedures, consumer protection, financial debt restructuring, and environmental dispute resolution in the Kingdom of Thailand. Documented under IDE Asian Law Series No. 19 by a judicial working party comprising Senior Justice Prasobsook Boondech, Judge Vichai Ariyanuntaka, Judge Jagkrit Jenjesda, Judge Pinyo Sangphu, Judge Pongratana Cruecrin, and Chief Prosecutor Dr. Chatchom Akapin in collaboration with IDE-JETRO, the policy outlines structural measures for court docket declogging, 2.5% court fee refund incentives, 45-day NPL financial mediation, tripartite labor adjudication, and OCPB consumer litigation. Note: Named entities Cecep Mustafa and Reihan Abdan Syakur are Not found in the available sources.

Summary

The framework addresses the severe trial court backlogs generated by the 1997 Asian economic crisis and the strict 2- to 3-judge trial quorum mandated by Section 236 of the 1997 Constitution. It outlines six core structural pillars: (1) Court-Annexed Mediation, empowering judges under Civil Procedure Code Sections 20 and 20 bis to conduct closed-door conciliation, caucus without attorneys present, and refund up to 2.5% in court fees (up to 200,000 baht) upon early settlement; (2) Financial NPL Restructuring, establishing the Court of Justice Regulation Pertaining to Mediation of Financial Dispute B.E. 2544 (2001) alongside CDRAC/Bank of Thailand frameworks for a 45-day mediation target; (3) Commercial Arbitration, administering domestic and international arbitration via the Thai Arbitration Institute (TAI) under the UNCITRAL-aligned Arbitration Act B.E. 2530; (4) Specialized IP & International Trade Adjudication, deploying 3-judge panels (2 career, 1 lay associate judge), Anton Piller orders, continuous trials, and direct leap-frog Supreme Court appeals under CIPITC Act B.E. 2539; (5) Tripartite Labour Adjudication, combining 3-party judicial panels with free, informal, and compulsory court mediation under the Central Labour Court; and (6) Consumer & Environmental Redress, deploying OCPB administrative enforcement and court representation alongside community-based compromise mechanisms.

Legal Basis

Authority and execution derive from constitutional provisions and statutory enactments:

  • Constitutional Charter: Constitution of the Kingdom of Thailand B.E. 2540 (1997, Secs. 236, 271, 276).
  • Procedural Codes & Arbitration Acts: Civil Procedure Code (Secs. 19, 20, 20 bis, 193, 210–222); Arbitration Act B.E. 2530 (1987).
  • Court Regulations: Court of Justice Regulation Pertaining to Mediation of Financial Dispute B.E. 2544 (2001); Court of Justice Regulation Pertaining to Mediation B.E. 2544 (2001); Practice Guidance Concerning Conciliation (1996); Civil Court Regulation on Mediation B.E. 2537 (1994).
  • Specialized & Administrative Statutes: Act Establishing the Intellectual Property and International Trade Court B.E. 2539 (1996); Act on Establishment of Labour Courts B.E. 2522 (1979); Labour Relations Act B.E. 2518 (1975); Labour Protection Act B.E. 2541 (1998); Consumer Protection Act B.E. 2522 (1979, as amended B.E. 2541); Enhancement and Conservation of National Environmental Quality Act B.E. 2535 (1992).

Background & Policy Problems

Background

Following the 1997 economic crisis and the expansion of international commercial transactions, Thai court registries faced an unprecedented influx of civil and NPL lawsuits, accumulating over 216,000 pending cases in 2001. To avoid trial paralysis under 1997 constitutional quorum mandates, the Court of Justice, after separating from the Ministry of Justice, established court-annexed mediation divisions and standardized national mediation regulations.

Policy Problems

  • Stalling Tactics by Litigants: Unscrupulous lawyers entering mediation solely to delay trial execution and extract higher fees.
  • Heavy Burden of Proof in Environmental Tort Claims: Citizens struggling to establish causal links between industrial toxic emissions and health impairments (Mayuree Teiviya aluminum case, Klity Mine lead case).
  • Execution Deficit in Arbitration Awards: Arbitrators lacking direct enforcement authority, requiring court judgment decrees.
  • Lack of Public Awareness in Rural Areas: Rural litigants remaining unfamiliar with formal arbitration and administrative consumer rights, relying heavily on informal village elders.

Objectives

1. Eliminate trial court backlogs through court-annexed mediation and pre-trial conciliation. 2. Resolve NPL financial disputes through structured 45-day mediation schedules. 3. Provide specialized, fast-track adjudication for IP and international trade disputes via CIPITC. 4. Ensure speedy, cost-free labor adjudication using tripartite quorums and mandatory court mediation. 5. Protect consumers against false advertising, deceptive labels, and hazardous goods via OCPB. 6. Promote "win-win" out-of-court settlements aligned with Thai cultural preferences for compromise.

Scope & Implementation

Scope

Applies across all Courts of First Instance (Civil Court, Kwaeng/Municipal Courts, Provincial Courts), specialized courts (CIPITC, Central Labour Court), TAI arbitration, OCPB, CDRAC, and administrative agencies in Thailand.

Key Provisions

  • Closed-Door Mediation & Caucusing (Sec. 20 bis CPC): Judicial authority to conduct mediation behind closed doors, caucus with individual parties without counsel present, and appoint expert conciliator panels.
  • Financial Mediation Timeframe: Mandatory 45-day mediation target (extendible twice by 15 days) for court-referred financial disputes.
  • CIPITC Special Procedures: 3-judge quorums, Anton Piller search orders, video-conferencing, un-translated English documents by consent, and direct Supreme Court appeals.
  • Tripartite Labour Bench: Requirement for equal representation of career judges, employer associate judges, and employee associate judges on Labour Court quorums, exempt from court fees.
  • OCPB Court Enforcement: Authority of consumer protection officers to sue business operators on behalf of consumers exempt from court fees.

Implementation

  • National Court Mediation Execution: Courts of First Instance across Thailand mediated 33,376 cases in 2001.
  • Labour Court Compromise Volume: Resolving 55.7% of all Central Labour Court cases in 1998 through mediated compromise or withdrawal.
  • TAI Commercial Arbitration Expansion: Handling 64 cases worth 38.4 billion baht in 2000.
  • OCPB Complaint Handling: Processing 7,946 complaints in 1999 and litigating 108 court cases in 2000.

Policy Problems

  • Stalling Tactics by Litigants: Unscrupulous lawyers entering mediation solely to delay trial execution.
  • Heavy Burden of Proof in Environmental Tort Claims: Citizens struggling to establish causal links between industrial toxic emissions and health impairments.
  • Execution Deficit in Arbitration Awards: Arbitrators lacking direct enforcement authority, requiring court judgment decrees.

Reform

  • Establishing national registries of qualified expert conciliators.
  • Setting strict statutory time limits on conciliation proceedings.
  • Creating tripartite conciliation quorums (judge, expert, law professor/lawyer).
