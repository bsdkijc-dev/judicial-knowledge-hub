4. POLICY ANALYSIS

  • Background: [FACT] Under Portuguese labor legislation (Código do Trabalho Arts. 56–57), employees with children under 12 or children with disabilities/chronic illnesses living in the same household have a statutory right to request a flexible working schedule (*horário de trabalho flexível*). The statutory framework balances the constitutional right to reconcile professional and family life (CRP Arts. 59.1.b, 67.2.h, 68.1) with the employer's operational directive powers (*poder de direção*) to organize work and establish work schedules (CT Arts. 200, 212).
  • Policy Problem: [FACT]
    • Unilateral Rest Day Determination by Employees: Friction arose when an employee requested fixed Saturday and Sunday rest days instead of rotating rest days across the week, claiming family obligations (husband working rotating shifts and daycare closed on weekends).
    • Conflict Between Management Power and Parental Rights: Employers contended that while flexible hours allow choosing start/end daily work times within set limits, the statutory definition does not grant employees the right to dictate fixed weekly rest days, which remains an employer prerogative under CT Article 212(1).
    • Judicial Overreach via Teleological Interpretation: The Supreme Court of Justice (STJ) overturned two lower court rulings (Barreiro Labour Court and Lisbon Court of Appeal) by holding that weekly rest days form part of the "working schedule" definition under CT Article 200 and can be unilaterally determined by employees.
  • Legal/Policy Gap: [FACT] The tension between statutory literal provisions—where CT Article 56(3) explicitly assigns schedule elaboration to the employer—and broad constitutional principles favoring work-life balance (*conciliação da vida profissional com a vida familiar*).
  • Policy Objectives: [FACT] Reconcile professional activity with family responsibilities; protect parental rights without paralyzing company operations; maintain clear boundaries on judicial interpretation of labor statutes; and respect the separation of powers by refraining from creating general binding norms (*Assentos*).
  • Policy Implementation: [FACT]
    • Case Facts (Proc. 423/20.9T8BRR.L1.S1): Employee hired in 2017 requested in 2019 that her rotating rest days (Mon–Sun) be fixed to Saturdays and Sundays due to childcare needs for two minor children.
    • Administrative & Lower Court Proceedings: Employer submitted refusal to CITE under CT Art. 57(5); CITE issued a negative opinion. Employer filed a court action where both Barreiro Labour Court and Lisbon Court of Appeal validated employer's refusal, ruling rest day determination belongs to the employer.
    • Supreme Court Ruling (12 Oct 2022): STJ reversed, ruling flexible hours allow employees to fix rest days based on favorable treatment principles and CITE opinions.
    • Doctrinal Critique (Arruda & Santos): Authors demonstrate that CT Art. 56(3) explicitly mandates that flexible schedules are "to be elaborated by the employer". Overriding statutory text via teleological interpretation violates Article 9 of the Civil Code and resembles unconstitutional *Assentos* (TC Ruling 810/93).
  • Evidence & Impact: [FACT] The STJ judgment created binding precedent within Portuguese labor jurisprudence, altering traditional employer schedule management and expanding employee parental protections, while generating debate regarding judicial law-making.
  • Reform & Unresolved Issues: [FACT] Potential constitutional challenge regarding judicial violation of the principle of legality and separation of powers (CRP Art. 112.5); operational burden on large employers with shift-based workforces; and the need for legislative clarification regarding the precise limits of employee choice in flexible working schedule requests.

Summary

Brief Commentary on the Supreme Court of Justice Judgment of 12/10/2022: Statutory Interpretation or the Return of Assentos as a Source of Law? (*Breve comentário ao Acórdão do Supremo Tribunal de Justiça de 12/10/2022...*) (Stable ID: JPW-WORK-000044) is an academic legal commentary written by Inês Arruda and Miguel Riço dos Santos of Pérez-Llorca. Published in Revista Jurídica Pérez-Llorca (#6, May 2024, pp. 120–125), the work evaluates Portuguese Supreme Court of Justice (*Supremo Tribunal de Justiça - STJ*) Judgment of 12 October 2022 (Proc. 423/20.9T8BRR.L1.S1). The ruling established that workers requesting flexible working schedules (*horário de trabalho flexível*) for family responsibilities under Articles 56 and 57 of the Labour Code (*Código do Trabalho - CT*) have the right to unilaterally fix their weekly rest days (e.g., Saturdays and Sundays). The authors criticize the STJ decision for overriding employer schedule elaboration powers (CT Art. 56.3), violating the principle of legality, and resurrecting normative *Assentos* declared unconstitutional by Constitutional Court Ruling 810/93.

Legal Basis

The policy and statutory framework analyzed in this work is grounded in Portuguese constitutional and labor legislation:

  • Constitution of the Portuguese Republic (CRP): Article 59.1.b (work-life balance), Article 67.2.h (family protection), Article 68.1 (parental rights), and Article 112.5 (principle of legality and separation of powers).
  • Portuguese Labour & Civil Legislation: Código do Trabalho (CT Arts. 56, 57, 59, 60, 200, 212); Código Civil (Arts. 2, 9); Law 99/2003; Law 35/2004; and Constitutional Court Ruling (*Acórdão TC*) No. 810/93.

Background

Under Portuguese Labour Code Articles 56–57, employees with minor children under 12 or children with disabilities are entitled to request flexible working schedules. This framework aims to reconcile professional activity with family life under CRP Article 59.1.b while respecting the employer's organizational and management authority (*poder de direção*) under CT Article 212.

Objectives

1. Analyze whether flexible working schedules include the unilateral right of employees to designate fixed weekly rest days. 2. Evaluate the STJ Judgment of 12 October 2022 (Proc. 423/20.9T8BRR.L1.S1) which overturned lower court rulings from Barreiro and Lisbon. 3. Assess the compatibility of STJ teleological interpretation with statutory interpretation rules under Article 9 of the Civil Code. 4. Identify risks of judicial overreach violating the separation of powers and resurrecting normative *Assentos*.

Scope

The study covers core labor law and constitutional issues: 1. Case Facts: An employee hired in 2017 requested fixing her rotating rest days to Saturdays and Sundays to care for two minor children due to conflicting spousal shifts and weekend daycare closures. 2. Administrative & Judicial Chain: CITE issued a negative opinion on the employer's refusal. Lower courts upheld the employer's management powers, but the STJ reversed in favor of the employee. 3. Doctrinal Critique: Authors show that CT Art. 56(3) explicitly assigns schedule elaboration to the employer ("a elaborar pelo empregador"), rendering the STJ interpretation contra legem. 4. Constitutional Implications: Judicial law-making that overrides statutory wording violates CRP Article 112(5) and TC Ruling 810/93.

Key Provisions & Principles

  • Schedule Elaboration Prerogative (CT Art. 56.3): Defining flexible work schedules, including presence slots and rest periods, remains an employer management right.
  • Limits of Teleological Interpretation (Civil Code Art. 9): Teleological goals cannot replace or override the clear literal text of statutory provisions.
  • Prohibition of General Binding Precedent (*Assentos*): Courts cannot issue general normative rulings that displace legislative mandates.

Implementation

Enforced through Labour Courts (*Juízo do Trabalho*), Courts of Appeal (*Tribunal da Relação*), the Supreme Court of Justice (*STJ*), and monitored administratively by the Commission for Equality in Labour and Employment (*CITE*).

Policy Problems

  • Conflict between parental flexibility rights and operational business requirements.
  • Operational uncertainty for employers managing shift-based or weekend workforces.
  • Judicial law-making (*judicial legislation*) encroaching on legislative competence.

Reform

  • Legislative clarification needed regarding statutory limits of employee requests under flexible working regimes.
  • Protection of the separation of powers and principle of legality under Article 112(5) CRP.

    • Factual Answer: [FACT] The authors cite the literal, historical, systematic, and teleological elements of interpretation under Article 9 CC, arguing that teleological principles cannot override the clear literal text of CT Article 56(3).
  10. Reforms & Constitutional Concerns: What potential constitutional violation do the authors highlight regarding the STJ decision?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] The decision potentially violates the principle of legality and separation of powers under Article 112(5) of the Portuguese Constitution.

6. MEDIAWIKI OUTPUT

Summary

Brief Commentary on the Supreme Court of Justice Judgment of 12/10/2022: Statutory Interpretation or the Return of Assentos as a Source of Law? (*Breve comentário ao Acórdão do Supremo Tribunal de Justiça de 12/10/2022...*) (Stable ID: JPW-WORK-000044) is an academic legal commentary written by Inês Arruda and Miguel Riço dos Santos of Pérez-Llorca. Published in Revista Jurídica Pérez-Llorca (#6, May 2024, pp. 120–125), the work evaluates Portuguese Supreme Court of Justice (*Supremo Tribunal de Justiça - STJ*) Judgment of 12 October 2022 (Proc. 423/20.9T8BRR.L1.S1). The ruling established that workers requesting flexible working schedules (*horário de trabalho flexível*) for family responsibilities under Articles 56 and 57 of the Labour Code (*Código do Trabalho - CT*) have the right to unilaterally fix their weekly rest days (e.g., Saturdays and Sundays). The authors criticize the STJ decision for overriding employer schedule elaboration powers (CT Art. 56.3), violating the principle of legality, and resurrecting normative *Assentos* declared unconstitutional by Constitutional Court Ruling 810/93.

Legal Basis

The policy and statutory framework analyzed in this work is grounded in Portuguese constitutional and labor legislation:

  • Constitution of the Portuguese Republic (CRP): Article 59.1.b (work-life balance), Article 67.2.h (family protection), Article 68.1 (parental rights), and Article 112.5 (principle of legality and separation of powers).
  • Portuguese Labour & Civil Legislation: Código do Trabalho (CT Arts. 56, 57, 59, 60, 200, 212); Código Civil (Arts. 2, 9); Law 99/2003; Law 35/2004; and Constitutional Court Ruling (*Acórdão TC*) No. 810/93.

Background

Under Portuguese Labour Code Articles 56–57, employees with minor children under 12 or children with disabilities are entitled to request flexible working schedules. This framework aims to reconcile professional activity with family life under CRP Article 59.1.b while respecting the employer's organizational and management authority (*poder de direção*) under CT Article 212.

Objectives

1. Analyze whether flexible working schedules include the unilateral right of employees to designate fixed weekly rest days. 2. Evaluate the STJ Judgment of 12 October 2022 (Proc. 423/20.9T8BRR.L1.S1) which overturned lower court rulings from Barreiro and Lisbon. 3. Assess the compatibility of STJ teleological interpretation with statutory interpretation rules under Article 9 of the Civil Code. 4. Identify risks of judicial overreach violating the separation of powers and resurrecting normative *Assentos*.

Scope

The study covers core labor law and constitutional issues: 1. Case Facts: An employee hired in 2017 requested fixing her rotating rest days to Saturdays and Sundays to care for two minor children due to conflicting spousal shifts and weekend daycare closures. 2. Administrative & Judicial Chain: CITE issued a negative opinion on the employer's refusal. Lower courts upheld the employer's management powers, but the STJ reversed in favor of the employee. 3. Doctrinal Critique: Authors show that CT Art. 56(3) explicitly assigns schedule elaboration to the employer ("a elaborar pelo empregador"), rendering the STJ interpretation contra legem. 4. Constitutional Implications: Judicial law-making that overrides statutory wording violates CRP Article 112(5) and TC Ruling 810/93.

Key Provisions & Principles

  • Schedule Elaboration Prerogative (CT Art. 56.3): Defining flexible work schedules, including presence slots and rest periods, remains an employer management right.
  • Limits of Teleological Interpretation (Civil Code Art. 9): Teleological goals cannot replace or override the clear literal text of statutory provisions.
  • Prohibition of General Binding Precedent (*Assentos*): Courts cannot issue general normative rulings that displace legislative mandates.

Implementation

Enforced through Labour Courts (*Juízo do Trabalho*), Courts of Appeal (*Tribunal da Relação*), the Supreme Court of Justice (*STJ*), and monitored administratively by the Commission for Equality in Labour and Employment (*CITE*).

Policy Problems

  • Conflict between parental flexibility rights and operational business requirements.
  • Operational uncertainty for employers managing shift-based or weekend workforces.
  • Judicial law-making (*judicial legislation*) encroaching on legislative competence.

Reform

  • Legislative clarification needed regarding statutory limits of employee requests under flexible working regimes.
  • Protection of the separation of powers and principle of legality under Article 112(5) CRP.
