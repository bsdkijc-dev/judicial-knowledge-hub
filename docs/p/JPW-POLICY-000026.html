JPW-POLICY-000026

By Cecep Mustafa, Indonesian Judicial College (IJC) · revision 1495, 2026-09-18 · wiki source · Markdown

The People's Court System of China Framework (Stable ID: JPW-POLICY-000026) is a national constitutional, statutory, and organizational judicial policy framework governing the structure, jurisdiction, judicatory principles, procedures, internal divisions, and reform trajectory of the court system in the People's Republic of China. Codified under Articles 123 to 128 of the 1982 PRC Constitution, the Organic Law of People's Courts (revised 1983), and the Supreme People's Court's Five-year Blueprint of Judicial Reform (1999), the framework establishes a four-tier court hierarchy comprising 1 Supreme People's Court, 31 Higher People's Courts, 309 Intermediate People's Courts, and 3,083 Basic People's Courts, alongside specialized Military, Maritime, and Railway Transportation Courts. Note: Named entities Cecep Mustafa and Reihan Abdan Syakur are Not found in the available sources.

Summary

The framework structures state judicial power under the constitutional theory of "One Mother and Three Sons", where the legislative authority (National People's Congress) produces and supervises the executive, judicial (courts), and procuratorial branches. It establishes six core structural pillars: (1) Constitutional Status & Inter-Organ Relations, defining courts as parallel to executive and procuratorial organs but responsible to People's Congresses; (2) Four-Tier Court Hierarchy, structuring grade jurisdiction across Basic, Intermediate, Higher, and Supreme People's Courts; (3) Ten Judicatory Principles, codifying independent trial, equal legal application, minority language rights, public hearings, right to defense, collegial panels, judicial committees (democratic centralism), second instance finality, judicial supervision, and withdrawal; (4) Procedural Specialization, establishing procedural tracks for civil, criminal, administrative, and special maritime litigation; (5) Internal Division Reorganization, mandating separate divisions for Case Filing, Trial, Judicial Supervision, Enforcement, Judicial Police, and Judge Education (College of Judge); and (6) Judicial Reform Directives, implementing the 1999 SPC Five-Year Blueprint to achieve judicial independence, efficiency, supervision, and professional judge management.

Legal Basis

Authority and execution derive from primary constitutional provisions, organic statutes, procedural codes, and special administrative region basic laws:

  • Constitutional Charter: Articles 123 through 128 and Article 135 of the 1982 Constitution of the People's Republic of China.
  • Organic & Procedural Enactments: The Organic Law of People's Courts of the PRC (revised 1983); Civil Procedure Law; Criminal Procedure Law; Administrative Procedure Law; Law on State Compensation.
  • Special Administrative Region Basic Laws: Basic Law of the Hong Kong SAR (establishing the Court of Final Appeal); Basic Law of the Macao SAR (establishing the Court of Final Appeal).
  • Supreme People's Court Directives: The Five-Year Blueprint of Judicial Reform by the Supreme Court of China (1999).

Background & Policy Problems

Background

Following the adoption of the 1982 Constitution, China re-established its formal judicial architecture. The court system expanded rapidly from 58,000 personnel in 1979 to 292,000 personnel by 1995. However, structural challenges—including local fiscal dependency, reliance on public security for enforcement, and a high proportion of non-adjudicatory staff—led the Supreme People's Court to enact its landmark 1999 Five-Year Blueprint of Judicial Reform.

Policy Problems

  • Lack of Centralized Judicial Budget: Courts have no independent budget allocation; local government expenditures fund operating fees and salaries, creating vulnerability to local administrative interference.
  • Absence of Autonomous Coercive Force: Courts rely on public security police at corresponding levels to execute court rulings.
  • Divergence Between Law and Practice: Local financial and operational dependencies impede the constitutional mandate for courts to exercise judicial power independently.
  • Overstaffing and Low Adjudicative Ratio: High proportion of court staff assigned to non-judging administrative or management roles.

Objectives

1. Realize the constitutional status of People's Courts to secure judicial independence. 2. Rationalize internal court divisions and eliminate non-adjudicatory personnel bloat to increase efficiency. 3. Strengthen judicial supervision and judicial committee oversight to prevent corruption. 4. Professionalize judge selection, appointment, and management under the Law for Judges.

Scope & Implementation

Scope

Applies across all Supreme, Higher, Intermediate, Basic, and Special People's Courts, judicial personnel, prosecutors, and court administrators in the People's Republic of China.

Key Provisions

  • Ten Judicatory Principles: Mandatory adherence to independent trial, equal protection, minority languages, public hearings, defense, collegial benches, judicial committees, second instance finality, judicial supervision, and withdrawal.
  • Division Separation Mandate: Strict operational separation between Case Filing, Trial Divisions, Judicial Supervision, and Enforcement.
  • Specialized Jurisdiction Rules: Grade jurisdiction governing case transfers and appeals across basic, intermediate, higher, and supreme courts.

Implementation

  • National Court Network: Operating 1 Supreme People's Court, 31 Higher Courts, 309 Intermediate Courts, and 3,083 Basic Courts.
  • Execution of 1999 Blueprint: Implementing 50 specific reform provisions promulgated by the Supreme People's Court.

Policy Problems

  • Local Fiscal Dependency: Lack of a centralized court budget forcing reliance on local government funding.
  • Coercive Execution Deficit: Reliance on public security police to enforce court decisions.
  • Non-Adjudicatory Personnel Ratio: Over 120,000 court staff working in administrative/police roles rather than judging cases.

Reform

  • Achieving constitutional judicial independence from local administrative interference.
  • Streamlining internal administrative organs and personnel.
  • Enhancing judge professional ethics and judicial supervision.

Related Key Figures & Institutions

Key Figures

  • President of the Supreme People's Court: Highest judicial officer in the PRC, elected and subject to removal by the National People's Congress (NPC).
  • Presidents of Local People's Courts: Head judicial officers at Higher, Intermediate, and Basic court levels, elected by local People's Congresses.
  • Chief Procurators of People's Procuratorates: Statutory heads authorized to attend judicial committee meetings at corresponding court levels without voting rights.

Key Institutions

  • Supreme People's Court of China (SPC)
  • National People's Congress (NPC)
