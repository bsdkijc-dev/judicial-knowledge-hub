The e-CODEX Framework and Greek European Payment Order (EPO) Pilot Implementation is a transnational European Union e-Justice interoperability policy framework and electronic court system architecture model (e-CODEX - e-Justice Communication via Online Data Exchange). Formulated by Prof. George Pangalos, Ioannis Salmatzidis, and Ioannis Pagkalos (Informatics Laboratory, Aristotle University of Thessaloniki / AUTH, 2014), it establishes decentralized interoperability standards (e-Delivery, e-Signature DSS-Tool, e-ID, e-Document XML), connecting the EU e-Justice Portal with national judicial systems, and operationalizes the European Payment Order (EPO) pilot system in Greece (at the Athens Court of First Instance) based on EU Regulations No. 1896/2006 and No. 936/2012 [1, 7–15, 20, 23].

Summary

Published by Prof. George Pangalos, Ioannis Salmatzidis, and Ioannis Pagkalos in the International Journal for Court Administration (IJCA), this policy framework outlines the e-CODEX system architecture and the execution of the European Payment Order (EPO) pilot in Greece. e-CODEX is an EU Large Scale e-Justice project that designs decentralized infrastructure to link the EU e-Justice Portal with the national judicial systems of 20 member states. Utilizing four decentralized components (Gateway, National Connector, National System/CMS, and e-Justice Portal), e-CODEX enables paperless electronic filing of cross-border debt recovery claims [9–12, 13]. The Greek EPO pilot, centered at the Athens Court of First Instance, has been fully operational since June 2014, eliminating physical mail delivery and circumventing language barriers via automated XML data conversion.

Legal Basis

  • Regulation (EC) No. 1896/2006 of the European Parliament and of the Council: The foundational regulation creating the European Order for Payment (EPO) procedure for undisputed cross-border financial claims [13–14, 33].
  • Regulation (EU) No. 936/2012: The regulation updating the EPO procedure standards and application forms across the EU [13–14].
  • EU ICT Policy Support Program: The European Union funding framework financing the e-CODEX project for 50 months starting in 2012.
  • Circle of Trust Agreement: An agreement ensuring data confidentiality, encryption, and mutual trust signed by all participating e-CODEX ministries of justice.
  • Not found in the available sources: Other specific formal statutory regulations are not mentioned in the source text.

Background

The increasing economic integration and mobility of EU citizens have led to a rapid growth in cross-border legal procedures. Traditional manual, paper-based legal communication caused protracted delays, opaque case statuses, and exorbitant costs. In the commercial sector, cross-border debt payment delays triggered SME bankruptcies. To combat this, the EU launched the e-Justice initiative and funded the e-CODEX project.

Objectives

1. Facilitate cross-border access to justice for EU citizens, enterprises, and legal professionals. 2. Build a decentralized, secure, and content-agnostic electronic delivery platform (e-Delivery) based on the ebMS 3.0 standard. 3. Operationalize the electronic European Payment Order (EPO) pilot integrated between the Greek judicial system and other EU member states. 4. Eliminate reliance on physical registered mail and reduce language barriers in case filings [1, 7, 9–10, 13–14].

Scope

This policy covers the implementation of the e-CODEX architecture across 20 EU member states, with a specific focus on the EPO Pilot operations at the Athens Court of First Instance and the involvement of Greek lawyers.

Key Provisions

  • Four Components of the e-CODEX Architecture:
  1. e-CODEX Gateway: The secure data transmission hub using the ebMS 3.0 standard (Holodeck b2b messaging) and ETSI REM.
  2. National Connector: The local module for semantic mapping, XML document conversion, and digital signature validation.
  3. National System (CMS): The internal court case management application (Athens Court of First Instance).
  4. European e-Justice Portal: The single EU portal communicating directly with the Gateway.
  • Three Main Building Blocks:
    • e-Delivery: Encrypted message transportation between Gateways.
    • e-Signature: Electronic signature verification based on the DSS-Tool to issue a trust-ok token.
    • e-ID: User identity authentication via national portals (e.g., the Greek Ermis portal) or Find a Lawyer 2.
  • Phased Roll-out Strategy:
    • The pilot implementation was initially restricted to lawyers (lawyers first) registered with the Athens Bar Association and Thessaloniki Bar Association before expanding to the general public.

Implementation

  • Operationalization of the Greek EPO Pilot: Fully functional since June 2014 at the Athens Court of First Instance (which manages ~60% of the national caseload).
  • Automated Testing Tool: Use of an automated testing application to simulate random message transmissions to validate the reliability of the National Connector prior to full deployment.

Policy Problems

  • Cross-Border Access Deficit: 63% of small cross-border financial claims in the EU were abandoned because the estimated handling costs exceeded the financial value claimed.
  • Non-Technical Systemic Limitations: e-CODEX cannot resolve national regulatory differences regarding court fee payment methods, official defendant notification rules, and difficulties in identifying foreign court jurisdictions.

Reform / Change

  • Expansion of the e-CODEX pilot into 8 other legal domains, including Small Claims (SC), European Arrest Warrants (EAW), and the recognition of financial penalties [12–13].
  • Plans to open direct e-filing access to ordinary citizens following the lawyer stabilization phase.
