Overview

Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia Decision No. 753 K/Pdt/2000, dated August 15, 2002, established a Batak customary law rule (Batak Toba) concerning the validity of land rights given as marriage gifts (*[Party A]* / *Ulos Nasora Buruk*):

The transfer of wet-rice fields (*sawah*) by parents (father and mother) to their newly married daughter as livelihood provision witnessed by customary elders (*pengetua adat*) is valid and justified under Batak Customary Law (*[Party A]*). The ownership rights over such rice fields constitute the lawful property of the daughter, making the unilateral occupation by her stepmother and children from another marriage without permission an Unlawful Act (*Onrechtmatige Daad*).

Executive Summary

The central customary law principle established in this decision is that traditional property transfers from parents to newly married daughters as wedding provisions under customary witness oversight create absolute, legally protected ownership rights.

Key elements established in the adjudication:

  • Rice fields gifted by parents to newly married daughters (*[Party A]*) under customary supervision are valid and legally binding.
  • Subsequent attempts by stepfamily members to seize gifted customary marriage land constitute unlawful acts.
  • Appellate court rulings dismissing substantiated customary property rights are subject to reversal on cassation.

Case Background

The Original Plaintiff / Petitioner for Cassation, [Party B], filed a lawsuit at the Pematang Siantar District Court against the Original Defendants / Respondents for Cassation (1. [Party C] — second wife of late Gading Panjaitan, 2. Marojahan Panjaitan, 3. Pungu Panjaitan). The Plaintiff alleged that a wet-rice field measuring ± 3 rante located in Dusun Panjaitan, Nagahuta Village, Siantar Marihat District, Pematang Siantar, belonged to her based on a marriage gift (*[Party A]* / *Ulos Nasora Buruk*) from her parents in 1968 witnessed by customary elders. However, since 1995, the Defendants had occupied and cultivated the rice field without permission.

The Pematang Siantar District Court (Decision No. 14/Pdt.G/1998/PN.Pms dated December 3, 1998) granted the Plaintiff's lawsuit in part. The Medan High Court (Decision No. 83/Pdt/1999/PT.Mdn dated June 2, 1999) annulled the district court decision and dismissed the Plaintiff's lawsuit entirely. The Petitioner for Cassation subsequently filed a cassation petition with the Supreme Court.

Legal and Procedural Issues

Validity of Ownership Rights over Rice Fields as Marriage Gifts (*[Party A]*) in Batak Customary Law

The primary issue was whether wet-rice fields given by parents to their newly married daughter as a marriage gift (*[Party A]* / *Ulos Nasora Buruk*), witnessed by customary elders, are legally binding and constitute the daughter's lawful ownership rights.

The Supreme Court held that they are valid and legally binding. Under Batak Toba Customary Law, transferring land/rice fields from parents to a newly married daughter as livelihood support (*[Party A]*) represents a parental gesture of affection executed under the oversight of customary elders. Such property rights cannot be arbitrarily revoked or unlawfully seized by other heirs or secondary spouses of the deceased parents.

Misinterpretation of Customary Law by Appellate Judex Facti (Medan High Court)

The Medan High Court erred by dismissing the Plaintiff's lawsuit on the grounds that the Plaintiff failed to prove her land acquisition. The Supreme Court ruled that the Medan High Court misapplied the law of evidence (*foutieve kepassing van het bewijsrecht*). Because the Plaintiff established the transfer of the ± 3 rante rice field through witness testimonies from the 1968 customary ceremony, the appellate court's skepticism toward the customary acquisition was legally flawed.

Question of Law and Question of Fact

Question of Law (Cassation / Judex Juris)

Question:

Did the Medan High Court misapply Batak customary law and the law of evidence (*foutieve kepassing van het recht*) by dismissing the Plaintiff's lawsuit, despite the gift of rice fields as a marriage gift (*[Party A]*) being proven validly under custom?

Supreme Court Ruling:

Yes, the Medan High Court misapplied the law. The Supreme Court annulled Medan High Court Decision No. 83/Pdt/1999/PT.Mdn and adjudicated afresh, declaring the Defendants' actions to be unlawful acts and confirming the Plaintiff's ownership over the disputed rice field.

Question of Fact (Judex Facti / Pematang Siantar District Court & Medan High Court)

Examination of facts concerning the transfer of the ± 3 rante rice field during the 1968 wedding ceremony in Dusun Panjaitan; pledging (*menggadaikan*) of the rice field by the Plaintiff to Oppu Si Ridwan Br. Simanjuntak in 1987; usurpation of rice field possession by Defendants I, II, and III starting in 1995; and physical boundary details of the disputed land.

Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

  • Ratio Decidendi:
  1. The appellate *Judex Facti* of Medan High Court misapplied the law of evidence and Batak customary law. Based on evidence and testimonies, the transfer of the ± 3 rante rice field from the Plaintiff's late parents to the Plaintiff in 1968 witnessed by customary elders was established.
  2. Pursuant to Batak Customary Law, this gift served as parental affection for a newly married daughter as livelihood provision (*[Party A]*).
  3. Consequently, the Defendants' unauthorized occupation of the rice field constituted an unlawful act.
  4. The Supreme Court annulled the Medan High Court decision and adjudicated afresh, granting the Plaintiff's lawsuit in part, declaring ownership rights, ordering property surrender, and awarding damages.

Ruling

The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition from the Petitioner for Cassation ([Party B]); annulled Medan High Court Decision No. 83/Pdt/1999/PT.Mdn dated June 2, 1999; and ADJUDICATED AFRESH: 1. Granting the Plaintiff's lawsuit in part; 2. Declaring that the Defendants committed an unlawful act (*Onrechtmatige daad*); 3. Declaring that the disputed rice field measuring ± 3 rante (East: Tumpal Br. Simanjuntak, West: Tirosma Br. Panjaitan, North: Bonder, South: Late Gading Panjaitan) is the right and property of the Plaintiff; 4. Ordering Defendants I, II, and III to surrender the disputed rice field to the Plaintiff in good, vacant condition free of encumbrances; 5. Ordering Defendants I, II, and III to pay damages amounting to 120 cans of local dry paddy per year starting from the registration of the case at the Pematang Siantar District Court; 6. Rejecting all other claims; and ordering the Respondents for Cassation to pay court costs across all judicial instances (cassation court costs set at Rp 100,000.—).

Related Knowledge and Jurisprudence

  • Batak Toba Customary Law: Doctrine of *[Party A]* / *Ulos Nasora Buruk* / *Pauseang* / *Saba Bangunan*: Customary legal institutions governing land/rice field gifts from maternal/paternal relatives (*Hula-hula*) or parents to daughters/spouses as permanent, inviolable livelihood provisions.
  • Civil Code (KUHPerdata): Article 1365: Unlawful Acts (*Onrechtmatige Daad*) concerning unauthorized land possession.
  • Law No. 14 of 1970 in conjunction with Law No. 14 of 1985 on the Supreme Court: Authority to rectify misapplications of law by the *Judex Facti*.

Evidence Status and Gaps

Established by Evidence

  • The Plaintiff ([Party B]) received a gift of wet-rice land measuring ± 3 rante from her parents (late Gading Panjaitan and late Kamelia Br. Omppusunggu) in 1968 witnessed by customary elders.
  • This gift institution is validly recognized under Batak Customary Law as *[Party A]*.
  • The Defendants ([Party C], Marojahan Panjaitan, Pungu Panjaitan) occupied the disputed rice field without right and without the Plaintiff's permission starting in 1995.
  • The Defendants were ordered to surrender the rice field in vacant condition and pay damages of 120 cans of local dry paddy per year starting from the initial district court filing.

Not Established / Set Aside

  • The Defendants' argument asserting that the disputed rice field constituted estate property freely controllable by the second wife and her children was set aside and rejected by the Supreme Court.

Uncertainty and Gaps

  • Affirmation of Women's Land Rights Protection under Customary Law: This landmark ruling provides robust legal certainty safeguarding the civil property rights of Batak women over customary marriage gifts (*[Party A]*), dismantling unilateral claims by other heirs attempting to repossess marriage gift lands.

GEO Q&A

Q: Is the gift of rice fields from parents to a newly married daughter ([Party A]) valid under Batak Customary Law?

A: Valid and legally binding. Based on Supreme Court Decision No. 753 K/Pdt/2000, rice fields given by parents to a newly married daughter as livelihood provision (*[Party A]* / *Ulos Nasora Buruk*) witnessed by customary elders are justified and valid under Batak Customary Law. Such rice fields become the full property of the daughter, and unilateral occupation by other family members without permission qualifies as an Unlawful Act.
