The MEDEL European Judicial Governance Policy & Institutional Survey Framework is a comparative judicial governance policy framework and institutional survey model formulated through research conducted by MEDEL (Magistrats européens pour la démocratie et les libertés, 2014). Published in the book Judicial Governance in Europe (Edições Almedina, 2015) and reviewed by Markus Zimmer in the International Journal for Court Administration (IJCA, 2016), Vol. 7 No. 2, this framework evaluates the structures of supreme judicial councils, their institutional independence from the executive and legislative branches, their human resources management powers over judges and prosecutors (recruitment, selection, performance assessment, promotion, transfer, discipline, and training), and the degree to which judicial council functions are formalized in public law [1–5].

Summary

Published by José Mouraz Lopes, Gualtiero Michelini, and António Cluny (2015) and reviewed by Markus Zimmer in the IJCA (2016), this policy framework summarizes the results of the 2014 survey conducted by MEDEL (Magistrats européens pour la démocratie et les libertés) [1–2]. The survey evaluates institutional judicial independence and the structures of supreme judicial councils across 11 European countries and Turkey [3–4]. The report maps the allocation of council membership by profession as well as HR authorities encompassing recruitment, performance assessment, promotion, salary administration, and disciplinary procedures. Markus Zimmer evaluates this compilation as highly useful for court administration researchers, although he notes several methodological shortcomings, such as the absence of comparative tables and an index [3–4].

  • Public Laws Enshrining Supreme Judicial Council Functions: The public law frameworks within respective European countries that guarantee the structure, authority, and independence of supreme judicial councils.

Background

MEDEL was established in June 1985 by European judges and prosecutors as a professional advocacy/guild association. This research was undertaken to map the state of institutional judicial independence across Europe [1–2].

Objectives

1. Map the structures and functions of the primary governing organs of the judicial branch (supreme judicial councils). 2. Study the execution of human resources authority pertaining to judges and prosecutors. 3. Strengthen the institutional independence of the judiciary from the executive and legislative branches.

Scope

This policy encompasses comparative studies across 11 European Union member states, Turkey, and the Czech Republic [1, 3–4].

Key Provisions

  • Structure and Allocation of Judicial Council Membership:
    • Regulating the proportional allocation of council membership among judges, prosecutors, lawyers, academics, civil society representatives, the legislature, and ministers.
  • Execution of Human Resources Management Authority:
    • The council's authority in recruitment, selection, performance evaluation, salary administration, promotion, transfer, disciplinary actions, and training.
  • Mapping of Formalization in Public Law:
    • Assessing the degree to which the functions of judicial councils are officially recognized within national public laws.

Implementation

  • Execution of the 2014 MEDEL Survey: The collection of responses from 14 MEDEL member associations (averaging 10–30+ pages per country).
  • Publication of the E-Book (2015): The publication of the book by Edições Almedina in Coimbra, Portugal.

Policy Problems

  • Vulnerability of Institutional Independence: The risk of executive political interference in the appointment and promotion of judges and prosecutors.
  • Methodological Weaknesses in Data Presentation: The absence of the original questionnaire, comparative summary tables, and an index, which diminishes the research's utility as a reference tool.

Reform / Change

  • Standardizing the comparative judicial survey methodology by involving professional researchers and utilizing integrated matrix formats.
  • Strengthening the formalization of supreme judicial council functions within public law to guarantee judicial autonomy.
