Improving issue representation with candidate-level voting advice applications is an empirical comparative political science research paper authored by Micha Germann (University of Bath), Fernando Mendez (University of Zurich), Jonathan Wheatley (Oxford Brookes University), Constantinos Djouvas (Cyprus University of Technology), Roula Nezi (University of Surrey), and Matthew Wall (Swansea University), published in the European Journal of Political Research (EJPR) in 2025.

Summary

This study tests whether candidate-level Voting Advice Applications (VAAs) — which match voters' preferences directly with individual candidates — improve issue representation and policy congruence compared to standard party-level VAAs. Through a randomized field experiment embedded in the MyVoteChoice app during the 2021 Welsh Senedd election (N > 6,500), the study demonstrates that candidate-level VAA recommendations significantly increase both propensity to vote (PTV) and vote intention for the policy-congruent candidate.

Legal Basis

Welsh Senedd Electoral System (Additional Member System): Wales's mixed electoral system, combining 40 First-Past-The-Post constituency seats and 20 regional closed party-list seats.

Background

Candidate-based electoral systems (such as open-list proportional representation, single transferable vote, or mixed-member systems) have grown increasingly common. However, individual candidates often hold views that diverge from their official party line. Voters frequently struggle to obtain information on individual candidates' policy positions, meaning their votes often fail to reflect their true policy preferences.

Objectives

Test the hypothesis (H): Exposure to candidate-level VAA advice increases policy congruence between candidates and voters' preferences compared to party-level VAA advice. Determine whether the effect of candidate-level VAAs is driven by substantive policy differences or merely by visual display cues (candidate photos and names). Assess whether the issue-representation benefits of candidate-level VAAs justify their higher operational cost and complexity.

Scope

The research consists of a randomized field experiment among voters in the 2021 Welsh Senedd election, using the MyVoteChoice app, which covered 28 policy-issue statements.

Key Provisions / Findings

Correlation Between Party and Candidate Advice: The correlation between candidate rankings and party rankings was high overall (r = 0.90; r = 0.73 for candidates who took the survey). However, for 30% of voters, the top-ranked candidate differed from the top-ranked party. Increase in Propensity to Vote (PTV): Exposure to candidate-level VAA advice increased PTV for the top-matched candidate by +0.25 points on a 0–10 scale (p < 0.05) compared to party-level advice. Role of Substantive Policy Differences: Among candidates who completed the survey (whose positions diverged from party lines), the effect doubled to +0.50 points (p < 0.001). For non-participating candidates (whose positions defaulted to the party line), the visual display alone produced no significant effect. Increase in Vote Intention: Candidate-level VAA advice increased the probability of intending to vote for the top-matched candidate by +3 percentage points overall (from 60% to 63%, p < 0.05), and by +6 percentage points (p < 0.01) for candidates who participated in the survey. Candidate Survey Participation Rate: The Welsh election recorded a candidate participation rate of 28%, far below Switzerland's smartvote, which achieves 75–85% thanks to a longer institutional tradition and precedent.

Implementation

The methodology was implemented through linear regression models with candidate fixed effects, standard errors clustered at the user level, and demographic control testing. Replication data and code are stored openly on Harvard Dataverse at https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/47XXEN.

Policy Issues

The high logistical and labor costs of building candidate-level VAAs (contacting candidates, collecting photos/bios, district-specific programming). Low candidate response rates to pre-election policy surveys in countries lacking a strong VAA tradition.

Reform / Implications

The study provides experimental evidence that candidate-level VAAs deliver substantive added value in strengthening issue representation within candidate-based electoral democracies.
