International Journal for Court Administration (IJCA) is an international academic and professional publication published under the auspices of the International Association for Court Administration (IACA). The journal focuses on improving how court systems are governed, managed, and administered globally by disseminating peer-reviewed research, editorial commentaries, comparative case studies, and practical judicial reform strategies.

Overview

The International Journal for Court Administration provides an international platform for judges, court administrators, and legal researchers to publish research on judicial leadership, operational efficiency, legal reform, and the impact of economic downturns on court systems.

Legal Basis

Not found in the available sources. The journal operates as a professional academic publication rather than a statutory regulatory body.

Background

Published in the context of a global economic downturn, the journal addresses declining public revenues, operational retrenchment in court systems, and growing social and gender inequalities.

Objectives

The primary objective of the publication is to support IACA's vision of improving how court systems are governed, managed, and administered worldwide through the dissemination of best practices and comparative studies.

Scope & Key Issues

  • Judicial Leadership: The emergence of contemporary court administration leadership (Australia case study).
  • Managing Fiscal Scarcity: Judicial retrenchment and reform during periods of economic austerity.
  • Social Media Integration: The impact of social media in court proceedings ("Trial by Tweet") as an innovation or degradation.
  • Anti-Corruption: Implementation of European anti-corruption measures in Romania.
  • Judicial Decision-Making: Enhancing and optimizing decision-making processes within the judiciary.
  • Cost-Saving Measures: Implementation of 21 practical judicial cost-saving measures.
  • Paths of Reform: Systemic versus incremental pathways for court system modernization in Italy.
  • Social Justice & Gender Equity: Addressing gender disparities and the vulnerable status of women and adolescent girls during global economic crises.

Policy Analysis & Empirical Evidence

  • World Bank Research (2007): A 1% drop in economic productivity disproportionately increases infant mortality by 7.4 deaths per 1,000 girls compared to 1.5 deaths per 1,000 boys across 59 developing nations.
  • UNESCO Report (2012): Approximately 39 million girls aged 11–15 were out of school globally, demonstrating how economic pressures subordinate female education to domestic roles.
