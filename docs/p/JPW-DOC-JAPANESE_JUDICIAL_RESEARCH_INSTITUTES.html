Policy Analysis

Major Institutes & Government Agencies

  • Legal Training and Research Institute (Supreme Court of Japan): Responsible for training judges and legal apprentices, running specialized workshops, and conducting core research for judicial practice.
  • Training and Research Institute for Court Officials (Supreme Court of Japan): Trains court administrative staff, court clerks, and family court probation officers, while researching judicial procedures and administrative improvements.
  • Research and Training Institute (Ministry of Justice, Japan): Houses a Research Department that analyzes criminal justice policy, prosecution, trial operations, and offender rehabilitation.
  • Institute of Law and Social Sciences (Meiji University): Conducts empirical socio-legal research, including evidence-based studies on legal professions and the lay judge system (*saiban-in*).
  • Institute of Comparative Law (Waseda University): Focuses on comparative legal studies, public policy, litigation trends, and judicial systemic reforms.
  • Institute of Comparative Law in Japan (Chuo University): Runs 2024 research initiatives covering comparative criminal justice, crime prevention policies, offender reintegration, and civic voting rights.

Key Research Projects

  • Judicial Policymaking in Japan and the United States (KAKENHI #17K03320): Examines policy litigation, minority rights cases (such as same-sex marriage), and judicial roles in policymaking.
  • Comparative Socio-Legal Study on Judicial System Reform: From the Perspective of New Institutionalism (Hokkaido University, KAKENHI #19330001): Evaluates historical and comparative perspectives of judicial system reform in Japan.
  • Dynamic Analysis of the Administration of Judicial Review in Japan and the United States (Kyoto University, KAKENHI #23730006): Focuses on administrative mechanisms and longitudinal comparisons of judicial review execution.
  • Disputing Process and Judicial Policy in Contemporary Japan: Civil Justice Research Project 2016-2020 (University of Tokyo): A large-scale empirical study examining public dispute resolution and institutional court interactions.

Interpretations & Structural Inferences

  • Japan's judicial policy research infrastructure operates on a dual structure: internal state bodies (Supreme Court, Ministry of Justice) focus on practical, operational, and professional training needs, while academic university institutes (Meiji, Waseda, Hokkaido, Kyoto, UTokyo, Chuo) conduct empirical socio-legal and comparative research.
  • Focus on social issues—such as same-sex marriage rights, the lay judge system, and offender reintegration—demonstrates that judicial policymaking utilizes empirical findings from government-funded KAKENHI projects to adapt to public needs.
