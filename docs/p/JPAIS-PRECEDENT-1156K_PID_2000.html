Supreme Court Decision No. 1156 K/PID/2000 is an Indonesian criminal procedure precedent concerning the jurisdiction of Pretrial Proceedings (Praperadilan) over an arrest and detention carried out by a foreign police authority outside Indonesia in connection with extradition proceedings.

The decision addresses the territorial limits of Indonesian pretrial jurisdiction, the application of Articles 17, 18, 20, and 77 of the Indonesian Criminal Procedure Code (KUHAP), the Article 18 paragraph (3) arrest-warrant requirement, and cassation in a pretrial matter involving an international-law dimension.

Case Background

Hendra Rahardja, President Commissioner of Bank Harapan Sentosa (BHS), filed a pretrial application before the South Jakarta District Court under Registration No. 07/Pid/Prap/2000/PN.Jak.Sel.

The application concerned his arrest and detention by the Australian Federal Police (AFP) in Sydney on 1 June 1999 in connection with extradition proceedings. The arrest was associated with an Interpol Red Notice and an affidavit issued by Rod Wissam.

Hendra Rahardja argued that the arrest and detention were unlawful because he had not received a copy of the Indonesian arrest warrant contemplated by Article 18 paragraph (3) of KUHAP. He also sought compensation of IDR 100 million.

The South Jakarta District Court, in its decision dated 23 June 2000, granted the pretrial application. The court declared the arrest and detention unlawful, ordered release from detention, and ordered the Indonesian Police to pay compensation of IDR 1,000,000.

The Indonesian National Police subsequently filed a cassation petition with the Supreme Court.

Legal Issues

  1. Whether Indonesian pretrial proceedings have jurisdiction to examine an arrest and detention physically carried out by a foreign police authority outside Indonesian territory.
  2. Whether Articles 77, 17, 18, and 20 of KUHAP apply to an arrest conducted by the Australian Federal Police in Australia pursuant to extradition arrangements.
  3. Whether the obligation to provide a copy of an arrest warrant under Article 18 paragraph (3) KUHAP arises before or only after a physical arrest is carried out by Indonesian investigators.
  4. Whether a pretrial decision may be challenged through cassation where the proceeding involves an international-law dimension.

Supreme Court's Reasoning

The Supreme Court held that the South Jakarta District Court, as Judex Facti, had misapplied the law.

According to the Supreme Court, the pretrial jurisdiction established under Article 77 of KUHAP, together with Articles 17, 18, and 20, concerns the legality of arrests and detentions physically carried out by Indonesian investigators within Indonesian sovereign territory.

The Supreme Court distinguished such Indonesian enforcement action from an arrest carried out by the Australian Federal Police in Australia pursuant to extradition arrangements.

Accordingly, an Indonesian pretrial court does not have jurisdiction to review the legality of an arrest or detention physically carried out by the AFP in Australian territory.

Article 18 Paragraph (3) KUHAP

The Supreme Court also addressed the requirement concerning delivery of a copy of an arrest warrant under Article 18 paragraph (3) KUHAP.

The Court reasoned that the obligation to provide the relevant copy arises in connection with a physical arrest carried out by Indonesian police authorities.

Because the Indonesian Police had not physically arrested Hendra Rahardja at the relevant stage, the Court found that the statutory obligation under Article 18 paragraph (3) had not yet been triggered.

Holding

The Supreme Court:

  1. Granted the cassation petition submitted by the Indonesian National Police cq. National Police Economic Directorate Detective Corps.
  2. Annulled the South Jakarta District Court decision dated 23 June 2000 No. 07/Pid/Prap/2000/PN.Jak.Sel.
  3. Adjudicated the matter itself.
  4. Rejected Hendra Rahardja's pretrial application in its entirety.
  5. Ordered the Cassation Respondent/Pretrial Petitioner to pay cassation-level court costs amounting to IDR 2,500.

Ratio Decidendi

The central ratio decidendi may be summarized as follows:

Indonesian pretrial jurisdiction over the legality of arrest and detention under KUHAP is territorially connected to physical enforcement action by Indonesian investigators within Indonesian territory.

An arrest physically conducted by a foreign police authority outside Indonesia in the context of extradition proceedings is not, by itself, an arrest subject to review under Indonesian pretrial jurisdiction.

The procedural obligation under Article 18 paragraph (3) KUHAP concerning delivery of an arrest warrant is likewise connected to a physical arrest carried out by Indonesian police authorities. Where such physical arrest has not yet occurred, the obligation has not yet arisen.
