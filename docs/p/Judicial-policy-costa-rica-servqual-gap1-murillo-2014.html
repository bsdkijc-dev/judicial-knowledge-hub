The Costa Rican Judicial SERVQUAL GAP 1 Evaluation and GICA-Justicia Service Quality Policy Framework is a judicial service quality evaluation and perception gap analysis (*GAP 1 SERVQUAL*) policy framework comparing the expectations of the public (*judiciary users*) with the understanding/perceptions of court employees and judges (*judicial servants*). Formulated by Rodrigo Murillo (Bradford School of Management & GICA-Justicia Project Consultant, 2014) in the *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA), it is based on a national survey of 1,200 (net sample 873) respondents across 15 jurisdictions in Costa Rica, participant observation, and semi-structured interviews at the Second Court of Appeal (*Sala Segunda*) of Costa Rica under the GICA-Justicia Quality Management Standard (*Poder Judicial de Costa Rica*) [1–2, 10–12, 21–24, 30–36].

Summary

Published by Rodrigo Murillo in the *International Journal for Court Administration* (IJCA), this policy framework evaluates the quality of judicial services in Costa Rica using the *SERVQUAL GAP 1* approach. The project is part of the implementation of the *GICA-Justicia* Quality Management Standard (an ISO9001-like accredited court office standard) supported by the European Union's EUROSOCIAL-Justice Program. Based on a national quota survey of 873 judicial user respondents and a qualitative study at the Second Court of Appeal (*Sala Segunda*), Murillo uncovered a massive gap between public expectations—which demand speed and service certainty—and the mindset of judicial staff [23–24, 33–36]. Interviews using a *Grounded Theory* approach revealed that the doctrine of "Judicial Independence" is routinely misused by judges and staff to justify inefficiencies and service delays [33–36, 87–94].

Legal Basis

  • Ley Orgánica del Poder Judicial de Costa Rica (Organic Law of the Judicial Power of Costa Rica): The primary organic law regulating the governance of the Costa Rican judiciary, the authority of Supreme Court Justices (*Magistrates*), and the administrative functions of the Courts of Appeal [14–20].
  • Norma GICA-Justicia 2010: The ISO9001-like Organizational Accreditation Standard for Costa Rican Court Offices (*Despachos Judiciales*).
  • Costa Rican Constitution of 1949: The constitutional foundation for judicial independence and the division of court jurisdictions.
  • Costa Rican Labor Code: Regulations providing employment protection for civil servants and judicial employees.
  • Not found in the available sources: Other specific formal statutory regulations are not mentioned in the source text [FACT].

Background

Judicial institutions in Latin America are frequently criticized for high costs, complex procedures, and slowness. The development of the GICA-Justicia Standard aimed to provide a systemic solution for judicial management in Costa Rica.

Objectives

1. Empirically measure the service quality expectations of judiciary users in Costa Rica using the 5 dimensions of SERVQUAL [11–12, 26–29]. 2. Analyze the *GAP 1* perception gap between judicial users and judicial servants. 3. Uncover the roots of the work culture that misuses the principle of judicial independence to justify inefficiency [33–36, 87–94]. 4. Provide objective data (*hard data*) to compel court leadership and staff to shift their work culture toward service quality orientation [4, 44–46].

Scope

This policy encompasses a national evaluation across 15 judicial jurisdictions in Costa Rica and a qualitative case study at the Second Court of Appeal (*Sala Segunda*) of Costa Rica [21–23].

Key Provisions

  • Key Findings on Judiciary User Expectations (Average 6.20 / 7.00):
    • The Top 10 user expectations are dominated by the *Assurance* and *Empathy* dimensions: QP.18 individualized attention (6.37), QP.3 neat/pulcra employee appearance (6.35), QP.19 convenient operating hours (6.31), QP.14 behavior conveying trust (6.29), and QP.17 adequate employee knowledge (6.29) [27–29].
  • The Phenomenon of Judicial Independence Misconception:
    • *Grounded Theory* coding results indicate that employees and judges use the pretext of "mind freedom" and "judicial independence" to reject case resolution quotas or timeframes, thereby justifying delays in hearings and the drafting of rulings [33–36, 87–94].
  • GAP 1 Inconsistencies:
    • Court staff fail to prioritize *Speed* and *Responsiveness*, even though these dimensions represent the primary sources of public dissatisfaction.

Implementation

  • National SERVQUAL Quota Survey: Administered by the *Contraloría de Servicios* across 15 jurisdictions yielding 873 clean samples [22–24].
  • Mixed-Method Case Study: Combining 2 years of participant observation at the *Sala Segunda* with 12 semi-structured interviews [21, 25, 30–33].

Policy Problems

  • Employee Resistance: Staff fears that service quality evaluations will be weaponized for *downsizing* rather than utilized for quality improvement [42–43].
  • Absence of Time Targets: A lack of standardized case handling time targets caused by the confusion and conflation of judicial and administrative functions [31, 33–36].

Reform / Change

  • Utilizing SERVQUAL *hard data* in employee training and coaching programs to shift orientation toward user satisfaction.
  • Firmly separating the material freedom of legal reasoning (adjudicative freedom) from the compliance with administrative time management standards [33–36, 92–94].
  • Plans to apply *Lean Service* principles within the judiciary.

3 Most Important Improvements

1. Formulation of Guidelines Separating Administrative and Adjudicative Functions: Issue official rules that explicitly distinguish the material freedom of a judge's reasoning (adjudicative freedom) from the obligation to comply with service administrative time limits (administrative time standards), ensuring that the pretext of judicial independence can no longer be misused to justify delays [33–36, 92–94]. 2. Integration of Quantitative SERVQUAL Data in Employee Training: Utilize empirical *hard data* concerning the GAP 1 disconnect in coaching modules and work-culture change workshops to dispel downsizing fears and cultivate a genuine service-quality orientation [44–45]. 3. Application of Lean Service Principles in the Judicial Management Chain: Adopt the Lean Service methodology to identify and eliminate non-value-adding bureaucratic steps (waste) within the Costa Rican judicial service process chain.
