Policy Analysis

  • Background & Core Facts:
    • FACT: The 2022 White Paper on Crime provides a comprehensive statistical analysis of Japanese crime trends and offender treatment through late July 2022 (covering FY 2021 statistical data). Data is drawn from the National Police Agency, Public Prosecutors Office, Supreme Court, Correction Bureau, and Rehabilitation Bureau.
    • Crime Trends (2021): Reported Penal Code offenses fell to 568,104 (post-WWII low) with a 46.6% clearance rate. Theft accounted for nearly 70% of all Penal Code offenses.
    • Recidivism: The rate of repeat offenders among total cleared Penal Code offenders reached 48.6%.
    • Special Feature 1 (COVID-19 Impact - Part 7): Analyzed correlations between reduced public mobility during COVID-19 and drops in general crime, while detailing infection management across correctional institutions.
    • Special Feature 2 (Offender Survey - Part 8): The 5th survey (Jan 2021, N=1,419) showed 75.3% of offenders believed the primary cause of their crime resided within themselves (85.2% among age 65+).
    • Key Legislative Reforms Analyzed:
      • Juvenile Act Revision (Act No. 47 of 2021): Enacted April 2022; established "Specified Juveniles" (特定少年, ages 18–19) and Class 5 Juvenile Training Schools.
      • Sexual Offense Revision (Act No. 72 of 2017): Expanded rape definitions, raised minimum sentences, created custodian offenses, and made sexual crimes non-complaint offenses.
      • Medical Care Act (Act No. 110 of 2003): Establishes district court panels (judge and psychiatrist) to determine inpatient/outpatient treatment for mentally ill offenders.
      • Victim Participation System: 1,523 victims participated in first-instance trials in 2021 (356 in Saiban-in trials).
  • Interpretations & Structural Inferences:
    • INTERPRETATION: Reflects Japan's shift toward evidence-based criminological analysis, emergency health adaptability, and juvenile law alignment with national legal adulthood changes.
    • INFERENCE: Post-WWII crime lows were driven by COVID-19 mobility constraints, but high recidivism rates (48.6%) confirm the urgency of individualized Risk-Need-Responsivity (RNR) and Case Formulation in Probation/Parole (CFP) strategies.
