The Kenosha County Felony Caseflow Management Workshop & Reform Policy Framework is a policy framework and one-day stakeholder workshop methodology developed by Kerry M. Connelly (District Court Administrator for Kenosha County, Wisconsin). Based on the theoretical framework of Barry Mahoney (Justice Management Institute / IACA 2008 Dublin), it convenes all stakeholders in the criminal justice system (judges, court commissioners, district attorneys, public defenders, clerk staff, victim-witness advocates, probation agents, police, and sheriff's deputies) to diagnose obstacles causing felony case delay and to establish five low-cost/no-cost caseflow reform recommendations [1–6, 14–15].

Summary

Published by Kerry M. Connelly in the International Journal for Court Administration (IJCA), this policy framework documents the success of the Kenosha County Circuit Court (Wisconsin) in addressing a surge in its felony backlog. Between 2001 and 2008, the number of pending felony cases skyrocketed from 344 to 730, with the median age of pending cases increasing from 88 to 119 days [2–3]. Through a 7-hour collaborative workshop involving 24 participants across various law enforcement and judicial agencies, stakeholders successfully diagnosed the root causes of delays and agreed upon five cost-effective caseflow improvement recommendations that could be implemented immediately [4–6, 14–15].

Legal Basis

  • Wisconsin Felony Case Processing Benchmarks: Case processing standards set by the Wisconsin Supreme Court, stipulating that 90% of all felony cases must be disposed of within 180 days (6 months) of filing.
  • Wisconsin Criminal Statutes: Legal provisions defining a felony as a crime punishable by imprisonment in the state prisons for one year or more.

Background

The resolution of felony cases in Kenosha County experienced severe delays due to an increasing volume of new cases (1,361 cases in 2008) and an increased number of jury trials [2–3]. Inspired by Barry Mahoney's presentation at the 2008 IACA Dublin Conference, the District Court Administrator designed an interactive workshop to bring together all judicial elements to find collaborative solutions.

Objectives

1. Cut the felony case backlog to achieve the Wisconsin Supreme Court's 180-day benchmark. 2. Diagnose specific and systemic factors causing caseflow delays from the perspectives of four distinct stakeholder groups [5, 9–13]. 3. Mainstream the use of the 5 CCAP (Consolidated Court Automation Programs) Case Management Reports [6–8]. 4. Enact 5 caseflow reform recommendations based on mutual stakeholder agreement [14–15].

Scope

This policy applies to the governance of felony cases at the Kenosha County Circuit Court (Wisconsin), involving 7 judges, 3 court commissioners, the district attorney's office, the public defender's office, police departments, the sheriff's department, probation agents, and clerk of court staff.

Key Provisions

  • Utilization of 5 Case Management Reports (Murphy Model):
  1. Caseload Summary Report: Summary of cases opened, disposed, and pending.
  2. Age of Pending Cases Report: Report on the age of pending cases and median values.
  3. Age at Disposition Summary: Report on the age of cases at the time of disposition.
  4. Disposition Summary: Breakdown of disposition methods (jury trial, plea, settlement, dismissal).
  5. Event and Activity Report: Record of the time intervals between a judge's daily trial activities [7–8].
  • Five Caseflow Reform Recommendations:
  1. Early Attorney Communication: The District Attorney's office provides dedicated time one week prior to the Judicial Pretrial for Public Defenders to discuss case settlements.
  2. Cut PSI Reports: Eliminating the requirement for Pre-Sentence Investigation (PSI) reports for low-level felonies and utilizing older PSI reports when available [14–15].
  3. Initial Appearance Scheduling: Strict division of initial appearance times (1:00 PM for in-custody felonies; 1:30 PM for in-custody misdemeanors; 2:00 PM for out-of-custody cases).
  4. Routine Case Clean-up: Periodic review of felony case files by judges and clerks to clear out residual cases.
  5. Video Conferencing Application: Utilizing video conferencing for initial appearances and non-evidentiary motion hearings.

Implementation

  • Pre-Workshop Survey: Distribution of an obstacle-ranking questionnaire (scale 1–10) prior to the workshop, identifying unprepared attorneys, unrepresented defendants, and transportation constraints as primary issues.
  • No-Cost Execution: The execution of four out of the five recommendations without requiring any new budget allocations.

Policy Problems

  • Projected Case Backlog for 2018: Linear trend analysis indicated the potential for a severe explosion in case backlogs if workflows were not altered.
  • Delayed PSI Reports: The creation of PSI reports by the Department of Corrections, taking 6–8 weeks, became a major trigger for sentencing delays.

Reform / Change

  • Adoption of a structured communication mechanism between prosecutors and public defenders prior to pretrial hearings.
  • Periodic oversight of the recommendations' execution by the Chief Judge and the Court Administrator.
