The Judges' Managerial Competencies and Judicial Performance Framework in Brazil is a competency-based judicial administration policy framework that evaluates the dual role of judges (adjudication and administration) and measures the influence of judges' managerial competencies on judicial efficiency and performance [2–5, 8–10].

Summary

Published in the International Journal for Court Administration (2025) by Borba, Lunardi, and Guimaraes, this framework analyzes the influence of first-instance judges' managerial competencies on the performance of judicial units at the Court of Justice of the State of Minas Gerais (TJMG), Brazil. Using an integrative competency approach, a statistical study of 60 judges and 199 court clerks proved that the application of Outcomes-based Management and Judicial Process Management competencies by judges significantly reduces the court congestion rate [24–25, 42–43, 72, 75, 79].

Legal Basis

  • Brazilian Federal Constitution 1988 (Article 99, Paragraph 1): Guarantees administrative and financial autonomy for State Courts of Justice.
  • CNJ Resolution No. 240/2016 (National Council of Justice / CNJ): Regulates the implementation of competency-based management across all judicial branch units in Brazil.
  • CNJ Resolution No. 219/2016: Regulates the proportional allocation and distribution of personnel and commissioned positions in courts.
  • Federal Law No. 11.416/2006: The career law for civil servants of the Union's judicial branch.
  • TJMG Resolution No. 819/2016: Establishes the Matrix Management System and the formula for calculating the congestion rate.
  • TJMG Resolution No. 953/2020: Standards for the career development of judicial employees in Minas Gerais.

Background

Under the influence of New Public Management, the Brazilian judiciary, which comprises 92 courts and 15,646 judicial units, is demanded to increase efficiency and accountability. First-instance judges bear a dual role: acting as adjudicators while simultaneously serving as operational managers of judicial units overseeing staff, case workflows, and CNJ targets [5, 8–10, 33]. However, this managerial role frequently causes role conflict and role ambiguity due to a lack of structured managerial training [9, 26–27].

Objectives

1. Identify the relevant managerial competency indicators for first-instance judges. 2. Analyze the differences in competency perceptions between judges (self-assessment) and court clerks [3, 39, 44–45]. 3. Statistically prove the correlation between judges' managerial competencies and the backlog index and congestion rate [3, 68–70, 71–75]. 4. Provide a policy reference for the CNJ and ENFAM in designing managerial training curricula for judges.

Scope

Empirical research was conducted in 176 general jurisdiction courts under the Court of Justice of the State of Minas Gerais (TJMG)—the second-largest state court in Brazil. The sample included 60 judges and 199 court clerks [42–43].

Key Provisions

  • Integrative Competency Approach: Combines the French tradition (the ability to mobilize internal/environmental resources) and the American tradition (knowledge, skills, and attitudes), assessed contextually [16, 21, 24–25, 77].
  • Judges' Managerial Competency Factors (Judges' Perspective - 4 Factors, Variance 62.81%) [45–46]:
  1. Resolvability: The ability to resolve cases quickly through conciliation agreements and accelerating public services.
  2. Outcomes-based Management: Monitoring delegated tasks, setting targets, holding evaluation meetings, and coordinating with Prosecutors/Lawyers.
  3. Communication: Encouraging team dialogue, providing feedback, and communicating targets.
  4. Judicial Process Management: Making decisions directly during hearings and issuing written instructions for clerks.
  • Judges' Managerial Competency Factors (Clerks' Perspective - 3 Factors, Variance 76.37%):
  1. Leadership and Team Management: Encouraging transformational motivation, humanized leadership, and team unification [57–58].
  2. Judicial Process Management: Supervising case workflows and eliminating procedural bottlenecks.
  3. Performance Monitoring: Organizing regular meetings to monitor unit performance.

Implementation

  • Congestion Rate (CR) Formula: Evaluated using the formula $CR = \frac{A}{B + A}$, where $A$ is the remaining active cases at year-end and $B$ represents the cases resolved/cleared within 12 months [68–69].
  • Linear Regression Evidence: Regression results proved that higher implementation of Outcomes-based Management by judges corresponds to a lower court congestion rate ($\beta = -0.46, p = 0.006$) [72–74, 79]. Similarly, the clerks' perception of Judicial Process Management significantly correlated with a reduction in the congestion rate ($\beta = -0.32, p = 0.010$).

Policy Problems

  • Resource Limitations & Recognition: Judges complain about the lack of administrative staff and the absence of moral recognition (emotional payoff) from top-level court leadership [54–55, 80].
  • Ignoring Targets & Senior Clerks: Clerks reported instances where judges ignored planning meetings or sidelined the expertise of senior clerks.

Reform / Change

  • Drafting ENFAM/CNJ Training Curricula: Incorporating modules on outcomes-based management, team leadership, and communication into the initial and ongoing training for judges.
  • Development of Humanized Management: Encouraging the CNJ to adopt evaluation indicators that encompass humanization, accessibility, and occupational health criteria (similar to Portugal's Programa Justiça Mais Próxima).
