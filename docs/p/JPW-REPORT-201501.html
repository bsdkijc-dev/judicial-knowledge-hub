4. Policy Analysis

Background
Law-related youth education requires adapting complex legal doctrines and court procedures to match student cognitive growth and national educational curricula. 8

Policy Problem
Traditional legal terminology creates abstract barriers for students, often fostering misperceptions of the judiciary as purely punitive or authoritative rather than protective of rights. 9

Legal/Policy Gap
National textbooks introduce legal concepts fragmentarily across social studies and ethics subjects without a unified, practical instructional guide for judges conducting lectures. 10

Objectives
Provide judges with standardized, level-specific lecture modules, slide layouts, time-management protocols, and pedagogical techniques tailored to primary and secondary school levels. 11

Implementation
Formulated structured lecture plans — 43 minutes for elementary (4th–6th grade), 50 minutes for middle school, and 65 minutes for high school — with full and condensed options. 12

Evidence
Empirical alignment with national elementary, middle, and high school textbook curricula (e.g., "Law and Politics") alongside judicial education objective frameworks. 13

Impact
Improves public confidence in court administration, instills democratic citizenship values, and aids student career understanding regarding judicial roles. 14

Reform
Recommends flexible module selection (full vs. condensed, civil vs. criminal tracks), pre-lecture teacher consultations, and interactive teaching techniques (ice-breaking, quizzes, reading aloud). 15

6. MediaWiki Output

Research on Different Level Lecture Programs Conducted by Judges for Elementary, Middle, and High School Students (Appendix) is an official, practical guide published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) to help judges deliver tiered legal lectures to elementary, middle, and high school students.

Summary

This document is an official, practical guide published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) to help judges deliver tiered legal lectures to elementary, middle, and high school students.

Legal Basis

The development of this educational module is grounded in:

  • Act on Support for Law-Related Education: Article 2.
  • Constitution of the Republic of Korea: Provisions on the right to trial, separation of powers, judicial independence, and human rights.
  • School Violence Prevention and Countermeasures Act: Articles 2 and 20.

Background

Many students perceive law and the courts as complicated, intimidating, or rigid. This manual was designed to address that barrier through a friendly, contextualized educational approach.

Objectives

  1. Help young people understand the judicial system and rational methods of dispute resolution.
  2. Strengthen public trust in judicial institutions.
  3. Provide judges with practical guidance for delivering material appropriate to students' developmental level.

Scope

The lecture modules are divided into three tiers:

  • Elementary level (Grades 4–6): Focuses on basic legal concepts as shared rules, symbols of justice (the Goddess of Justice and Haetae), human rights, and internet ethics.
  • Middle school level: Focuses on legal classification, types of courts, court structure, and the right to trial.
  • High school level: Focuses on in-depth understanding of civil and criminal procedure, constitutional rights guarantees, and real case analysis.

Key Provisions

  • Standard time allocations: Elementary (43 minutes), Middle school (50 minutes), High school (65 minutes).
  • Alignment of material with the national curriculum (e.g., the "Law and Politics" subject in high school).
  • Provision of sample complaint documents, response letters, and copies of court judgments for case studies.

Implementation

Judges may use this module either when visiting schools directly or when receiving student visits to the courts. Interactive teaching methods such as quizzes, animated screenings, and shared reading exercises are recommended to maintain student concentration.

Policy Issues

Variation in comprehension levels among students, combined with limited classroom time, requires judges to exercise flexibility in trimming or selecting lecture topics.

Reform / Amendments

The manual provides condensed module variants as well as specialized civil-track or criminal-track options, allowing instruction to be adapted to field conditions.
