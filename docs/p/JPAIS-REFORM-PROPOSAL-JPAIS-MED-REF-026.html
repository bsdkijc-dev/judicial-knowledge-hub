Description / Deskripsi

Provides a global benchmark report analyzing comparative mock trial and judicial simulation methodologies across institutions such as NITA, UNODC, ICC, ICJ, and the Singapore Judicial College[cite: 8]. It categorizes simulation designs into operational archetypes (systemic chain-of-justice simulations, adversarial micro-simulations, and appellate moots), evaluates adult learning frameworks including the Hampel Method and NITA experiential cycles, and details competency assessment rubrics[cite: 8].

Problem / Masalah

  • JPAIS-MED-PROB-022: Traditional legal education relied heavily on anecdotal, lecture-based pedagogy rather than structured experiential learning, leaving practitioners unprepared for complex courtroom dynamics[cite: 8].
  • JPAIS-MED-PROB-023: High-level courtroom advocacy is rendered ineffective if the underlying multi-agency investigative chain, intelligence gathering, or evidence handling is compromised[cite: 8].
  • JPAIS-MED-PROB-024: Institutional divergences between active "hot bench" moot interrogations and formal judicial restraint complicate the realistic training of judges and advocates[cite: 8].

Current Indonesian Practice / Praktik Indonesia Saat Ini

  • JPAIS-MED-ID-026: Indonesian judicial and prosecutorial training incorporates case-based discussions and simulation modules to bridge investigative protocols with courtroom adjudication[cite: 8].

Japanese & Comparative Practice / Praktik Perbandingan

  • JPAIS-MED-JP-026: Leading institutions like the Singapore Judicial College utilize continuous career-long developmental tracks and 360-degree peer feedback to cultivate the "Learning Judge"[cite: 8].

Gap / Kesenjangan

  • JPAIS-MED-GAP-009: Isolated trial advocacy training frequently fails to address systemic inter-agency operational gaps across the criminal justice chain[cite: 8].

Proposed Adaptation / Adaptasi Usulan

Adopt a diversified simulation architecture that aligns scenario scope with operational goals—utilizing NITA micro-performances for advocate mechanics, Hampel Method instruction for international tribunals, and UNODC multi-agency simulations for systemic chain-of-justice stress-testing[cite: 8].

Action / Aksi

  • JPAIS-MED-ACT-022: Implement the NITA four-component critique formula (Headline, Playback, Fix, Rationale) during practical advocacy training[cite: 8].
  • JPAIS-MED-ACT-023: Structure multi-agency operational simulations spanning the entire legal continuum from maritime interdiction to judicial trial[cite: 8].
  • JPAIS-MED-ACT-024: Integrate structured judicial competency frameworks and peer review mechanisms into ongoing professional development for judges[cite: 8].

Pilot / Pilot Proyek

  • JPAIS-MED-PILOT-026: 180-day institutional simulation pilot combining UNODC chain-of-justice maritime scenarios with Singapore Judicial College peer review methodologies[cite: 8].

KPI / Indikator Kinerja Utama

  • JPAIS-MED-KPI-026: 100% participant engagement in active performance-critique loops; measurable improvement in trial advocacy mechanics and inter-agency coordination efficiency[cite: 8].

Policy Implication / Implikasi Kebijakan

Shift professional legal and judicial education toward experiential, competency-based learning models that integrate systemic criminal justice operations[cite: 8].

Regulatory Implication / Implikasi Regulasi

Formalization of structured accreditation standards and continuous professional development requirements across judicial colleges and bar associations[cite: 8].

Evidence / Bukti Empiris

  • Plea bargaining and trial execution frameworks benefit significantly from simulation training that breaks complex litigation into manageable micro-performance snapshots[cite: 8].
  • UNODC mock trials successfully eliminate procedural distractions by restricting preliminary objections, forcing practitioners to engage with substantive evidentiary burdens[cite: 8].
