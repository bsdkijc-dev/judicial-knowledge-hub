Definition

An official judicial policy research publication issued by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of the Supreme Court of Korea that analyzes the constitutional and international human rights foundations of children's right to birth registration, evaluates the newly implemented birth notification (출생통보제) and protected childbirth (보호출산제) systems, conducts comparative legal analysis across Germany, France, and Japan, and formulates comprehensive statutory amendments to the Civil Act, the Act on Registration of Family Relations, and a standalone Foreign Child Birth Registration Act.

4. Policy Analysis

Background
Human existence as a subject of rights begins at birth. However, to exercise rights, receive public healthcare, enroll in school, or receive state protection, a child's existence must be officially recorded on public registers. Unregistered children exist as "shadow children" (유령아동 / 그림자 아이), vulnerable to illegal online adoption, child trafficking, abuse, and abandonment. Audit Board inspections in 2023 revealed over 6,000 unregistered children born between 2015 and 2022 (2,154 domestic, 4,025 foreign) who existed only as temporary newborn screening numbers (임시신생아번호).

Policy Problem
Passive Parent-Dependent Reporting: The traditional birth reporting system depended entirely on voluntary reports by parents. If parents failed or refused to report, children remained unrecorded.

Strict Presumption of Paternity & Rigid Paternity Rules: Under Civil Act Article 844, a child born within 300 days of divorce is legally presumed to be the former husband's child, even when biologically belonging to another partner. This created severe barriers for mothers and biological fathers (생부), preventing unwed fathers from registering their biological children without complex litigation.

Exclusion of Foreign Children: Korea's Family Relation Register is strictly limited to Korean nationals (Article 1). Foreign children born in Korea have no public birth register, and undocumented foreign parents avoid embassy reports due to fear of deportation under immigration reporting duties (출입국관리법 통보의무).

Legal/Policy Gap
Supreme Court Order 2020So575 (June 8, 2020): Established for the first time that a child's right to birth registration is a constitutional fundamental right derived from Article 10 (human dignity and general personality rights).

Constitutional Court Decision 2021Hun-Ma975 (March 23, 2023): Declared that failing to provide effective birth registration mechanisms for unwed fathers and children born to married women by non-spousal partners violates the constitutional right to birth registration.

Enactment of Birth Notification & Protected Childbirth: On June 30, 2023, the National Assembly passed the Birth Notification System (출생통보제, Family Relation Registration Act Arts. 44-3 & 44-4), requiring medical institutions to report birth info to HIRA (심평원), which notifies local heads to record births ex-officio (직권기록) if parents do not report within 1 month. Concurrently, the Protected Childbirth System (보호출산제) was enacted to allow crisis mothers to give birth anonymously. Both took effect on July 19, 2024.

Objectives
Move beyond mere "unregistered child" prevention toward establishing a comprehensive "Right to Birth Registration as a Foundation for Growth." Eliminate discrimination between children born in and out of wedlock, empower biological fathers, protect foreign children as a human right, and modernize administrative forms.

Implementation & Proposed Statutory Reforms
Civil Act Amendments:

  1. Article 844 (Presumption of Paternity): Restrict presumption of paternity to children born during marriage. For marriage termination by death, maintain presumption within 300 days. If remarriage occurs within 300 days of death, presume paternity of the later husband.
  2. Articles 846, 847, 848, 850, 851, 852 (Denial of Paternity): Grant independent rights to deny paternity (친생부인권) to adult children and biological fathers (생부), subject to judicial evaluation of biological ties, raising history, and child welfare.
  3. Articles 854-2 & 855-2 (Permissions for Denial/Recognition): Delete redundant permission procedures (친생부인의 허가 / 인지의 허가) made obsolete by the new paternity presumption model.

Act on Registration of Family Relations Amendments:

  1. Article 44(2) (Registration Items): Eliminate mandatory recording of origin of surname (본) for parents; restrict place of registration (등록기준지) to cases where identification is impossible by resident ID number alone; replace wedlock distinction with statutory presumption check.
  2. Article 46 & 46-2 (Reporting Parties & Unwed Fathers): Ease wedlock distinctions; explicitly recognize biological fathers' right to file birth reports with effect of recognition (인지 효력 있는 출생신고) in the main reporting section.
  3. Article 46-3 (Prosecutorial/Local Head Reporting): Convert prosecutor/local government head reporting from an optional right (적격자) into a mandatory duty (의무자) when a child's welfare is endangered.
  4. Articles 121 & 122 (Fines): Increase late reporting fines from 50,000 / 100,000 KRW to 100,000 / 200,000 KRW; exempt fines when parent-child litigation is pending.

Foreign Child Birth Registration Act (Standalone Legislation): Enact a standalone law using event-based registration (사건별 편제방식) to register all foreign children born in Korea, issuing official birth certificates (외국인 아동 출생등록부) regardless of parental residence status, with explicit exemption from immigration reporting duties (불법체류자 발견 통보의무 면제).

Evidence & Empirical Data
Public & Official Surveys: Nationwide survey of 787 citizens (252 paper, 535 online) and 3,189 family register public officials showed 76.6% supporting abolition of in/out-of-wedlock terms. 81.6% of officials and 67% of paper reporters favored maintaining the 1-month reporting deadline. 49.52% supported establishing a foreign child birth registration system, citing universal human rights (76.33%).

Divorce & Paternity Statistics: 87.1% of children born within 300 days of marriage termination (and 91.3% in divorce cases) were proven NOT to be the biological offspring of the former husband through court permission grants (2,131 cases between 2019 and 2023).

6. MediaWiki Output

Strategies for Effectively Guarantee Children's Right to Birth Registration is an official judicial policy research report published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of South Korea's Supreme Court.

Summary

This document is an official judicial policy research report published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of South Korea's Supreme Court. It analyzes the constitutional right of a child to birth registration (아동의 출생등록될 권리) as a fundamental human right, evaluates the Birth Notification System (출생통보제) and Protected Childbirth System (보호출산제), both effective July 19, 2024, and formulates amendments to the Civil Act, the Act on Registration of Family Relations, and a proposed Foreign Child Birth Registration Act.

Legal Basis

The legal frameworks analyzed include:

  • Constitution of the Republic of Korea: Article 10 (Human Dignity & General Right of Personality) and Article 37(2).
  • Act on Registration of Family Relations (가족관계의 등록 등에 관한 법률): Articles 1, 9, 10, 14, 15, 44, 44-3 (Birth Notification), 44-4 (Ex-Officio Registration), 46, 48, 57, 121, 122.
  • Civil Act of the Republic of Korea (민법): Articles 3, 781 (Child's Surname & Origin), 844 (Presumption of Paternity), 846, 847 (Action for Denial of Paternity), 854-2, 855-2, 859, 865.
  • Special Act on Protected Childbirth (위기임신보호출산법): Articles 1, 2, 9, 11, 12, 14, 15, 17.
  • International Conventions: UN Convention on the Rights of the Child (CRC Articles 2, 3, 6, 7, 8, 9, 13, 19, 24) and the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (ICCPR Article 24).

Background

A 2023 audit by the Board of Audit and Inspection found over 6,000 unregistered children (유령아동 / unregistered children) who existed only as temporary newborn screening numbers (임시신생아번호). Cases of exploitation, abuse, and infant death led to Supreme Court Order 2020So575 and Constitutional Court Decision 2021Hun-Ma975, which established birth registration as an absolute constitutional right.

Objectives

  1. Ensure immediate birth registration for every child born in South Korea, regardless of the parents' marital status or nationality.
  2. Reform the presumption of paternity (친생추정) rules so that a child born after divorce is not automatically attributed to the former husband.
  3. Provide legal certainty and birth registration rights for children of foreign nationals.

Scope

  • Empirical Study: A survey of 787 citizens and 3,189 family register officials, along with analysis of 2,131 paternity denial/recognition permission cases (2019–2023).
  • Three-Country Comparative Study: Germany (Vertrauliche Geburt & BGB § 1592/1600), France (Accouchement sous x & Code civil Art. 326/333), and Japan (Family Register Act & 2024 Civil Code Article 772 amendments).

Key Provisions

  • Birth Notification System (출생통보제): Hospitals are required to send birth information to the Health Insurance Review and Assessment Service (HIRA) within 14 days, and local government heads perform ex-officio registration if parents fail to report within one month.
  • Protected Childbirth System (보호출산제): Mothers in crisis may give birth anonymously after undergoing staged counseling.
  • Simplified Forms: Removes the requirement to record the parents' clan origin (본) and restricts the place of registration (등록기준지).

Implementation

Courts and local governments expand ex-officio verification and require social welfare or police agencies that discover unregistered children to promptly process birth registration reports through a prosecutor or local government head.

Policy Issues

  • 87.1% of children born within 300 days of divorce were proven not to be the biological child of the former husband.
  • Undocumented foreign children risk deportation if their parents report to immigration authorities.

Reform / Amendments

  1. Amendment of Civil Act Article 844 (restricting the presumption of paternity to births occurring during marriage).
  2. Granting the right to deny paternity (친생부인권) to adult children and biological fathers (생부).
  3. Enactment of a Foreign Child Birth Registration Act (외국인 아동의 출생등록에 관한 법률) with exemption from immigration reporting obligations (통보의무 면제).
