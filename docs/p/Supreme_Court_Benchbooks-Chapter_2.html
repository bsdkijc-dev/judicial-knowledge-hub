Core Definition

Chapter 2 of the Supreme Court Benchbook establishes the comprehensive procedural, technical, and evidentiary guidelines for managing and adjudicating civil disputes across Indonesian courts of general and religious jurisdiction.

  • Simple Definition: The official technical instruction manual for judges on how to process a civil lawsuit from electronic filing, pre-trial mediation, hearings, and evidentiary appraisal to final judgment and execution.
  • Legal / Technical Definition: The authoritative institutional restatement and harmonization of classical statutory civil procedural codes (HIR/RBg) with contemporary Supreme Court Regulations (PERMA) and Chamber Plenary policies, establishing standard operating procedures for claim admissibility, provisional measures, party autonomy, proof standards, and judicial execution.
  • Plain-Language Explanation: It provides civil court judges with step-by-step guidance on running civil lawsuits fairly, efficiently, and without arbitrary delays.

Why It Matters

  • Harmonizes Fragmented Civil Codes: Bridges historical dualities (HIR for Java/Madura and RBg for outer islands) with modern electronic litigation rules.
  • Standardizes Case Management: Establishes strict procedural calendars, preventing trial stagnation and unnecessary continuances.
  • Promotes Consensual Dispute Resolution: Integrates mandatory court-annexed mediation to encourage amicable settlements and alleviate appellate caseloads.
  • Secures Legal Certainty in Commercial & Civil Transactions: Provides predictable thresholds for burden of proof, default judgments (verstek), summary proceedings, and execution of court orders.

Key Principles

Principle of Party Autonomy and Judge's Inquisitorial Oversight in Procedure (Litis Finiri Oportet & Dominus Litis)

  • Meaning: The parties determine the scope of the dispute (civil claim limits), but the judge actively manages the proceeding calendar and procedural decorum.
  • Why It Matters: Prevents procedural abuse while respecting substantive party choices.
  • Legal Basis / Source: HIR Art. 130; RBg Art. 154; Law No. 48/2009 Art. 4(2).
  • Related Concepts: Active case management, judicial calendar.

Principle of Audi Et Alteram Partem (Asas Mendengar Kedua Belah Pihak)

  • Meaning: The court must give equal opportunity to both plaintiff and defendant to submit claims, defenses, evidence, and arguments.
  • Why It Matters: Essential guarantee of natural justice and fair hearing in private litigation.
  • Legal Basis / Source: Law No. 48/2009 Art. 4(1); HIR Art. 121; RBg Art. 145.
  • Related Concepts: Due process, equality of arms, right of reply (Replik/Duplik).

Principle of Mandatory Pre-Trial Mediation (Mediasi Wajib)

  • Meaning: All civil contentious lawsuits must attempt formal mediation before proceeding to examination of the merits, unless explicitly exempted by law.
  • Why It Matters: Preserves social harmony, reduces litigation costs, and produces win-win settlements (*Acta Van Dading*).
  • Legal Basis / Source: PERMA No. 1/2016; HIR Art. 130; RBg Art. 154.
  • Related Concepts: Alternative Dispute Resolution, Settlement Deed (Akta Perdamaian).

Principle of Formal Evidence System (Sistem Pembuktian Formil)

  • Meaning: In civil cases, judges seek formal truth (kebenaran formil) based on the statutory hierarchy of evidence, primarily valid written documents and deeds.
  • Why It Matters: Protects the certainty of registered rights and written contracts without forcing judges into speculative investigations beyond the parties' evidence.
  • Legal Basis / Source: HIR Art. 164; RBg Art. 284; Indonesian Civil Code (BW) Art. 1866.
  • Related Concepts: Authentic deeds, burden of proof (*Actori incumbit probatio*).

Indonesia Section

  • Procedural Structure: Civil proceedings in Indonesia follow a standardized linear sequence:
  1. Filing & Registration: Electronic registration via e-Court, e-Payment (e-SKUM), and automatic case number allocation.
  2. First Hearing & Formalities: Verification of party identity, legal counsel power of attorney (*Surat Kuasa Khusus*), and mediator appointment.
  3. Mediation Stage: Mandatory 30-day window (extendable by 30 days) presided over by a certified judge-mediator or non-judge mediator.
  4. Litigation Stage (e-Litigation / Conventional): Reading of the Claim (*Gugatan*), Response and Exceptions (*Jawaban & Eksepsi*), Counter-plea (*Replik*), Rejoinder (*Duplik*).
  5. Evidentiary Stage (*Pembuktian*): Presentation of written documents, witness testimony, expert statements, local inspection (*Pemeriksaan Setempat*), and oaths.
  6. Conclusions & Judgment: Submission of final conclusions (*Kesimpulan*) followed by open-court pronouncement of the judgment (*Putusan*).
  7. Execution (*Eksekusi*): Formal application for execution, warning hearing (*Aanmaning*), attachment for execution (*Sita Eksekusi*), and physical eviction or public auction.
  • Specialized Civil Procedures:
    • Small Claims Court (Gugatan Sederhana): Fast-track adjudication by a single judge for claims under IDR 500,000,000 resolved within 25 days without formal replik/duplik or appeal/cassation (only internal court objection).
    • Summary Consensual Decisions: Direct homologation of settlements into binding non-appealable Deeds of Settlement (*Akta Perdamaian*).

Important Decisions & Chamber Plenary Guidance

Decision
Supreme Court Plenary Resolution on Contractual Default vs. Tort

  • Issuing Body: Mahkamah Agung RI (Civil Chamber Plenary)
  • Instrument / SEMA Ref: SEMA No. 3/2023 jo. SEMA No. 1/2017
  • Legal Principle: Strict separation between breach of contract (*Wanprestasi*) and unlawful act (*Perbuatan Melawan Hukum / PMH*); mixing both causes of action in a single claim without clear separation renders the lawsuit obscure (*Obscuur Libel*).
  • Why It Matters: Guides judges to dismiss defective claims at the preliminary exception stage without wasting judicial resources.
  • Official Source: Supreme Court Chamber Reports.

Decision
Precedential Rule on Unregistered Land Rights Execution

  • Issuing Body: Mahkamah Agung RI
  • Instrument / SEMA Ref: SEMA No. 7/2012
  • Legal Principle: Execution on immovable property requires precise boundary identification during local inspection (*Pemeriksaan Setempat*) to ensure the judgment is executable (*Non-Executable / Non-Eksekutabel Prevention*).
  • Why It Matters: Prevents empty judgments that cannot be physically enforced by court bailiffs (*Jurusita*).
  • Official Source: Jurisprudensi Mahkamah Agung.

  • Number / Year: PERMA No. 1 of 2016

  • Status: In force
  • Relevant Provision: Articles 2, 3, 4, 7 (Failure to undertake mandatory mediation renders the judgment null and void / *Batal Demi Hukum*).

  • Number / Year: PERMA No. 1/2019 jo. PERMA No. 7/2022
  • Jurisdiction: Indonesia
  • Status: In force
  • Relevant Provision: Digital filing, e-Summons, electronic document exchange, and virtual examination of witnesses.

Debates & Controversies

Debate
Sanctioning "Bad Faith" in Mediation vs. Party Autonomy

  • Position A (Pro-Sanction / PERMA 1/2016): Parties who refuse to submit mediation proposals or fail to attend mediation sessions without valid justification must be penalized with case dismissal or bearing mediation costs to prevent mediation from being treated as a mere procedural formality.
  • Position B (Party Autonomy Advocates): Mediation is inherently voluntary; penalizing non-settlement or passive negotiation infringes on a party's constitutional right to seek formal adjudication.
  • Evidence: National court data showing a significant increase in mediated settlement rates following the implementation of PERMA No. 1/2016 bad-faith clauses.
  • Current Status: Settled law under PERMA 1/2016: Bad faith during mediation carries concrete procedural dismissal sanctions without examining the lawsuit merits.

Rule-of-Law Connection

  • Legal Certainty: Predictable evidentiary rules and strict adherence to formal truth protect registered civil rights and contractual promises.
  • Access to Justice: E-Court, e-Litigation, and Small Claims mechanisms reduce litigation travel costs and attorney expenses for small-scale litigants.
  • Integrity of Enforcement: Clear benchbook rules on conservatory attachment (*Sita Jaminan*) and execution protect judgment creditors from bad-faith asset dissipation.

AI & Future of Law

  • Automated Formal Assessment: AI algorithms can assist court clerks in screening procedural completeness (e.g., verifying power of attorney barcodes, court fee calculations) before docketing.
  • Digital Evidence Authentication: Chapter 2 incorporates technical criteria for verifying electronic signatures, cryptographic logs, and blockchain-anchored smart contracts in civil evidentiary hearings.
  • Judicial Integrity in ADR: Predictive settlement outcome tools can assist mediators in demonstrating litigation risk scenarios to parties without compromising mediator neutrality.

Common Misconceptions

Myth
A civil judge is required to find absolute material truth (*Kebenaran Materil*) like a criminal court judge.

  • Reality: Civil judges seek formal truth (*Kebenaran Formil*), meaning the court decides based on the claims, admissions, and legal evidence presented by the parties, without investigating extraneous facts unpleaded by either side.
  • Source: Civil Code Art. 1866; Supreme Court Technical Guidelines (Buku II).

Myth
Failure to mediate can be resolved later in the appellate court without affecting the trial judgment.

  • Reality: If a first-instance court fails to conduct mandatory mediation under PERMA 1/2016, the High Court or Supreme Court must declare the lower proceedings flawed and order a retrial from the mediation stage.
  • Source: PERMA No. 1/2016 Article 2(3).

Practical Example

  • Scenario: Plaintiff files a breach of contract lawsuit claiming IDR 300,000,000 in unpaid supply goods. Defendant files an exception claiming the lawsuit is vague (*Obscuur Libel*) because Plaintiff combined a debt claim with a claim for tortious property damage.
  • Legal Question: How should the civil panel rule on the preliminary exception?
  • Applicable Principle: Clarity of Claims & Separation of Actions (HIR Art. 125; SEMA Kamar Perdata).
  • Possible Analysis: Under Chapter 2 guidelines, the panel examines whether the operative facts (*fundamentum petendi*) for both claims are inextricably intertwined or contradictory. If the claims rely on irreconcilable legal bases, the court must sustain the exception and declare the lawsuit inadmissible (*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard / NO*).
  • Limitation: The ruling does not prevent the Plaintiff from refiling two separate, properly structured lawsuits.

Key Terms

Term Definition Related Term
Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard (NO) A formal court judgment declaring the lawsuit inadmissible due to procedural or formal defects without examining the substantive merits. Formal Exception, Obscuur Libel
Sita Jaminan (Conservatory Attachment) A pre-judgment court seizure of a defendant's assets to prevent disposal during litigation and secure future execution. Sita Eksekusi, Revindicatoir Beslag
Akta Perdamaian (Acta van Dading) A formal court-approved settlement deed possessing the final, binding legal force of a definitive judgment (*Res Judicata*). Court-Annexed Mediation, PERMA 1/2016
Gugatan Sederhana (Small Claims) An expedited civil procedure for claims with a monetary value under IDR 500 million, adjudicated by a single judge within 25 working days. PERMA 2/2015, Fast-Track Litigation
