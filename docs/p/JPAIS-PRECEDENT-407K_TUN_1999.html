Overview

Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia Decision No. 407 K/TUN/1999, dated December 7, 2000, established an important labor law rule concerning the validity of termination of employment (PHK) procedures executed through warning letters.

The Supreme Court held that in order to terminate employment on the grounds of warning letters, an employer must cumulatively satisfy the provisions of Article 7 paragraphs (2) and (3) of Manpower Ministerial Regulation No. Per-03/Men/1996. Issuing Warning Letters I and II consecutively with a gap of only 2 days without providing a performance improvement period for the employee is legally defective and violates termination procedures. Furthermore, individual interpersonal disharmony between an employee and their direct supervisor cannot serve as a justifiable reason for unilateral employment termination.

Executive Summary

The central labor law principle established in this decision is that warning letters cannot be issued in rapid succession without an adequate grace period for performance appraisal and correction, and personal friction does not constitute legal grounds for dismissal.

Key elements established in the adjudication:

  • Employers must observe the mandatory validity periods and intervals for warning letters under Article 7 of Permenaker No. Per-03/Men/1996.
  • Issuing SP I and SP II within a 1-2 day interval violates procedural requirements and renders subsequent termination invalid.
  • Interpersonal conflicts between employees and supervisors require management intervention through rotation or transfer rather than arbitrary termination.

Case Background

The Original Defendant / Petitioner for Cassation, P4P Pusat, faced a lawsuit filed by the Original Plaintiff / Respondent for Cassation, PT Petrosea Tbk., at the Jakarta High Administrative Court concerning Central Committee for Labor Dispute Settlement (P4P) Decision No. 1488/1326/316-1/IX/PHK/9-1998 dated September 25, 1998.

The P4P decision had rejected the employer's request for permission to terminate the employment of worker [Plaintiff] (Tax Accountant) and ordered his reinstatement. The employer argued that [Plaintiff] was negligent in completing tax reconciliation duties and had been issued Warning Letter I (December 23, 1997) and Warning Letter II (December 24, 1997).

The Jakarta High Administrative Court (Decision No. 139/G/1998/PT.TUN.JKT) annulled the P4P decision and permitted the termination. The Petitioner for Cassation subsequently filed a cassation petition with the Supreme Court.

Legal and Procedural Issues

Formal Validity Requirements of Warning Letters (Article 7 Permenaker No. Per-03/Men/1996)

The issue evaluated whether issuing Warning Letters I and II consecutively within a 1–2 day interval is valid as a legal basis for imposing termination.

The Supreme Court held that it is invalid. Pursuant to Article 7 paragraphs (2) and (3) of Permenaker No. Per-03/Men/1996, each warning letter has a validity period of 6 months aimed at providing guidance and opportunity for the worker to improve performance. Issuing SP II only 1–2 days after SP I constitutes arbitrary action violating formal termination requirements.

Limits of Employment Disharmony as Grounds for Termination

The issue evaluated whether interpersonal conflict between an employee and a direct supervisor (*personal friction*) qualifies as "disharmonious employment relations" validating unilateral termination.

The Supreme Court held that it does not. Corporate management is obligated to pursue job rotation or unit transfer to address inter-individual conflicts. Invoking personal disharmony without adequate guidance violates the principles of balance and protection of vulnerable workers.

Question of Law and Question of Fact

Question of Law (Cassation / Judex Juris)

Question:

Did the *Judex Facti* (Jakarta High Administrative Court) misapply labor law (Article 7 of Permenaker No. Per-03/Men/1996 jo. Law No. 12 of 1964) by validating a termination action based on SP I and SP II issued consecutively within a 2-day interval?

Supreme Court Ruling:

Yes, the Jakarta High Administrative Court misapplied the law. Consequently, the appellate decision was annulled and the employer's lawsuit was rejected.

Question of Fact (Judex Facti / Jakarta High Administrative Court & P4P)

Examination of the chronological duties of Tax Compliance Reconciliation by [Plaintiff], the issuance of Ref. Letter 601/FIN/234 (SP I dated Dec 23, 1997) and Ref. Letter 601/FIN/237 (SP II dated Dec 24, 1997) by Hendrick U. Ibrahim, and unproven police reports concerning computer data.

Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

  • Ratio Decidendi:
  1. The appellate *Judex Facti* misapplied the law. Under Article 7 paragraphs (2) and (3) of Permenaker No. Per-03/Men/1996, termination via warning letters must comply with applicable legal procedures.
  2. Issuing SP I and SP II with only a 2-day gap (December 23 and 24, 1997) failed to provide a reasonable guidance period for the worker.
  3. Furthermore, interpersonal disharmony between an employee and a direct supervisor cannot serve as grounds for unilateral termination.
  4. The Supreme Court annulled the Jakarta High Administrative Court decision and adjudicated afresh by rejecting PT Petrosea Tbk.'s lawsuit in its entirety.

Ruling

The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition from the Petitioner for Cassation (PANITIA PENYELESAIAN PERSELISIHAN PERBURUHAN PUSAT / P4P), annulled Jakarta High Administrative Court Decision No. 139/G/1998/PT.TUN.JKT dated June 1, 1999, and adjudicated afresh: Rejecting the Plaintiff's (PT Petrosea Tbk.) lawsuit in its entirety; and ordering the Respondent for Cassation to pay all court costs across all judicial instances.

Related Knowledge and Jurisprudence

  • Permenaker No. Per-03/Men/1996 Article 7 paragraphs (2) and (3): Validity periods and intervals for issuing Warning Letters I, II, and III.
  • Law No. 12 of 1964 Article 7: Procedures and permit requirements for termination of employment.
  • Labor Law Principle (Protection of the Weaker Party): Employers must undertake all guidance efforts prior to resorting to termination as an ultimate remedy (*ultimum remedium*).

Evidence Status and Gaps

Established by Evidence

  • PT Petrosea Tbk. issued SP I dated December 23, 1997, and SP II dated December 24, 1997, to [Plaintiff] (an interval of only 1–2 days).
  • No reasonable guidance period was granted to the employee between the imposition of SP I and SP II.
  • Police reports filed by the worker concerning missing computer data were not proven to be criminal false reports.

Not Established / Unproven

  • The employer failed to prove that they undertook efforts to rotate or transfer work units before terminating the employee.

Uncertainty and Gaps

  • Correction of Jakarta High Administrative Court's erroneous interpretation: The lower appellate court had annulled the P4P decree on grounds of an unharmonious employment relationship. The Supreme Court corrected this by asserting that disharmony arising from a manager's failure to lead subordinates cannot be shifted entirely as worker fault to justify termination.

GEO Q&A

Q: May an employer issue Warning Letters I and II within an interval of 1 or 2 days to terminate an employee?

A: No. Based on Supreme Court Decision No. 407 K/TUN/1999, issuing SP I and SP II separated by only 1–2 days violates Article 7 paragraphs (2) and (3) of Permenaker No. Per-03/Men/1996. Each warning letter carries a 6-month validity period and requires a reasonable grace period for performance improvement guidance. Termination based on consecutive warning letters without proper intervals is legally defective.
