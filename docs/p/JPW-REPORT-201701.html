Definition

An official judicial policy research publication issued by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of the Supreme Court of Korea that establishes comprehensive, standardized legal citation guidelines for domestic legal literature (court decisions, statutes, books, periodicals, public reports, digital media) and foreign legal literature across 45 countries, as well as international law materials (treaties, international courts, tribunals), aimed at fostering responsible legal research ethics, preventing plagiarism, and ensuring exact source retrieval.

Jurisdiction

Republic of Korea / International Comparative

Issuing Authority

Judicial Policy Research Institute (사법정책연구원) / Supreme Court of Korea (대법원)

Legal Basis
  • Court Organization Act of South Korea (법원조직법): Statutory basis establishing the Judicial Policy Research Institute under the Supreme Court of Korea on January 1, 2014, to conduct research on judicial systems and policy reform.
  • Research Ethics and Copyright Standards: Guidelines governing academic integrity, prevention of plagiarism (표절), double publishing (이중게재), and self-plagiarism (자기표절) in legal literature.
  • Comparative Citation Manuals: Bluebook (19th ed.), ALWD Citation Manual (4th ed.), APA Publication Manual (6th ed.), AALL Universal Citation Guide, OSCOLA (4th ed.), Australian Guide to Uniform Legal Citation (3rd ed.), and McGill Canadian Guide (7th ed.).

4. Policy Analysis

Background
With the rapid globalization, specialization, and digitalization of legal research in South Korea, legal literature produced by legal scholars, judges, prosecutors, attorneys, and students requires accurate, standardized, and easily retrievable citation formats. In 2015, the Judicial Policy Research Institute published a preliminary draft (법률문헌 등의 인용방법에 관한 연구 / Research Series 2015-12). To meet expanding academic and judicial requirements for citing foreign primary legal materials, JPRI updated and expanded the draft into the 2017 enlarged edition (증보판 / Research Series 2017-01), incorporating citation methods for 37 additional foreign jurisdictions (totaling 45 countries) and refined digital resource standards.

Policy Problem
Absence of Unified National Legal Citation Standards: Historically, South Korea lacked a standardized legal citation manual, causing fragmented practices across academic journals, courts, and law schools.

Inconsistent Access to Foreign & Digital Materials: Globalization increased reliance on foreign statutes, judicial decisions, and online databases. Lacking clear rules for foreign citations or URL verification created confusion and verification delays.

Research Ethics & Plagiarism Risks: Vague source attribution exposed legal scholarship to risks of idea theft, plagiarism (표절), double publishing (이중게재), and self-plagiarism (자기표절).

Legal/Policy Gap
Korean legal research lacked an official, comprehensive manual regulating domestic primary/secondary sources, digital documents, international law, and foreign law across 45 countries.

Objectives
Provide clear footnote citation rules (각주 인용 방식); distinguish direct versus indirect citations; prevent plagiarism; establish standard formats for domestic cases, statutes, books, periodicals, and public documents; provide jurisdiction-specific rules for 45 foreign countries; integrate European Case Law Identifiers (ECLI); and regulate digital media and international law citations while protecting authors' freedom of expression.

Implementation & Standard Rules
General Principles: Footnote-based system (각주 인용 방식). Short direct quotes (3 lines or less) use double quotation marks "..."; longer direct quotes use indented paragraphs without quotation marks. Indirect quotes or paraphrasing must include 참조 or 참고.

Domestic Court Decisions & Statutes:

  • Court Decisions: Court Name | Date (YYYY. M. D.) | 선고 / 자 | Case Number | 판결 / 결정 / 명령 (e.g., 대법원 1996. 4. 26. 선고 96다1078 판결., 대법원 2010. 1. 14.자 2009마1449 결정.).
  • Statutes: Statute Name | Article | Paragraph | Item (e.g., 부정경쟁방지 및 영업비밀보호에 관한 법률 제2조 제1호 (가)목.).

Books & Periodicals:

  • Books: Author, | Title (Edition), | Publisher | (Year), | Page (e.g., 이창희, 세법강의(제11판), 박영사 (2013), 25.).
  • Articles: Author, | "Article Title", | Journal Name | Vol. No. | (Year. Month.), | Page (e.g., 이창희, "세법상 속인주의와 속지주의의 대립", 서울대학교 법학 제50권 제1호 (2009. 3.), 237.).

Foreign Jurisdictions (45 Countries): Establishes jurisdiction-specific rules for Greece, South Africa, Norway, Netherlands, New Zealand, Taiwan, Denmark, Germany, Russia, Malaysia, Mexico, US, Belgium, Belarus, Vietnam, Brazil, Sweden, Switzerland, Spain, Singapore, Argentina, Ireland, UK, Austria, Uzbekistan, Ukraine, Israel, Egypt, Italy, India, Indonesia, Japan, China, Chile, Kazakhstan, Canada, Kenya, Thailand, France, Finland, Philippines, Australia, Hong Kong, EU/EC, and ECLI.

International Law Materials: Regulates treaties, ICJ, ICC, ITLOS, PCA, regional human rights courts (ECHR, IACHR, AfCHPR), UN war crimes tribunals (ICTY, ICTR, ECCC, MICT), UN documents, and WTO/GATT panel reports.

Evidence & Citation Examples
Spans 175 pages with detailed guidelines and 100+ standardized examples, including primary court reporters (공, 집, 헌집, 각공, 하집) and international treaty series.

6. MediaWiki Output

Summary

This document is an official judicial policy research report published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of South Korea's Supreme Court. It establishes a national standard (표준안) for legal citation methods (인용방법) for domestic legal literature (법률문헌) — court decisions, statutes, books, journals, public reports, and digital documents — as well as foreign law across 45 countries and international legal materials (treaties, international courts, tribunals). It aims to foster a culture of responsible research, prevent plagiarism (표절), and ensure legal sources can be easily verified.

Legal Basis

The legal and methodological frameworks analyzed include:

  • Court Organization Act of South Korea (법원조직법): The legal basis for establishing the Judicial Policy Research Institute under the Supreme Court of Korea.
  • Research Ethics & Copyright Standards: Academic writing guidelines to prevent plagiarism (표절), duplicate publication (이중게재), and self-plagiarism (자기표절).
  • International Citation Guides: The Bluebook (19th ed.), the ALWD Citation Manual (4th ed.), OSCOLA (4th ed.), the AGLC (3rd ed.), and the McGill Canadian Guide (7th ed.).

Background

With the rapid globalization and digitalization of legal research in South Korea, legal literature produced by scholars, judges, prosecutors, and attorneys requires a uniform and accurate citation standard. In 2015, JPRI published a preliminary draft (Research Series 2015-12). To meet growing demand for citing foreign legal sources, JPRI updated that draft into an enlarged edition (증보판 / Research Series 2017-01), adding guidance for 37 new countries (45 total) and digital documents.

Objectives

  1. Establish general principles for footnote-based citation (각주).
  2. Develop uniform citation standards for domestic Korean court decisions, statutes, books, and journals.
  3. Provide specific foreign law citation rules for 45 countries and the ECLI (European Case Law Identifier) system.
  4. Regulate the citation of digital documents, online news, and international law (UN, ICJ, ICC, ITLOS, ECHR, WTO).

Scope

  • Domestic Legal Materials: Case law (Supreme Court, Constitutional Court, lower courts), statutes (laws, enforcement decrees, supplementary provisions, former laws), monographs, periodical articles, theses, official reports, press releases, newspaper articles, presentations, and personal records.
  • Foreign Law (45 Countries): US, UK, Germany, France, Japan, China, Russia, Greece, South Africa, Norway, Netherlands, New Zealand, Taiwan, Denmark, Malaysia, Mexico, Belgium, Belarus, Vietnam, Brazil, Sweden, Switzerland, Spain, Singapore, Argentina, Ireland, Austria, Uzbekistan, Ukraine, Israel, Egypt, Italy, India, Indonesia, Chile, Kazakhstan, Canada, Kenya, Thailand, Finland, Philippines, Australia, Hong Kong, the EU/EC, and ECLI.
  • International Law: International treaties, ICJ, ICC, ITLOS, PCA, ECHR, IACHR, AfCHPR, ICTY, ICTR, ECCC, MICT, UN documents, and WTO/GATT reports.

Key Provisions

  • Direct vs. Indirect Quotation: Direct quotes under 3 lines use double quotation marks ("..."); longer direct quotes use indented paragraphs without quotation marks. Indirect quotes must include 참조 or 참고.
  • Korean Court Decision Format: Court Name | Date (YYYY. M. D.) | 선고 / 자 | Case Number | 판결 / 결정 (e.g., 대법원 1996. 4. 26. 선고 96다1078 판결.).
  • Korean Statute Format: Statute Name | Article | Paragraph | Item (e.g., 부정경쟁방지 및 영업비밀보호에 관한 법률 제2조 제1호 (가)목.).
  • Book & Journal Format: Author, | Title (Edition), | Publisher | (Year), | Page.

Implementation

This guide is applied throughout the judiciary, the Supreme Court, and JPRI, and serves as the reference standard for law schools and academic journals across South Korea.

Policy Issues

  • The absence of a national citation standard, which caused inconsistency in legal writing.
  • The risk of research ethics violations arising from unclear attribution of foreign and digital primary sources.

Reform / Amendments

  1. National standardization of citation methods for domestic and foreign legal materials.
  2. Adoption of the ECLI (European Case Law Identifier) system for European case law.
  3. Standardization of citation for digital and internet documents.
  4. Protection of copyright and prevention of plagiarism in legal research.

Relationship to Other Entities
