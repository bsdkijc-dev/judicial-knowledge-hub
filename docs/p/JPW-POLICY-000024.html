JPW-POLICY-000024

By Cecep Mustafa, Indonesian Judicial College (IJC) · revision 1430, 2026-09-18 · wiki source · Markdown

The IDE Asian Law Series Comparative Judicial Systems Framework (Stable ID: JPW-POLICY-000024) is a comprehensive comparative legal research and judicial systems policy framework established by the Institute of Developing Economies, Japan External Trade Organization (IDE-JETRO) in FY 2000. The framework evaluates the role of law in social and economic development alongside ongoing judicial system reforms across seven Asian nations (China, India, Indonesia, Malaysia, Philippines, Thailand, and Vietnam). Published in March 2001, the series provides comparative analysis covering judiciaries, judges, prosecutors, lawyers, legal education, procedural rules, alternative dispute resolution (ADR), and statistical data. Note: Named entities Cecep Mustafa and Reihan Abdan Syakur are Not found in the available sources.

Summary

The framework responds to expanding market economies and cross-border transactions in Asia by establishing a comparative research structure. It encompasses two core research pillars established under FY 2000 IDE-JETRO committees: (1) Law and Development Studies, focusing on establishing a legal theoretical framework regarding the role of law in social and economic development in Indonesia and the Philippines; and (2) Judicial Systems and Reforms Studies, analyzing court structures, judges, prosecutors, legal practitioners, legal education, dispute resolution, ADR, and statistical indicators across China, India, Malaysia, Philippines, Thailand, and Vietnam.

Legal Basis

Specific statutory legal bases are Not found in the available sources. The framework functions as an international comparative legal research project executed by IDE-JETRO in collaboration with counterpart research institutions in seven Asian countries.

Background & Policy Problems

Background

Driven by market-oriented economic reforms and growing international trade in Asia, IDE-JETRO identified an urgent need for comparative judicial research. In FY 2000, IDE-JETRO formed two specialized research committees to lead joint research projects with Asian counterpart institutions, culminating in the March 2001 publication of the IDE Asian Law Series.

Policy Problems

  • Market Evolution & Legal Deficits: Accelerating cross-border trade and market-oriented economic reforms created an urgent need to evaluate diverse Asian judicial structures and dispute resolution mechanisms.
  • Theoretical Framework Gaps: A lack of comprehensive legal theoretical frameworks addressing the specific role of law in economic and social development across developing Asian nations.
  • Information Asymmetry on Asian Judiciaries: Scarcity of comprehensive, standardized comparative data covering judiciaries, judges, prosecutors, lawyers, legal education, court procedures, ADR, and judicial statistics.

Objectives

1. Develop a theoretical legal framework on law in social and economic development. 2. Analyze judicial systems, reform processes, and dispute resolution in Asia. 3. Conduct joint research with counterpart institutions in seven Asian nations. 4. Provide comprehensive comparative data on judiciaries, prosecutors, lawyers, education, and ADR. 5. Foster mutual understanding of shared legal challenges across Asia.

Scope & Implementation

Scope

Applies across comparative legal research, judicial administration studies, and legal education frameworks covering China, India, Indonesia, Malaysia, Philippines, Thailand, and Vietnam.

Key Provisions

  • Dual Research Committee Architecture: Committee on "Law and Development in Economic and Social Development" and Committee on "Judicial Systems in Asia".
  • Seven-Nation Research Coverage: Joint research executed with counterpart institutions across China, India, Indonesia, Malaysia, Philippines, Thailand, and Vietnam.
  • Standardized Comparative Parameters: Comparative documentation of judiciary, judges, prosecutors, lawyers, legal education, procedures, ADR, and statistics.

Implementation

  • Joint Committee Projects: Execution of research tasks by IDE-JETRO committees and Asian counterpart institutions.
  • Publication of Series: Formal release of the *IDE Asian Law Series* in March 2001.

Policy Problems

  • Cross-Border Legal Information Deficits: Scarcity of comparative data on Asian judicial systems.
  • Lack of Theoretical Frameworks: Absence of frameworks addressing law's role in economic development.

Reform

  • Continuous tracking of judicial reform processes and dispute resolution mechanisms across Asia.
