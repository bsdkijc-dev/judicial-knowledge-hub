Knowledge Management

Knowledge management concerns the processes, systems, people, technologies, and governance mechanisms used to create, capture, organize, preserve, discover, share, and reuse knowledge.

Judicial Knowledge Management

In judicial environments, knowledge management may connect:

  • Laws
  • Decisions
  • Procedures
  • Policies
  • Research
  • Training
  • Institutional experience
  • Data
  • Technology

Knowledge Lifecycle

CREATE → CAPTURE → CLASSIFY → STORE → CONNECT → RETRIEVE → REUSE → UPDATE → ARCHIVE

Knowledge Repository

A repository may contain:

  • Documents
  • Structured pages
  • Metadata
  • Datasets
  • Multimedia
  • References
  • Learning materials

Wiki as Knowledge Infrastructure

A MediaWiki-based repository can provide:

  • Version history
  • Internal links
  • Categories
  • Templates
  • Structured metadata
  • Search
  • Collaborative editing
  • Knowledge navigation

A wiki is a tool; its value depends on governance, content quality, structure, and maintenance.

Knowledge Graph

Knowledge management becomes more powerful when entities and relationships are explicitly represented.

Example:

Court → decides → Case

Case → applies → Law

Law → protects → Right

AI System → affects → Judicial Process

AI and Knowledge Management

AI can assist with:

  • Classification
  • Summarization
  • Metadata
  • Search
  • Translation
  • Relationship discovery
  • Duplicate detection
  • Updating

Human verification remains essential.

Knowledge Governance

Governance should address:

  • Ownership
  • Access
  • Editorial standards
  • Versioning
  • Verification
  • Security
  • Retention
  • Archiving
