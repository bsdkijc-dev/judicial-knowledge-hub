'Could Innovation also Emerge from the Public Sector? Creating an ISO-like Judiciary Quality Management Standard' (Murillo & Zuniga, 2013)

Wiki Entity Page

GICA-Justicia Project (and its main product: Norma GICA-Justicia 2010: Organización del Despacho Judicial) is the world's first tailor-made, ISO-9000-like quality management standard and judicial accreditation system specifically designed for judicial environments in Costa Rica. This initiative was developed by the Supreme Court of Costa Rica (Poder Judicial) between 2009 and 2010, and scientifically analyzed by Rodrigo Murillo (Bradford School of Management) and Roy Zuniga (INCAE Business School) in the article titled "Could Innovation also Emerge from the Public Sector? Creating an ISO-like Judiciary Quality Management Standard" published in the International Journal for Court Administration (IJCA, Vol. 5, No. 2, October 2013).

Summary

The GICA-Justicia Project responded to high public dissatisfaction (reaching 85%) regarding slow judicial services and case backlogs in Costa Rica. The project produced two main instruments: the GICA-Justicia Quality Management Model (GJP-Model) and the GICA-Justicia Quality Management Standard (GJP-Standard / Norma GICA-Justicia 2010). Unlike international quality models such as RechtspraaQ (Netherlands), IFCE, or CEPEJ which are based on self-assessment, GICA-Justicia adopts an independent third-party accreditation model through the national body SINCA-Justicia.

Legal Basis

  • Supreme Court Resolution (2009): Official decision to launch the GICA-Justicia Project to fill the gap in judicial quality standards.
  • National Accreditation Bill of 2010: Signed by the President of Costa Rica, the State Minister, and the Minister of Justice to establish the independent accreditation body SINCA-Justicia (Sistema Nacional de Acreditación y Calidad para la Justicia).
  • Organic Law of the Judiciary (Ley Orgánica del Poder Judicial): Statutory foundation defining the jurisdiction, structure, and administrative competencies of the courts in Costa Rica.

Background & Policy Problems

1. Incompatibility of Generic ISO 9001: Standard ISO 9001 guidelines do not integrate technical judicial jargon or specific provisions such as vulnerable group protection and alternative dispute resolution. 2. Lack of Third-Party Accreditation: Most global judicial quality models lack an independent assessment mechanism from a legitimized national accreditation body. 3. High Citizen Dissatisfaction (85%): National survey results based on an adapted ServQual tool indicated that the public's main complaints pertained to service delivery speed, missed time promises, and the lack of accurate case conclusion reporting [10, 11, 69–71].

Main Components of the GICA-Justicia Model & Standard

1. GICA-Justicia Quality Management Model (GJP-Model)

  • 3 Mainstays: Public policies in service management, User rights, and Integral system approach.
  • 3 Crosscutting Axes: User satisfaction, Organizational commitment, and Good management practices.
  • 3 Continuous Improvement Phases: Phase 1 (Redesign), Phase 2 (Accreditation), and Phase 3 (Repeatability/Replication) [75–83].

2. GICA-Justicia Quality Management Standard (Norma GICA-Justicia 2010)

Consists of 5 Main Chapters [12–15]: 1. QMS Organization: Basic QMS principles and transversal guidelines on gender equality, vulnerable populations' accessibility, transparency, ethics, citizen participation, and environmental management. 2. Documentation & Communication Management: Document hierarchy, version control, and user feedback channels. 3. Resources' Management: Management of facilities, IT services, and judicial human resource competencies. 4. Judicial Process Realization: A 4-step process delivery approach (Inputs, Appearances, Resolutions, Outputs) and 4 ancillary activities (Time management, Case/document management, Supportive activities, Alternative Dispute Resolution) [14, 84–87]. 5. Performance Appraisal & QMS Improvement: Performance indicators, internal auditing, non-conformity management, and user satisfaction measurement.

Accreditation Mechanism & Implementing Agencies

  • SINCA-Justicia: An independent national third-party accreditation body consisting of 7 cross-sectoral institutions. It utilizes a weighted scoring scale with an 80% minimum compliance threshold.
  • CEGECA (Centro de Gestión de la Calidad): The internal quality management office of the judiciary tasked with coordinating training, pre-audits, and standard replication across district courts.
  • Pilot Court Results:
    • Juzgado de Violencia Doméstica de Heredia (Domestic Aggression Court): Achieved 1,423 / 1,500 points (95% compliance).
    • Tribunal de Apelación de la Sentencia (Second Court of Appeal): Achieved 1,215 / 1,500 points (81% compliance).

Evaluation Results & Real-World Impacts

  • Case Backlog Drainage: Within 6 months after deploying the GJP-Standard, the Second Court of Appeal successfully reduced its case backlog from ~650 cases to 200 cases (its target average).
  • Systemic Discipline: The establishment of written performance indicators, daily work quotas, and official documented procedures.

Policy Problems & Limitations

1. Lack of Cost-of-Quality Metrics: The standard does not measure the financial cost of processes, meaning a court can be highly effective but financially inefficient. 2. Absence of a Public Trust Chapter: Unlike the IFCE, the GJP-Standard does not have a dedicated chapter directly measuring public trust.
