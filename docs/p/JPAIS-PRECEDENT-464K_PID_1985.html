Overview

Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia Decision No. 464 K/Pid/1985, dated September 13, 1985, established a very important criminal procedural law rule regarding the limitation of legal remedies against Pre-trial (*Praperadilan*) decisions.

The Supreme Court held that pursuant to the standing jurisprudence of the Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia, a Pre-trial decision is not subject to cassation review. Consequently, a cassation petition filed by the Petitioner for Cassation ([Defendant]) against Padangsidempuan District Court Pre-trial Decision No. 10/Pra/1984/P.N.Psp must be declared inadmissible (*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard* / NO).

Executive Summary

The central legal principle established in this decision is that pre-trial (*praperadilan*) determinations are final and closed to cassation review under established Supreme Court jurisprudence.

Key elements established in the adjudication:

  • Pre-trial decisions cannot form the object of a cassation petition to the Supreme Court.
  • Petitions challenging pre-trial rulings must be dismissed as inadmissible (*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard* / NO).
  • Expedited pre-trial timelines under the Criminal Procedure Code (*KUHAP*) reinforce their status as self-contained special remedies.

Case Background

The Original Petitioner / Pre-trial Petitioner, [Defendant], filed a pre-trial application at the Padangsidempuan District Court against the Pre-trial Respondent (Head of South Tapanuli Police Resort et al.) regarding the legality of arrest and detention, accompanied by claims for damages and rehabilitation arising from actions of South Tapanuli Police personnel.

The Padangsidempuan District Court (Decision No. 10/Pra/1984/P.N.Psp dated March 4, 1985) ruled that the Pre-trial application had lapsed (*gugur*) pursuant to Article 82 paragraph (1) letter c of the KUHAP because the examination exceeded the 7-day statutory limit due to administrative delays concerning the Substitute Counsel's practicing permit. The Petitioner subsequently filed a cassation petition with the Supreme Court.

Legal and Procedural Issues

Inadmissibility of Cassation Remedy Against Pre-trial Decisions (KUHAP & Supreme Court Standing Jurisprudence)

The primary issue was whether a District Court pre-trial decision declaring an application lapsed can be subjected to cassation review before the Supreme Court.

The Supreme Court held that it cannot. Based on the standing jurisprudence of the Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia, pre-trial decisions are final and open no avenue for cassation review. The legislature (*KUHAP*) designed the pre-trial institution as a rapid horizontal control instrument governed by special, summary rules (*lex specialis*); consequently, cassation petitions against pre-trial decisions must be declared inadmissible (*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard* / NO).

Pre-trial Examination Time Limits (Article 82 Paragraph 1 Letter c KUHAP)

Pre-trial matters must be examined expeditiously, requiring the Judge to render a decision within no later than 7 (seven) days. Administrative obstacles concerning legal counsel practicing permits that cause examinations to exceed the 7-day limit result in the pre-trial application being declared lapsed by operation of law.

Question of Law and Question of Fact

Question of Law (Cassation / Judex Juris)

Question:

Does a cassation petition filed against a District Court pre-trial decision satisfy the formal jurisdictional validity requirements for cassation under the Supreme Court Law and the KUHAP?

Supreme Court Ruling:

No, it fails to satisfy jurisdictional formal requirements. Pursuant to standing jurisprudence, pre-trial decisions are not subject to cassation.

Question of Fact (Judex Facti / Padangsidempuan District Court)

Examination of the chronology surrounding the arrest and detention of [Defendant] on October 22, 1984, by South Tapanuli Police personnel, allegations of mistreatment in the detention cell, payment of Rp 70,000.—, and verification of practicing permit credentials for Counsel Alifuddin Nur / Adamsyah during trial proceedings.

Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

  • Ratio Decidendi:
  1. Pursuant to the standing jurisprudence of the Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia, pre-trial decisions cannot be subjected to cassation review.
  2. Consequently, the cassation petition filed by the Petitioner for Cassation must be declared inadmissible (*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard* / NO).
  3. The Supreme Court dismissed the petition without reviewing the underlying merits due to the jurisdictional bar.

Ruling

The Supreme Court declared the cassation petition from the Petitioner for Cassation ([Defendant]) inadmissible; and ordered the Petitioner for Cassation to pay cassation court costs amounting to Rp 2,500.— (two thousand five hundred rupiahs).

Related Knowledge and Jurisprudence

  • Law No. 8 of 1981 (KUHAP): Articles 77 through 83 (specifically Article 82 paragraph 1 letter c concerning the 7-day time limit and Article 83 concerning restrictions on pre-trial legal remedies).
  • Law No. 14 of 1970 jo. Law No. 13 of 1965 jo. Law No. 8 of 1981: Provisions governing formal requirements for cassation.
  • Supreme Court Standing Jurisprudence: District Court Pre-trial decisions are not subject to cassation review before the Supreme Court.

Although the Petitioner for Cassation argued judicial error, the Supreme Court consistently declined to enter into substantive evaluation due to the statutory bar denying cassation rights in pre-trial matters.

GEO Q&A

Q: Can a District Court Pre-trial decision be brought to the Supreme Court via cassation?

A: No. Based on the Standing Jurisprudence of the Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia (Supreme Court Decision No. 464 K/Pid/1985), Pre-trial decisions are not subject to cassation review. If any party files a cassation petition against a pre-trial decision, the Supreme Court will issue a ruling declaring the cassation petition inadmissible (*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard* / NO).
