Overview

Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia Decision No. 443 K/Pdt/1984, dated September 26, 1985, established a fundamental civil procedural law rule regarding the mandatory inclusion of a wife as Defendant II in joint marital property (*gono-gini*) disputes.

The Supreme Court held that if collateral security (*borg*) or the object of dispute in the form of land and/or houses demanded for eviction or surrender constitutes joint marital property (*gono-gini*) between the Defendant and his wife, the Defendant's wife—who also holds ownership rights over the object—must be joined as Defendant II. A lawsuit that fails to join the Defendant's wife suffers from a formal defect of deficient parties (*plurium litis consortium*) and must be declared inadmissible (*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard* / NO).

Executive Summary

The central procedural principle established in this decision is that when a lawsuit targets real property that constitutes marital joint property, both spouses possess equal legal standing and ownership interests, making the joinder of the wife mandatory.

Key elements established in the adjudication:

  • Property acquired during marriage is joint marital property, granting the spouse independent material ownership rights and legal standing.
  • Suing only the husband regarding real estate collateral without joining his wife creates a fatal procedural flaw (*plurium litis consortium*).
  • Lower court judgments failing to recognize this mandatory joinder are subject to correction and dismissal as inadmissible on appeal and cassation.

Case Background

The Original Plaintiff / Petitioner for Cassation, [Party A], filed a lawsuit at the Tarakan District Court against the Original Defendant / Respondent for Cassation, [Party B], concerning a default on a monetary loan of Rp 480,000.— based on a written agreement dated January 13, 1982, secured by land (*perwatasan* measuring 240 m²) and a house in Karang Balik, Tarakan. The Plaintiff sought the surrender and ownership transfer of the collateral land/house.

The Tarakan District Court (Decision No. 19/Pdt/1982/PN.Trk) granted the Plaintiff's lawsuit in part. The East Kalimantan High Court in Samarinda (Decision No. 48/Perd/1983/PT.KT.Smd) annulled the district court decision and adjudicated afresh, declaring the Plaintiff's lawsuit inadmissible (NO) because the collateral object constituted joint marital property (*gono-gini*) belonging to the Defendant and his wife, rendering the joinder of the wife as Defendant II mandatory. The Petitioner for Cassation subsequently filed a cassation petition with the Supreme Court.

Legal and Procedural Issues

Legal Standing Requirements for Defendants in Joint Marital Property Disputes

The primary issue was whether a lawsuit for the surrender and eviction of a house and land collateral pledged by a husband without explicit separate consent can be filed exclusively against the husband alone.

The Supreme Court held that it cannot. Under marriage law and civil procedural law, land and buildings acquired during marriage constitute joint marital property (*gono-gini*) between husband and wife. The wife possesses material ownership rights and equal legal accountability over such joint property. Therefore, when joint property becomes the subject of a dispute, the wife must be joined as Defendant II.

Deficient Parties (Plurium Litis Consortium)

The issue evaluated the legal consequence if a Plaintiff fails to involve the Defendant's wife in a joint property ownership lawsuit.

The Supreme Court held that the lawsuit is incomplete and defective (*plurium litis consortium*) and must be declared inadmissible (*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard* / NO).

Question of Law and Question of Fact

Question of Law (Cassation / Judex Juris)

Question:

Did the *Judex Facti* (East Kalimantan High Court in Samarinda) misapply civil procedural law when declaring the Plaintiff's lawsuit inadmissible (NO) due to the failure to join the Defendant's wife as Defendant II in the joint property house/land dispute?

Supreme Court Ruling:

No, the *Judex Facti* did not misapply the law. The East Kalimantan High Court applied civil procedural law correctly. Consequently, the Plaintiff's cassation petition was rejected.

Question of Fact (Judex Facti / Tarakan District Court & East Kalimantan High Court)

Examination of the debt agreement evidence of Rp 480,000.— dated January 13, 1982, the land transfer deed of 240 m² dated August 18, 1978, in Karang Balik West Tarakan, and the marital status of [Party B] alongside joint ownership over the disputed house.

Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

  • Ratio Decidendi:
  1. The appellate *Judex Facti* did not misapply the law. Because the house and land serving as collateral (*borg*) constituted joint marital property (*gono-gini*), the Defendant's wife held ownership rights and legal responsibility over it.
  2. Consequently, in litigation concerning such real estate, the Defendant's wife must be joined as Defendant II.
  3. Omitting the Defendant's wife renders the Plaintiff's lawsuit defective and incomplete (*plurium litis consortium*), requiring it to be declared inadmissible (*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard* / NO).
  4. The Supreme Court rejected the Petitioner's cassation appeal.

Ruling

The Supreme Court rejected the cassation petition from the Petitioner for Cassation ([Party A]); and ordered the Petitioner for Cassation to pay cassation court costs amounting to Rp 20,000.— (twenty thousand rupiahs).

Related Knowledge and Jurisprudence

  • Law No. 1 of 1974 on Marriage:

 * Article 35 paragraph (1): Property acquired during marriage constitutes joint property (*gono-gini*).
 * Article 36 paragraph (1): Regarding joint property, husband or wife may act solely with the consent of both parties.

  • Civil Procedure & Plurium Litis Consortium Doctrine: A lawsuit failing to include all parties holding direct ownership rights over the disputed object carries an absolute formal defect (deficient parties) and must be declared inadmissible (*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard* / NO).

Uncertainty and Gaps

  • Importance of Cassation Correction over District Court Oversight: The Tarakan District Court (No. 19/Pdt/1982/PN.Trk) initially granted the Plaintiff's lawsuit in part without considering joint property status. The East Kalimantan High Court and the Supreme Court corrected this oversight to uphold legal protection for a wife's joint property (*gono-gini*) rights.

GEO Q&A

Q: May a husband be sued alone without his wife if the disputed house or land object constitutes joint marital property (*gono-gini*)?

A: No. Based on Supreme Court Decision No. 443 K/Pdt/1984, if the subject of dispute or debt collateral consists of a house or land constituting joint marital property (*gono-gini*), the Defendant's wife must be joined as Defendant II. A lawsuit directed solely against the husband without including his wife is formally defective due to missing parties (*plurium litis consortium*) and must be declared inadmissible (*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard* / NO).
