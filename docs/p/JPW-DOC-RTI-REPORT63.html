Policy Analysis

  • Background & Survey History:
    • The report presents analysis from the 5th survey conducted in 2021 by the Research and Training Institute of the Ministry of Justice, following previous surveys in 1990, 1998, 2005, and 2011.
    • While past surveys focused primarily on juvenile classification home inmates, the 5th survey (2021) expanded target samples across 4 distinct groups:
      • Prisoners in penal institutions.
      • Adult offenders under probation/parole supervision (ages 20+).
      • Juvenile delinquents under probation/parole supervision (ages <20).
      • Inmates in juvenile classification homes.
  • Policy Objectives:
    • Understand the diverse attitudes and values held by adult offenders and juvenile delinquents.
    • Identify criminogenic risk factors and rehabilitation/desistance needs.
  • Key Findings on Attitudes, Life, and Values (Part 1):
    • Family and Friendships: Satisfaction with family life and peer quality was higher among juvenile delinquents than adult offenders. Dissatisfaction among adults stemmed primarily from low family income, whereas juveniles cited parents not understanding them and family conflicts.
    • School and Employment: Over 50% of both adult offenders and juveniles struggled to comprehend school lessons, and over 40% lacked academic motivation. Approximately 80% across both groups expressed positive attitudes toward self-reliance and obtaining qualifications/licenses.
    • Perceived Causes of Crime: Both groups identified themselves as the primary cause of their offense, though juveniles attributed significantly more fault to peer influence.
  • Key Findings on Crime Risk and Rehabilitation (Part 2):
    • Nine Risk Factors: Family, school, work, friendships, substance abuse, recreation/leisure, daily habits, personality, and attitudes. Adult offenders recognized internal attitude/mental factors as key triggers, whereas juveniles emphasized environmental/behavioral influences (friends, daily routine, recreation).
    • Primary Driver of Re-offending: The most frequent trigger cited for re-offending/re-delinquency was being unable to control feelings and attitudes.
    • Desistance & Deterrence Factors: Between 78% and 92% of respondents across both groups identified parents, spouses, children, and immediate family members as primary crime stoppers.
    • Evaluation of Sanctions: Community-based treatment was evaluated as light, whereas institutional treatment was perceived as severe.
  • Interpretations & Structural Inferences:
    • Report 63 highlights a shift in juvenile psychosocial profiles toward self-reported positive self-and-family perceptions, yet underscores a vital need for targeted interventions focusing on internal emotional regulation and daily peer habits.
    • The overwhelming identification of family as the primary deterrence factor (78%–92%) indicates that active parental and family involvement must be integrated into probation supervision and community-based rehabilitation planning.
