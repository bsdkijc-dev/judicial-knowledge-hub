Policy Analysis

Background & Legal Mandate

  • Officially published by the Ministry of Justice, Japan in January 2025 as the 7th annual report under Article 10 of the Act on Promotion of Prevention of Recidivism (Act No. 104 of 2016).
  • Serves as the first White Paper evaluating the execution of the Second Plan for Promotion of Recidivism Prevention (approved by Cabinet in March 2023).

Special Feature & Desistance Factors

  • Focuses on "Process of Desistance from Crime and Delinquency - Restart of Life Told by Persons Concerned and Supporters" (当事者と支援者が語る人生のリスタート).
  • Analyzes narrative qualitative interviews of repeat offenders and supporters (Cooperative Employers, Probation Offices, Half-way Houses, NPOs).
  • Refines the desistance analytical framework into 5 Primary Desistance Factors:
  1. Motivation for recovery (立ち直りへの動機).
  2. Securing basic needs & employment/educational stability (衣食住の確保と仕事・学業の安定), including a "psychological place" (mental housing).
  3. Building healthy interpersonal relationships (良好な人間関係の構築).
  4. Developing self-esteem & a sense of usefulness (自己肯定感及び自己有用感の形成).
  5. Aligning needs between offenders and supporters (当事者と支援者によるニーズの共有 — *new factor*).

Key Statistical Metrics

  • General Penal Code Recidivist Ratio (2023): Out of 183,269 total cleared penal code offenders, 86,099 were re-offenders, yielding a 47.0% recidivist ratio.
  • 2-Year Re-imprisonment Rate: The national target (<=16% by 2021) was surpassed; 2022 released inmates achieved 13.0% (2,218 out of 17,116 inmates).
  • Parole vs. Full-Term Release Disparity: The 2-year re-imprisonment rate for full-term released inmates stood at 20.2% (1,306 out of 6,480), whereas parolees recorded a substantially lower 8.6% (912 out of 10,636).
  • Breakdown by Offense & Demographics (2022 Inmates): Theft (19.4%), Elderly 65+ (18.3%), Injury/Assault (11.5%), Stimulants Control Act (10.6%), Sex Offenses (6.2%), Female (10.8%).
  • Juvenile Training School Re-entry Rate (2022): 2-year re-entry rate into Juvenile Training Schools reached 9.1% (124 youth), and combined youth re-entry + adult prison commitment reached 10.1% (138 youth).

Employment, Housing, & Volunteer Support

  • Cooperative Employers: 24,969 registered firms, with 912 actively employing 1,287 former offenders/juveniles.
  • Unemployment & Homelessness: Unemployed former offenders at the end of probation supervision stood at 25.1% (5,079 people). Inmates without a residence upon full-term release reached 16.0% (2,591 people).
  • Special Welfare Adjustment & Entrance Support: Welfare adjustments completed for 796 elderly/disabled/mentally ill inmates upon release. Entrance Support assisted 345 detained suspects/defendants from April to November 2023.
  • Volunteer Probation Officers (VPOs): Active VPOs stood at 46,584 (88.7% fulfillment against the 52,500 target quota).
  • Local Recidivism Prevention Plans: 47/47 Prefectures (100%), 20/20 Designated Cities (100%), and 748 Municipalities established local plans as of April 1, 2024.

Regulatory Reforms & Innovative Programs

  • Incarceration Sentence (拘禁刑): Scheduled for enforcement in June 2025 to replace imprisonment with/without hard labor, optimizing rehabilitative guidance flexibility.
  • Ichihara Youth Correctional Center: Implemented transferred treatment for young offenders (<26 years) with intellectual/developmental disabilities since November 2023.
  • EBPM & Behavioral Nudges: Randomized Controlled Trials (RCT) utilizing nudge brochures to boost employment guidance participation across 5 prisons won the Best Nudge Award.
  • Ristapo Desks (りすたぽ): Regional consultation posts for crime and delinquency concerns launched by Probation Offices in December 2023.

Interpretations & Structural Inferences

  • Demonstrates a paradigm shift from punitive isolation toward Social Inclusion (地域による包摂), where desistance relies on seamless support across all judicial stages.
  • Because full-term release re-imprisonment (20.2%) is more than double that of parolees (8.6%), expanding parole schemes and probation supervision is vital to ensure transitional community support for all released inmates.
