JPW-SITUATION-005

By Cecep Mustafa, Indonesian Judicial College (IJC) · revision 1007, 2026-09-16 · wiki source · Markdown

Preliminary Examination of the Situation in Colombia (ICC) is an independent legal and factual assessment conducted by the Office of the Prosecutor (OTP) of the International Criminal Court since June 2004, concerning alleged crimes against humanity and war crimes committed in Colombian territory. The examination sits at Phase 3 (Admissibility), focused on assessing the complementarity of national judicial proceedings, including the Special Jurisdiction for Peace (JEP) and the Office of the Attorney General (FGN).

Summary

The examination centers on whether Colombia's domestic judicial mechanisms — the ordinary justice system, the Justice and Peace framework, and the transitional JEP — are conducting genuine proceedings against those most responsible, sufficient to render ICC intervention unnecessary.

Legal Basis

  • Rome Statute: Article 7 (Crimes Against Humanity), Article 8 (War Crimes), Article 12 (Jurisdiction), Article 15 (Communications), Article 53(1) (Preliminary Examination), and Article 124 (Declaration Deferring War Crimes Jurisdiction).
  • Colombia ratified the Rome Statute on 5 August 2002 (crimes against humanity jurisdiction effective 1 November 2002; war crimes jurisdiction effective 1 November 2009, following the Article 124 declaration).

Background

Colombia experienced a non-international armed conflict involving government forces, paramilitary groups (AUC), and rebel groups (FARC-EP and ELN). The creation of the JEP under the 2016 Peace Agreement is the central focus of the complementarity analysis.

Objectives

  • Assess whether national judicial proceedings meet the admissibility criteria under Article 53(1) of the Rome Statute.
  • Develop a referential benchmarking framework to determine whether to close the preliminary examination or open a formal investigation.

Key Provisions

OTP's analysis focuses on four principal areas:

  • Promotion and Expansion of Paramilitary Groups: Investigations into civilian third parties (terceros civiles) and non-military state agents.
  • Forced Displacement: Cases under the Justice and Peace Law and JEP Cases No. 002, 004, and 005.
  • Sexual and Gender-Based Crimes: Victim accreditation in JEP Cases No. 002, 004, and 007 (including LGBTI victims).
  • "Falsos Positivos" (False Positives): Investigation of extrajudicial killings across five National Army divisions and JEP Case No. 003.

Implementation

As of November 2020, the JEP had issued 35,015 decisions related to seven macro-cases and recorded 12,625 individuals who had signed submission acts (9,767 former FARC-EP members, 2,733 armed forces members, and 115 non-military state agents). The FGN was managing 2,314 active "falsos positivos" cases against 10,949 military personnel, with 1,749 convictions.

Policy Issues

  • Challenges in inter-institutional coordination among domestic judicial bodies, and alleged de facto suspension of FGN investigations in cases falling under JEP jurisdiction.
  • Rising violence in rural areas and threats against human rights defenders following the Peace Agreement.

Relationships with Other Entities

  • Office of the Prosecutor (ICC): Organ conducting the preliminary examination.
  • Jurisdicción Especial para la Paz (Special Jurisdiction for Peace): Transitional special justice system.
  • Fiscalía General de la Nación (Office of the Attorney General): Ordinary justice enforcement body.
  • James Stewart: ICC Deputy Prosecutor who delivered a presentation on complementarity and transitional justice in Bogotá on 29 October 2020.
  • Rodrigo Pérez Alzate (alias "Julián Bolívar"): Former leader of the *Bloque Central Bolívar* paramilitary group, facing charges over 702 incidents of forced displacement and 84 counts of gender-based violence under the Justice and Peace Law system.
  • David Char Navas: Former Colombian congressman whose case is handled by the JEP's Chamber for the Definition of Legal Situations, in relation to ties with the *Bloque Norte* paramilitary group.
  • Álvaro Ashton Giraldo: Former official undergoing review and evidence collection at the JEP concerning links to paramilitary groups.
  • Ramiro Suárez Corzo: Former official requested by the JEP to submit a detailed truth-contribution commitment regarding alleged killings carried out by the AUC in 2003.

  • Ferdi Raditya Akbar: Not found in the available sources.
