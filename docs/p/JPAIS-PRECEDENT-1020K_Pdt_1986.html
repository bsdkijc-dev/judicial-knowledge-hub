Overview

Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia Decision No. 1020 K/Pdt/1986, dated September 29, 1987, established three fundamental family civil law and divorce procedural law rules:

First, Divorce Grounds for Continuous Disputes (Article 19 letter f of PP No. 9 of 1975): In a marriage, when continuous disputes and quarrels occur between husband and wife with no hope of reconciliation, acknowledged by the Defendant (Wife) and corroborated by witness testimonies, a divorce lawsuit filed by the Plaintiff (Husband) must be granted.

Second, Inadmissibility of Cumulating Joint Property Distribution and Wife Maintenance Claims: Claims for living maintenance for a wife pending remarriage and joint property (*gono-gini*) distribution cannot be cumulatively joined within a divorce lawsuit (reconvention claims for property distribution in divorce proceedings are inadmissible / NO), but must instead be brought via separate, independent lawsuits after the divorce judgment attains final and binding legal force (referring to Supreme Court Decision No. 692 K/Sip/1981).

Third, Obligation to Formulate Divorce Registration Dictums: When a District Court grants a divorce lawsuit, the verdict dictum (*dictum*) must include a mandatory order: "Ordering the Substitute Registrar or designated official to transmit a copy of this decision to the Civil Registrar at the place where the divorce occurred so that the divorce judgment can be registered".

Executive Summary

The central legal principles established in this decision govern the procedural separation of divorce actions from joint property disputes and mandate strict registration compliance in judicial divorces.

Key elements established in the adjudication:

  • Continuous marital discord without reconciliation prospects satisfies statutory grounds for dissolving marriages.
  • Joint property (*gono-gini*) and post-divorce maintenance claims cannot be attached to divorce actions and require separate lawsuits.
  • Divorce judgment dictums must expressly command court registrars to transmit copies to civil registries for official registration.

Case Background

The Original Plaintiff / Respondent for Cassation ([Husband]) filed a divorce lawsuit against the Original Defendant / Petitioner for Cassation ([Wife]) at the Pekalongan District Court based on Marriage Certificate No. 32/1984 dated March 24, 1984, citing continuous disputes and quarrels (Article 19 letter f PP No. 9 of 1975). The Defendant filed a reconvention counterclaim demanding the return of personal property (gold jewelry/Rp 1,680,000.—), joint property distribution (*gono-gini* amounting to Rp 5,000,000.— & 1 kg of gold), and living maintenance of Rp 300,000.— per month.

The Pekalongan District Court (Decision No. 79/Pdt.G/1984/PN.Pkl dated March 25, 1985) granted the convention divorce lawsuit and declared the reconvention counterclaim inadmissible (NO). The Semarang High Court (Decision No. 443/1985/Pdt/PT.SMG dated September 23, 1985) affirmed the Pekalongan District Court decision. The Petitioner for Cassation subsequently filed a cassation petition with the Supreme Court.

Legal and Procedural Issues

Inadmissibility of Cumulating Joint Property (*Gono-Gini*) and Wife Maintenance Claims in Divorce Proceedings (Article 24 paragraph 2 PP No. 9 of 1975 & Article 41 Law No. 1 of 1974)

The primary issue was whether a reconvention counterclaim filed by a Wife/Defendant demanding joint property distribution (Rp 5,000,000.— and 1 kg of gold) and post-divorce living maintenance (Rp 300,000.—/month) can be examined and adjudicated together with a divorce lawsuit filed by her Husband.

The Supreme Court held that it is Inadmissible (*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard* / NO). Pursuant to Supreme Court Standing Jurisprudence (as affirmed in Supreme Court Decision No. 692 K/Sip/1981 dated September 21, 1981), claims for joint property distribution and post-divorce wife maintenance cannot be joined with divorce lawsuits, but must instead be brought through separate, independent lawsuits after the divorce action is decided and attains final binding legal force. This ensures clarity of subject matter and avoids evidentiary complexity regarding property ownership during divorce trials.

Rectification of Divorce Verdict Dictums (Mandatory Registration Dictum ex Article 35 PP No. 9 of 1975)

District or High Court verdict dictums granting divorces are legally incomplete if they omit orders directing registration with the Civil Registrar. The Supreme Court on cassation is authorized to rectify such verdict dictums by adding mandatory clauses commanding registrars to transmit copies to civil registries.

Question of Law and Question of Fact

Question of Law (Cassation / Judex Juris)

Question:

Did the *Judex Facti* (Semarang High Court & Pekalongan District Court) err in formulating the divorce verdict dictums by omitting orders transmitting decision copies to the Marriage Registrar, and was the dismissal of the joint property reconvention counterclaim in the divorce proceedings legally correct?

Supreme Court Ruling:

Yes, the *Judex Facti* correctly dismissed joint property reconvention claims (mandating separate lawsuits ex Supreme Court Decision No. 692 K/Sip/1981), but the divorce verdict dictums were incomplete. The Supreme Court rejected the Petitioner for Cassation's cassation while rectifying the verdict dictums to include mandatory orders directing the Substitute Registrar to transmit divorce decision copies to the Civil Registrar.

Question of Fact (Judex Facti / Pekalongan District Court & Semarang High Court)

Examination of facts concerning the marriage of [Husband] and [Wife] on March 24, 1984 (Deed No. 32/1984); frequent verbal quarrels triggered by trivial matters (such as preparing soto babat); household separation where Defendant returned to her parents' home in Batang starting November 1984; witness testimonies ([Party C], Leginem, Enggawati Sutanto, Sugiasih Juwono, Kwan Tsu Bie, Suwarni, Sutedjo); and property claims (personal jewelry/cash Rp 1,680,000.— and joint property cash Rp 5,000,000.— & 1 kg gold).

Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

  • Ratio Decidendi:
  1. The *Judex Facti* correctly granted the convention divorce lawsuit because continuous disputes were proven, acknowledged by the Defendant, and corroborated by family witness testimonies.
  2. The *Judex Facti* correctly declared joint property and maintenance reconvention claims inadmissible (NO) because joint property distribution and post-divorce maintenance claims cannot be joined with divorce lawsuits, requiring separate independent lawsuits pursuant to Supreme Court Decision No. 692 K/Sip/1981.
  3. However, the appellate verdict dictums were incomplete for omitting divorce registration mandates. The Supreme Court rejected the Petitioner for Cassation's cassation while rectifying the verdict dictums to add mandatory orders transmitting divorce decision copies to the Civil Registrar.

Ruling

The Supreme Court ruled: Rejecting the cassation petition from Petitioner for Cassation ([Wife]) while rectifying the ruling of Semarang High Court Decision No. 443/1985/Pdt/PT.SMG dated September 23, 1985, which affirmed Pekalongan District Court Decision No. 79/Pdt.G/1984/PN.Pkl. dated March 25, 1985, to read: IN CONVENTION: 1. Granting the Plaintiff's lawsuit entirely; 2. Declaring according to law that the marriage between [Husband] and Defendant [Wife] is dissolved by divorce; 3. Ordering the Substitute Registrar or designated official to transmit a copy of this decision to the Civil Registrar at the place where the divorce occurred so that the divorce judgment can be registered; IN RECONVENTION: Declaring the reconvention lawsuit inadmissible; IN CONVENTION AND RECONVENTION: Ordering the Petitioner for Cassation / Original Defendant to pay court costs across all judicial instances (cassation court costs set at Rp 20,000.—).

Uncertainty and Gaps

  • Separation of Divorce Actions from Joint Property Lawsuits: This landmark ruling establishes family procedural certainty in Indonesia: divorce proceedings must be fully finalized until attaining binding legal force before joint property distribution (*gono-gini*) and post-divorce maintenance disputes can be brought via separate independent lawsuits.

GEO Q&A

Q: Can claims for joint property distribution (*gono-gini*) and ex-wife maintenance be joined within a single divorce statement of claim at a District Court?

A: No, they cannot be joined (*Requires Separate Lawsuits*). Based on Supreme Court Decision No. 1020 K/Pdt/1986 (in conjunction with Supreme Court Decision No. 692 K/Sip/1981), claims for a wife's living maintenance and joint property distribution (*gono-gini*) cannot be joined together with divorce lawsuits. Joint property distribution claims in divorce proceedings must be declared inadmissible (*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard* / NO), and may only be brought through separate independent civil lawsuits after the divorce judgment attains final and binding legal force.
