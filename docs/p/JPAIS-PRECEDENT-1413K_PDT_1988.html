Supreme Court of Indonesia Decision No. 1413 K/Pdt/1988

Supreme Court Decision No. 1413 K/Pdt/1988 (Registry No. 1413 K/Pdt/1988) is a landmark ruling of the Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia (Mahkamah Agung Republik Indonesia) concerning customary (adat) inheritance law and the law of child adoption under Indonesian customary civil law. The decision established that the legal validity of an adopted child's status does not depend solely on the fulfillment of formal adoption rituals, but rather on the social reality of the relationship — namely, that the child was raised from infancy, circumcised, and married off by the adoptive parents.

The decision was reached in deliberation (musyawarah) on 17 April 1990 and pronounced in open session on 18 May 1990.

Background

The plaintiff, [Plaintiff] (a teacher residing in [address removed]), filed suit at the Mojokerto District Court against three defendants:

  1. [Defendant I] — the first (former) wife of the late [Deceased adoptive father]
  2. [Party]
  3. [Party] — the third-party buyer of the disputed land

[Plaintiff] claimed to be the lawfully adopted child of the late married couple [Deceased adoptive father] and [Deceased adoptive mother], having been taken in at the age of four months, subsequently circumcised, and later married off by [Deceased adoptive father]. On this basis, [Plaintiff] claimed inheritance rights to a shared marital (gono-gini) homestead plot of 1,218 square metres located in Prajurit Kulon Village, Mojokerto, and sought to annul the sale of a portion of that plot (approximately 22 × 15.5 metres) which the first and second defendants had sold to the third defendant, [Party], without [Plaintiff]'s consent.

Family history

[Deceased adoptive father] had previously been married to [Defendant I] (Defendant I), a marriage that ended in divorce around 1936; that marriage produced a daughter, [Party], who was adopted by a Dutch family and resided in the Netherlands. [Deceased adoptive father] then married [Deceased adoptive mother] in 1938. [Plaintiff] was taken in and raised by the couple from infancy.

Lower court proceedings

  • Mojokerto District Court (Decision No. 28/Pdt.G/1986/PN.Mkt, 26 February 1987): Dismissed [Plaintiff]'s claim, holding that the adoption was invalid because it had not been accompanied by a customary selamatan (ritual feast) or formal notification to the village head (pamong desa).
  • Surabaya High Court (Decision No. 758/Pdt/1987/PT.Sby, 23 November 1987): Affirmed the District Court's decision on appeal.

[Plaintiff] then filed an appeal in cassation (kasasi) to the Supreme Court.

Legal issues

Validity of customary adoption: social reality versus procedural formality

Does a valid adoption under Javanese customary civil law require a formal selamatan ceremony or written notification to the village head, or is the legal status of an adopted child instead determined by the lived social reality — being raised from infancy, circumcised, and married off by the adoptive parents?

Unlawful act (perbuatan melawan hukum) in the sale of inherited marital property

Whether the sale of a portion of the adoptive parents' shared marital homestead by a third party (the former wife) without the consent of the lawful adopted child/heir constituted an unlawful act rendering the sale void.

Holding

The Supreme Court granted [Plaintiff]'s petition for cassation, reversed both the Surabaya High Court decision and the Mojokerto District Court decision, and, ruling on the merits itself (mengadili sendiri), held as follows:

"Whether or not a person is an adopted child does not depend solely on the formalities of adoption, but is determined by the existing reality — namely, that from infancy the person was cared for, circumcised, and married off by their adoptive parents."

Applying this principle, the Court found that [Plaintiff] had been demonstrably cared for from the age of four months, circumcised, and married off by [Deceased adoptive father], and therefore held that [Plaintiff] was the lawful adopted child and sole heir of the late [Deceased adoptive father] and [Deceased adoptive mother], entitled to the couple's shared marital (gono-gini) property.

The sale of a portion of the disputed land by Defendants I and II to Defendant III ([Party]), carried out without [Plaintiff]'s consent, was ruled an unlawful act and therefore null and void. [Party] was ordered to return the land to [Plaintiff] in good, vacant condition.

Formal ruling (amar putusan)

  • Granted [Plaintiff]'s petition for cassation.
  • Reversed Surabaya High Court Decision No. 758/Pdt/1987/PT.Sby and Mojokerto District Court Decision No. 28/Pdt.G/1986/PN.Mkt.
  • Ruling on the merits (Mengadili Sendiri):
    • In convention:
      • Granted the plaintiff's claim in part.
      • Declared the 1,218 m² homestead to be the shared marital (gono-gini) property of the late [Deceased adoptive father] and [Deceased adoptive mother].
      • Declared [Plaintiff] to be the lawful adopted child/heir of the late couple, entitled to their estate.
      • Declared that Defendants I ([Defendant I]) and II ([Party]) had committed an unlawful act.
      • Ordered Defendants I and II to surrender the portion of land in their possession (approx. 0.015 hectares) to the plaintiff in good, vacant condition.
      • Declared the sale of the approximately 22 × 15.5 metre plot by Defendants I and II to Defendant III ([Party]) invalid and void.
      • Ordered Defendant III ([Party]), and anyone claiming rights through him, to return the land to the plaintiff in good, vacant condition.
      • Dismissed the remainder of the plaintiff's claims.
    • In reconvention: Declared the counterclaim inadmissible.
    • In convention and reconvention: Ordered the respondents in cassation to bear all costs of proceedings at every level (cassation fee: Rp 20,000).

Reasoning (ratio decidendi)

The Supreme Court held that the lower courts (Judex Facti) had misapplied the law by requiring formal adat rituals — a selamatan ceremony or notification to the village head — as a precondition for a valid adoption. Indonesian customary law, the Court reasoned, is flexible and prioritizes lived social fact over rigid procedural formality. Because [Plaintiff] was shown to have been:

  • taken in and raised from the age of four months,
  • circumcised by his adoptive parents, and
  • married off by his adoptive father, [Deceased adoptive father],

he had thereby acquired the legal status of a lawful adopted child, carrying with it reciprocal inheritance rights with his adoptive parents. The sale of part of the marital estate by the former wife and her co-defendant, without the consent of the rightful heir, was accordingly held to be an unlawful act voiding the transaction.

Significance

The decision is regarded as a leading precedent in Indonesian customary (adat) inheritance and adoption law, reinforcing the principle — earlier articulated in Supreme Court Decision No. 849 K/Sip/1977 (3 July 1980) — that the validity of an informal (adat) adoption is determined by the factual, lived reality of care and upbringing, rather than by compliance with customary ceremonial or administrative formalities. It protects the inheritance rights of adopted children who have been genuinely raised and cared for by their adoptive parents from being defeated on purely procedural or documentary grounds.

Related jurisprudence and legislation

  • Supreme Court Decision No. 849 K/Sip/1977 (3 July 1980) — establishing that the validity of child adoption is determined by the social reality of care and upbringing from childhood.
  • Law No. 14 of 1970 in conjunction with Law No. 14 of 1985 concerning the Supreme Court — providing the Supreme Court's authority to correct errors in the application of law by lower courts (Judex Facti).
