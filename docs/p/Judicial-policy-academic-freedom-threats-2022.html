By Cecep Mustafa, Indonesian Judicial College (IJC) · revision 1473, 2026-09-18 · wiki source · Markdown

The Protection of Academic Freedom and Internal Threats Policy Framework (officially centered on the editorial work New Threats to Academic Freedom) is a public law, human rights, and judicial policy framework that evaluates the constitutional foundations of academic freedom in Europe, diagnoses emerging internal and social threats within higher education institutions, critiques traditional judicial tests, and proposes transnational protective mechanisms 1.

Overview

Authored by Giacinto della Cananea, Full Professor of Administrative Law at Bocconi University in Milan, this policy analysis highlights the shifting nature of threats to academic freedom in Europe and internationally 2. Although European universities were historically founded during the Middle Ages (Bologna, Oxford, Paris, Coimbra, Vienna, Heidelberg) on the right to teach and learn without interference from governmental or societal authorities, modern threats increasingly originate from within universities themselves (insiders: academics, students, administrators) and wealthy donors bowing to pressure from vociferous minorities 3.

Legal Basis

The framework relies on key supranational legal instruments and judicial precedent:

  • Article 13 EU Charter of Fundamental Rights (CFR): Guarantees that the arts and scientific research shall be free of constraint and explicitly safeguards academic freedom 4.
  • Freedom of Expression Clause of the ECHR: Interpreted by the European Court of Human Rights (ECtHR) in Kula v. Turkey (App. No. 20233/06, 2018) to establish that the allowable limits of criticism are wider for academic speech regarding their own institutions 5.
  • CJEU Jurisprudence: In European Commission v. Hungary (Case C-66/18, 2020), the Court of Justice of the European Union affirmed that academic freedom encompasses institutional establishment, research, and teaching, which cannot be restricted disproportionately 6.

Background

Historical infringements on academic freedom in Italy include landmark cases such as Cordero and Lombardi Vallauri 7. However, the editorial notes the emergence of novel internal threats, where universities cancel invitations to international speakers or dismiss faculty members due to social pressure from internal groups or financial donor considerations 8.

Objectives

  • Identify insidious internal threats (*insiders*) to academic freedom originating within university governance structures 9.
  • Evaluate the structural shortcomings of traditional constitutional tests (*reasonable speaker / reasonable listener tests*) derived from US First Amendment jurisprudence 10.
  • Formulate new transnational protective instruments to safeguard the intellectual independence of universities 11.

Scope

The scope encompasses public law, academic freedom of expression, European human rights law, and administrative university governance across Europe and the United States 12.

Key Provisions & Critique of Judicial Tests

  • Flaws of Reasonable Person Tests: The *reasonable speaker* or *reasonable listener* test risks banning "heretical" or unpopular ideas simply because they offend majority or vocal minority public sentiment 13.
  • Failure to Account for Preventive Measures: Traditional judicial tests fail to give adequate weight to preparatory steps taken by educators, such as issuing advance trigger warnings before presenting sensitive material 14.

Implementation

Evaluated through four real/hypothetical case scenarios:

  1. Awarding a doctorate to the son of a non-democratic ruler following a substantial financial donation 15.
  2. A university rector revoking an invitation to an ambassador (e.g., from Israel) out of fear of sparking student political debates after receiving multimillion-euro grants 16.
  3. Cancellation of a public lecture by religious authorities due to intense internal protests 17.
  4. Dismissal of a history lecturer for displaying religious artwork despite providing advance warnings to students 18.

Policy Issues

  • Neglect of Freedom by University Insiders: University administrators and stakeholders assigning lower priority to academic freedom compared to avoiding controversy from vociferous minorities 19.
  • Inability of Courts to Reach Indirect Threats: The insufficiency of traditional judicial remedies in curbing discretionary cancellations by university rectors or administrators 20.

Proposed Reforms

The editorial proposes two novel protective mechanisms:

  1. Formulation of precise protective standards by independent transnational bodies such as the Venice Commission 21.
  2. Establishment of a network of national university funding agencies to share information on academic freedom violations, generating negative public reputational impacts for non-compliant universities 22.
