Populism and intra-party democracy is an empirical comparative political science and public policy research paper authored by Tobias Böhmelt (University of Essex), Lawrence Ezrow (University of Essex), and Roni Lehrer (University of Mannheim & Goethe University Frankfurt), published in the European Journal of Political Research (EJPR) in 2022.

Summary

This study examines how populism relates to the internal organization of political parties, specifically intra-party democracy (IPD). Using data from the Populism and Political Parties Expert Survey (POPPA; Meijers & Zaslove, 2021), covering more than 200 political parties across 26 European countries (2017/2018), the study tests two competing theoretical approaches: the ideational perspective, which predicts that populist parties will be more internally democratic, and the leadership/organizational perspective, which predicts the opposite. The findings show that populist parties systematically have less democratic internal structures and tend to adopt personalized leadership styles [1, 9, 17–21].

Background

Populism has become a central topic in the media, academia, and policymaking across Europe. Political science literature is divided into two main views on populism: the ideational view, which defines populism as a thin-centered ideology built around anti-elitism and people-centrism, and the leadership view, which defines populism as a personalistic political-organizational strategy. Large-scale quantitative research was needed to test whether populism's ideological claims about "people-power" are actually reflected in parties' internal structures.

Objectives

1. Test whether a party's populism score is positively or negatively associated with its level of intra-party democracy. 2. Test the effect of populism on personalized leadership. 3. Analyze whether the effect of populism on party organization differs between left-wing and right-wing parties.

Scope

The research analyzes POPPA expert survey data (2017/2018) covering 229 to 236 political parties across 26 to 28 European countries represented in parliament.

Key Provisions / Findings

  • Decline in Intra-Party Democracy (IPD): The populism score has a statistically significant negative association with intra-party democracy (β = -0.43, p < 0.05) [17–19]. Moving from the lowest to the highest level of populism reduces the predicted IPD score from ~7 to ~2 on a 0–10 scale.
  • Increase in Personalized Leadership: The populism score has a statistically significant positive association with personalized leadership (β = +0.38, p < 0.05) [21–25].
  • Left-Wing vs. Right-Wing Comparison: The negative relationship between populism and intra-party democracy holds for both left-wing and right-wing parties (β = -0.33, p < 0.05 for left-wing parties). However, left-wing populist parties are generally somewhat more internally inclusive than right-wing populists, owing to a more inclusive view of society.
  • Support for the Leadership Perspective: These findings directly confirm the predictions of the leadership perspective (Weyland 2001, 2021; Kenny 2019; Pappas 2016; Urbinati 2017) and reject the assumption that populist ideology automatically produces democratic party organization.

Implementation

The methodology was implemented through hierarchical linear models with random intercepts for country and party family, controlling for median voter position (Eurobarometer). Replication data are sourced from the POPPA project (Meijers & Zaslove, 2021).

Policy Issues

1. The contradiction between populist parties' public rhetoric celebrating "the voice of the people" and their authoritarian, centralized internal practices. 2. The risk of power concentration in charismatic leaders, which reduces internal party accountability.

Reform / Implications

1. Incorporating indicators of internal party organizational structure into models of coalition formation, cabinet portfolio allocation, and party survival. 2. Recognizing that undemocratic organizational structures may be strategically adopted by populist parties to project an image of party unity to voters.
