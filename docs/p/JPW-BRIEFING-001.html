JPW-BRIEFING-001

By Cecep Mustafa, Indonesian Judicial College (IJC) · revision 1151, 2026-09-16 · wiki source · Markdown

Information Package for the Fifth Diplomatic Briefing of the International Criminal Court (Paket Informasi Taklimat Diplomatik Kelima Mahkamah Pidana Internasional) is the official information package and institutional operational report published by the International Criminal Court (ICC) for its Fifth Diplomatic Briefing held on 26 October 2005 in The Hague [2, 14–16, 23].

Summary

This document outlines operational updates regarding ICC investigative activities in Northern Uganda, the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC), and Darfur (Sudan). Key updates include the issuance and unsealing of arrest warrants in the Uganda situation, budget allocation distributions, and inter-organ cooperation [2, 14–16, 23].

Legal Basis

  • Rome Statute of the International Criminal Court: Legal framework governing investigations, cooperation, and the issuance of arrest warrants by Pre-Trial Chamber II.

Background

The Fifth Diplomatic Briefing was convened on 26 October 2005 to provide diplomatic corps members and state representatives with transparent updates concerning judicial proceedings, investigative milestones, and administrative management.

Objectives

  • Provide transparent updates on situations under investigation (Uganda, DRC, and Darfur) [2, 14–16, 23].
  • Outline the progression of warrant applications and unsealing procedures [15–16].
  • Strengthen field presence, security, and witness protection frameworks [14–15].

Key Provisions

  • **Uganda Situation**: Application for arrest warrants against five individuals filed on 6 May 2005, issued under seal on 8 Juli 2005, and unsealed on 13 October 2005 by Pre-Trial Chamber II [15–16].
  • **Witness Protection**: Close collaboration between the Office of the Prosecutor (OTP) and the Victims and Witnesses Unit (VWU).
  • **Budget Allocations**: Financial distribution dedicated to operations in the DRC (33%), Uganda (20.3%), and Darfur (20%).

Implementation

The OTP conducted field missions, witness interviews, and executed joint training workshops with the Registry for 64 local government representatives in Northern Uganda [14–15].

Policy Issues

  • Managing witness security risks in volatile conflict zones.
  • Executing arrest warrants following the public unsealing of indictments [14–16].
