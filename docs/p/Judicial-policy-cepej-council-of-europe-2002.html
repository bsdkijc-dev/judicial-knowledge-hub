European Commission for the Efficiency of Justice (*Commission Européenne Pour l'Efficacité de la Justice* – CEPEJ) is an intergovernmental body headquartered in Strasbourg, France, established by the Committee of Ministers of the Council of Europe. CEPEJ is tasked with gathering knowledge, facilitating the exchange of operational analysis, and improving the efficiency, quality, and timeliness of national justice systems across the Council of Europe's 47 member states (including all 27 EU member states), with the ultimate goal of reducing case backlogs at the European Court of Human Rights (ECtHR) without lowering standards of legal protection.

Summary

Established around 2002, CEPEJ was formed in response to a workload crisis at the ECtHR, where the number of applications surged from 8,400 cases in 1999 to 57,200 cases in 2009, with a total backlog reaching 119,300 applications. In its operations, CEPEJ established the Centre for the Study and Analysis of Judicial Time Use Research Network (SATURN) and a Working Group on Quality of Justice. CEPEJ employs a methodology of close collaboration among academics, judges, and judicial civil servants, led by its Bureau (Fausto de Santis and John Stacey) and Secretariat (Stéphane Leyenberger).

Legal Basis

  • Resolution of the Committee of Ministers of the Council of Europe: The basis for establishing CEPEJ as a judicial efficiency commission headquartered in Strasbourg.
  • European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR): The international legal framework guaranteeing the right to a fair and timely trial.
  • Protocol No. 14 to the European Convention on Human Rights: Entered into force on 10 June, enabling the ECtHR to adapt its internal organization and proceedings to manage a massive volume of applications.

Background & Policy Issues

The backlog crisis at the ECtHR threatened the effectiveness of Europe's human rights protection system. ECtHR jurisprudence consistently affirms that, while timeliness and fairness of trial fall under ECHR oversight, the internal organization of courts remains within the sovereign authority of individual member states. CEPEJ was created to bridge this gap by helping modernize judicial organization at the national level.

ECtHR Application Backlog Statistics (1999–2009)

  • 1999: 8,400 applications allocated.
  • 2003: 27,200 applications allocated; ~65,000 applications pending.
  • 2009: 57,200 applications allocated; total backlog rose to 119,300 applications.

Scope & Key Initiatives

1. Judicial Time Use Research Network (SATURN): A center for analytical study of case-processing time and duration in courts. 2. Working Group on Quality of Justice: Develops quality standards for judicial management and service delivery. 3. Tripartite Collaboration: Fosters ongoing cooperation among academics, judges, and Ministry of Justice officials. 4. Public Repository: All CEPEJ analyses and guidelines are published openly at `www.coe.int/cepej`.

Policy Issues

  • The need to balance improved judicial administrative efficiency with maintaining high standards of legal protection.
  • The diversity of legal infrastructures across 47 member states, most of which follow civil law traditions.
