The Troika’s Portuguese Ministry of Justice Civil Enforcement Policy Framework is a public policy evaluation framework and reform model for resolving civil enforcement actions. It was formulated by Prof. Dr. Pedro Miguel Alves Ribeiro Correia and Susana Antas Videira (2015) in the International Journal for Court Administration (IJCA). The framework is based on the implementation of the Memorandum of Understanding (MoU 2011) between Portugal and the Troika (European Commission, European Central Bank, International Monetary Fund). It combines the new Civil Procedure Code reforms (New CPC 2013), electronic bank account attachment, the extinction of unviable cases, and the deployment of specialized operational teams to empirically reduce the judicial backlog across 217 first-instance courts in Portugal [1–16].

Summary

Published by Prof. Dr. Pedro Miguel Alves Ribeiro Correia and Susana Antas Videira in the International Journal for Court Administration (IJCA), this policy framework reviews the successful experiment of Portugal's judicial sector reforms under the conditions of the Troika's international financial assistance (2011–2014). Analyzing a 78-month sample (2008–2014) across 217 court districts, the empirical study proves that the reforms introduced by the New Civil Procedure Code (Law 41/2013) and Decree-Law 4/2013 successfully slashed a backlog of over 210,000 cases. Through the automation of debtor bank account attachments (Articles 749(6) & 780 CPC) and the extinction of cases lacking asset prospects (Article 849 CPC), the judicial clearance rate soared from 71.2% to 116.4%, while the disposition time was dramatically reduced by 743 days (more than 2 years) [10–12, 38–40].

Legal Basis

  • Memorandum of Understanding on Specific Economic Policy Conditionality (MoU 2011): The international agreement of May 17, 2011, between Portugal and the Troika (IMF/EC/ECB) mandating judicial sector reforms.
  • Decreto-Lei n.º 4/2013 (January 11, 2013): Government regulations regarding temporary measures to clear the old case backlog [15–16].
  • Lei n.º 41/2013 (June 26, 2013 - New Civil Procedure Code / CPC): New provisions relating to electronic bank account attachment (Articles 749(6) & 780), case extinction (Article 849), and the prohibition of dual filings (Article 85) [9–12].

Background

The global economic crisis triggered widespread over-indebtedness in Portugal, leading to a surge in civil enforcement lawsuits that amassed a backlog of over 1.2 million pending cases by 2012. The Troika's financial bailout required the clearance of this backlog to restore legal certainty for the economy [7–8, 35–37].

Objectives

1. Drastically reduce the civil enforcement backlog [3–5, 8]. 2. Automate asset attachment through the national electronic banking system [9–10]. 3. Extinguish unviable enforcement actions. 4. Accelerate the disposition time without compromising the legal guarantees for debtors.

Scope

This policy covers the handling of civil enforcement cases across 217 first-instance courts (concelhos) throughout Portugal.

Key Provisions

  • Electronic Bank Account Attachment (Articles 749(6) & 780 CPC):
    • The Bank of Portugal is mandated to provide debtor account data to enforcement agents for electronic blocking to settle debts.
  • New Grounds for Case Extinction (Article 849 CPC):
    • Automatic extinction if no attachable assets are found within 3 months, if salary/income attachments flow directly to the creditor, or if total freezing occurs due to competing creditors [11–13].
  • Revoking the Enforcement Strength of Private Documents:
    • Private documents without notarial authentication no longer hold direct enforcement power, serving to protect debtors.
  • Establishment of Specialized Operational Teams:
    • The deployment of specialized teams in Lisbon, Porto, Gaia, Maia, and Oeiras to handle debts under €10,000.

Implementation

  • Implementation of the New CPC (September 2013): Operationalization of electronic bank account freezing and automatic case extinction across all district courts.
  • Mann-Whitney Statistical Evaluation: The testing of 6 primary variables that proved a significant reduction in disposition time and procedural balance [37–40].

Policy Problems

  • Commercial and Family Over-indebtedness: The burden of mass debt collection stemming from past credit facilitation [7–8].
  • Unviable Enforcement Cases: Thousands of pending cases lacking debtor assets that overburdened court capacity.

Reform / Change

  • Transformation of civil enforcement from a manual bureaucratic system to electronic automation based on banking integration [9–10].
  • The adoption of objective, statistical data-driven public policy evaluation within judicial administration [1, 44–45].
