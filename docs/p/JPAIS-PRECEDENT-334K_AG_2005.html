Overview

Supreme Court Decision Reg. No. 334 K/AG/2005 dated February 22, 2006, establishes important Islamic inheritance jurisprudence regarding formal procedural requirements for inheritance lawsuits: "Because both the substitute heirs and the heirs being replaced have passed away, the exact time of death of each must be clearly stated both in the statement of claim and in the court's factual findings (*konstatering*). If not, the lawsuit must be declared inadmissible (*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard* / NO) for being vague (*obscuur libel*)."

Case Background

The Propositus / Original Heir ([Deceased]) passed away in 1976 and during his lifetime contracted three marriages:

  • First wife (Dg. Tija) gave birth to a son named [Party B] (who predeceased him, leaving behind wife [Party C] and two children: [Heir] and [Heir]);
  • Second wife (Dg. Lu'mu) gave birth to a son named H. [Party A] (Defendant / Petitioner for Cassation);
  • Third wife (Dg. Puji) gave birth to four children: Bahar, Sugi, Haris, and Said (Plaintiffs IV to VII).

The Plaintiffs ([Party C], [Heir], [Heir], Bahar, Sugi, Haris, and Said) filed a lawsuit against [Party A] at the Makassar Religious Court concerning the unilateral control and sale of 8 land parcels constituting the estate of the late [Deceased] in Makassar. The Makassar Religious Court (Decision No. 261/Pdt.G/2003/PA.Mks. dated September 10, 2003) granted the Plaintiffs' lawsuit in part. The Makassar High Religious Court (Decision No. 114/Pdt.G/2003/PTA.Mks. dated July 6, 2004) upheld the Makassar Religious Court judgment. The Defendant ([Party A]) filed a cassation petition to the Supreme Court.

Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

  • Ratio Decidendi:
  1. The *Judex Facti* (Makassar Religious Court and Makassar High Religious Court) erred in applying the law / exceeded their judicial authority.
  2. Neither the factual foundation (*posita*) of the claim nor the court's factual findings (*konstatering*) revealed the year in which [Party B] passed away; if [Party B] predeceased the propositus [Deceased] (before 1976), positioning [Party B]'s children ([Heir] and [Heir]) as substitute heirs (*ahli waris pengganti*) is correct (entitling them to a 21/240 share).
  3. Conversely, if [Party B] was still alive at the time the propositus [Deceased] passed away in 1976, [Party B] was a direct heir, meaning his children inherited his share from him (yielding a 42/240 share rather than 21/240).
  4. The *Judex Facti* erred in adjudicating based on uncertain status regarding whether [Party B] was a direct heir or whether his children acted as substitute heirs; the decisive factor is whether [Party B] was alive when the propositus [Deceased] passed away in 1976.
  5. Because the date of death of the heirs or substitute heirs was not clearly stated in the claim or the court's findings, the lawsuit is deemed vague and must be declared inadmissible (*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard* / NO).
  • Ruling: Granting the cassation petition from Petitioner for Cassation: [Party A]; Annulling the Makassar High Religious Court decision dated July 6, 2004, No. 114/Pdt.G/2003/PTA.Mks. and the Makassar Religious Court decision dated September 10, 2003, No. 261/Pdt.G/2003/PA.Mks.; and Independently Adjudicating: Declaring the Plaintiffs' lawsuit inadmissible (*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard*); and ordering the Respondents for Cassation to pay court costs across all levels of jurisdiction, with cassation costs assessed at Rp 500,000 (five hundred thousand rupiahs). *(Decided in the deliberation meeting of the Supreme Court on Wednesday, January 18, 2006, and pronounced in an open session on Wednesday, February 22, 2006, by Drs. H. Andi Syamsu Alam, S.H., M.H. [Junior Chairman of the Supreme Court / Presiding Judge], Drs. H. Habiburrahman, M.Hum., and Dr. H. Abdul Manan, S.H., S.IP., M.Hum. [Associate Justices]).*
