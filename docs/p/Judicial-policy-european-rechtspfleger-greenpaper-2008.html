The Green Paper for a European Rechtspfleger is a policy framework, qualification standard, and model position description formulated by the European Union of Rechtspfleger (EUR) (completed in November 2008) to standardize the position of the European Rechtspfleger across member states of the European Union and the Council of Europe. Its purpose is to independently transfer routine judicial tasks from judges to independent legal administrative officers to improve judicial efficiency and guarantee the right to a fair trial within a reasonable time (Article 6 of the ECHR) [1, 3–8, 13, 15].

Summary

Published by Thomas Kappl and the EUR executive board in the International Journal for Court Administration (IJCA), this Green Paper is designed to spark academic and public discussion regarding the establishment of standardized qualifications for the European Rechtspfleger. Responding to the surge in caseloads across European courts, the document proposes the independent delegation of routine judicial tasks to higher-level judicial civil servants possessing a minimum of 3 years of legal education. This transfer has proven to free judges to focus on complex cases, thereby producing higher-quality judgments.

Legal Basis

  • Model Statute for a European Rechtspfleger (1995 EUR Congress, Spain): The primary foundation for the professional framework of the European Rechtspfleger.
  • Recommendation No. R (86) 12 of the Committee of Ministers of the Council of Europe: Recommendation regarding measures to prevent and reduce the excessive workload in the courts.
  • Article 6 of the European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR): The guarantee of everyone's right to a fair and public hearing within a reasonable time.
  • Recommendation No. R (80) 3 of the Council of Europe: Recommendation concerning teaching, research, and training in the field of "Computers and Law" (Electronic Data Processing - EDP).
  • EC Directive 89/48/EEC: Provisions on a general system for the recognition of higher-education diplomas awarded on completion of professional education and training of at least three years' duration.

Background

As caseloads increase in European courts, judges face serious obstacles as they spend significant time and resources handling administrative and routine judicial tasks. This condition threatens the fulfillment of the Article 6 ECHR mandate. The EUR, an international organization founded in 1967 encompassing associations of higher-level judicial civil servants in 16 European countries and several non-European countries (e.g., Japan, Mali, Morocco, Tunisia), initiated the drafting of the Green Paper. Led by EUR President Thomas Kappl, the EUR Commission drafted this document throughout 2007–2008 and officially submitted it to the Directorate-General for Justice, Freedom and Security of the European Commission in Brussels (Dec 2, 2008) and the CEPEJ Plenary Session in Strasbourg (Dec 11, 2008).

Objectives

1. Liberalize and relieve judges of routine judicial tasks so they can focus on severe cases that generate higher-quality judgments. 2. Standardize minimum educational qualifications (a Bachelor of Law / minimum 3 years of higher education) for prospective European Rechtspflegers across all EU countries. 3. Develop a model position description that can be independently applied across various types and levels of courts. 4. Foster public discussion and consensus among the Ministries of Justice of EU and Council of Europe member states.

Scope

This policy covers the judicial systems in the member states of the European Union and the 47 member states of the Council of Europe.

Key Provisions

  • Judicial Independence of the Rechtspfleger: The Rechtspfleger is an objective and independent officer of the judicature. In making decisions, they are bound only by the law and the principles of justice.
  • Uniform Education Standards: Prospective Rechtspflegers must complete a specialized legal higher education program of at least 3 years (Bachelor of Law or equivalent) based on a standardized curriculum.
  • Model Position Description: Standardizing functions and responsibilities across all levels of courts to optimize the efficiency of legal administration based on a standard EUR questionnaire.
  • Integration of ICT / EDP: Mandatory provision of basic and advanced training in electronic data processing (EDP) for all judicial officers.

Implementation

  • Submission to the European Commission (December 2, 2008): Submitted directly by the EUR Delegation led by Thomas Kappl to Mrs. Lana Papamichalopoulou (Head of Unit, DG Justice) in Brussels in 5 languages (French, English, Italian, Romanian, German) for distribution to all EU Ministries of Justice.
  • Submission to CEPEJ (December 11, 2008): Submitted by EUR Secretary General Adelheid Hell and Jean-Jacques Kuster to CEPEJ President Fausto De Santis during the Plenary Session in Strasbourg.

Policy Problems

  • Variations in Domestic Training Systems: Differences in the duration and content of Rechtspfleger training across EU member states requiring curriculum harmonization.
  • Excessive Judicial Workload: The lack of routine authority delegation causing delays in sentencing and adjudication in several member states.

Reform / Change

  • Harmonization of professional qualifications based on the European diploma equivalency standard (Directive 89/48/EEC).
  • Expansion of the use of electronic data processing (EDP/e-Justice) in executing Rechtspfleger duties in accordance with Recommendation No. R (80) 3.

Relationships with Other Entities

  • CEPEJ / Council of Europe: The intergovernmental forum where the EUR holds official observer status.

Deutsch (Schweizer Hochdeutsch)

Grünbuch für einen Europäischen Rechtspfleger Dieser von der Europäischen Union der Rechtspfleger (EUR) und Thomas Kappl verfasste und im International Journal for Court Administration (IJCA) veröffentlichte Rahmen zielt darauf ab, das Berufsbild des Rechtspflegers in der Europäischen Union und im Europarat zu standardisieren. Das Grünbuch (2008) schlägt die Übertragung von Routineaufgaben von Richtern auf unabhängige Rechtspfleger vor, um die Effizienz der Gerichte zu steigern und das Recht auf ein faires Verfahren innerhalb angemessener Frist (Art. 6 EMRK) zu gewährleisten. Es fordert ein einheitliches dreijähriges Jurastudium und beschreibt eine Modell-Stellenbeschreibung.

  • Rechtsgrundlage: EMRK (Art. 6); Modellstatut 1995; Empfehlungen des Europarates R(86)12, R(80)3; EU-Richtlinie 89/48/EWG.

Français

Livre vert pour un greffier européen (Rechtspfleger) Rédigé par l'Union Européenne des Greffiers (EUR) et Thomas Kappl, et publié dans l' International Journal for Court Administration (IJCA), ce cadre vise à standardiser la profession de greffier européen (Rechtspfleger) au sein de l'Union européenne et du Conseil de l'Europe. Le Livre vert (2008) propose le transfert des tâches judiciaires de routine des juges vers des greffiers indépendants afin d'améliorer l'efficacité des tribunaux et de garantir le droit à un procès équitable dans un délai raisonnable (Art. 6 CEDH). Il exige une formation juridique standardisée de trois ans et décrit un modèle de fiche de poste.

  • Base Légale: CEDH (Art. 6) ; Statut modèle de 1995 ; Recommandations du Conseil de l'Europe R(86)12, R(80)3 ; Directive européenne 89/48/CEE.
