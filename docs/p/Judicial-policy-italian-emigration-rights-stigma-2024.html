The Italian Emigration Law, Migrants' Rights, and Social Stigma Policy Framework (Kerangka Kebijakan Hukum Emigrasi Italia, Hak-Hak Migran, dan Stigma Sosial) is a legal-historical and judicial policy analytical framework examining the evolution of Italian emigration law during *La Grande Emigrazione* (1861–1920s), the shift in migrants' legal status to *popolo di mezzo* ("people in the middle"), racial criminal stigmatization, and transnational judicial responses 1.

Summary

Authored by Carmela Maria Spadaro, Professor at the University of Naples Federico II, this study analyzes the legal dimensions of the mass migration wave involving 15 to 20 million Italians post-unification 2. Spadaro demonstrates how liberal legal doctrine permitted state absenteeism under the guise of individual liberty (*jus migrandi*), leaving migrants trapped in a state of *minorità giuridica* (loss of rights and legal protection) while suffering racial and criminal stigmatization both domestically and in destination countries such as the United States 3.

Legal Basis

This framework examines key domestic and international legal instruments:

  • Legge Crispi 30 dicembre 1888 n. 5866: Early Italian legislation penalizing illegal labor brokers without granting substantive civil rights protections to emigrants 4.
  • Legge organica 31 gennaio 1901 n. 23: Comprehensive emigration law establishing the *Commissariato Generale per l'Emigrazione* and setting maritime transport health standards 5.
  • Legge Pica 15 agosto 1863 n. 1409: Anti-brigandage legislation in Southern Italy that triggered forced political exile and displacement 6.
  • US Immigration Act 1882 & Literacy Act of 1917: Restrictive US federal laws imposing entry restrictions, literacy tests, and racial quotas targeting Italian immigrants 7.

Background

Following Italian unification in 1861, severe economic imbalances between the industrialized North and neglected South sparked a mass exodus 8. Italian regional judicial authorities routinely described emigrants in derogatory terms as idlers or vagrants 9. In the United States, Southern Italian immigrants faced systematic racial discrimination, frequently targeted with the slur *dagoes* 10.

Objectives

  • Expose the legal mechanisms behind state abandonment of migrant rights in origin and host nations 11.
  • Analyze the misuse of positivist criminology (Cesare Lombroso) in legitimizing judicial prejudice 12.
  • Elevate oral folk tradition (*canti di spartenza*) as counter-narrative evidence in legal history 13.

Scope

The framework encompasses Italian public law history, US immigration law, human rights, positivist criminology, and transnational criminal justice 14.

Key Provisions & Judicial Evidence

  • Concept of Popolo di Mezzo: Emigrants existed in legal limbo without clear protection of citizenship rights 15.
  • 1891 New Orleans Lynching: The murder of 11 Sicilian immigrants following the death of police chief David Hennessy, illustrating mob action fueled by anti-Italian prejudice 16.
  • Sacco and Vanzetti Trial (1920–1927): The trial and execution of two Italian anarchists based on political and racial bias, leading to full judicial rehabilitation by Massachusetts Governor Michael Dukakis on August 23, 1977 17.

Implementation

Enforced through administrative monitoring by the *Commissariato Generale per l'Emigrazione*, passport controls, and the formation of self-protective *Little Italy* enclaves 18.

Policy Issues

  • State Absenteeism: The misuse of *jus migrandi* doctrine to justify state disengagement, leaving migrants vulnerable to private exploitation 19.
  • Pseudo-Scientific Racial Bias: The integration of positivist criminal theories into judicial reasoning to pre-condemn Italian immigrants 20.

Reform & Proposed Changes

Governor Michael Dukakis's 1977 Proclamation underscored the imperative to purge judicial processes of xenophobic and political bias 21.
