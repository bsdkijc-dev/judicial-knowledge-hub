Supreme Court Decision Reg. No. 154 K/Pdt/2012 (dated May 31, 2012) is a fundamental civil law and marital property jurisprudence precedent established under Chief Justice Decree No. 45/KMA/SK/III/2014 concerning the Supreme Court Jurisprudence Compilation. It establishes vital rules regarding the legal standing (persona standi in judicio) of children to sue their parents over joint marital property (harta gono-gini) that has been gifted (hibah), the prohibition of unilateral revocation of gifts, and the qualification of default (breach of contract) and tort (perbuatan melawan hukum / PMH) when parents sell gifted property.

Executive Summary

The Supreme Court established three primary legal principles:

  1. Children's Right to Sue Parents Over Gifted Marital Property: "Children can sue their parents over joint marital property (harta gono-gini) that has been gifted to the children prior to the divorce of both parents."
  2. Prohibition of Unilateral Gift Revocation: "A gift of joint marital property executed by both parents to their children cannot be withdrawn or revoked, except for reasons restrictively regulated under Article 1688 of the Civil Code (BW)." A unilateral Deed of Gift Revocation drawn up before a Notary is null and void (nietig).
  3. Default and Tort Qualification of Parents: "Parents who fail to hand down joint marital property that has been gifted constitute a default, and the alienation or sale of such gifted joint marital property by parents to third parties without the children's consent constitutes an unlawful act (Tort / PMH)."

The Supreme Court rejected the cassation petition of the Petitioner for Cassation (H. [Party A]) and affirmed Surabaya High Court Decision No. 646/PDT/2010/PT.SBY dated January 9, 2011, jo. Banyuwangi District Court Decision No. 119/PDT.G/2008/PN.BWI dated August 6, 2009. That decision partially granted the Plaintiffs' (biological children) lawsuit, declared the Statement Letter of Marital Property Handover dated September 17, 1996, valid, declared the land sales to Defendants IV, V, and VI null and void, and ordered Defendants I and II to evacuate and surrender the disputed object to the Plaintiffs.

Legal / Procedural Issues

1. Legal Standing (Persona Standi in Judicio) of Children Suing Parents Over Gifted Marital Property

Do biological children possess legal standing (persona standi in judicio) to file civil default and tort lawsuits against their biological parents who sell or refuse to surrender joint marital property formally gifted to them?

Supreme Court Rule: Possesses Legal Standing / Lawful.
Biological children as lawful gift recipients (receptive legatee / rechterhebbende) hold the right to file civil lawsuits in District Courts against their parents. Once marital property gifts are agreed upon and executed by both parents prior to divorce, ownership instantly transfers to the children, granting them full legal standing to demand physical surrender and nullify unilateral parental transactions with third parties.

2. Absolutism of the Irrevocability Principle of Gifts (Irrevocability of Inter Vivos Gifts ex Article 1688 Civil Code)

Can parents (donors) unilaterally cancel or revoke marital property gifts transferred to their children via a Notarial Deed/Revocation Statement?

Supreme Court Rule: Cannot Be Unilaterally Revoked.
Pursuant to Article 1688 of the Civil Code, a completed gift cannot be revoked or annulled unilaterally unless 3 exclusive conditions are met:

  1. non-fulfillment of gift conditions,
  2. recipient committing or assisting in crimes against the donor's life, or
  3. recipient refusing maintenance when the donor falls into poverty.

Unilateral Notarial Revocation Deeds executed without Article 1688 grounds are null and void (nietig) and hold no evidentiary force.

3. Absolute Competence Jurisdiction & Division of Authority between Religious and General District Courts

Where a Religious Court in a joint marital property partition lawsuit between ex-spouses rules that property cannot be partitioned because it was validly gifted to children, such a ruling constitutes res judicata, serving as the legal foundation for District Courts to grant children's lawsuits and qualify parental retention/sales as defaults and torts.

Question of Law vs Question of Fact

Question of Law (Judex Juris / Supreme Court)

Did the Judex Facti Surabaya High Court (Decision No. 646/PDT/2010/PT.SBY) and Banyuwangi District Court (Decision No. 119/PDT.G/2008/PN.BWI) correctly apply the law (juiste toepassing van het recht ex Article 1688 Civil Code) in ruling that the marital property gift agreement dated September 17, 1996, could not be unilaterally revoked by Defendant I via Notarial Deed No. 1/2008, and declaring sales of gifted land to third parties unlawful?

Supreme Court Legal Answer: Yes. The Judex Facti reasoning was correct and aligned with the law. Marital property gifts to children cannot be unilaterally revoked, and selling gifted land without children's consent constitutes a Tort. The Supreme Court rejected H. [Party A]'s cassation petition and affirmed the lower court decisions.

Jurisprudence and Related Knowledge

Statutory Regulations

  • Civil Code (KUHPerdata / BW): Articles 1243 (Default), 1365 (Tort), 1666, & 1688 (Rules & Conditions for Gift Annulment).
  • HIR (Herziene Inlandsch Reglement): Article 163 (Burden of Proof).
  • Law No. 7 of 1989 jo. Law No. 3 of 2006 jo. Law No. 50 of 2009 on Religious Courts: Jurisdiction over Marital Property (Gono-Gini).
  • Law No. 14 of 1985 jo. Law No. 5 of 2004 jo. Law No. 3 of 2009 on the Supreme Court: Cassation Review Authority.

Established Supreme Court Jurisprudence

  • Doctrine of Irrevocability of Inter Vivos Gifts: Once a gift is perfected and accepted by the recipient, ownership transfers instantly and cannot be unilaterally withdrawn by the donor.
  • Complementary Jurisdiction Doctrine between Religious and General Courts: Religious Court rulings declaring property vested in children preclude re-litigation of marital property partitions and serve as absolute foundations for General Courts (District Courts) to enforce children's civil rights.
