JPW-SITUATION-006

By Cecep Mustafa, Indonesian Judicial College (IJC) · revision 1016, 2026-09-16 · wiki source · Markdown

Preliminary Examination of the Situation in Palestine (ICC) is an independent legal and factual assessment conducted by the Office of the Prosecutor (OTP) of the International Criminal Court since 16 January 2015, concerning alleged war crimes committed in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem, and the Gaza Strip.

Summary

The preliminary examination was formally concluded on 20 December 2019, after the OTP determined that all legal requirements under the Rome Statute for opening an investigation had been met. Because the matter arose from a State Party referral, Pre-Trial Chamber authorization was not required to open an investigation; the Prosecutor nonetheless submitted a request under Article 19(3) to obtain a judicial ruling confirming the scope of the Court's territorial jurisdiction.

Legal Basis

  • Rome Statute: Article 8 (War Crimes), Articles 12(1) and 12(2)(a) (Territorial Jurisdiction), Article 12(3) (Declaration Accepting Jurisdiction), Articles 13(a) and 14 (State Party Referral), Article 17 (Admissibility), Article 19(3) (Jurisdictional Ruling), and Article 53(1) (Requirements for Opening an Investigation).
  • Article 12(3) declaration dated 1 January 2015, and Palestine's accession to the Rome Statute on 2 January 2015.

Background

On 1 January 2015, the State of Palestine lodged a declaration under Article 12(3) of the Rome Statute accepting ICC jurisdiction over crimes in the Occupied Palestinian Territory since 13 June 2014. On 2 January 2015, Palestine deposited its instrument of accession to the Rome Statute, which entered into force on 1 April 2015. Prosecutor Fatou Bensouda opened the preliminary examination on 16 January 2015, and on 22 May 2018 received a formal referral from Palestine under Articles 13(a) and 14 of the Rome Statute.

Objectives

  • Determine whether the legal requirements of Article 53(1) of the Rome Statute have been satisfied.
  • Confirm Palestine's status as a State Party.
  • Obtain a prompt legal determination from Pre-Trial Chamber I under Article 19(3) on the limits of the Court's territorial jurisdiction.

Key Provisions

  • Alleged IDF Crimes: Disproportionate attacks, wilful killing, causing serious injury to body or health, and attacks against protected emblems under the Geneva Conventions.
  • Alleged Hamas / Palestinian Armed Group Crimes: Direct attacks on civilians, use of human shields, denial of fair trial rights, torture, and outrages upon personal dignity.
  • West Bank Settlements: Alleged war crime of transferring Israeli civilians into the West Bank, under Article 8(2)(b)(viii).

Implementation

On 20 December 2019, the Prosecutor announced the conclusion of the preliminary examination, having found that all legal criteria for opening an investigation were satisfied. Pre-Trial Chamber I issued an order on 28 January 2020 setting out the procedure and schedule for written submissions from the parties, victims, member states, and organizations. The OTP submitted its final observations on 30 April and 8 June 2020, reaffirming that the Court's jurisdiction extends to the entire Occupied Palestinian Territory.

Policy Issues

  • Ongoing legal debate over whether statehood criteria are satisfied for purposes of exercising Rome Statute jurisdiction.
  • Continuing complementarity assessment in relation to ongoing domestic investigations in Israel.

Relationships with Other Entities

  • Office of the Prosecutor (ICC): Organ conducting the preliminary examination.
  • Pre-Trial Chamber I: Chamber handling the Article 19(3) jurisdictional ruling.
  • Government of the State of Palestine: State Party that submitted the Article 14 referral.
  • Fatou Bensouda: ICC Prosecutor who opened the preliminary examination on 16 January 2015 and announced its conclusion on 20 December 2019.
  • Mahmoud Abbas (President Abbas): President of Palestine, whose 19 May 2020 statement was the subject of a request for further clarification by Pre-Trial Chamber I regarding its implications for Palestine's State Party status and the Court's jurisdiction.
