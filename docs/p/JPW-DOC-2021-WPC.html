Policy Analysis

  • Background & Policy Problem:
    • FACT: The White Paper on Crime 2021 documents crime trends and offender treatment in Japan based on statistical data through late July 2021 (primarily FY2020 data).
    • FACT: The report notes a decline in reported Penal Code offenses (down to 614,231 cases in 2020), while highlighting challenges in special fraud (特殊詐欺), cybercrime, and juvenile justice adaptations following the lowering of the age of majority from 20 to 18.
  • Major Legal & Judicial Reforms:
    • Penal Code Amendments on Sexual Offenses (Act No. 72 of 2017): Expanded the definition of rape regardless of victim gender, increased minimum statutory penalties, established new offenses for custodians, and converted sexual offenses into non-complaint-based crimes.
    • Juvenile Act Amendments (Act No. 47 of 2021): Enacted May 2021 (effective April 1, 2022) to create the "Specified Juveniles" (特定少年) category for 18–19 year-olds. Offenses punishable by death, life imprisonment, or minimum statutory terms of 1 year or more require mandatory referral from Family Courts to Public Prosecutors.
    • Phasing Out Graduated Treatment & CFP Implementation: Graduated treatment was discontinued in January 2021 and replaced by Case Formulation in Probation/Parole (CFP). CFP evaluates 8 risk/need domains (family, peers, employment/school, substance use, leisure, financial condition, criminal history, mental health) across 5 intervention levels.
    • 14th UN Crime Congress in Kyoto: Held March 7–12, 2021 in hybrid format (attended by over 5,000 delegates from 152 member states), resulting in the adoption of the Kyoto Declaration and resolutions on re-offending prevention.
    • Special Fraud Study: Survey of 1,343 fraud offenders revealed structured roles: masterminds (9.7%), logistics (15.8%), callers (28.1%), and receivers/drawers (46.4%). Primary motives were financial need (66.1%) and peer pressure/solicitation (44.8%).
  • Interpretations & Structural Inferences:
    • INTERPRETATION: The 2021 White Paper reflects a policy shift in Japanese criminal justice from conventional enforcement toward evidence-based criminal justice, risk-need-responsivity (RNR/CFP) frameworks, and global leadership in recidivism prevention standards.
    • INFERENCE: Despite dropping overall crime rates, high youth involvement as receivers/drawers and callers in special fraud networks necessitates targeted rehabilitation strategies for 18–19 year-olds under the amended Juvenile Act.
