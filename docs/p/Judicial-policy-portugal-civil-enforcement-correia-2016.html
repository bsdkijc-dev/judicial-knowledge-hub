The Troika’s Portuguese Ministry of Justice Civil Enforcement Evaluation Framework is a public policy evaluation and judicial reform framework in Portugal. Analyzed by Pedro Miguel Alves Ribeiro Correia and Susana Antas Videira (2016) in the International Journal for Court Administration (IJCA), Vol. 8 No. 1, it evaluates the long-term impact on civil enforcement actions based on the May 2011 Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) between Portugal and the Troika (IMF/EC/ECB). The empirical study covers the pre-Troika period (January 2007–May 2011), the Troika period (June 2011–March 2014), and the post-Troika period (April 2014–March 2016), documenting a massive backlog reduction of over 360,000 civil enforcement cases [1–6, 19, 28].

Summary

Published by Pedro Miguel Alves Ribeiro Correia and Susana Antas Videira in the IJCA (October 2016), this policy framework presents an independent empirical evaluation of Portugal's judicial reforms following the Troika (IMF/EC/ECB) financial assistance program [1–4]. Analyzing 111 months of official statistical data (January 2007–March 2016) from the Directorate General for Justice Policy (DGPJ) of the Portuguese Ministry of Justice, the study proves that the successful reduction of the civil enforcement actions backlog continued significantly 24 months after the Troika exited Portugal in March 2014 [4, 19, 34–35]. Kruskal-Wallis statistical testing (p = 0.000) confirmed the elimination of more than 360,000 pending cases since the August 2012 peak, an increase in the clearance rate to a median of 142.3% during the post-Troika period, and a reduction in disposition time by 631 days (1 year and 9 months) [19, 22, 24, 30–32].

Legal Basis

  • Memorandum of Understanding on Specific Economic Policy Conditionality (MoU May 2011): The economic and judicial reform agreement between Portugal and the Troika (IMF, European Commission, ECB).
  • Código de Processo Civil (Lei n.º 41/2013, June 26, 2013): The new Portuguese Civil Procedure Code, which eliminated useless legal formalism and accelerated enforcement procedures.
  • Decreto-Lei n.º 4/2013 (January 11, 2013): Temporary regulatory measures designed to eradicate the backlog of civil cases in the courts.
  • Not found in the available sources: Other specific formal statutory regulations are not mentioned in the source text [FACT].

Background

An explosion of private debt and the 2008 economic crisis triggered a surge in civil enforcement claims in Portugal, accumulating a massive case backlog over two decades due to strict adherence to rigid bureaucratic procedures (the Barnard & Hayek adaptation problem).

Objectives

1. Ensure the timely and effective enforcement of contracts to support economic recovery. 2. Reduce the accumulation of civil enforcement backlogs through the simplification of the Civil Procedure Code and judicial map restructuring. 3. Assess the sustainability of the positive judicial reform results during the post-Troika period (2014–2016).

Scope

This policy covers the evaluation of all civil enforcement actions in Portuguese First-Instance Courts (Tribunais Judiciais de 1ª Instância) over a 111-month period.

Key Provisions

  • Pending Cases Reduction:
    • Slashing over 360,000 pending cases since the August 2012 peak, reaching a median of 974,527 cases in the post-Troika period (dropping below the 1 million case mark).
  • Post-Troika Clearance Rate Surge:
    • An increase in the clearance rate from a median of 76.7% (pre-Troika) to 105.9% (Troika) and 142.3% (post-Troika), with 95.8% of post-Troika months recording values above 100%.
  • Disposition Time Reduction:
    • A decrease in disposition time by 631 days (from 2,010 days to 1,379 days).

Implementation

  • Enactment of the New Civil Procedure Code (2013): The elimination of useless formalism, restructuring of the judicial map, and strengthening oversight of enforcement agents.
  • Kruskal-Wallis Econometric Evaluation (DGPJ Data): Processing 111 months of official SIEJ statistical data to verify the statistical significance of performance changes.

Policy Problems

  • Risk of Post-Adjustment Backlog Reversion: Initial concerns that the backlog reduction would stall after the Troika program ended in March 2014.
  • High Disposition Times: A disposition time exceeding 1,300 days remains considerably long for business operations.

Reform / Change

  • Replicating this SIEJ statistical data-driven public evaluation model for insolvency cases and corporate recovery procedures.
  • Maintaining the policies of civil procedure simplification permanently.
