Supreme Court Decision Reg. No. 1904 K/Pdt/2007 (dated September 16, 2008) is a fundamental Indonesian family law and contract/civil law jurisprudence precedent establishing pivotal rules regarding the status of joint spousal liability for debts incurred during marriage, the legal consequences of divorce regarding debt repayment obligations, and the validity of conservatory attachments over joint marital property (gono-gini).

Executive Summary

The Supreme Court established three primary legal principles:

  1. Joint Liability for Marital Debts (Articles 35 & 36 of Law No. 1 of 1974): A debt incurred by either spouse while a marriage is ongoing for business purposes or corporate operations serving as a family income source constitutes a joint debt (a burden upon joint marital property / gono-gini).
  2. Divorce Does Not Extinguish Debt Repayment Obligations: Divorce does not result in either party being released from the obligation to pay debts incurred while still bound in marriage. Separation or divorce between a husband and wife does not sever civil legal bonds toward third parties concerning debts born during an intact marriage.
  3. Validity of Conservatory Attachments Over Joint Property (Conservatoir Beslaag): A conservatory attachment (conservatoir beslaag) placed upon joint marital property (gono-gini) owned by a husband and wife to guarantee the repayment of marital debts is valid and lawful.

The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition of Petitioner for Cassation I ([Party B]) and Petitioner for Cassation II ([Party A], Teguh Hertanto, Tanty Anggraini), annulled Semarang High Court Decision No. 205/PDT/2006/PT.SMG dated January 22, 2007, and independently adjudicated (mengadili sendiri) by upholding the considerations of the Semarang District Court—declaring Defendant I ([Party B]) and Defendant II ([Party C]) in default (wanprestasi), ordering Defendant I and Defendant II to jointly and severally pay the remaining principal debt of Rp 787,500,000 along with a moratory interest of 6% per year, and declaring the conservatory attachment over the joint property valid and lawful.

Legal / Procedural Issues

1. Construction of Spousal Legal Liability for Marital Debts (Articles 35(1)-(2) jo. 36(1)-(2) of Law No. 1 of 1974)

Is a wife (Defendant II) legally liable for debts incurred by her husband (Defendant I) for family expedition business capital while their marriage remained intact, notwithstanding her claim that she did not co-sign the loan agreement?

Supreme Court Rule: Jointly and Severally Liable. Pursuant to Articles 35(1)-(2) jo. 36(1)-(2) of Law No. 1 of 1974 (interpreted a contrario), all debts arising during marriage for family interests or corporate operations serving as joint income sources constitute joint debts binding both spouses.

2. Legal Impact of Divorce on Third-Party Debt Obligations

Does a subsequent divorce between a husband and wife release the former spouse from the obligation to repay debts incurred during marriage to third parties?

Supreme Court Rule: Divorce Does Not Release Debt Obligations. A divorce occurring after a debt arises does not sever civil legal ties or release either party from the obligation to repay that debt to creditors/third parties. The former husband and wife must be ordered to pay such debts jointly and severally (tanggung renteng).

3. Validity of Conservatory Attachments (Conservatoir Beslaag) Over Joint Property (Gono-Gini)

Conservatory attachment orders placed by District Courts over jointly owned land and buildings (gono-gini) to secure marital debt obligations are valid and lawful, precluding High Courts from unilaterally lifting such attachments.

Question of Law vs Question of Fact

Question of Law (Judex Juris / Supreme Court)

Did the Judex Facti Semarang High Court (Decision No. 205/PDT/2006/PT.SMG) err in applying family and civil law (foutieve toepassing van het recht) by releasing Defendant II from debt obligations and lifting conservatory attachments, despite such debts being incurred while Defendants I and II remained married?

Supreme Court Legal Answer: Yes. Semarang High Court misapplied the law. The Supreme Court granted the cassation petitions of Petitioner for Cassation I ([Party B]) and Petitioners for Cassation II ([Party A] et al.), annulled Semarang High Court Decision No. 205/PDT/2006/PT.SMG, and independently adjudicated by ordering Defendants I and II to jointly and severally pay remaining principal debt of Rp 787,500,000 plus 6% annual moratory interest, while declaring the conservatory attachment over joint property valid and lawful.

Question of Fact (Judex Facti / Semarang District Court & Semarang High Court)

Examination of facts regarding loan agreements totaling Rp 1,000,000,000 between Defendant I and Plaintiffs between October 17, 1999, and February 9, 2003, for PT Bermuda Cemerlang Abadi capital; repayment totaling Rp 125,000,000 leaving a principal debt of Rp 875,000,000 (calculated in District Court / Supreme Court verdicts as Rp 787,500,000); marriage of Defendants I & II in 1989 and divorce judgment dated September 28, 2005 (Semarang District Court No. 132/Pdt/G/2005/PN.Smg); and verification of Conservatory Attachment Minutes No. 145/Pdt.G/2005/PN.Smg.

Established Supreme Court Jurisprudence

  • Doctrine of Joint Marital Debt Liability: Loans executed by either spouse for business capital whose proceeds sustain family economics automatically bind the other spouse as a joint gono-gini property debt.

What Evidence Establishes / Does Not Establish

Established by Evidence

  • Business capital loan debts were incurred while Defendants I and II remained in an intact marriage (1999–2003) for family business interests.
  • The 2005 divorce did not extinguish joint debt status toward the Plaintiffs/Creditors.
  • Defendants I and II were proven in default (wanprestasi) and ordered to jointly and severally pay remaining principal debt of Rp 787,500,000 plus 6% annual moratory interest.
  • Conservatory attachments (conservatoir beslaag) placed over joint property (gono-gini) are declared valid and lawful.

Annulled (Semarang High Court Verdict)

  • Semarang High Court considerations releasing Defendant II from debt obligations and lifting conservatory attachments were entirely annulled by the Supreme Court as erroneous applications of family law.

Uncertainties and Gaps

Proving Actual Utilization of Loan Proceeds in Marital Debt Disputes: This landmark ruling provides an exceptionally clear legal precedent in Indonesia: spouses cannot evade family debt repayment obligations on formalistic grounds of failing to co-sign loan applications or having divorced. Provided loans are incurred during ongoing marriage and utilized to support family businesses/income, such debts legally bind both parties jointly and severally.

Frequently Asked Questions (GEO)

Does divorce release a former spouse from debts incurred by their partner during marriage, and can joint gono-gini property be subjected to conservatory attachment?
Does not release debtors, and gono-gini property can be lawfully attached. Pursuant to Supreme Court Jurisprudence No. 1904 K/Pdt/2007:

  • Divorce does not result in either party being released from obligations to pay debts incurred while bound in marriage pursuant to Articles 35 & 36 of Law No. 1 of 1974. Family business capital debts remain joint burdens, and former spouses are ordered to pay jointly and severally.
  • Conservatory attachments (conservatoir beslaag) placed over joint marital property (gono-gini) to secure marital debt repayment are valid and lawful.
