Judicial-policy-transnational-judicial-review-2021

By Cecep Mustafa, Indonesian Judicial College (IJC) · revision 1526, 2026-09-18 · wiki source · Markdown

The Transnational Judicial Review Doctrine in European Administrative Law (Doktrin Peninjauan Peradilan Transnasional dalam Hukum Administrasi Eropa) is a judicial policy doctrine empowering and requiring national judges in European Union Member States (acting as juges de droit commun) to review the legality of preparatory administrative acts (*preparatory acts*) issued by authorities of another EU Member State in horizontal composite procedures (*horizontal composite procedures*), particularly in cross-border tax recovery, to ensure the right to an effective judicial remedy under Article 47 of the Charter of Fundamental Rights of the European Union (CFREU) 1.

Summary

Authored by Paolo Mazzotti (Graduate Institute Geneva) and Mariolina Eliantonio (Professor of European and Comparative Administrative Law at Maastricht University), this study conceptualizes the emergence of the transnational judicial review doctrine based on landmark judgments of the Court of Justice of the European Union (ECJ) in Berlioz (2017) and Donnellan (2018) 2. The study evaluates the doctrine's application at the national level through a case study of the Italian Court of Cassation (Corte di Cassazione) in the Intini case (Court Order No. 22652/2019) 3.

Legal Basis

The framework is grounded in supranational instruments and EU jurisprudence:

  • Article 47 Charter of Fundamental Rights of the European Union (CFREU): The primary basis for the right to an effective remedy and fair trial, serving as both a cause of action and a benchmark for transnational review 4.
  • Article 51 & Article 52(1) CFREU: Provisions defining the scope of the Charter and the obligation to respect the essence of rights 5.
  • Council Directive 2010/24/EU & Directive 76/308/EEC: Regulatory frameworks governing mutual assistance for the recovery of claims relating to taxes 6.
  • ECJ Opinion 2/13: Jurisprudence establishing exceptions to mutual trust in exceptional cases to safeguard fundamental EU rights 7.

Background

Administrative cooperation in cross-border tax recovery forms horizontal composite procedures, where preparatory acts in one Member State (e.g., tax assessment in Austria) serve as the foundation for enforcement in another (e.g., collection in Italy) 8. Traditional division of jurisdiction under Directive 2010/24/EU limited requested-state courts to domestic enforcement acts, creating gaps in judicial protection (*gaps in judicial protection*) when derivative illegality arose 9. To resolve this, the ECJ established that Article 47 CFREU overrides rigid jurisdictional boundaries 10.

Objectives

  • Eliminate gaps in judicial protection for taxpayers in horizontal composite procedures 11.
  • Empower national judges as juges de droit commun to review unlawful foreign preparatory acts 12.
  • Guarantee linguistic comprehensibility of cross-border tax documents served on citizens 13.

Scope

The doctrine covers European administrative law, transnational tax recovery, EU fundamental rights, the interaction between CJEU and ECtHR jurisprudence (Article 6 ECHR), and domestic judicial review 14.

Key Provisions & Case Studies

  • Dual Function of Article 47 CFREU: Operates as a cause of action (opening jurisdiction for transnational review) and a benchmark (evaluating compliance with defense rights) 15.
  • Language Requirements for Documents (Weiss und Partner & ZZ): Taxpayers must receive assessment documents in a language they understand to exercise defense rights effectively 16.
  • The Intini Case (Italian Court of Cassation): An Italian taxpayer (Intini) faced collection via an Italian payment notice based on an Austrian tax assessment written solely in German 17. The Court of Cassation erred by declining jurisdiction under legacy domestic case law (Judgement No. 760/2006) and failing to apply Donnellan 18.

Implementation

Applied when a taxpayer challenges domestic enforcement in requested-state courts: national judges must review procedural flaws in foreign preparatory acts if those flaws impair the essence of the right to an effective remedy 19.

Policy Issues

  • Domestic Jurisprudential Resistance: Reluctance of national supreme courts to deviate from legacy national precedents that block transnational review 20.
  • Uncertainties in Non-Criminal Tax Matters: Tension with ECtHR case law (Ferrazzini v. Italy) excluding pure tax disputes from Article 6 ECHR, whereas Article 47 CFREU applies broadly across EU law 21.

Reform & Evaluation

Proposes expanding transnational judicial review to protect all EU fundamental rights (e.g., Article 7 privacy and Article 8 data protection) and ensuring all EU integrated administrative processes remain subject to the Rule of Law 22.
