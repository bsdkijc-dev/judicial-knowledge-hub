Ideal Management Plan for the Termed Civil Servant System: Focusing on the Judiciary's Termed Civil Servants (Korean: 임기제공무원제도의 바람직한 운영방안에 관한 연구 - 사법부 임기제공무원을 중심으로) is an official judicial policy research report published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of the Supreme Court of Korea as JPRI Research Series 2019-05.1 It analyzes personnel-practice problems in Korea's termed (fixed-term) civil servant system, evaluates the comparative legal frameworks of the United States, United Kingdom and Japan, and sets out operating guidelines and a legislative reform package to improve the management of termed employees across the judiciary.

Legal basis

The research was conducted under the Supreme Court Annual Research Plan 2018. The principal references are:

  • State Public Officials Act, Article 26-5 (국가공무원법 제26조의5)
  • Court Public Servants Rules, Articles 4-2, 24-2 and 24-3 (법원공무원규칙)
  • Public Officials Remuneration Regulation and the court staff pay rules (공무원보수규정)

Background

The termed civil servant system was first introduced in Korea in 1998, after the IMF economic crisis, as the Contractual Civil Servant System (계약직공무원제도), to break the rigidity of the closed career (grade-based, 계급제) system, inject private-sector professionalism and increase personnel flexibility. In 2013, through a public-position structural reform, this contract system was folded into the general civil service category as the "Termed Civil Servant" to strengthen status security during the term and broaden position integration. As of December 2017, termed employees across all Korean government agencies numbered 12,588 (1.2% of all civil servants); the judiciary alone employed 569 termed employees (3.2% of its 17,736 civil servants), of whom 74.5% (424) were women.2

Objectives

  1. Analyze real-world personnel-practice problems facing termed employees in Korea and the judiciary.
  2. Evaluate the management mechanisms for termed staff in the United States, United Kingdom and Japan.
  3. Develop operating guidelines and draft amendments to the Court Public Servants Rules to protect termed employees' rights and prevent abuse of appointment power.

Policy problems

  • Status uncertainty and fear of reappointment. Initial terms are capped at five years; after five years, agencies often terminate the employee or run a new open recruitment for the same position.
  • The "dummy applicant" phenomenon (들러리). When reappointment recruitment is held after five years, the experienced incumbent is almost always selected, so outside applicants serve only as formal, token competitors.
  • Unconstitutional salary reset. Incumbents who are reselected for the same position through reappointment recruitment are often treated as new hires and have their salary reset to first-year levels, disregarding five years of accumulated experience.
  • No promotion path and single-evaluator appraisal. Termed employees are barred from promotion. Performance appraisal is subjective and dominated by a single direct supervisor, with no participation right or feedback interview.

Legal and policy gaps

  • Ambiguous "outstanding performance" standard. Since 2015/2016, regulations have allowed extending a term to a total of ten years without new recruitment for employees with "outstanding performance" (탁월한 성과), but no clear quantitative or qualitative indicator defines this standard within the judiciary.
  • No notice procedure or right to request extension. Agencies have no legal duty to give early notice of an approaching term expiry, and termed employees have no administrative right to apply for an extension evaluation.

Comparative analysis

Jurisdiction System (stable ID) Key features
United States Federal term and temporary appointment system (JPW-SYS-US-TERM) Term Appointment (one to four years, four-year maximum) and TAPER (Temporary Appointment Pending Establishment of a Register); uses GS/WG evaluation and a one-year trial period.
United Kingdom Fixed term and casual appointment system (JPW-SYS-UK-FIXEDTERM) Fixed Term Appointment (five-year maximum) and Casual Appointment (12–24 months maximum); permits conversion to permanent appointment or temporary promotion for employees meeting a high performance standard.
Japan Termed employee system (JPW-SYS-JP-TERM) The 2000 Act on Termed Employees (임기부직원법) divides employees into Specific Termed and General Termed categories, each with a five-year cap, and ensures transparency through a Personnel Change Notification (인사이동통지서) issued at each stage.

Domestic practice: the Seoul Metropolitan Government and the National Assembly Secretariat (National Assembly Research Service/Budget Office) have already defined "outstanding performance" criteria based on average evaluation scores (e.g. average grade A or S) and multi-source peer evaluation.

Reform proposals

  1. Define outstanding-performance criteria and protect salary: legislate objective evaluation-score standards and require preserving the prior salary (종전 연봉 보전) for employees reselected to the same position.
  2. Flexible initial terms: apply a flexible term scheme based on position characteristics, such as 2+3 years or 1+4 years, for ongoing (상시·계속적) work.
  3. Institutionalize an expectation right to request extension (기대권): require notice of expiry 80 days in advance, a right to apply for extension 60 days in advance, and an objection (이의신청) mechanism.
  4. Establish a Judiciary Termed Personnel Committee: a dedicated body to oversee recruitment, term extension and prevention of nepotism or abuse of appointment power.
  5. Allow conversion to permanent career status: permit termed employees managing ongoing, routine work to convert to general career civil servant status through a limited, non-competitive selection examination (비다수인대상채용).

Frequently asked questions

What are the report's title, series number, lead author and publication date?
Ideal Management Plan for the Termed Civil Servant System: Focusing on the Judiciary's Termed Civil Servants (Series 2019-05), by lead researcher Seo, Hyunwoong, published by JPRI on 13 June 2019.

Why was the termed civil servant system introduced in Korea?
To overcome the rigidity and lack of professionalism of the closed career (grade-based) system, bring private-sector expertise into the public bureaucracy, and increase personnel flexibility and efficiency.

How many termed employees worked in Korea's judiciary as of December 2017?
569 out of 17,736 total judiciary civil servants (3.2%), of whom 74.5% (424) were women.

What is the main problem when termed employees undergo reappointment recruitment after five years?
The "dummy applicant" phenomenon, suspicion of nepotism, and resetting the base salary to first-year levels without recognizing five years of prior experience.

What does the rule allowing a term extension to ten years for "outstanding performance" mean?
A regulation introduced in 2015/2016 allowing an additional extension of up to five years (ten years total) without new open recruitment for employees shown to have exceptionally outstanding performance.

How does the Seoul Metropolitan Government define "outstanding performance" for termed employees?
Using criteria such as an average evaluation score above A, receiving an S grade at least three times in two years, ranking in the top 10% in multi-source peer evaluation, or a special recommendation from the agency's evaluation committee.

Why does Japan's system require a "Personnel Change Notification"?
To ensure legal certainty and protect termed employees' rights at appointment, term extension, and formal termination.

What initial-term scheme is recommended for judiciary employees handling ongoing work?
A 2+3 year or 1+4 year scheme (an initial one- or two-year contract followed by a direct extension for the remaining three or four years) to secure status stability and continuity of judicial functions.

What procedural reform is proposed to secure termed employees' expectation right to extension?
Requiring agencies to give notice of expiry 80 days in advance, a right to apply for extension 60 days in advance, a decision communicated 30 days before expiry, and a formal objection channel.

배경

임기제공무원제도는 1998년 IMF 경제위기 이후 계약직공무원제도로 처음 도입되어 폐쇄적 계급제의 경직성을 완화하고 민간의 전문성을 주입하며 인사관리의 유연성을 높이고자 하였다. 2013년 직제 구조 개편을 통해 이 계약제는 일반직 범주로 통합되어 "임기제공무원"이라는 명칭으로 재편되었고, 재직 기간 동안의 신분 보장을 강화하고 직위 통합을 확대하였다. 2017년 12월 기준 전체 정부기관의 임기제공무원은 12,588명(전체 공무원의 1.2%)이며, 사법부는 전체 17,736명의 공무원 중 569명(3.2%)의 임기제공무원을 두고 있고 그중 74.5%(424명)가 여성이었다.3

연구 목적

  1. 한국과 사법부 임기제공무원의 실제 인사실무 문제를 분석한다.
  2. 미국, 영국, 일본의 임기제 직원 관리 체계를 평가한다.
  3. 임기제공무원의 권리를 보호하고 임용권 남용을 방지하기 위한 운영 지침과 법원공무원규칙 개정안을 마련한다.

정책 문제

  • 신분 불안정과 재임용 불안: 최초 임용기간은 최대 5년으로 제한되며, 5년이 지나면 기관은 종종 해당 직원을 면직하거나 같은 직위에 대해 신규임용절차를 새로 실시한다.
  • "들러리" 현상: 5년 후 재임용 공고가 열리면 경험 많은 기존 직원이 거의 항상 재선발되어, 외부 지원자는 형식적인 들러리에 불과하다.
  • 위헌적 보수 초기화: 같은 직위에 재임용된 기존 직원은 종종 신규 채용으로 취급되어 보수가 1년차 수준으로 초기화되며, 이전 5년간의 누적 경력이 반영되지 않는다.
  • 승진 경로와 1인 평가의 부재: 임기제공무원은 승진이 허용되지 않는다. 또한 성과평가가 주관적이고 한 명의 직속상관이 평가를 독점하며 참여권이나 피드백 면담이 없다.

법제 공백

  • "탁월한 성과" 기준의 모호성: 2015·2016년부터 규정은 "탁월한 성과"를 보인 직원에 대해 신규 공모 없이 최대 10년까지 임용기간을 연장할 수 있도록 허용하지만, 사법부 내에서 그 기준을 구체화하는 정량·정성 지표가 없다.
  • 통지 절차와 연장 신청권의 부재: 기관이 임기 만료를 사전에 통지할 법적 의무가 없고, 임기제공무원이 연장 평가를 신청할 행정적 권리도 없다.

개선 제안

  1. 탁월한 성과 기준 설정과 보수 보호: 객관적인 평가점수 기준을 법제화하고, 같은 직위에 재선발된 직원의 종전 연봉을 보전하도록 의무화한다.
  2. 유연한 최초 임용기간: 상시·계속적 업무에는 2+3년 또는 1+4년 방식의 유연한 임용기간 체계를 적용한다.
  3. 연장 신청 기대권의 제도화: 만료 80일 전 통지, 60일 전 연장 신청권, 이의신청 절차를 의무화한다.
  4. 사법부 임기제공무원 인사위원회 설치: 채용, 임기 연장, 연고주의·권한 남용 방지를 감독할 전담 위원회를 둔다.
  5. 일반 경력직 전환 허용: 상시·반복 업무를 담당하는 임기제공무원이 비다수인대상채용을 통해 일반 경력직 공무원으로 전환할 수 있도록 허용한다.

"탁월한 성과"에 대한 10년 연장 규정이란?
2015·2016년에 도입된 규정으로, 탁월한 성과가 입증된 직원에 대해 신규 공모 없이 최대 5년을 추가로(총 10년까지) 연장할 수 있도록 하는 것이다.

평균 평가등급 A 이상, 2년 내 S등급 3회 이상, 다면평가 상위 10% 진입, 또는 기관 평가위원회의 특별 추천 등의 기준을 사용한다.

임용, 임기 연장, 공식 해임 시 법적 안정성을 보장하고 임기제공무원의 권리를 보호하기 위해서이다.

2+3년 또는 1+4년 방식으로, 최초 1~2년 계약 후 나머지 3~4년을 곧바로 연장하여 신분 안정성과 사법 기능의 연속성을 확보하는 것이다.
