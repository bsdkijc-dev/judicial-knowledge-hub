Description / Deskripsi

Presents ten comprehensive maritime criminal case files spanning self-propelled semi-submersible (SPSS) drug trafficking, Gulf of Guinea piracy, EEZ IUU fishing, high-seas human trafficking and forced labor, MARPOL Annex I oily water separator bypasses, containerized wildlife trafficking, flagless WMD/arms proliferation, dark fleet AIS manipulation and unverified STS oil transfers, front-company sanctions evasion, and SUA-governed maritime terrorism.

Problem / Masalah

  • JPAIS-MED-PROB-028: Transnational maritime criminal syndicates exploit jurisdictional boundaries, unflagged stateless vessels, and transponder manipulation to execute high-value smuggling and security threats[cite: 10].
  • JPAIS-MED-PROB-029: Prosecuting maritime offenses requires establishing precise legal foundations across diverse treaty frameworks, including UNCLOS Article 110, the SUA Convention, and MARPOL[cite: 10].
  • JPAIS-MED-PROB-030: Complex evidence chains—ranging from digital GPS tracks and OWS electronic logs to cryptographic telemetry and DNA origin tracing—demand rigorous chain-of-custody protocols[cite: 10].

Current Indonesian Practice / Praktik Indonesia Saat Ini

  • JPAIS-MED-ID-028: Indonesian maritime enforcement coordinates interagency assets across archipelagic waters, integrating naval patrols, customs inspections, and fisheries enforcement to prosecute maritime crimes[cite: 10].

Japanese & Comparative Practice / Praktik Perbandingan

  • JPAIS-MED-JP-028: Advanced jurisdictions utilize automated risk-profiling algorithms, 3D X-ray CT container scanning, and multi-sensor data fusion to intercept contraband and prosecute offenders[cite: 10].

Gap / Kesenjangan

  • JPAIS-MED-GAP-009: Gaps in flag state verification speed and coordination during high-seas interdictions risk allowing suspect vessels to scuttle or evade jurisdiction before boarding authorization is secured[cite: 10].

Proposed Adaptation / Adaptasi Usulan

Implement standardized operational case files and prosecutorial briefs across ten maritime crime domains, ensuring rigorous alignment with UNCLOS, SUA, and MARPOL enforcement standards[cite: 10].

Action / Aksi

  • JPAIS-MED-ACT-028: Apply UNCLOS Article 110 right of visit and stateless vessel verification procedures during high-seas interdictions[cite: 10].
  • JPAIS-MED-ACT-029: Enforce port state jurisdiction under UNCLOS Article 218 and MARPOL Annex I to prosecute oil record book fraud and OWS bypasses[cite: 10].
  • JPAIS-MED-ACT-030: Integrate financial intelligence and corporate registry audits to dismantle front companies and trace ultimate beneficial ownership in sanctions and wildlife trafficking cases[cite: 10].

Pilot / Pilot Proyek

  • JPAIS-MED-PILOT-028: 180-day multi-agency operational pilot deploying container risk-profiling algorithms and accelerated SUA flag-verification channels across regional transshipment hubs[cite: 10].

KPI / Indikator Kinerja Utama

  • JPAIS-MED-KPI-028: 100% evidentiary completeness across all ten case file domains; zero evidentiary suppression motions granted due to chain-of-custody gaps; successful prosecution conversion across maritime interdictions[cite: 10].

Policy Implication / Implikasi Kebijakan

Harmonization of national prosecutorial strategies with international maritime law enforcement standards, ensuring robust legal finishes for complex maritime criminal cases[cite: 10].

Regulatory Implication / Implikasi Regulasi

Strengthening domestic penal legislation to incorporate stateless vessel jurisdiction, universal suppression mandates under the SUA Convention, and strict port state controls[cite: 10].

Evidence / Bukti Empiris

  • Ten comprehensive case scenarios demonstrate the practical application of international maritime law, digital forensics, and evidentiary exhibit logging across diverse criminal typologies[cite: 10].
  • Cross-case comparative analysis reveals that statelessness and documentation fraud serve as common operational denominators across illicit maritime networks[cite: 10].
