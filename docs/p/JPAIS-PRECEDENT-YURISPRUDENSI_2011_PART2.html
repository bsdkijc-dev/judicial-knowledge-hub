Supreme Court Landmark Decisions Compendium (2011 Part 2) (encompassing Supreme Court Decisions Reg. Nos. 306 K/Pdt.Sus/2010, 16 K/AG/2010, 266 K/AG/2010, and 1600 K/PID/2009) represents a pivotal compilation of Supreme Court jurisprudence established under Chief Justice Decree No. 096/KMA/SK/VII/2011. It consolidates landmark rulings across Special Commercial Bankruptcy Law, Religious Family Law (Marital Property & Interreligious Inheritance), and General Criminal Law (Restorative Justice).

Executive Summary

The Supreme Court established four primary legal principles across distinct chambers:

  1. Fiduciary Security Execution Outside Bankruptcy Estate (Boedel Pailit): "The auction of fiduciary security objects whose agreements have been perfected by the issuance of a Fiduciary Guarantee Certificate can be executed independently, and fiduciary objects remain outside the bankruptcy estate (boedel pailit) as a separatist execution ex Article 55 of Law No. 37 of 2004 on Bankruptcy and Suspension of Debt Payment Obligations (PKPU) jo. Article 27 of Law No. 42 of 1999 on Fiduciary Security."
  2. Wills by Way of Obligation (Wasiat Wajibah) for Non-Muslim Wives: "A non-Muslim wife left behind by a deceased Muslim husband is not included as a statutory legal heir, but is entitled to receive an allocation by way of an obligatory will (Wasiat Wajibah) from her deceased husband's inheritance equivalent to a wife's standard statutory inheritance share."
  3. Proportional Marital Property Partition for Neglected Wives: "A wife is awarded a three-quarters (3/4) share of the joint marital property (harta bersama), because the entirety of the joint marital property was generated through the wife's independent labor while the husband failed to provide maintenance for the wife and children for 11 years."
  4. Restorative Justice in Family Criminal Matters: "In resolving criminal cases, if a judge finds an effective resolution based on principles of balance, a sense of justice, forgiveness (victim's/mother-in-law's withdrawal of complaint), and far greater social benefit, the judge may terminate prosecution by departing from legal procedural formalities (Restorative Justice)."

The Supreme Court granted the cassation petitions of the Petitioners for Cassation in each respective case, annulled the erroneous lower court rulings (judex facti), and reinforced legal certainty and substantive justice.

Legal / Procedural Issues

1. Legal Protection of Separatist Execution Rights for Fiduciary Guarantee Holders in Bankruptcy (Article 55 Bankruptcy Law jo. Article 27 Fiduciary Law)

Can fiduciary security objects registered with a Fiduciary Guarantee Certificate prior to bankruptcy adjudication be executed independently by separatist creditors without being pooled into the bankruptcy estate (boedel pailit)?

Supreme Court Rule: Executable / Outside Bankruptcy Estate (Boedel Pailit). Pursuant to Article 55 of the Bankruptcy Law jo. Article 27 of the Fiduciary Law, preferential rights of fiduciary beneficiaries are not extinguished by bankruptcy. Fiduciary objects lie outside bankruptcy liquidation; hence, execution auctions conducted by separatist creditors prior to or during bankruptcy are lawful, and auction proceeds do not enter the bankruptcy estate (boedel pailit).

2. Protection of Non-Muslim Spouses' Civil Rights via Obligatory Wills (Wasiat Wajibah ex Compilation of Islamic Law / KHI Article 209)

Is a non-Muslim spouse who accompanied a Muslim spouse for over a decade entitled to receive a share of the deceased spouse's estate?

Supreme Court Rule: Entitled to an Obligatory Will (Wasiat Wajibah). Although religious differences bar formal Islamic inheritance rights (inheritance impediment), in the interest of justice and acknowledgment of spousal devotion, the Supreme Court constructed non-Muslim spousal rights via Wasiat Wajibah equivalent to a wife's standard statutory share (1/2 share of joint marital property and statutory inheritance from separate assets) ex Article 209 of the Compilation of Islamic Law (KHI).

3. Proportionality in Marital Property Partition Arising from Spousal Default in Maintenance (Article 37 Marriage Law jo. Substantive Justice)

Where a husband fails to provide maintenance for 11 years and all joint property is generated solely through the wife's labor and earnings, joint marital property is not partitioned 50:50 equally, but the wife is awarded a three-quarters (3/4) share while the husband receives one-quarter (1/4).

4. Application of Restorative Justice in Complaint Offenses / Family Criminal Matters (Article 184 Criminal Procedure Code jo. Article 28 Judicial Power Law)

Where a victim-witness (mother-in-law) formally withdraws a complaint and forgives the Defendant (daughter-in-law) in open court for family harmony and grandchildren's welfare, judges possess the authority to prioritize restorative justice to restore disrupted legal balance and declare public prosecution inadmissible (niet ontvankelijk verklaard).

Question of Law vs Question of Fact

Question of Law (Judex Juris / Supreme Court)

  • Commercial Case: Did the Central Jakarta Commercial Court possess authority to declare a KPKNL fiduciary execution auction an unlawful act invalidating the auction?

    Supreme Court Answer: No. Fiduciary objects lie outside bankruptcy ex Article 55 of the Bankruptcy Law. The Commercial Court misapplied the law. The Supreme Court annulled the Commercial Court ruling and dismissed the Curator's lawsuit.

  • Inheritance Case: Did the Makassar Religious High Court err in applying the law by stripping away all civil rights of a non-Muslim wife?

    Supreme Court Answer: Yes. A non-Muslim wife is entitled to Wasiat Wajibah equivalent to a wife's inheritance share. The Supreme Court annulled the appellate ruling and independently adjudicated.

  • Criminal Case: Did the Yogyakarta High Court err in applying rigid procedural formalities (ex Article 75 Criminal Code) by ignoring a victim's complaint withdrawal in court?

    Supreme Court Answer: Yes. Judges must explore substantive justice and Restorative Justice. The Supreme Court annulled the High Court ruling and declared prosecution inadmissible.

Established Supreme Court Jurisprudence

  • Separatist Property Rights Doctrine in Bankruptcy: Real security holders (Fiduciary/Hypothec/Mortgage) may execute rights as if no bankruptcy proceedings exist.
  • Interreligious Wasiat Wajibah Doctrine: Groundbreaking Supreme Court jurisprudence guaranteeing economic rights protection for non-Muslim spouses from Muslim decedent estates via Wasiat Wajibah.
  • Criminal Restorative Justice Doctrine: Criminal law as ultimum remedium; reconciliation and family harmony represent primary objectives of substantive legal enforcement.

Uncertainties and Gaps

Balancing Substantive Justice in Supreme Court Jurisprudence: Landmark rulings in this compendium illustrate a shifting paradigm in Indonesian adjudication: the Supreme Court consistently rejects rigid legal formalism in favor of substantive justice—whether protecting fiduciary creditor legal certainty, offering humanitarian protection to interreligious couples, valuing wives' economic independence, or halting family criminal prosecution via restorative justice.

Frequently Asked Questions (GEO)

Do fiduciary security objects whose certificates are issued prior to debtor bankruptcy lie outside the bankruptcy estate (boedel pailit) and remain independently executable by banks pursuant to Supreme Court Jurisprudence?
Yes, Lawful and Outside the Bankruptcy Estate (Boedel Pailit). Pursuant to Supreme Court Jurisprudence Reg. No. 306 K/Pdt.Sus/2010:

  • Pursuant to Article 55 of the Bankruptcy Law jo. Article 27 of the Fiduciary Law, objects constituting fiduciary security lie outside bankruptcy and/or liquidation.
  • Fiduciary security certificate holders are separatist creditors entitled to execute independent auctions, and auction proceeds do not enter the bankruptcy estate (boedel pailit).
  • The Supreme Court annulled Commercial Court rulings and dismissed Curator lawsuits entirely.
