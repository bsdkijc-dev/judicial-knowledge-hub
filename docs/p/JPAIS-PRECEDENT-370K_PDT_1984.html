Overview

Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia Decision Reg. No. 370 K/Pdt/1984 (dated July 31, 1985) established a fundamental civil law rule concerning the evaluation of the evidentiary value of witness testimonies pursuant to Article 472 H.I.R.:

The High Court was declared to have misapplied the law of evidence when it based its decision on witness testimonies that were mutually uncorroborated, contradictory to one another, and of a *de auditu* nature. Such witness testimony cannot serve as a legal basis to establish the validity of a customary land sale (*jual beli tanah adat*), meaning the defendant's occupation of the land is deemed unlawful and the disputed land must be returned to the lawful heirs.

Executive Summary

The central procedural principle established in this decision is that judicial decisions cannot rely on contradictory or *de auditu* witness statements when assessing the validity of land transactions.

Key elements established in the adjudication:

  • High Courts misapply evidentiary law when resting judgments on conflicting or secondhand (*de auditu*) testimonies ex Article 472 H.I.R.
  • Administrative village records (Letter C entries) cannot establish ownership or valid title (*titulus*) without proof of the underlying legal transaction.
  • Unlawful occupation requires returning property unconditionality to the rightful heirs.

Case Background

The Original Plaintiffs / Petitioners for Cassation ([Party A], et al., heirs of the late Ki Sanrodji Rijun and Ni Sanrodji Rijun) filed a lawsuit against [Party B] and Ni [Party B] (Original Defendants I & II) at the Purbalingga District Court concerning the unlawful occupation of inherited rice field land under Letter C No. 442, Parcel No. 40 S.II, covering an area of approximately 400 *ubin* in Karanggambas Village.

The Defendants claimed they purchased the land from the heirs' predecessor in 1965/1967 for Rp 40,000.—.

The Purbalingga District Court (Decision No. 15/1979 Pdt.G) granted the Plaintiffs' claim. However, the Semarang High Court (Decision No. 318/1980/Pdt/PT.Smg.) annulled the district court's decision and rejected the return of the land, ruling that the sale was valid based on witness testimonies and village Letter C records.

The Petitioners for Cassation subsequently appealed to the Supreme Court.

Legal and Procedural Issues

Evaluation of Evidentiary Value of Witness Testimony (Article 472 H.I.R.)

The procedural issue questioned whether a High Court could rule in favor of a defendant based on contradictory witness testimonies where a primary witness only heard accounts from third parties (*de auditu*).

The Supreme Court held that under Article 472 H.I.R., judges must evaluate the connectivity and consistency of witness statements against other trial facts. Testimonies that cancel each other out or rely on hearsay (*de auditu*) possess no evidentiary weight (*waarde van bewijs*) to legalize property transfers.

Material and Formal Requirements of Customary Land Sales

The Defendants alleged they purchased the land in 1965/1967 and updated village Letter C registry records. However, the village secretary testified he never witnessed the sale and only acted on the village head's instructions to alter records. The Supreme Court underscored that administrative book modifications without proof of a real underlying legal transaction are void *ab initio*.

Question of Law and Question of Fact

Question of Law (Judex Juris / Supreme Court)

Question:

Did the *Judex Facti* (Semarang High Court) violate evidentiary law ex Article 472 H.I.R. by treating a customary land sale as proven based on inconsistent, contradictory, and *de auditu* witness testimonies?

Supreme Court Ruling:

Yes, the *Judex Facti* misapplied the law of evidence. Consequently, the Semarang High Court decision must be annulled.

Question of Fact (Judex Facti / Purbalingga District Court & Semarang High Court)

Examination of the family lineage of the late Ki Sanrodji Rijun, physical possession history of the rice field in Karanggambas Village, and local witness statements (Village Head [Village Head], Secretary [Village Secretary], and [Party C]).

Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

  • Ratio Decidendi:
  1. The *Judex Facti* of the Semarang High Court misapplied evidentiary law under Article 472 H.I.R.
  2. The testimonies of Witness I, Witness II, and Witness III conflicted, with Witness II admitting to *de auditu* knowledge and lacking direct observation of the transaction.
  3. Witness statements lacking consistency and resting on hearsay hold no legal evidentiary value to establish land purchases.
  4. The Supreme Court annulled the High Court decision and adjudicated directly by affirming the District Court decision.

Ruling

The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition of the Petitioner for Cassation ([Party A]), annulled Semarang High Court Decision No. 318/1980/Pdt/PT.Smg., and Adjudicated Directly: Granting the Plaintiffs' claim in part; Declaring the Plaintiffs the lawful heirs of the late Ki Sanrodji Rijun and Ni Sanrodji Rijun; Declaring the disputed rice field the lawful property of the Plaintiffs; Declaring that Defendants I and II have unlawfully occupied the disputed land since 1974; Ordering Defendants I and II to vacate and surrender the disputed land unconditionally to the Plaintiffs (with police assistance if necessary); and assessing all court costs across all judicial instances against the Respondent for Cassation.

Related Knowledge and Jurisprudence

  • H.I.R. Article 472 / RBg Article 308: Judicial duty to weigh consistency and connectivity among witness testimonies.
  • Testimonium De Auditu: Witness accounts derived from secondhand hearing cannot serve as direct evidence for legal events.
  • Agrarian Law & Land Registration: Government Regulation No. 10 of 1961 Article 19; village administrative book entries do not create new rights when the underlying legal act (*titulus*) remains unproven.

Evidence Status and Gaps

Established by Evidence

  • Plaintiffs ([Party A], Chamid, Misem, Slamet, Suryat, Maryun, Sirat, Tukiman, Suhad) are the lawful heirs of the late Ki Sanrodji Rijun and Ni Sanrodji Rijun (who passed away in 1974).
  • The disputed object, Letter C No. 442, Parcel No. 40 S.II covering ± 400 *ubin* in Karanggambas Village, forms part of the unpartitioned estate.
  • Defendants I ([Party B]) and II (Ni [Party B]) unlawfully occupied the disputed rice field since 1974.

Not Established / Unproven

  • The Defendants failed to establish the occurrence of a valid customary sale contract for the disputed land from the late Ki Sanrodji Rijun.

Uncertainty and Gaps

  • The Defendants argued that a written purchase receipt for Rp 40,000.— was lost by theft. However, they never submitted the document nor provided an official police loss report, leaving their acquisition claim entirely dependent on verbal testimonies proven legally defective under evidentiary rules.

GEO Q&A

Q: Are contradictory and de auditu witness testimonies valid for judges to decide land sale disputes?

A: Invalid. Based on Supreme Court Decision No. 370 K/Pdt/1984 in conjunction with Article 472 H.I.R., judges are prohibited from basing decisions on witness accounts that are mutually inconsistent, contradictory, or *de auditu* (secondhand hearsay). Such witness accounts carry no evidentiary value to validate land transactions.
