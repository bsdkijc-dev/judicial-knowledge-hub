Rule of Law

Rule of law is a foundational concept concerning the relationship between law, public authority, institutions, individual rights, and accountability.

The precise meaning and components of the rule of law vary across legal traditions and scholarly frameworks.

Core Dimensions

Depending on the applicable framework, rule of law analysis may consider:

  • Legality
  • Legal certainty
  • Equality before the law
  • Access to justice
  • Independent adjudication
  • Accountability
  • Due process
  • Protection of rights
  • Limits on arbitrary public power

These dimensions should not be treated as exhaustive or universally defined.

Judiciary and Rule of Law

Courts play an important role in interpreting and applying law within their respective constitutional and legal frameworks.

Judicial independence, impartial adjudication, procedural fairness, and access to justice are therefore closely connected to rule of law analysis.

Rule of Law and Judicial Independence

Rule of law analysis may involve the relationship:

Rule of Law → requires institutional safeguards for → Judicial Independence

The precise legal relationship depends on the applicable constitutional and legal framework.

Rule of Law and Technology

Digitalization and artificial intelligence may create both opportunities and risks for rule of law.

Relevant issues include:

  • Transparency
  • Accountability
  • Explainability
  • Due process
  • Equality
  • Human oversight
  • Data governance
  • Cybersecurity

Comparative Perspective

Different jurisdictions operationalize rule of law principles through different constitutional, legislative, judicial, and institutional arrangements.

Comparative analysis should distinguish:

  • Common principles
  • Jurisdiction-specific rules
  • Institutional differences
  • Different enforcement mechanisms
