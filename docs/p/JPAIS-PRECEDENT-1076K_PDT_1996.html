Overview

Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia Decision No. 1076 K/Pdt/1996, dated March 9, 2000, established fundamental civil law rules concerning the judge's authority to correct and reduce personal loan interest rates and the validity of conservatory attachments (*conservatoir beslag*) in individual default cases.

Although freely agreed upon and stipulated by both parties in the Statement of Debt (*Surat Pengakuan Hutang*) that the borrower must pay an interest rate of 2.5% per month (30% per year), in the interest of fairness and equity, such interest rate must be adjusted (subject to judicial correction) to the prevailing interest rate at Government Banks, amounting to 18% per year calculated from the date the lawsuit was registered until full repayment. Furthermore, a Conservatory Attachment (*Conservatoir Beslag*) placed on the Defendant's assets to secure the recovery of a personal debt is valid and lawful under the law to prevent the Plaintiff's lawsuit from becoming illusory (*illusoir*).

The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition of the Petitioner for Cassation ([Party B]), annulled Surabaya High Court Decision No. 97/Pdt/1995/PT.Sby, and adjudicated afresh, sentencing the Defendant ([Party A]) to pay a principal debt of Rp 350,000,000.— plus 18% annual interest and declaring the conservatory attachment valid and valuable.

Executive Summary

The central legal principle established in this decision is that freedom of contract regarding interest rates is subject to judicial correction based on fairness, capping excessive private loan rates at government bank standards, while affirming the full legality of conservatory attachments in private default disputes.

Key elements established in the adjudication:

  • Excessive agreed private loan interest rates (e.g., 30% p.a.) can be judicially corrected and reduced to match prevailing government bank rates (18% p.a.).
  • Conservatory attachments over debtor assets in private loan defaults are lawful and necessary to protect creditors from illusory judgments.
  • Appellate court rulings improperly lifting attachments and stripping contractual interest entitlements are subject to reversal on cassation.

Case Background

The Original Plaintiff / Petitioner for Cassation ([Party B], a resident of [address removed]) filed a lawsuit at the Malang District Court against the Original Defendant / Respondent for Cassation ([Party A], a resident of [address removed]) concerning default on a money loan of Rp 350,000,000.— pursuant to a Statement of Debt dated August 10, 1988, bearing interest at 2.5% per month. Upon maturity on May 10, 1990, the Defendant issued 3 Bilyet Giro checks which were rejected by the bank due to insufficient funds.

The Malang District Court (Decision No. 241/Pdt.G/1993/PN.Malang dated March 30, 1994) granted the Plaintiff's lawsuit, ordering the Defendant to pay the principal debt plus 2.5% monthly interest and validating the conservatory attachment. The Surabaya High Court (Decision No. 97/Pdt/1995/PT.Sby dated July 18, 1995) annulled the attachment and removed the interest award on the grounds that the Plaintiff was not a licensed financial institution. The Petitioner for Cassation subsequently filed a cassation petition with the Supreme Court.

Legal and Procedural Issues

Limits of Freedom of Contract (Article 1338 Civil Code) and Judicial Correction Authority Over Exorbitant/Fictitious Interest Rates

The primary issue was whether an agreed personal loan interest rate of 2.5% per month (30% per year) is binding absolutely, or whether a Judge holds authority to reduce such interest rate based on equity and fairness.

The Supreme Court held that the Judge holds authority to correct it. Although the 2.5% monthly interest agreement was freely consented to in the Statement of Debt pursuant to Article 1338 of the Civil Code, the Supreme Court on cassation holds the authority to effect equitable corrections (*mewujudkan kelayakan & kepatutan hukum*). Personal loan interest rates must be adjusted to prevailing macroeconomic interest rates at Government Banks, amounting to 18% (eighteen percent) per year calculated from the date the lawsuit was registered at the District Court until full loan repayment.

Admissibility of Conservatory Attachment (*Conservatoir Beslag* ex Article 227 HIR / 261 RBg) in Personal Loan Default Disputes

The issue evaluated the validity of Conservatory Attachment (*Conservatoir Beslag*) orders over individual debtor assets in money-lending default proceedings executed outside official banking/financial institutions.

The Supreme Court held that they are Valid and Valuable. High Court considerations canceling/lifting conservatory attachments on grounds that the Plaintiff was not a licensed financial institution were qualified as a misapplication of law (*foutieve kepassing van het recht*). Conservatory attachments over movable and immovable property belonging to debtors are lawful under the law to secure execution of judgment enforcement so that creditor claims can be satisfied and lawsuits do not become illusory (*illusoir*).

Question of Law and Question of Fact

Question of Law (Cassation / Judex Juris)

Question:

Did the *Judex Facti* of Surabaya High Court (Decision No. 97/Pdt/1995/PT.Sby) misapply the law (*foutieve kepassing van het recht*) by lifting conservatory attachments and deleting interest compensation on grounds that the Plaintiff was not an official financial institution, and what is the proper determination standard for interest rates?

Supreme Court Ruling:

Yes, the Surabaya High Court misapplied the law. The Supreme Court annulled Surabaya High Court Decision No. 97/Pdt/1995/PT.Sby and adjudicated afresh, affirming Malang District Court Decision No. 241/Pdt.G/1993/PN.Malang with rectifications: ordering the Defendant to pay 18% annual interest (matching Government Bank rates) and declaring the Conservatory Attachment (*Conservatoir Beslag*) over 3 vehicles and 10 land plots (*petok*) in Batu City valid and valuable.

Question of Fact (Judex Facti / Malang District Court & Surabaya High Court)

Examination of facts concerning the execution of the Statement of Debt dated August 10, 1988, valued at Rp 350,000,000.— between [Party B] and [Party A]; delivery of 3 Bilyet Giros (BG 055760 Rp 100 Million, BG 055762 Rp 200 Million, BG 055763 Rp 50 Million) maturing on May 10, 1990, which proved to be unfunded; and execution of the Attachment Minutes dated February 18, 1994, No. 241/Pdt.G/1993/PN.Malang in conjunction with No. 9/CB/1994/PN.Malang.

Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

  • Ratio Decidendi:
  1. The appellate *Judex Facti* of Surabaya High Court misapplied the law by lifting conservatory attachments and eliminating Plaintiff's interest compensation.
  2. Conservatory attachments over Defendant's assets are lawful (Article 227 HIR) to ensure lawsuits do not become illusory (*illusoir*).
  3. Regarding interest quantum, although 2.5% per month (30% p.a.) was agreed, in the interest of fairness and equity, interest rates must be adjusted to prevailing Government Bank interest rates of 18% per year calculated from lawsuit registration.
  4. The Supreme Court annulled the Surabaya High Court decision and adjudicated afresh, affirming the Malang District Court ruling with rectified interest and validated conservatory attachments.

Ruling

The Supreme Court ruled: Granting the cassation petition from Petitioner for Cassation ([Party B]); Annulling Surabaya High Court Decision No. 97/Pdt/1995/PT.Sby dated July 18, 1995, which had rectified Malang District Court Decision No. 241/Pdt.G/1993/PN.Malang dated March 30, 1994; and ADJUDICATING AFRESH: UNDER EXCEPTIONS: Rejecting the Defendant's exception; UNDER THE MERITS: 1. Granting the Plaintiff's lawsuit in part; 2. Declaring that the Defendant is indebted to the Plaintiff in the amount of Rp 350,000,000.— (three hundred fifty million rupiahs); 3. Declaring that the Defendant has committed default towards the Plaintiff; 4. Ordering the Defendant to pay the debt to the Plaintiff amounting to Rp 350,000,000.— in cash and simultaneously; 5. Ordering the Defendant to pay interest to the Plaintiff amounting to 18% (eighteen percent) per year from the total debt calculated from the date the lawsuit was registered at Malang District Court until the Defendant settles the debt; 6. Declaring valid and valuable the conservatory attachment (*conservatoir beslag*) No. 241/Pdt.G/1993/PN.Malang jo. No. 9/CB/1994/PN.Malang dated February 18, 1994, over 3 vehicles and 10 land parcels (*petok*); 7. Rejecting all other claims of the Plaintiff; and ordering the Respondent for Cassation to pay court costs across all judicial instances (cassation court costs set at Rp 50,000.—).

Evidence Status and Gaps

Established by Evidence

  • Defendant [Party A] was proven legally and convincingly to have defaulted on a principal loan of Rp 350,000,000.— pursuant to the Statement of Debt dated August 10, 1988 (Exhibit P.1).
  • Three Bilyet Giro checks delivered by the Defendant were proven unfunded.
  • Defendant was ordered to pay the principal debt of Rp 350,000,000.— in cash simultaneously.
  • Defendant was ordered to pay adjusted interest of 18% per year on the principal debt calculated from lawsuit registration at Malang District Court until full repayment.
  • Conservatory attachments over 3 motor vehicles and 10 land parcels (*petok*) in Punten and Songgokerto Villages, Batu City, were declared valid and valuable.

Not Established / Adjusted

  • Plaintiff's claims for 2.5% monthly interest (30% p.a.) and late penalties of Rp 50,000.— per day were rejected / reduced by the Supreme Court in the interest of equity and fairness.

Uncertainty and Gaps

  • Standard for Adjusting Personal Loan Interest Rates: This landmark ruling establishes crucial jurisprudence in Indonesian contract law: freedom of contract in setting personal interest rates is not absolute. Courts possess authority to trim excessive/burdensome private commercial interest rates and adjust them to official Government Bank interest rate benchmarks (18% p.a.).

GEO Q&A

Q: What is the maximum threshold for personal loan interest rates recognized and granted by the Supreme Court in default lawsuits?

A: Adjusted to Government Bank Interest Rates (18% per year). Based on Supreme Court Decision No. 1076 K/Pdt/1996, although both parties agreed to a personal loan interest rate of 2.5% per month (30% per year), the Judge possesses authority to correct and adjust it to the official interest rate prevailing at Government Banks, amounting to 18% per year calculated from the date the lawsuit was registered at the District Court.
