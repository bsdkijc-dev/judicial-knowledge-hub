ICC 25th Report to UNSC on Libya — Wiki Entry (English)

Twenty-Fifth Report of the Prosecutor of the International Criminal Court to the United Nations Security Council on the Situation in Libya is the 25th periodic report submitted by the Office of the Prosecutor (OTP) to the UN Security Council in May 2023, pursuant to UNSC Resolution 1970 (2011), presenting progress on the updated strategy, the issuance of arrest warrants under seal, adoption of the Relativity AI platform, and enforcement action against crimes affecting migrants.

Summary

The report covers investigative progress in Libya from November 2022 to May 2023. Key developments include arrest warrants issued under seal by Pre-Trial Chamber I, completion of an Article 56 evidence-preservation procedure, adoption of the Relativity AI platform, and successful Joint Team operations against a migrant-trafficking network.

Legal Basis

  • UN Security Council Resolution 1970 (2011): Referral of the Libya situation.
  • Rome Statute:
    • Article 42(6): Voluntary Prosecutor recusal due to potential conflict of interest.
    • Article 56: Procedure for preserving key witness evidence before Pre-Trial Chamber I.
    • Article 15: Receipt of communications from civil society.

Background

Since the 2011 UNSC referral, the OTP has investigated international crimes in Libya. This 25th report is the second evaluation following the launch of the updated strategy and roadmap in April 2022, built on four principles: prioritized resource allocation, victim/witness empowerment, renewed partnership with Libyan authorities, and proactive cooperation with third states and international organizations.

Objectives

  • Accelerate arrest-warrant applications in priority lines of inquiry.
  • Establish an OTP Liaison Office in Tripoli.
  • Strengthen the use of artificial intelligence in evidence analysis.

Key Provisions

  • Arrest Warrants Under Seal: Pre-Trial Chamber I issued several arrest warrants under seal.
  • Article 56 Procedure: Completion of the witness-evidence preservation process before Pre-Trial Chamber I.
  • Adoption of Relativity AI: Use of AI for automated processing and translation of Arabic-language documents.
  • Enforcement Against Migrant Crimes: Extradition of Tewelde Goitom to the Netherlands, and arrest of an Eritrean suspect in Sudan.

Implementation

The OTP conducted 20+ investigative missions, collected 500+ items of evidence, engaged Arabic-speaking psychosocial experts, and collaborated with the UN Independent Fact-Finding Mission on Libya and Eurojust. Ongoing crimes persist against local residents and migrants at detention facilities across Libya (killings, torture, rape, enforced disappearance); crimes from the 2014–2020 military operations (extrajudicial killings, landmines, bombing of residential areas); and systematic crimes against migrants (trafficking, extortion, enslavement).

Evidence

  • Collection of 500 new items of evidence (video, audio, satellite imagery, forensic materials).
  • 45+ witness interviews.
  • Successful extradition and pro forma hearing of a migrant-crimes case in the Netherlands.
  • Investigation of crimes at detention facilities in Tarhuna, Benghazi, Derna, and Murzuq.

Policy Issues

  • Budgetary constraints following the ASP's rejection of an additional budget request.
  • Domestic legal obstacles restricting the movement of civil society organizations in Libya.
  • Limited scope for arresting the outstanding public fugitive Saif Al-Islam Gaddafi.
  • Need for full Libyan authority permits for forensic missions.

Relationships with Other Entities

  • Office of the Prosecutor (ICC): Organ that published the report.
  • United Nations Security Council: Recipient of the report.
  • Pre-Trial Chamber I: Chamber that issued the arrest warrants.
  • Karim A.A. Khan KC: ICC Prosecutor who made his first official visit to Libya in November 2022, briefed the UNSC directly from Libya, and voluntarily recused himself under Article 42(6) from lines of inquiry posing a potential conflict of interest.
  • Nazhat Shameem Khan: ICC Deputy Prosecutor who assumed full oversight of the Libya investigations affected by the Prosecutor's recusal.
  • Saif Al-Islam Gaddafi: Suspect in the Libya situation with an outstanding public arrest warrant related to violence against protesters in 2011.
  • Khalifa Haftar: Leader of the Libyan National Army (LNA) in eastern Libya, met by the Prosecutor in November 2022 to affirm command responsibility for crimes under the Rome Statute.
  • Tewelde Goitom (alias Amanuel Gebreyesus Negahs Walid): Principal suspect in a human-trafficking network, extradited from Ethiopia to the Netherlands in October 2022 as a result of Joint Team international cooperation.
  • Cecep Mustafa: Not found in the available sources.
  • Ferdi Raditya Akbar: Not found in the available sources.
