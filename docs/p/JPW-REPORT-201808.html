Definition

An official judicial policy research publication issued by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of the Supreme Court of Korea (authored by Senior Research Fellow Lee, Jong Youp and former Research Fellow Kim, Ju-kyung) that systematically establishes general and specific fundamental-rights-based standards of constitutional review (위헌심사기준) for regular courts conducting daily statutory interpretation and constitutional adjudication (법원의 헌법판단) through the Principle of Constitutional Interpretation of Statutes (합헌적 법률해석의 원칙), examines general constitutional review principles (proportionality, protection of trust, non-retroactivity, prohibition of blanket delegation, due process) and area-specific review standards (equality, freedom of occupation, property rights, social rights), and conducts a comparative empirical analysis of conflicting constitutional rulings between regular courts and the Constitutional Court in norm-control constitutional complaint cases (헌바 cases under Constitutional Court Act Art. 68(2)).

4. Policy Analysis

Background
Although the Constitutional Court of Korea holds ultimate authority to review the constitutionality of statutes (Article 111(1) of the ROK Constitution), the regular judiciary (ordinary courts) carries an inherent constitutional function through the resolution of concrete disputes and the application of the "Principle of Constitutional Interpretation of Statutes" (합헌적 법률해석의 원칙). When interpreting and applying the law, judges consistently limit, exclude, supplement, or correct the meaning of statutory provisions to align them with the Constitution, which substantially performs a norm-control function (규범통제). Therefore, ordinary courts require careful, systematic, and standardized benchmarks and standards for reviewing constitutionality (위헌심사기준).

Policy Problem
Uncertainty and Inconsistency of Review Standards in Ordinary Courts: Judges in ordinary courts often fail to explicitly articulate the standard or intensity of constitutional review (심사강도) in their legal reasoning, resulting in a lack of rigor in constitutional argumentation.

Divergence in Rulings Between Ordinary Courts and the Constitutional Court: In norm-control constitutional complaint cases (헌바 cases under Article 68(2) of the Constitutional Court Act), there are cases where ordinary courts dismissed requests for constitutional review (위헌제청신청 기각), yet the Constitutional Court subsequently declared the same statutory provision unconstitutional (333 cases out of 6,606 petitions between 1988 and 2018, or 11.8% of substantively decided cases).

Passive Handling of Human Rights and Social Rights Cases: The absence of a structured framework leads ordinary courts to tend to reject constitutional review petitions by merely citing "broad legislative discretion" without conducting precise, tiered scrutiny.

Legal/Policy Gap
The absence of a comprehensive manual mapping general and specific standards of constitutional review (organized by type of constitutional right) specifically designed for the practical needs of judges in ordinary courts.

Objectives
Provide detailed guidance on general review standards (proportionality, protection of trust, prohibition of retroactivity, prohibition of blanket delegation, and due process) as well as specific review standards (equality, freedom of occupation, property rights, and social rights); enhance the certainty and precision of constitutional analysis in ordinary courts; and ensure the effective protection of citizens' fundamental rights.

Implementation & Core Review Framework
Definition of Standards of Constitutional Review (위헌심사기준의 의의):

  • Scale/Method (척도 내지 방법): Substantive benchmarks such as the principle of proportionality (과잉금지원칙), the principle of equality (평등원칙), and the principle of protection of trust (신뢰보호원칙).
  • Intensity of Scrutiny (심사강도): The level of strictness of review (strict scrutiny, intermediate scrutiny, rational basis test / 자의금지원칙) determined by the degree of legislative discretion and the importance of the right being restricted.

General Standards of Constitutional Review (일반적 위헌심사기준):

  • Principle of Proportionality / Prohibition of Excessive Restriction (과잉금지원칙 — Article 37(2)): Tests four tiered elements: (1) Legitimacy of Purpose (목적의 정당성), (2) Suitability of Means (수단의 적절성), (3) Minimal Restriction (피해의 최소성), and (4) Balance of Legal Interests (법익의 균형성).
  • Principle of Protection of Trust (신뢰보호원칙): Derived from the Principle of the Rule of Law (Rechtsstaat / 법치국가원리), weighing citizens' trust interests against the public interest sought through legal change.
  • Prohibition of Retroactive Legislation (소급입법금지원칙 — Article 13): Distinguishes genuine retroactive legislation (진정소급입법 — generally prohibited) from non-genuine retroactive legislation (부진정소급입법 — tested via the principle of protection of trust).
  • Prohibition of Blanket Delegation (포괄위임금지원칙 — Article 75): Promotes certainty and predictability in criminal and tax law.
  • Principle of Due Process (적법절차원칙 — Article 12): Guarantees formal/procedural as well as substantive due process across all state actions.

Area-Specific Standards of Constitutional Review (개별적 위헌심사기준):

  • Right to Equality (평등권 — Article 11): Determines whether differential treatment occurs among essentially similar groups (본질적으로 같은 것), then applies either the arbitrariness test (자의금지원칙) or strict proportionality review.
  • Freedom of Occupation (직업의 자유 — Article 15): Applies the 3-Stage Theory (단계이론): (1) Restrictions on the exercise of an occupation (rationality review), (2) Restrictions on subjective qualifications (intermediate proportionality review), and (3) Restrictions on objective qualifications (strict proportionality review).
  • Property Rights (재산권 — Article 23): Distinguishes content/limitation restrictions on property [Article 23(1)(2)] from expropriation [Article 23(3)], adopting the Separation Theory (분리이론) over the Boundary Theory (경계이론).
  • Social Rights (사회적 기본권 — Article 34): Applies the Prohibition of Minimal Protection (과소보호금지원칙) and adopts Alexy's Principles Model (원칙모델), combining substantive and procedural review.

Evidence & Empirical Data
Of the 6,606 헌바 cases filed with the Constitutional Court between 1988 and 2018, 333 cases (11.8% of substantively decided cases) were declared unconstitutional by the Constitutional Court after having previously been dismissed by ordinary courts.

This report conducts an in-depth analysis of 15 Constitutional Court decisions related to 과잉금지원칙, 3 decisions related to 신뢰보호원칙, 4 decisions related to 소급입법금지원칙, 4 decisions related to 포괄위임금지원칙, 3 decisions related to 적법절차원칙, 8 decisions related to 평등권, and 2 decisions related to 직업의 자유.

6. MediaWiki Output

A Study on Standards of Constitutional Review for Constitutional Judgment of Court is an official judicial policy research report published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of South Korea's Supreme Court.

Summary

This document is an official judicial policy research report published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of South Korea's Supreme Court. It systematically develops standards of constitutional review (위헌심사기준) for ordinary court judges performing constitutional adjudication (법원의 헌법판단) through the Principle of Constitutional Interpretation of Statutes (합헌적 법률해석의 원칙). The study analyzes general review principles (proportionality, protection of trust, prohibition of retroactivity, prohibition of blanket delegation, due process) and area-specific review standards (equality, freedom of occupation, property rights, social rights), and conducts a comparative empirical study of divergent rulings between ordinary courts and the Constitutional Court in 헌바 cases (norm-control cases under Article 68(2) of the Constitutional Court Act).

Legal Basis

The legal frameworks analyzed include:

  • Constitution of the Republic of Korea:
    • Article 101(1) (Judicial Authority Vested in Courts) & Article 103 (Judicial Independence Under the Constitution and Law).
    • Article 107(1) (Court's Right to Request Constitutional Review / 위헌법률심판제청권) & Article 107(2) (Supreme Court's Authority for Final Review of Executive Orders/Dispositions).
    • Article 37(2) (General Limitation Clause & Principle of Proportionality / 과잉금지원칙).
    • Article 11(1) (Equality), Article 12 (Due Process), Article 13 (Prohibition of Retroactivity), Article 15 (Freedom of Occupation), Article 23 (Property Rights), Article 34 (Social Rights), and Article 75 (Prohibition of Blanket Delegation).
  • Constitutional Court Act (헌법재판소법): Article 41 & Article 68(2) (Norm-Control Constitutional Complaint Cases / 헌바 사건).
  • Court Organization Act (법원조직법): Article 2(3) & Article 15.

Background

Although the Constitutional Court holds final authority over judicial review of statutes, ordinary courts perform constitutional adjudication on a daily basis through constitutionally-aligned statutory interpretation (합헌적 법률해석). Interpreting statutes to align with the Constitution practically functions and has the same effect as norm control (규범통제). The lack of a structured review standard in ordinary courts frequently leads to the dismissal of constitutional review petitions that are later overturned by the Constitutional Court (reaching 11.8% of substantively decided 헌바 cases).

Objectives

  1. Map general and specific standards of constitutional review for ordinary court judges.
  2. Improve the precision and rigor of constitutional argumentation in ordinary court rulings.
  3. Empirically analyze the divergence in rulings between ordinary courts and the Constitutional Court to draw practical implications.

Scope

  • General Review Standards: 과잉금지원칙 (Proportionality), 신뢰보호원칙 (Protection of Trust), 소급입법금지원칙 (Prohibition of Retroactivity), 포괄위임금지원칙 (Prohibition of Blanket Delegation), and 적법절차원칙 (Due Process).
  • Area-Specific Review Standards: 평등권 (Right to Equality), 직업의 자유 (Freedom of Occupation / 3-Stage Theory), 재산권 (Property Rights / Separation Theory), and 사회적 기본권 (Social Rights / Alexy's Model).
  • Empirical Study of 헌바 Cases: Comparative analysis of 15–47 Constitutional Court decisions against prior dismissals of constitutional review petitions by ordinary courts.

Key Provisions

  • Proportionality Test Structure (과잉금지원칙): Applies 4 tiered stages: Legitimacy of Purpose, Suitability of Means, Minimal Restriction (피해의 최소성), and Balance of Legal Interests (법익의 균형성).
  • Freedom of Occupation Review (단계이론): Applies rationality review for restrictions on the exercise of an occupation; intermediate proportionality review for subjective qualifications; and strict review for objective qualifications.
  • Property Rights Review (분리이론): Adopts the Separation Theory, distinguishing content/limitation restrictions on property (Article 23(1)(2)) from expropriation/compensation (Article 23(3)).
  • Social Rights Review (과소보호금지원칙): Applies the prohibition of minimal protection and combines substantive review with procedural control.

Implementation

This draft and research standard is applied as an official guideline for constitutional analysis by judges at all court levels in South Korea, as well as a reference for drafting petitions for constitutional review submitted to the Constitutional Court.

Policy Issues

  • Lack of transparency in constitutional argumentation within ordinary court rulings.
  • Passive dismissal of constitutional review petitions by ordinary courts due to a tendency to over-rely on legislative discretion.

Reform / Amendments

  1. Standardization of a tiered constitutional review framework in ordinary courts.
  2. Active application of constitutionally-aligned statutory interpretation (합헌적 법률해석의 활성화).
  3. Strengthening procedural control in the review of social rights and property rights.

3 Most Important Improvements:

  1. Institutionalization of Tiered Constitutional Review Standards in the Ordinary Court Judgment-Writing Manual: Integrate the 과잉금지원칙 (4-tier proportionality) framework, the 평등권 arbitrariness/proportionality review, and the 직업의 자유 3-Stage Theory into judicial judgment-writing manuals to improve the rigor of constitutional argumentation in ordinary courts.
  2. Strengthening the Active Use of Constitutionally-Aligned Statutory Interpretation (합헌적 법률해석의 적극적 활용): Optimize the function of ordinary court judges to limit or correct the meaning of statutory provisions that may violate fundamental rights before filing a petition for constitutional review, thereby providing direct and immediate protection of rights to the parties.
  3. Adoption of Procedural Control in the Review of Social Rights & Property Rights (사회적 기본권 절차적 통제 도입): Expand judicial review of social rights and property rights provisions by requiring the legislature (입법자) to demonstrate sufficient data, rational calculation methods, and the adequacy of the legislative process (modeled on the German Bundesverfassungsgericht/Betreuungsgericht review standard).

Suggested Next Steps: As a further avenue of analysis, two strategic directions could be pursued: (1) a detailed comparative matrix of constitutional review frameworks (Korean General Courts vs. the Constitutional Court vs. US Federal Strict Scrutiny vs. the German Bundesverfassungsgericht), examining review structures, burden-of-proof allocation, and the margin of legislative discretion across major jurisdictions; and (2) an empirical analysis of divergence in 헌바 case rulings (Article 68(2) of the Constitutional Court Act), mapping 15 concrete cases in which a lower court's dismissal of a constitutional review petition was later overturned by the Constitutional Court, to identify shifts in legal reasoning within the judiciary.
