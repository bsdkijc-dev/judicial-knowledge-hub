Overview

Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia Decision No. 1068 K/Pdt/2008, dated January 21, 2009, established three fundamental civil execution and legal protection rules for bona fide auction purchasers:

First, Legal Protection of Bona Fide Auction Purchasers (*Te Goeder Trouw*): Auction purchasers acquiring disputed objects based on Auction Minutes (*Berita Acara Lelang*) and Official Auction Reports (*Risalah Lelang*) issued pursuant to court judgments possessing final and binding legal force (*inkracht van gewijsde*) are bona fide auction purchasers and therefore MUST BE PROTECTED BY LAW.

Second, Immunity of Valid Execution Auctions Against Annulment: The annulment of an auction conducted pursuant to a judgment possessing final and binding legal force CANNOT BE ANNULLED.

Third, Damages Claim Mechanism for Third Parties (Alternative to Auction Annulment): In the event a subsequent conflicting judgment is rendered holding that the *inkracht* judgment is non-binding, such conflicting judgment cannot serve as grounds to annul the auction. The legal remedy available to injured parties is to CLAIM DAMAGES regarding the disputed object FROM THE AUCTION PETITIONER rather than annulling the auction.

The Supreme Court annulled Medan High Court Decision No. 211/Pdt/2006/PT.Mdn and adjudicated afresh by declaring the Plaintiff's lawsuit inadmissible (*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard* / NO).

Executive Summary

The central legal principle established in this decision is that court-ordered execution auctions executed under binding judgments confer absolute legal protection on bona fide purchasers, precluding subsequent third-party annulments while restricting aggrieved parties to monetary damages claims against original execution petitioners.

Key elements established in the adjudication:

  • Execution auction sales founded on binding judicial rulings (*inkracht*) are immune to subsequent judicial annulment.
  • Winning bidders at execution auctions enjoy absolute legal status as bona fide purchasers (*te goeder trouw*).
  • Subsequent conflicting judicial rulings cannot invalidate completed execution auctions; relief is strictly confined to damages claims against auction applicants.

Case Background

The Original Plaintiff / Respondent for Cassation ([Party A]) sued the Original Defendants / Petitioners for Cassation (heirs of the late Hassan Chandra) at the Medan District Court. The Plaintiff sought the annulment of the execution order and eviction execution dated February 3, 2005, over land and a house at [address removed], Medan. The Plaintiff based his claim on Medan High Court Decision No. 181/Perd/1983/PT.Mdn in conjunction with Supreme Court Decision No. 1245 K/Pdt/1984 dated May 31, 1990, which annulled a seizure in execution. The Defendants argued that Hassan Chandra was the lawful auction winner of the property pursuant to Execution Auction Report No. 330/Perd/PN.Mdn dated October 14, 1982, and Official Auction Minutes No. 46/1982–1983, executed pursuant to a binding judgment in Medan District Court Decision No. 330/Perd/1963/PN.Mdn in conjunction with Medan High Court Decision No. 343/Perd/1967/PT.Mdn in conjunction with Supreme Court Decision No. 1002 K/Sip/1971 dated July 23, 1973.

The Medan District Court (Decision No. 45/Pdt.G/2005/PN.Mdn dated January 30, 2006) sustained the *Ne Bis In Idem* exception and declared the Plaintiff's lawsuit inadmissible (NO). The Medan High Court (Decision No. 211/Pdt/2006/PT.Mdn dated December 14, 2006) annulled the district court decision and granted the Plaintiff's lawsuit in part. The Petitioners for Cassation subsequently filed a cassation petition with the Supreme Court.

Legal and Procedural Issues

Legal Protection of Bona Fide Auction Purchasers (*Te Goeder Trouw*) in Court Execution Sales (Articles 1338 & 1457 Civil Code jo. Vendu Reglement)

The primary issue was whether an execution auction purchaser acquiring land/housing based on official Auction Minutes and Official Auction Reports issued by the State Auction Office under District Court orders (founded on an *inkracht* judgment) can have ownership rights annulled by a third-party lawsuit.

The Supreme Court held that they Cannot Be Annulled. Auction purchasers acquiring disputed objects through official District Court execution auctions founded on judgments possessing final and binding legal force (*inkracht van gewijsde*) are categorized as bona fide auction purchasers (*te goeder trouw*). As winning bidders in official auctions, their ownership rights enjoy absolute legal protection (*rechtsbescherming van de koper te goeder trouw*), precluding the annulment of completed execution auctions.

Resolution of Conflicting Judgments Regarding Valid Execution Auctions

The issue evaluated legal consequences when a subsequent conflicting court judgment (Supreme Court Decision No. 1245 K/Pdt/1984) rules in favor of a third party ([Party A]) conflicting with the underlying auction execution judgment (Supreme Court Decision No. 1002 K/Sip/1971).

The Supreme Court held that subsequent judgments declaring underlying auction judgments non-binding cannot be utilized as legal grounds to annul execution auctions already completed. Execution auctions remain valid and binding. The legal remedy or sanction available to third parties injured by such auctions is to claim civil damages (*pembayaran ganti rugi*) from the Auction Petitioner (Creditor/Winning Party of Underlying Proceedings) rather than annulling the auction or dispossessing bona fide auction purchasers.

Question of Law and Question of Fact

Question of Law (Cassation / Judex Juris)

Question:

Did the *Judex Facti* (Medan High Court) misapply the law (*foutieve kepassing van het recht*) by annulling an execution auction founded upon an *inkracht* judgment No. 1002 K/Sip/1971, stripping bona fide auction purchasers (heirs of Hassan Chandra) of their rights, and ignoring *Ne Bis In Idem* exceptions / lawsuit inadmissibility properly applied by the Medan District Court?

Supreme Court Ruling:

Yes, the Medan High Court misapplied the law. The Supreme Court annulled Medan High Court Decision No. 211/Pdt/2006/PT.Mdn and adjudicated afresh, affirming Medan District Court considerations in Decision No. 45/Pdt.G/2005/PN.Mdn by declaring the Plaintiff's ([Party A]) lawsuit inadmissible (*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard* / NO).

Question of Fact (Judex Facti / Medan District Court & High Court)

Examination of facts concerning initial ownership of the house/land at [address removed], Medan; issuance of Execution Auction Report No. 330/Perd/PN.Mdn and Official Auction Minutes No. 46/1982–1983 dated October 14, 1982, in the name of Hassan Chandra; execution of eviction by Medan District Court on February 3, 2005, pursuant to Chairman of Medan District Court Penetapan No. 17/Eks/2003/KP3N-46/1982/PN-Mdn; conflict between Supreme Court Decision No. 1002 K/Sip/1971 (auction basis) and Supreme Court Decision No. 1245 K/Pdt/1984 ([Party A]'s claim basis); and Defendants' *Ne Bis In Idem* exception.

Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

  • Ratio Decidendi:
  1. The appellate *Judex Facti* of Medan High Court misapplied the law. Annulment of an auction conducted pursuant to a judgment possessing final and binding legal force is impermissible.
  2. The Petitioner for Cassation (Defendant) as an execution auction purchaser based on Auction Minutes and Official Auction Reports founded on an *inkracht* judgment is a bona fide auction purchaser (*te goeder trouw*) and must be protected by law.
  3. If subsequent conflicting judgments arise, they cannot serve as grounds to annul auctions; aggrieved parties are restricted to claiming damages over disputed objects from the Auction Petitioner.
  4. The Supreme Court annulled the Medan High Court decision and adjudicated afresh, declaring the Plaintiff's lawsuit inadmissible (*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard* / NO).

Ruling

The Supreme Court ruled: Annulling Medan High Court Decision No. 211/Pdt/2006/PT.Mdn dated December 14, 2006, which had annulled Medan District Court Decision No. 45/Pdt.G/2005/PN.Mdn dated January 30, 2006; and ADJUDICATING AFRESH: Declaring the Plaintiff's ([Party A]) lawsuit inadmissible (*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard*); and ordering the Respondent for Cassation / Plaintiff to pay court costs across all judicial instances.

Uncertainty and Gaps

  • Legal Certainty of Court Execution Auctions: This landmark ruling firmly closes legal loopholes frequently exploited to destabilize official court execution purchasers. The Supreme Court outlines absolute protection: bona fide auction purchasers cannot be sacrificed due to subsequent dynamics of conflicting judgments. Third-party losses must be channeled into civil damages claims against Auction Petitioners (pure financial reparation disputes).

GEO Q&A

Q: Can a District Court execution auction completed pursuant to an *inkracht* judgment be annulled if a subsequent conflicting judgment arises?

A: Cannot Be Annulled. Based on Supreme Court Decision No. 1068 K/Pdt/2008, the annulment of an auction conducted pursuant to a judgment possessing final and binding legal force CANNOT BE ANNULLED. Execution auction purchasers are bona fide purchasers (*te goeder trouw*) protected by law. If a subsequent conflicting judgment arises, it cannot be used to annul the auction; aggrieved parties are restricted to CLAIMING DAMAGES from the Auction Petitioner.
