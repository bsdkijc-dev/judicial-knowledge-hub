Definition

An official judicial policy research publication issued by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of the Supreme Court of Korea that evaluates the legal, constitutional, and practical defects of Korea's criminal trial in absentia system (피고인 불출석 재판 / 궐석재판); conducts comparative legal research across 4 foreign jurisdictions (US, Germany, Japan, France); categorizes non-attendance into a 4-tier model based on defendant fault and procedural stage; and formulates statutory amendments to the Criminal Procedure Act, the Act on Special Cases Concerning Expedition, etc. of Legal Proceedings, and the Protection of Communications Secrets Act.

4. Policy Analysis

Background
The defendant's presence in criminal trials is both a constitutional fundamental right (guaranteeing defense rights and the right to be heard) and a statutory obligation. Under Korean law, trials in absentia (피고인 불출석 재판) are restricted as narrow exceptions under the Criminal Procedure Act (CPC) and Article 23 of the Act on Special Cases Concerning Expedition, etc. of Legal Proceedings (Special Expedited Act). Following the Constitutional Court's 1998 unconstitutionality decision (97Hun-Ba22), Article 23 was revised in 1999 to exclude crimes punishable by death, life imprisonment, or minimum 10+ years imprisonment.

Policy Problem
Rigid Uniformity & Lack of Defendant-Fault Differentiation: The current system applies a uniform, strict 6-month wait and complex procedural hurdles (address correction, location search 소재탐지촉탁, detention warrant issuance) regardless of whether the defendant is genuinely unaware of the trial or intentionally evading court appearance / fleeing.

Severe Trial Delays & Systemic Abuse: Defendants intentionally fail to appear at trial or sentencing dates to delay judgment, avoid arrest, or push their crimes past statutory probation expiration periods (집행유예 기간 도과). In 1st instance court-notice (고단) cases, trial in absentia averages 380 days (over double standard cases).

Ineffective Appearance Enforcement: Police execution rates for bench/detention warrants (구인영장/구금영장) for missing defendants are under 20%.

Routine Reversals & Recovery of Appeal Rights: Because courts often miss minor service steps during public notice (공시송달), upper courts routinely grant recovery of appeal rights (상소권회복), forcing appellate courts to retry cases from scratch without ever having seen the defendant in person.

Legal/Policy Gap
Korea's positive law treats a defendant who never received an indictment bill identically to a defendant who appeared at trial, received full rights warnings, and then intentionally fled before sentencing.

Objectives
Eliminate defendant incentives to flee; protect victim rights and public judicial power; establish a 4-tier model reflecting defendant fault; and streamline criminal proceedings while safeguarding due process.

4-Tier Categorization Model & Proposed Statutory Reforms
Type 1 (Indictment Unserved / 공소장 부본 미송달):

  • Status: Defendant never received the indictment bill and is unaware of prosecution.
  • Reform: Shift responsibility to the prosecution. Amend CPC Articles 266 & 328 to stipulate that if the indictment bill remains unserved 2 months after an address correction order is issued to the prosecutor, the prosecution automatically loses its effect, and the court shall issue a Dismissal of Prosecution Decision (공소기각결정) (modeled after Japanese CPC Art. 271).

Type 2 (Indictment Served but Never Appeared / 공소장 부본 송달 후 미출석):

  • Status: Indictment bill served, but defendant never attended any trial date.
  • Reform: Maintain Special Expedited Act Article 23(1) 6-month wait and public notice safeguards to protect defense rights.

Type 3 (Appeared 1+ Times then Fled/Disappeared / 1회 이상 출석 후 불출석):

  • Status: Defendant appeared for arraignment/rights warnings, then intentionally fled.
  • Reform: Enact Special Expedited Act Article 23(2). Eliminate the 6-month wait; allow trial in absentia after 2 non-consecutive absences following formal rights warnings. Service for subsequent dates shall be executed by mail dispatch (우체에 부치는 방법).

Type 4 (Fled at Sentencing Date / 변론종결 후 선고기일 불출석):

  • Status: Defendant attended full trial until pleadings closed, then fled before sentencing.
  • Reform: Enact Special Expedited Act Article 23(3). Allow immediate sentencing in absentia on the 1st unexcused absence date without wait or crime category limits.

Additional Statutory Reforms
CPC Articles 365 & 458 (Summary/Formal Trial & Appeals): Amend to allow trial in absentia upon 2 non-consecutive absences (removing the "2 consecutive absences" restriction).

CPC Article 277-2 (Arrested Defendants): Remove the requirement that "production by prison officers is impossible or extremely difficult," allowing trial in absentia when an arrested defendant refuses appearance without good cause.

CPC Article 455 (Formal Trial Request Rejection): Allow courts to issue a Decision Rejecting Formal Trial Request (정식재판청구 기각결정) when a defendant fails to appear twice.

Protection of Communications Secrets Act Article 15-2: Mandate telecommunications carrier compliance with court requests for location and communications data to locate missing defendants.

Evidence & Empirical Data
Judicial Survey of 378 Korean Judges:

  • 76% of judges stated the current Special Expedited Act Art. 23 system is improper and causes severe trial delays.
  • 93.7% of judges supported establishing a new trial in absentia system for defendants who flee after attending initial trial dates.
  • 93% of judges supported relaxing CPC Art. 365 to allow trials in absentia upon 2 non-consecutive absences.
  • 91% of judges supported introducing formal trial request rejection decisions for 2-time non-appearances.

6. MediaWiki Output

Research on Improvement Measures for Trial in Absentia in Korea is an official judicial policy research report published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of South Korea's Supreme Court.

Summary

This document is an official judicial policy research report published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of South Korea's Supreme Court. It comparatively and empirically analyzes criminal trial in absentia (피고인 불출석 재판 / 궐석재판), diagnoses structural defects in Article 23 of the Act on Special Cases Concerning Expedition, etc. of Legal Proceedings, and formulates a 4-tier categorization model along with draft amendments to the Criminal Procedure Act and the Special Expedited Act.

Legal Basis

The legal frameworks analyzed include:

  • Constitution of the Republic of Korea: Article 12(1) (Due Process Principle) and Article 27(1) (Right to a Fair Trial).
  • Act on Special Cases Concerning Expedition, etc. of Legal Proceedings (소송촉진 등에 관한 특례법 / 소촉법): Article 23 (Special Rules for First-Instance Trial in Absentia) and Article 23-2 (Retrial / 재심).
  • Criminal Procedure Act of the Republic of Korea (형사소송법): Articles 26, 27, 28, 63, 65, 72, 73, 81, 249(2), 266, 276, 277, 277-2, 278, 306, 328, 365, 453, 455, 458, 473.
  • Protection of Communications Secrets Act (통신비밀보호법): Proposed Article 15-2 (Telecommunications Carrier Cooperation Obligation).
  • Constitutional Court Decisions: Decision 97Hun-Ba22 (July 16, 1998 — Unconstitutional) and 2005Hun-Ba21 (July 21, 2005 — Constitutional).

Background

A defendant's presence at a criminal trial is both a constitutional right and obligation. However, the rigid, uniform 6-month deadline in Article 23 of the Special Expedited Act, applied without regard to defendant intent, has been exploited by fleeing defendants to delay proceedings until their probation period (집행유예) expires. This has caused trial delays (averaging 380 days in 고단 cases), wasted judicial resources, and neglect of crime victims' rights.

Objectives

  1. Eliminate incentives for defendants to flee from trial.
  2. Categorize defendant non-appearance into a 4-Tier Model based on the degree of defendant fault and procedural stage.
  3. Enact draft amendments to the Criminal Procedure Act and the Special Expedited Act to accelerate execution of trial in absentia.

Scope

  • Empirical Study: A survey of 378 South Korean judges (76% stated the current system is improper; 93.7% supported in absentia reform for fleeing defendants).
  • Four-Country Comparative Study: United States (Parker Warnings & FRCP Rule 43), Germany (StPO §§ 230, 231, 231a, 231b, 232, 233, 329), Japan (Japanese CPC Article 271 on the 2-month expiration of prosecutorial authority), and France (CPP Arts. 270, 320, 379-2).

Key Provisions

The 4-Tier Reform Model:

  • Type 1 (공소장 부본 미송달): The indictment was never delivered. Proposed amendment to CPC Articles 266 & 328: the court issues a Dismissal of Prosecution Decision (공소기각결정) if the indictment fails to be delivered within 2 months after an address correction order.
  • Type 2 (공소장 부본 송달 후 미출석): The indictment was delivered but the defendant never appeared. The 6-month deadline under Special Act Article 23(1) continues to apply.
  • Type 3 (1회 이상 출석 후 불출석): The defendant appeared at an initial hearing and received rights warnings, then fled. Proposed Special Act Article 23(2): trial in absentia may proceed after 2 non-consecutive absences without waiting 6 months.
  • Type 4 (변론종결 후 선고기일 불출석): The defendant attended until the close of pleadings, then fled on the sentencing date. Proposed Special Act Article 23(3): sentencing may proceed in absentia immediately upon the first unexcused absence.

Implementation

Courts adopt a rights-warning form (Parker Warnings) at the first hearing and use mail dispatch service (우체에 부치는 방법) for notifying defendants of subsequent absences.

Policy Issues

  • Police execution rates for detention warrants (구금영장) are below 20%.
  • A high rate of Supreme Court reversals of in absentia judgments due to minor defects in the public notice (공시송달) procedure.

Reform / Amendments

  1. Enactment of Special Expedited Act Article 23(2) & (3) (In Absentia Trial for Types 3 & 4).
  2. Amendment of Criminal Procedure Act Articles 266 & 328 (Dismissal of Prosecution Decision for Type 1).
  3. Amendment of Criminal Procedure Act Articles 365 & 458 (relaxing the requirement to 2 non-consecutive absences).
  4. Amendment of Criminal Procedure Act Article 277-2 (relaxing in absentia trial requirements for detained defendants).
  5. Amendment of Criminal Procedure Act Article 455 (Decision Rejecting a Formal Trial Request).
