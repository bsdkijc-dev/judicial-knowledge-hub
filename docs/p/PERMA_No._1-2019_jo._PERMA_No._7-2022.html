Core Definition

    • Supreme Court Regulation (PERMA) No. 1 of 2019** is the landmark legal instrument that formally established the mandatory framework for electronic case administration (*Administrasi Perkara Secara Elektronik*) and electronic litigation (*e-Litigation*) across the courts of Indonesia.
  • **Simple Definition:** The pioneering Supreme Court rulebook that officially introduced electronic filing, online summons, and digital trials into the Indonesian court system.
  • **Legal / Technical Definition:** A binding regulatory enactment issued by the Supreme Court that authorizes electronic case registration (*e-Filing*), electronic fee payment (*e-Payment*), electronic service of process (*e-Summons*), and electronic exchange of trial documents (Answers, Replies, Duplies, and Evidence) in civil, religious, and state administrative litigation.
  • **Plain-Language Explanation:** It acts as the legal foundation that brought Indonesian courts into the digital age, allowing lawyers and citizens to file lawsuits and conduct parts of their trials online without physically walking into a courthouse.

Why It Matters

  • **Revolutionized Access to Justice:** Eliminated geographic and logistical barriers by allowing legal practitioners to file lawsuits and access court dockets remotely 24/7.
  • **Drastically Reduced Trial Delays:** Shortened bureaucratic paperwork processing times and eliminated physical mail delivery delays for court summons and legal briefs.
  • **Laid the Legal Foundation for Modern Digital Courts:** Created the statutory authority that enabled subsequent expansions, including remote video hearings and full e-Litigation integration under PERMA No. 7 of 2022.
  • **Enhanced Administrative Transparency:** Linked e-Court filings directly with internal case tracking systems (SIPP), ensuring every document is digitally timestamped and traceable.

Key Principles

Principle of Simple, Fast, and Low-Cost Justice via Electronic Means (Asas Peradilan Sederhana, Cepat, dan Biaya Ringan Secara Elektronik)

  • **Meaning:** Electronic court administration must actively reduce litigation expenses, paper consumption, and trial durations while preserving full due process.
  • **Why It Matters:** Modernizes the constitutional mandate of fast and low-cost justice through digital efficiency.
  • **Legal Basis / Source:** Law No. 48/2009 Art. 2(4); PERMA No. 1/2019 Art. 2.
  • **Related Concepts:** E-justice, procedural economy, digital transformation.

Principle of Mutual Consent in e-Litigation (Asas Persetujuan Para Pihak dalam Persidangan Elektronik)

  • **Meaning:** Transitioning from traditional physical trials to electronic document exchange (*e-Litigation*) generally required the consent of the litigants, particularly for defendants upon their initial appearance. *(Note: Later expanded and streamlined under PERMA No. 7/2022).*
  • **Why It Matters:** Protects procedural fairness and ensures parties understand digital proceedings.
  • **Legal Basis / Source:** PERMA No. 1/2019 Arts. 19 & 20.
  • **Related Concepts:** E-Litigation consent, procedural due process.

Principle of Authenticity and Legal Equivalence of Electronic Documents (Asas Kesetaraan Hukum Dokumen Elektronik)

  • **Meaning:** Electronic documents, digital signatures, and electronic records submitted via the e-Court portal possess the same legal validity and evidentiary force as traditional paper documents.
  • **Why It Matters:** Establishes legal certainty for digital transactions and court filings under national electronic information laws.
  • **Legal Basis / Source:** PERMA No. 1/2019 Art. 5; Law No. 11/2008 (UU ITE).
  • **Related Concepts:** Electronic documents, digital signatures, legal equivalence.

Indonesia Section

  • **Promulgation & Historical Significance:** Enacted on August 8, 2019, PERMA No. 1/2019 replaced earlier experimental e-filing rules (PERMA No. 3/2018), expanding digital administration nationwide across General Courts, Religious Courts, and State Administrative Courts (*PTUN*).
  • **Core Services Introduced:**

1. *e-Filing:* Online submission of civil, religious, and administrative lawsuits. 2. *e-Payment:* Integrated electronic court fee calculation and payment via virtual accounts (*e-SKUM*). 3. *e-Summons:* Serving court notices and electronic summons to registered email addresses or portal accounts. 4. *e-Litigation:* Electronic exchange of written answers (*Jawaban*), replies (*Replik*), duplies (*Duplik*), and evidentiary document uploads.

  • **Nomenclature:** PERMA Nomor 1 Tahun 2019 — *Supreme Court Regulation No. 1 of 2019*; Administrasi Perkara di Pengadilan Secara Elektronik — *Electronic Court Case Administration*.

Important Decisions & Regulatory Milestones

  • Decision: Supreme Court Administrative Decree on Universal e-Court Adoption

 * *Issuing Body:* Mahkamah Agung RI (Office of the Chief Justice)
 * *Instrument Reference:* Chief Justice Administrative Directives enforcing PERMA 1/2019
 * *Legal Principle:* All registered advocates practicing across Indonesian district and religious courts are mandated to utilize the e-Court portal for case filings and document exchanges.
 * *Why It Matters:* Shifted electronic filing from an optional alternative to the mandatory default standard for legal practitioners.
 * *Official Source:* Mahkamah Agung Official Circulars.

Debates & Controversies

  • Debate: Mandatory Electronic Filing vs. Digital Access Disparities for Pro Se Litigants

 * *Position A (Mandatory Digital Efficiency):* Enforcing electronic filing eliminates physical paper backlogs and speeds up court administration.
 * *Position B (Access to Justice for Non-Advocates):* Citizens representing themselves (*pro se* / *pro litigant*) who lack internet access or technical literacy may struggle with digital portals unless courts provide physical helpdesks (*Posbakum*).
 * *Evidence:* Implementation challenges reported by rural litigants during initial e-Court rollouts.
 * *Current Status:* Resolved by making e-Court mandatory for registered advocates while maintaining physical court registry helpdesks and assisted e-filing kiosks for ordinary citizens.

Rule-of-Law Connection

  • *Access to Justice:* Reduces the time and travel costs required to file lawsuits and attend procedural hearings.
  • *Legal Certainty:* Digital timestamps eliminate disputes over whether legal documents and briefs were filed before statutory deadlines.
  • *Transparency:* Electronic dockets ensure complete traceability of all procedural submissions.

AI & Future of Law

  • *AI-Assisted Document Intake:* Integrating PERMA 1/2019 e-Court portals with AI document scrapers to check incoming electronic petitions for missing statutory attachments automatically.
  • *Smart Hearing Integration:* Linking e-Court document timelines with automated scheduling algorithms in SIPP.

Common Misconceptions

  • *Myth:* PERMA No. 1/2019 completely abolished physical courtrooms and oral trials in Indonesia.

 * *Reality:* PERMA No. 1/2019 introduced electronic document exchange (*e-Litigation*), but oral witness examinations, expert testimonies, and final verdict pronouncements still frequently involve physical or hybrid courtroom sessions.
 * *Source:* PERMA No. 1/2019; PERMA No. 7/2022.

  • *Myth:* Ordinary citizens without a lawyer are barred from using the e-Court system.

 * *Reality:* While lawyers are strictly mandated to use e-Court, non-advocate individual litigants (*pro se*) are fully permitted to register and file electronically with assistance from court staff.
 * *Source:* PERMA No. 1/2019 operational guidelines.

Practical Example

  • *Scenario:* A Jakarta-based advocate wishes to file a civil breach of contract lawsuit against a company located in Semarang.
  • *Question:* How does PERMA No. 1/2019 govern the filing process?
  • *Applicable Principle:* Electronic Filing & Jurisdictional Access (PERMA No. 1/2019 Arts. 4–8).
  • *Analysis:*

 1. The advocate logs into the official e-Court portal using verified credentials.
 2. Uploads the PDF petition, pays court fees electronically via virtual account (*e-SKUM*), and receives an instant digital filing receipt.
 3. The district court in Semarang receives the electronic docket via SIPP, registers the case, and issues electronic summons (*e-Summons*) to both parties.

  • *Limitation:* The plaintiff's lawyer must still ensure the court possesses proper territorial jurisdiction over the defendant under civil procedural rules (HIR/RBg).

Key Terms

Term Definition Related Term
e-Filing The electronic submission of lawsuits, petitions, and legal documents through the official Supreme Court e-Court portal. PERMA 1/2019, Civil Procedure
e-Payment The electronic calculation and settlement of court fees through integrated banking virtual accounts (*e-SKUM*). Court Costs, SIPP Integration
e-Litigation The electronic exchange of written trial documents (Answers, Replies, Duplies) and evidence uploads between parties and judges online. PERMA 7/2022, Digital Trial
e-Summons (Panggilan Elektronik) Electronic service of process delivering court notices and hearing schedules to registered digital accounts or emails. Service of Process, Due Process
