Overview

Supreme Court Decision No. 06 K/PID.HAM.AD HOC/2005 establishes a crucial legal standard regarding the application of mandatory minimum criminal penalties for gross violations of Human Rights (HAM) and the principles of superior/command responsibility. The Supreme Court affirmed that the Ad Hoc High Human Rights Court committed an error in law and exceeded its authority by sentencing the defendant below the statutory minimum penalty prescribed by law (imposing a 5-year prison sentence against a statutory minimum threshold of 10 years). Imposing sentences below statutory minimums in a determinate sentencing system creates an adverse precedent that undermines legal certainty and the sense of justice.

Case Background

The Defendant, [Defendant] (Former Deputy Commander of the Pro-Integration Fighters / PPI and Commander of the Aitarak Group), was charged under the doctrine of superior responsibility pursuant to Article 42 paragraphs (2)(a) and (b) jo. Article 7(b), Article 9(a) and (h), and Article 37 of Law No. 26 of 2000 on the Human Rights Court. The Defendant was accused of failing to exercise effective control over subordinates who carried out a systematic attack against the civilian population in Dili, East Timor, on April 17, 1999, including the attack on the residence of Manuel Viegas Carrascalao, which resulted in the deaths of 12 civilians and injuries to several others. The Ad Hoc Human Rights Court at the Central Jakarta District Court sentenced the Defendant to 10 years of imprisonment. The Ad Hoc High Human Rights Court of Jakarta modified the ruling by reducing the sentence to 5 years of imprisonment. Dissatisfied with this decision, both the Public Prosecutor and the Defendant filed cassation petitions.

Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

  • Ratio Decidendi: The Ad Hoc High Human Rights Court misapplied the law and exceeded its competence by imposing a lighter sentence below the statutory minimum threshold (namely a 5-year prison term, whereas the minimum threat under Article 37 jo. Article 42(2) jo. Article 9 of Law No. 26 of 2000 is 10 years of imprisonment). Sentencing below the statutory minimum within a determinate sentencing framework establishes a detrimental precedent that obstructs the fulfillment of sentencing objectives—namely utility, justice, and legal certainty.
  • Ruling: Declaring the cassation petition filed by Petitioner for Cassation [Defendant] inadmissible; Granting the cassation petition filed by the Ad Hoc Public Prosecutor at the Attorney General's Office of the RI; Annulling the Jakarta Ad Hoc High Human Rights Court Decision No. 02/PID.HAM/AD.HOC/2004/PT.DKI; and Independently Adjudicating: Declaring Defendant [Defendant] legally and convincingly proven guilty of committing "GROSS HUMAN RIGHTS VIOLATIONS IN THE FORM OF CRIMES AGAINST HUMANITY," and sentencing the Defendant to 10 (ten) years of imprisonment.
