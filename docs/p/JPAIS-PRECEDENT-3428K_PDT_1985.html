Supreme Court Decision Reg. No. 3428 K/Pdt/1985 (dated February 26, 1990 / deliberation on February 5, 1990) is a fundamental civil evidentiary law jurisprudence precedent establishing vital rules concerning the legal standing and evidentiary value of out-of-court written statements compared to sworn witness testimony given in open court pursuant to Article 1895 of the Civil Code / Articles 171–172 of the HIR.

Executive Summary

The Supreme Court established a primary legal principle:

  • Out-of-Court Statements Do Not Constitute Courtroom Witness Testimony: A written statement or acknowledgment executed outside of a court hearing (such as a statement made before the Police / Kosek Polri) cannot be equated with sworn witness testimony given in open court, and therefore possesses no binding evidentiary value (bindende bewijskracht) to prove kinship affiliation (sekaum) and rights over high ancestral property (harta pusaka tinggi).

Where the Padang High Court granted the Plaintiff's lawsuit and annulled the Koto Baru District Court decision based solely on exhibits P.3 through P.5 (police statements) treated as binding proof, the Supreme Court held that the High Court misapplied the law of evidence. Witness testimonies in open court failed to establish that the Plaintiff ([Party A] gelar Datuk Rajo Kuaso) and Defendant I ([Party B]) belonged to the same lineage, shared ancestral property, and shared burial grounds under Minangkabau customary law.

The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition of the Petitioners for Cassation ([Party B], et al.), annulled Padang High Court Decision No. 30/G/B/K/T/1985/PT.PDG dated March 14, 1985, and independently adjudicated (mengadili sendiri) by affirming Koto Baru District Court Decision No. 33/G/B/K/T/1983/PN.Kbr dated November 5, 1984, which dismissed the Plaintiff's lawsuit entirely.

Legal / Procedural Issues

1. Evidentiary Weight of Out-of-Court Unsworn Statements vs. Courtroom Witness Testimony (Articles 1867, 1895 Civil Code / Articles 164, 171–172 HIR)

Can a written statement or acknowledgment executed by parties outside of court (such as at a police station / Kosek Polri) be classified and evaluated by a court as binding witness testimony (bindende bewijskracht)?

Supreme Court Rule: Cannot Be Equated / Non-Binding.

Written statements executed outside of court possess value merely as private documents (onderhands geschrift). Such documents cannot be equated with witness testimony (getuigenbewijs) mandated by civil procedural law to be delivered under oath during open court proceedings. An appellate court that relies upon out-of-court statements as primary witness evidence commits an error in applying the law of evidence (foutieve toepassing van het bewijsrecht).

2. Burden of Proof for Kinship Affiliation and High Ancestral Property Under Minangkabau Customary Law (Article 163 HIR / Article 1865 Civil Code)

A plaintiff asserting a shared lineage, common ancestral property, and shared burial grounds with a defendant bears the burden of proving blood ties or maternal lineages (ranji) lawfully through sworn witnesses in open court proceedings. Where trial witnesses fail to establish such blood ties and genealogical records do not substantiate shared lineage, lawsuits seeking to invalidate grants or sales of high ancestral property must be dismissed.

Question of Law vs Question of Fact

Question of Law (Judex Juris / Supreme Court)

Did the Judex Facti Padang High Court (Decision No. 30/G/B/K/T/1985/PT.PDG) err in applying civil evidentiary law (foutieve toepassing van het bewijsrecht) by evaluating Exhibits P.3 through P.5 (police station statements) as binding witness testimony to overturn the Koto Baru District Court decision?

Supreme Court Legal Answer: Yes. Padang High Court misapplied the law. Out-of-court written statements cannot be equated with sworn testimony in open court. The Supreme Court granted [Party B] et al.’s cassation petition, annulled Padang High Court's decision, and independently adjudicated by affirming Koto Baru District Court's ruling dismissing the Plaintiff's lawsuit entirely.

Verification of claims regarding shared lineage between [Party A] gelar Datuk Rajo Kuaso and [Party B] in Bawah Duku, Koto Baru, Kubung, Solok; inspection of disputed properties comprising fish pond land Sub A measuring 235 m2 and orchard land Sub B measuring 1,070 m2 in Guguk Dama, Koto Baru; examination of Grant Deed No. 24/PPAT/1983 and Sale Deed No. 73/PPAT/1982; preparation of Exhibits P.2 and P.4 at the Kubung Police Sector; and examination of 10 Plaintiff witnesses and 7 Defendant witnesses during Koto Baru District Court hearings.

Jurisprudence and Related Knowledge

Statutory Regulations

  • HIR (Herziene Inlandsch Reglement): Article 164 (Civil Modes of Proof), Articles 171, 172 (Requirements for Sworn Witness Testimony in Open Court).
  • Civil Code (KUHPerdata / BW): Articles 1867, 1895 (Documentary and Witness Evidence).
  • Minangkabau Customary Law: Provisions concerning Head of Clan Managers (Mamak Kepala Waris), Allotted Customary Land (Ganggam Bauntuk), and Clan High Ancestral Property Rights.
  • Law No. 14 of 1970 jo. Law No. 1 of 1950 jo. Law No. 14 of 1985 on the Supreme Court: Cassation and Independent Adjudication Authority.

Established Supreme Court Jurisprudence

  • Doctrine of Courtroom Testimony Validity: Only oral statements delivered by witnesses under oath during open court proceedings satisfy the legal qualification of witness evidence.
  • Established Precedent on Out-of-Court Statements: Supreme Court Decision Reg. No. 3428 K/Pdt/1985 serves as a landmark decision establishing that private out-of-court statements cannot replace courtroom witness testimony and possess no binding evidentiary force.

Annulled High Court Reasoning

  • Padang High Court considerations holding that Plaintiff and Defendant I shared lineage based on police station statements were ANNULLED ENTIRELY BY THE SUPREME COURT.

Uncertainties and Gaps

Legal Certainty in Civil Evidence Regarding Customary Claims via Private Instruments: This landmark ruling establishes strict civil procedural certainty in Indonesia: litigants disputing ancestral property or customary rights cannot rely on statements executed before non-judicial authorities (such as police stations or village officials) to establish kinship status or invalidate property transactions. Procedural law demands direct proof via sworn witnesses in court to ensure material truth.

Frequently Asked Questions (GEO)

Do written statements executed outside of court (such as at a police station) possess binding evidentiary force equivalent to courtroom witnesses pursuant to Supreme Court Jurisprudence?
No. Pursuant to Supreme Court Jurisprudence Reg. No. 3428 K/Pdt/1985:

  • Written statements executed outside of court cannot be equated with witness testimony delivered under oath in open court.
  • Such statements represent merely private out-of-court documents lacking binding evidentiary force (bindende bewijskracht) to prove land rights or customary kinship affiliation.
  • An appellate court granting a lawsuit based solely on out-of-court statements commits an error in applying evidentiary law, resulting in Supreme Court annulment.
