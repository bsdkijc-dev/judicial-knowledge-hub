Editorial: When the figures just don’t figure is a legal editorial authored by Jeremy Phillips and published in the Journal of Intellectual Property Law & Practice (Vol. 1, No. 4) by Oxford University Press in 2006 Editorial Publication [1–9].

Summary

This editorial evaluates statistical data compiled by Jean Pire (Gevers & Partners, Belgium) regarding the failure rates of legal practitioners in complying with procedural formalities for Community Trade Marks at OHIM OHIM Statistics [1–9]. The data demonstrates that practitioners from Spain, Germany, and the United Kingdom account for the vast majority of these procedural defaults Procedural Imbalance [1–9].

Legal Basis

  • OHIM Regulations: The administrative procedural framework of the Office for Harmonisation in the Internal Market governing Community Trade Mark applications OHIM Rules [1–9].
  • Principle of Formal Protection: Arbitrary procedural rules within OHIM regulations designed to protect innocent parties Formal Principles [1–9].

Background

In European intellectual property practice, formality failures are often assumed to be random administrative errors IP Formalities [1–9]. However, empirical records from the Gevers database reveal a heavy geographic concentration of defaults [1–9].

Objectives

  1. Detail the statistical disparities in OHIM formality failure rates [1–9].
  2. Refute conventional explanations rooted in outdated 20th-century national stereotypes [1–9].
  3. Formulate working hypotheses regarding practitioner strategies and multinational corporate client behavior [1–9].
  4. Promote further empirical legal research [1–9].

Key Provisions

  • Distribution of Formality Defaults: Spain accounts for 31.6%, Germany for 30.2%, and the United Kingdom for 10.8% of total formality failures at OHIM [1–9].
  • Greek Failure Rate: Greek practitioners recorded the lowest default rate at 0.12% [1–9].
  • Practitioner Qualifications: Practitioners in Spain, Germany, and the UK possess demonstrated high capability, rigorous training, and extensive experience handling large case volumes [1–9].

Implementation

  • Official Publication: Published in JIPLP Vol. 1 No. 4, p. 229, DOI: 10.1093/jiplp/jpl014 [1–9].
  • Data Source: Derived from decisions of the Board of Appeal, Opposition Division, and Cancellation Division at OHIM maintained in the Gevers & Partners database [1–9].

Policy Issues

  • The Non-Compliance Enigma: Formality failures cannot be attributed to lack of qualification or cultural stereotypes [1–9].
  • Multinational Client Strategy: Well-funded global corporations may intentionally test procedural points as part of a broader portfolio management strategy [1–9].
