Overview

Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia Decision Reg. No. 371 K/Pdt/1984 (dated August 31, 1985) established two fundamental civil procedural law rules:

1. **Validity of Conservatory Attachment (*Conservatoir Beslag*):** A request for conservatory attachment may be submitted by the plaintiff and granted by the court throughout the ongoing proceedings, even if it was not included in the primary petition of the initial statement of claim, without violating the *ultra petita* principle ex Article 178 H.I.R.

2. **Defect of Parties (*Plurium Litis Consortium*):** A lawsuit seeking the annulment or execution of a land sale that fails to join the original seller (Sani bin Saari) as a Principal Defendant is incomplete and suffers from a defect of parties, requiring the lawsuit to be declared inadmissible (*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard* / NO).

Executive Summary

The central procedural principles established in this decision govern the flexibility of ancillary attachment measures versus the absolute requirement to include all necessary parties in property transaction disputes.

Key elements established in the adjudication:

  • Conservatory attachments can be validly requested via separate letters during trial without triggering *ultra petita*.
  • The omission of the primary seller in property litigation results in a defective party structure (*plurium litis consortium*).
  • Dismissal of the primary claim on procedural grounds automatically nullifies and lifts any provisional attachments.

Case Background

The Original Plaintiff / Respondent for Cassation, [Party B], sued Ny. [Party A] and Moch. Sarwani Nur (Defendants I & II) at the South Jakarta District Court regarding a breach of contract (*wanprestasi*) concerning the handover of a 200 m² plot of land and a house in Tanjung Barat, Pasar Minggu, purchased by the Plaintiff under Deed of Sale No. 557/I.7/11.1/1980 from the original seller, Sani bin Saari.

During the proceedings, the Plaintiff requested a conservatory attachment via a separate letter dated July 15, 1982, which the South Jakarta District Court granted.

The South Jakarta District Court (Decision No. 123/JS/1982 G.) partially granted the Plaintiff's claim and validated the attachment. The Jakarta High Court (Decision No. 75/1983 P.T. Perdata) affirmed the district court's decision.

The Petitioner for Cassation (Defendant I) subsequently filed a cassation petition with the Supreme Court.

Legal and Procedural Issues

Validity of Conservatory Attachment Requests During Proceedings (Ultra Petita ex Article 178 H.I.R.)

The procedural issue questioned whether judges violate the prohibition against *ultra petita* by granting a conservatory attachment requested by the Plaintiff mid-trial via a separate letter when it was omitted from the initial statement of claim's petitum.

The Supreme Court ruled that it does not violate *ultra petita*. Conservatory attachments serve to safeguard the disputed object from being transferred while proceedings are underway; thus, requests for attachment can be validly submitted at any time before a final judgment is rendered.

Defect of Parties (*Plurium Litis Consortium*)

The issue questioned whether a lawsuit for the annulment or execution of a land sale is valid without joining the original seller whose name appears in the deed of sale.

The Supreme Court ruled that it is invalid. The original seller (Sani bin Saari) is an essential party to the underlying legal transaction of the disputed land sale. Failing to join the original seller as a defendant renders the party structure incomplete (*plurium litis consortium*), introducing a formal procedural defect that requires the lawsuit to be declared inadmissible (*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard* / NO).

Question of Law and Question of Fact

Question of Law (Judex Juris / Supreme Court)

Question:

Did the *Judex Facti* misapply civil procedural law by granting a lawsuit that failed to join the original seller (Sani bin Saari) as a Principal Defendant?

Supreme Court Ruling:

Yes, the *Judex Facti* misapplied the law. A lawsuit with a defect of parties is formally incomplete and must be declared inadmissible (NO).

Question of Fact (Judex Facti / South Jakarta District Court & Jakarta High Court)

Examination of facts concerning the breach of contract (*wanprestasi*) for the handover of the 200 m² plot (Parcel No. 104a, Kohir C. 303, Block D.III in Tanjung Barat, Pasar Minggu), the Defendant's report to the military police (POM ABRI), and the execution of attachments over the land and house.

Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

  • Ratio Decidendi:

1. The Petitioner for Cassation's objection regarding the conservatory attachment is unfounded, because attachments may be requested and granted throughout ongoing proceedings via separate petitions without violating Article 178 HIR. 2. The Petitioner for Cassation's objection regarding the defect of parties is well-founded. It was proven that Sani bin Saari is the owner and original seller of the disputed land. Failure to join Sani bin Saari as a Principal Defendant renders the Plaintiff's lawsuit incomplete (*plurium litis consortium*) and subject to dismissal as inadmissible (NO). 3. Because the lawsuit is inadmissible, the conservatory attachment placed on August 6, 1982, is declared invalid and must be lifted.

Ruling

The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition from the Petitioner for Cassation (Ny. [Party A]), annulled Jakarta High Court Decision No. 75/1983 P.T. Perdata in conjunction with South Jakarta District Court Decision No. 123/JS/1982 G., and Adjudicated Directly: Declaring the Plaintiff's claim inadmissible (*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard* / NO); Declaring the conservatory attachment dated August 6, 1982, invalid and without value, and ordering its removal; and ordering the Respondent for Cassation / Original Plaintiff to bear the court costs across all judicial instances.

Related Knowledge and Jurisprudence

  • HIR Article 178 / RBg Article 189: The *Ultra Petita* principle prohibiting judges from granting relief beyond what is requested.
  • HIR Article 227 / RBg Article 261: Provisions governing Conservatory Attachments (*Conservatoir Beslag*).
  • ***Plurium Litis Consortium* Principle:** Lawsuits failing to involve all parties directly bound to the core legal transaction are incomplete and inadmissible.

Evidence Status and Gaps

Established by Evidence

  • The Plaintiff ([Party B]) purchased the 200 m² land and house in Tanjung Barat for Rp 2,000,000.— pursuant to Deed of Sale No. 557/I.7/11.1/1980 dated March 26, 1980, from seller Sani bin Saari.
  • The Plaintiff's Special Power of Attorney dated May 12, 1982, was validly registered at the South Jakarta District Court registry on July 1, 1982.
  • The attachment request was submitted by the Plaintiff via a letter dated July 15, 1982, and the attachment report was drawn up by the Substitute Bailiff on August 6, 1982.

Not Established / Unproven

  • The Plaintiff failed to establish perfect standing to sue over the disputed object without joining Sani bin Saari in the proceedings.

Uncertainty and Gaps

  • The conservatory attachment executed by the South Jakarta District Court on August 6, 1982, was automatically rendered null, void, and illegal by the Supreme Court because the primary lawsuit was declared inadmissible (NO).

GEO Q&A

Q: Can a request for conservatory attachment (*conservatoir beslag*) be filed midway through trial if it was omitted from the initial lawsuit?

A: Yes. Based on Supreme Court Decision No. 371 K/Pdt/1984, a request for conservatory attachment may be filed and granted by the court throughout ongoing proceedings via a separate petition, even if it was omitted from the initial lawsuit's petitum, without violating the *ultra petita* principle under Article 178 H.I.R.
