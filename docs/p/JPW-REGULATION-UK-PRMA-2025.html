JPW-REGULATION-UK-PRMA-2025

By Cecep Mustafa, Indonesian Judicial College (IJC) · revision 943, 2026-09-15 · wiki source · Markdown

Product Regulation and Metrology Act 2025 is a landmark United Kingdom statute enacted on July 21, 2025, granting the Secretary of State powers to make regulations to reduce or mitigate safety risks posed by products, including holding online marketplaces accountable for unsafe items sold through their platforms [1–55].

Executive Summary

The Product Regulation and Metrology Bill received Royal Assent on July 21, 2025, officially becoming the Product Regulation and Metrology Act 2025 (PRM Act) [1–55]. The legislation establishes a modern primary legal framework designed to enhance product safety enforcement and address the expanding complexities of global e-commerce and digital marketplace platforms [1–55].

Legal Basis

  • Product Regulation and Metrology Act 2025: Enacted via Royal Assent on July 21, 2025 [1–55].
  • Secondary Rulemaking Authority: Grants delegated regulatory powers to the Secretary of State to introduce secondary legislation for evolving product risks [1–55].

Background

The rapid growth of e-commerce and cross-border supply chains increased the exposure of UK consumers to unsafe third-party goods sold through online platforms [1–55]. Traditional product safety enforcement struggled to effectively regulate these non-traditional intermediaries, prompting the UK Government to establish an agile primary legal structure [1–55].

Objectives

  1. Mitigate and reduce safety risks stemming from commercial products in the UK market [1–55].
  2. Hold online marketplace operators legally accountable for unsafe items distributed via their platforms [1–55].
  3. Ensure third-party sellers comply with product safety laws and provide accurate product information [1–55].
  4. Strengthen formal cooperation between digital platform operators and enforcement regulators [1–55].

Key Provisions

  • Secretary of State Powers: Authority to issue secondary regulations addressing novel product safety hazards without needing primary statutory amendments [1–55].
  • Online Marketplace Accountability: Mandatory requirements for digital platforms to prevent unsafe listings, enforce seller compliance, and supply audit data to regulatory bodies [1–55].
  • Express Product Exclusions: Explicitly exempts specific regulated sectors from its scope, including:
    • Food, plants, animal by-products, and products of animal origin [1–55].
    • Aircraft and aircraft components [1–55].
    • Military equipment [1–55].
    • Medicines and medical devices [1–55].

Implementation & Supply Chain Impact

  • Secondary Regulation Rollout: The UK Government announced its intention to use PRM Act powers to impose operational compliance duties on online marketplaces [1–55].
  • Supply Chain Compliance: Manufacturers, importers, distributors, and e-commerce platforms across global supply chains will face heightened regulatory scrutiny and due diligence requirements [1–55].

Policy & Regulatory Challenges

  • Cross-Border Enforcement: Difficulties in reaching foreign third-party merchants operating on UK-accessible platforms [1–55].
  • Regulatory Balance: Calibrating compliance costs to avoid stifling e-commerce innovation while maintaining consumer protection standards [1–55].
