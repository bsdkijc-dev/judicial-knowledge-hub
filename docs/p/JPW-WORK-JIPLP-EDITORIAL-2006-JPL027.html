Editorial: Obviousness and IP: time to reconsider is a legal editorial authored by Jeremy Phillips and published in the Journal of Intellectual Property Law & Practice (Vol. 1, No. 5) by Oxford University Press on March 7, 2006 Editorial Publication [1–9].

Summary

This editorial discusses the consultation paper launched by the United Kingdom Patent Office on February 6, 2006, regarding the inventive step requirement in patent law UK Patent Office Consultation [1–9]. The author critically assesses the subjective and costly nature of the obviousness standard and contrasts it with trade mark and copyright regimes Inventive Step Critique [1–9].

Legal Basis

  • Consultation on the Inventive Step Requirement: The UK Patent Office consultation paper examining patent eligibility criteria and trivial patents Consultation Document [1–9].
  • Burden of Patent Proof: Novelty examination is objective, whereas obviousness testing is subjective, speculative, and time-consuming Patent Criteria [1–9].

Background

On February 6, 2006, the UK Patent Office issued a discussion paper titled Consultation on the Inventive Step Requirement in United Kingdom Patent Law and Practice with a public response deadline of May 31, 2006 Consultation Background [1–9].

Objectives

  1. Investigate whether too many trivial patents are granted under the present patent system Trivial Patents [1–9].
  2. Test whether innovation and competitiveness are better served by easy, low-barrier patent systems Competitiveness [1–9].
  3. Question the continued necessity and relevance of the inventive step requirement in international patent law Inventive Step Relevance [1–9].

Key Provisions

  • Two Consultation Questions: Assessing the grant rate of trivial patents and the impact of low-threshold patents on market competition Consultation Questions [1–9].
  • Comparison with Other IP Rights: The obviousness doctrine does not exist in trade mark law (which relies on distinctiveness) or copyright law IP Comparison [1–9].
  • Characteristics of Obviousness: The obviousness test is speculative (conjecture), expensive, subjective, and uncertain when compared to the objective novelty test Characteristics of Obviousness [1–9].

Implementation

  • Official Publication: Published via Advance Access on March 7, 2006 (Vol. 1 No. 5, p. 297, DOI: 10.1093/jiplp/jpl027) Advance Access [1–9].
  • Response Deadline: Public submissions for the consultation closed on May 31, 2006 Response Deadline [1–9].

Policy Issues

  • Subjectivity in Examination: Determining obviousness in opposition proceedings (such as at the EPO) is costly and time-consuming without offering legal certainty EPO Subjectivity [1–9].
  • Local Reform Barriers: Patent law reform initiated by a single jurisdiction at the periphery is unlikely to affect global standards without central engagement from WIPO or the EPO Global Reform Barriers [1–9].
