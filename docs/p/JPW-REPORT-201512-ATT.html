JPW-REPORT-201512-ATT

By Cecep Mustafa, Indonesian Judicial College (IJC) · revision 1518, 2026-09-18 · wiki source · Markdown

Summary

This document is an official legal citation standard draft published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of the Supreme Court of Korea as supplementary material to JPRI Research Report 2015-12. The manual establishes unified, systematic rules for citing domestic court decisions, statutes, books, academic journals, public reports, digital documents, foreign legislation and case law across seven jurisdictions, and international law instruments. The standard is designed to prevent plagiarism, protect copyright, enforce research ethics, and ensure the reliability and traceability of South Korean legal research.

Legal Basis

The operational framework and foundational authority analyzed in this report include:

  • Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) Research Authority: Published as the official attachment to JPRI Research Series 2015-12 ("Research on Legal Citation Methods").
  • Research Ethics & Copyright Principles: Grounded in principles of good faith citation, copyright protection, and the strict distinction between original analysis and referenced material.

Background

With the rapid growth of legal research and the digitalization of legal literature, the absence of a uniform citation standard across academics, legal practitioners, and the judiciary created confusion, inconsistent citations, and potential research ethics risks such as plagiarism and self-plagiarism. A national standard proposal was required to unify legal citation practices in South Korea.

Objectives

1. Prevent plagiarism and uphold research ethics in South Korean legal scholarship. 2. Ensure easy verification and source traceability for readers and researchers. 3. Establish unified citation conventions for domestic court decisions, statutes, digital literature, foreign laws, and international legal instruments.

Scope

  • General Citation Rules: Footnote system (`각주방식`), direct and indirect quotations, re-citation rules (`재인용`), and short-form citations.
  • Domestic Legal Materials: Decisions of the Supreme Court, Constitutional Court, lower courts, quasi-judicial bodies, active statutes, former statutes (`구법`), and North Korean legal materials.
  • Digital Literature & New Media: Webpages, online news articles, blogs, and electronic databases.
  • Foreign & International Law: Legal systems across seven jurisdictions (United States, United Kingdom, Germany, France, European Union, Japan, and China), international treaties, ICJ/ICTY/ICTR precedents, UN documents, and WTO reports.

Key Provisions

  • South Korean Court Judgments: Formatted as `[Court Name] [Date YYYY. M. D.] [선고] [Case Number] [Judgment Type]` (e.g., `대법원 1996. 4. 26. 선고 96다1078 판결`).
  • Statutes and Regulations: Formatted as `[Statute Name] [Article No.] [Paragraph No.] [Item No.]`.
  • Digital Literature: Mandatory inclusion of the complete URL alongside the last confirmation date in parentheses, e.g., `(YYYY. M. D. 확인)`.
  • Secondary Sources: Mandates direct verification of primary sources whenever possible; secondary citations require explicit attribution to both primary and secondary works using the tag `재인용`.

Implementation

This draft standard serves as an official writing guideline for JPRI research reports, judicial journals, and official legal filings within the Supreme Court of Korea.

Policy Issues

  • Inconsistency in citation formatting across legal journals, law schools, and judicial institutions in South Korea.
  • Absence of standardized citation conventions for digital materials, electronic resources, and online official publications.

Reforms

1. Promulgation of the National Standard Draft for Legal Citation Methods (`법률문헌 인용방법 표준안`). 2. Standardization of rules governing digital and new media legal citations. 3. Alignment of foreign legal citations with the official standards of their respective jurisdictions (e.g., Bluebook, neutral citations, BVerfGE/BGHZ, J.O., OJ).

Key Policy Improvements

1. Official Adoption by Judiciary and Law Schools: Formal integration of the JPRI draft standard into the style guides of law school journals and judicial publications nationwide. 2. AI-Based Automated Citation Validation Tool: Developing an automated verification module within the judicial research portal to check footnote formats, URL access dates, and secondary citation tags (`재인용`). 3. Periodic Updates for Digital and Foreign Standards: Establishing annual updates to maintain standard abbreviations, e-journal citations, and international neutral citation formats.
