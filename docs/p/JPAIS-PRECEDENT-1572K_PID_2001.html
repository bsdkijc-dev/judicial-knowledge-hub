By Cecep Mustafa, Indonesian Judicial College (IJC) · revision 1758, 2026-09-23 · wiki source · Markdown

Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia Decision No. 1572 K/Pid/2001 (decided 31 July 2002) is a Indonesian criminal law precedent concerning psychotropic substance possession offences under Law No. 5 of 1997 on Psychotropics. The Supreme Court reversed an acquittal handed down by the Denpasar District Court and convicted the defendant, a Dutch national, of unlawfully storing methamphetamine ("sabu-sabu") found in a bathroom cabinet within her private bedroom suite. The decision is a leading authority on three points of Indonesian criminal procedure and substantive narcotics law: (1) the admissibility of cassation appeals against a "not-genuine acquittal" (niet-zuivere vrijspraak); (2) the doctrine that a household's principal occupant — the "master of the house" — is presumed to know of, and be criminally responsible for, contraband found in areas of the home under her exclusive daily control; and (3) the mandatory judicial duty to weigh circumstantial evidence (alat bukti petunjuk) under Article 188 of the Indonesian Code of Criminal Procedure (KUHAP).

Background

Parties

The defendant, [Defendant], was a Dutch national (born [details removed]), a homemaker residing at [address removed], Kerobokan, Kuta, Badung, Bali, Indonesia. She was prosecuted by the Public Prosecutor of the Denpasar District Attorney's Office (Kejaksaan Negeri Denpasar).

Facts

On Wednesday, 30 August 2000, at approximately 17:00 local time, police searched the defendant's residence in Kerobokan, Kuta. In a wall cabinet inside the bathroom attached to the defendant's bedroom — a room said to be used only by the defendant and her young child — officers found:

  • two plastic bags containing crystalline methamphetamine, with a combined weight of 5.6 grams;
  • one box of plastic clip (resealable) bags; and
  • additional plastic and paper packaging materials.

Forensic testing by the Indonesian National Police's Criminalistics Laboratory (Laboratorium Forensik Polri, Denpasar branch), recorded in Official Report No. LAB: 159/KNF/2000 dated 29 September 2000, confirmed the crystalline substance to be methamphetamine, a Category II psychotropic substance under Law No. 5 of 1997.

The defendant denied knowledge of the drugs and asserted that they belonged to her husband, [Husband], who she said returned to the home only occasionally. She was charged, in the primary indictment, with unlawfully storing psychotropics under Article 62 of Law No. 5 of 1997, and, in the alternative (subsidiary) indictment, with failing to report psychotropic substance abuse under Article 65 of the same law.

Proceedings below

By Decision No. 534/Pid.B/2000/PN.Dps of 30 March 2001, the Denpasar District Court (Pengadilan Negeri Denpasar) acquitted the defendant of both the primary and subsidiary charges (a "vrijspraak"), accepting her denial of knowledge. The Public Prosecutor of Denpasar filed an application for cassation with the Supreme Court, arguing that the trial court had misapplied the rules of evidence.

Issues

  1. Admissibility of cassation against an acquittal. Under Article 244 of KUHAP, cassation is generally not available against an acquittal. Was the Denpasar District Court's acquittal, however, a "not-genuine acquittal" (niet-zuivere vrijspraak) reviewable by the Supreme Court, on the ground that it rested on a misapplication of the law of evidence (Article 185(6) KUHAP) and a failure to set out the court's factual and evidentiary reasoning as required by Article 197(1)(d) KUHAP?
  2. Criminal responsibility of a household occupant ("master of the house") for contraband in private space. Could the defendant, as the principal occupant of the household, avoid criminal liability for methamphetamine found in a cabinet of her private ensuite bathroom merely by asserting that she did not know of it, or by attributing it to a husband who visited infrequently?
  3. Duty to evaluate circumstantial evidence. Did the trial court err in failing to weigh, as circumstantial evidence (alat bukti petunjuk) under Article 188 KUHAP, the layout of the bathroom, the presence of clip-seal packaging, and the testimony of the witnesses, in determining whether the element of "unlawfully storing psychotropics" under Article 62 of Law No. 5 of 1997 had been proven?

Holding

The Supreme Court granted the Public Prosecutor's application for cassation, set aside the Denpasar District Court's decision, and, exercising its power to try the matter itself (mengadili sendiri), convicted the defendant.

On the admissibility of cassation

The Court held that the Denpasar District Court's acquittal was a "not-genuine acquittal" because it was the product of an erroneous application of the law of evidence under Article 185(6) KUHAP, compounded by the trial court's failure to set out, concisely, its findings of fact and its assessment of the evidence as required by Article 197(1)(d) KUHAP. Such a defect takes the decision outside the ordinary bar on cassation against acquittals in Article 244 KUHAP, and the Supreme Court accordingly had full authority to examine, review, and set aside the decision.

On the "master of the house" doctrine

The Court held that the bathroom in which the drugs were found was reached only through the defendant's own bedroom and was used, in the ordinary course, only by the defendant and her young child. As the household's principal occupant, who made daily, exclusive use of that private space, the defendant was, as a matter of law, presumed to know of — and to be responsible for — items kept in the bathroom cabinet, including the 5.6 grams of methamphetamine. A bare denial of knowledge, or an unsubstantiated claim that the drugs belonged to a husband who visited only occasionally, did not displace this presumption of knowledge and possession.

On circumstantial evidence

The Court held that a court may not disregard the cumulative and mutually consistent character of facts proven at trial — including the layout and limited access to the bathroom, the discovery of resealable packaging materials, and the testimony of witnesses (including police officers I Putu Suta, M. Rifai, and I Wayan Darmadi, and household staff Ni Komang Mersini and Aminadap Tualaka) — which together constituted valid circumstantial evidence (alat bukti petunjuk) under Article 188 KUHAP sufficient to prove the element of "unlawfully storing psychotropics" under Article 62 of Law No. 5 of 1997.

The Court also had regard to the defendant's prior record, noting that she had previously been convicted in a narcotics/psychotropics matter involving approximately 20,781 ecstasy tablets.

Disposition (Amar Putusan)

The Supreme Court:

  1. granted the cassation application of the Public Prosecutor of the Denpasar District Attorney's Office;
  2. set aside Denpasar District Court Decision No. 534/Pid.B/2000/PN.Dps of 30 March 2001;
  3. trying the case itself, declared the defendant, [Defendant], lawfully and convincingly proven guilty of the offence of unlawfully storing psychotropic substances;
  4. sentenced her to one (1) year's imprisonment and a fine of Rp 200,000 (two hundred thousand rupiah), to be substituted by one (1) month's detention if unpaid;
  5. ordered that time already spent in pre-trial detention be credited in full against the sentence;
  6. ordered the confiscation and destruction of the physical evidence — two packets of methamphetamine (5.6 grams), one box of clip-seal plastic bags, one white paper wrapping, and a white plastic box; and
  7. ordered the defendant to bear the costs of both the trial and cassation proceedings (cassation costs: Rp 2,500).

Ratio decidendi

The trial court had misapplied the law of evidence under Articles 185(6) and 197(1)(d) KUHAP; its acquittal was therefore a "not-genuine acquittal" and reviewable on cassation under Article 244 KUHAP. Because the bathroom in which the drugs were found was accessible only through the defendant's own bedroom and was used exclusively by the defendant and her young child, the defendant — as the household's principal occupant, exercising daily exclusive control over that space — was, as a matter of law, presumed to know of and be responsible for its contents. Assessed together with the circumstantial evidence admissible under Article 188 KUHAP, this presumption was sufficient to establish the defendant's intentional possession of the psychotropic substance.

Applicable law

Statutes

  • Law No. 5 of 1997 on Psychotropics (Undang-Undang No. 5 Tahun 1997 tentang Psikotropika), Article 62 (unlawfully possessing, storing, and/or carrying Category II psychotropic substances) and Article 65 (failure to report psychotropic substance abuse).
  • Law No. 8 of 1981 on Criminal Procedure (KUHAP):
    • Article 185(6) — limits on the evaluation of witness testimony and evidentiary consistency;
    • Article 188 — circumstantial evidence (alat bukti petunjuk);
    • Article 197(1)(d) — requirement that a criminal judgment set out its legal reasoning;
    • Article 244 — cassation against acquittals.
  • Law No. 14 of 1970, as amended by Law No. 14 of 1985, on the Supreme Court — power of the Supreme Court to try a matter itself (mengadili sendiri) on cassation.

Related doctrine

  • Presumption of knowledge/custody in private premises: possession of an illegal item in a private space with restricted access gives rise to a legal presumption that the principal occupant of that space controls, and is aware of, the item.
  • Cassation against a "not-genuine acquittal": the Supreme Court may set aside an acquittal where the trial court has misapplied the law of evidence.

Significance

The decision is frequently cited in Indonesian narcotics jurisprudence for the proposition that an occupant of a household cannot readily escape criminal liability for contraband found in personal living space — such as an ensuite bathroom or bedroom — merely by disclaiming knowledge or attributing ownership to another family member, where that space is under the occupant's exclusive day-to-day control.
