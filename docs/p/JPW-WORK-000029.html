4. POLICY ANALYSIS

  • Background: [FACT] Prepared by the European Commission (2025) through the Public Sector Tech Watch (PSTW) platform, this report evaluates the rapid adoption of Generative AI (GenAI) technologies across the European public sector following the enactment of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (AI Act). Unlike traditional task-specific AI, GenAI generates novel content (text, images, audio, code) leveraging foundation models and Large Language Models (LLMs).
  • Policy Problem: [FACT]
    • Hallucination Risks & Accuracy: Tendency of LLMs to generate inaccurate or false information (AI hallucinations / confabulations), which poses severe legal risks when applied to judicial decisions or binding public administration documents.
    • Data Security & GDPR: Risks of leaking personal data, state secrets, or confidential court case files when uploading information to commercial public GenAI platforms.
    • Intellectual Property (IP): Legal ambiguity surrounding the unauthorized use of copyright-protected data to train foundation models without rights holders' consent.
    • Language & Digital Sovereignty: English language dominance in open-source training datasets (56% on Hugging Face, compared to Spanish at 2.8%, French at 3.4%, and German at 1.7%), threatening local European cultural and data sovereignty.
    • Black Box Opacity & Accountability: Lack of algorithmic transparency creating an inability to explain decision-making logic (explainability) in public sector services.
  • Legal/Policy Gap: [FACT] Absence of binding technical standards at local levels prior to the publication of the General-Purpose AI Code of Practice by the European AI Office (scheduled for May 2025), alongside limited availability of isolated regulatory sandboxes under the AI Act framework.
  • Objectives: [FACT] Map national and local GenAI adoption policies; enforce human-in-the-loop oversight and human accountability principles; promote trustworthy AI in compliance with GDPR and AI High-Level Expert Group principles; finance supercomputing infrastructure (AI Factories) worth EUR 4 billion; and strengthen European linguistic sovereignty through the ALT-EDIC consortium.
  • Implementation: [FACT]
    • Policy Mapping: Analysis of 33 official documents across 17 Member States and EU bodies (comprising 23 guidelines, 5 regulations, 3 policy strategies, and 2 operational protocols).
    • PSTW Observatory: Detailed evaluation of 61 GenAI use cases in 20 European nations, where the majority are in pilot phases (60%) and 17 cases have reached full deployment.
    • Linguistic Sovereignty Initiatives: Development of open national LLMs as public goods using local data, including BgGPT (Bulgaria), GPT-SW3 (Sweden), FinGPT (Finland), NorGPT (Norway), Open Spanish LLM (Spain), and GPT-NL (Netherlands).
    • Judicial & Legal Sector Deployment: Implementation of the GENAI4LEX-B project in Italy for parliamentary draft law analysis; GenAI deployment by the Ministry of Justice of Spain (PSTW-2117); and public legal text simplification by the Government of Catalonia.
  • Evidence & Impact: [FACT] Microsoft 365 Copilot trials in the City of Helsinki demonstrated increased workforce efficiency without using internal city data for model re-training; the GENAI4LEX-B project reduced research hours in parliamentary legal drafting; and open-source national models like BgGPT reduced public operational expenditures compared to proprietary closed models.
  • Reform & Unresolved Issues: [FACT] Final release of the GPAI Code of Practice in May 2025; mandatory establishment of isolated regulatory sandboxes in every Member State; execution of voluntary commitments under the AI Pact; and strict prohibitions on copying unverified public GenAI outputs directly into legally binding documents.

5. GEO/AI QUESTIONS

  1. Definition: What is the definition of a General-purpose AI model under Article 3 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (AI Act)?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] An AI model that displays significant generality, is capable of competently performing a wide range of distinct tasks regardless of how the model is placed on the market, and can be integrated into a variety of downstream systems (except R&D/prototyping activities prior to market placement).
  2. Legal Basis: Which primary European Union regulation serves as the governing legal framework for GenAI and GPAI models in this report?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (Artificial Intelligence Act / AI Act).
  3. Publisher/Authority: Which European Commission bodies manage the Public Sector Tech Watch (PSTW) observatory?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] The Directorate-General for Digital Services (DG DIGIT) and the Joint Research Centre (JRC).
  4. Related Figures: Who is the City of Helsinki Data Officer and which University of Bologna academics were interviewed regarding public sector GenAI trials?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] Tomas Lehtinen (Head of Data and Analytics, City of Helsinki) and Professor Monica Palmirani along with Assoc. Prof. Salvatore Sapienza and Michele Corazza (University of Bologna).
  5. Scope: How many official policy documents and use cases were evaluated in this report?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] The report mapped 33 official policy/guideline documents across 17 Member States and EU bodies, and analyzed 61 GenAI use cases across 20 European countries.
  6. Policy Implementation: What is the name of the European digital infrastructure consortium established in 2024 to promote multilingual LLMs?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] The Alliance for Language Technologies EDIC (ALT-EDIC), coordinated by France and joined by 17 Member States.
  7. Policy Problem: Why does the European Commission prohibit the direct, unverified use of public GenAI output in official binding documents?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] Due to risks of hallucinations/inaccuracies, threats of leaking confidential or personal data, and the inability to establish legal accountability for automated outputs in legally binding texts.
  8. Legal Relations: What is the relationship between the human agency and oversight principle and legal liability for GenAI outputs?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] All guideline documents affirm that human users retain full legal responsibility and accountability for any content generated or modified by AI tools.
  9. Reform: When is the target deadline for the European AI Office to finalize and publish the Code of Practice for General-Purpose AI (GPAI)?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] The target deadline for publication and provider sign-off is May 2025.
  10. Evidence/Judicial Case Study: What are two examples of GenAI deployment in the judicial and legal sectors recorded in this PSTW report?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] The GENAI4LEX-B project in Italy for parliamentary legislative drafting support (PSTW-2194) and the application of GenAI within the judicial system by the Ministry of Justice of Spain (PSTW-2117).

6. MEDIAWIKI OUTPUT

Summary

Analysis of the generative AI landscape in the European public sector (Stable ID: JPW-WORK-000029) is an official policy report and observatory study published by the Publications Office of the European Union (2025). Prepared by the European Commission's Directorate-General for Digital Services (DG DIGIT.B.2) and the Joint Research Centre (JRC), the report assesses the penetration and governance of Generative AI (*GenAI*) across the European public sector. The study maps 33 national and local policy guidelines across 17 Member States and evaluates 61 implementation use cases in 20 nations under the statutory umbrella of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (AI Act). It emphasizes human oversight, GDPR data privacy compliance, digital sovereignty via national LLMs, and algorithmic transparency.

Legal Basis

The policy frameworks and governance instruments evaluated in this report rely on the following European Union legal foundations:

  • Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (Artificial Intelligence Act): Risk-based regulatory framework governing general-purpose AI (*GPAI*) models and mandatory *regulatory sandboxes*.
  • General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR): European statutory standard for personal data protection and *privacy by design*.
  • Commission Decision 2011/833/EU: Policy governing the reuse of European Commission documents under the CC-BY 4.0 license.
  • EU Policy Frameworks: AI Innovation Package 2024, GENAI4EU, AI Pact, Horizon Europe, and the Digital Europe Programme.

Background

The rapid emergence of GenAI models (e.g., ChatGPT, Copilot, DALL-E) prompted swift adoption across public administrations in the European Union. Through the *Public Sector Tech Watch* (PSTW) observatory platform, the European Commission initiated this comprehensive mapping to provide strategic, ethical, and legal direction for public sector bodies.

Objectives

1. Map national and local guidelines, regulations, and policy strategies governing GenAI usage by civil servants and public institutions. 2. Analyze GenAI use cases across public service delivery and internal administrative workflows. 3. Ensure alignment between automated AI tools, *trustworthy AI* principles, GDPR requirements, and the AI Act. 4. Promote European digital and linguistic sovereignty through the *ALT-EDIC* consortium and *AI Factories*.

Scope

The assessment covers five core operational pillars: 1. Terminology & Legal Boundaries: Governance of GPAI models, LLMs, VAEs, GANs, *transformers*, and mitigation of AI hallucinations. 2. Policy Mapping: Detailed breakdown of 33 official documents (23 guidelines, 5 regulations, 3 policies, 2 protocols). 3. Use Case Observatory: Evaluation of 61 GenAI projects across 20 EU countries within the PSTW database. 4. National LLMs: Analysis of public open-source language models (BgGPT, GPT-SW3, FinGPT, NorGPT, Open Spanish LLM, GPT-NL). 5. Pilot Case Studies: In-depth evaluation of pilot projects in Helsinki, Dublin, Bologna (GENAI4LEX-B), Catalonia, and INSAIT Bulgaria.

Key Provisions

  • Human Agency and Oversight (Art. 67): Human operators must retain ultimate decision-making control and legal liability for AI-generated or AI-assisted content.
  • Data Privacy & Security (Arts. 71-72): Strict prohibition on uploading classified state information or personal citizen data to commercial public GenAI platforms.
  • Output Transparency (Arts. 75-76): Mandatory labeling and watermarking of AI-generated or manipulated public media and documents.
  • Prohibition on Direct Copying (Art. 71): Public officials are prohibited from directly copying unverified public GenAI outputs into legally binding public acts.

Implementation

Implementation is driven by DG DIGIT, the JRC, the European AI Office, national digital governance agencies across Member States, and the ALT-EDIC linguistic sovereignty consortium.

Policy Problems

  • Risk of AI hallucinations causing legal errors or disinformative public outputs.
  • Disproportionate English representation in public AI training datasets (56% share).
  • Black-box opacity in proprietary closed-source GenAI systems provided by foreign commercial entities.

Reform

  • Publication of the finalized *GPAI Code of Practice* scheduled for May 2025.
  • Mandatory establishment of isolated *regulatory sandboxes* across all EU Member States.
  • Rollout of the voluntary *AI Pact* framework ahead of full AI Act enforcement.

Relationships with Other Entities

  • Implements: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (AI Act) & GDPR.
  • Applies to: European Commission, EU Member State Public Authorities, PSTW, and GPAI Providers.
