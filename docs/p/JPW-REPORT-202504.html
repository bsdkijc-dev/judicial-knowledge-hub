Definition

An official judicial policy research publication issued by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of the Supreme Court of Korea that analyzes the procedural characteristics, constitutional guarantees, "plaintiff-friendliness" principles (원고 친화성 원칙), and honorary judge system (명예법관제도) of the German Social Courts (Sozialgerichte under the Social Court Act / Sozialgerichtsgesetz - SGG 1953); compares German social security litigation with Korean administrative litigation practices; and formulates policy implications and legislative proposals for South Korea, including mandatory enforcement lawsuits (의무이행소송), relaxation of venue rules, ex officio investigation (직권심리주의), quasi-lay judge participation (준참심제), preliminary injunctions (가처분), and specialized social security court divisions (사회보장 전문재판부).

4. Policy Analysis

Background
In a modern welfare state, social security is a core state function, and effective legal remedies for social benefit entitlements must be guaranteed through the judicial system. In South Korea, social security caseloads in courts are exceptionally low compared to advanced welfare states. Annual social security cases filed in German 1st instance Social Courts (Sozialgericht) reached 236,429 cases in 2023 (representing 9.75% of all court cases nationwide), while UK First-tier Tribunals receive ~200,000 cases and French judicial social divisions receive 100,000–200,000 cases annually. By contrast, Korean 1st instance administrative courts handled roughly 20,000 social security cases over an entire decade (2011–2020). In 2023, out of 21,953 total 1st instance administrative cases in South Korea, occupational/industrial accident cases made up only 10.1% (2,226 cases) and national merit cases made up 2.0% (429 cases).

Policy Problem
Severe Barriers to Access to Justice (사법접근권의 제약): Plaintiffs in social security litigation are overwhelmingly socially and economically vulnerable individuals (low-income, elderly, disabled, ill, or unemployed). However, complex legal structures, heavy litigation costs (court stamps, service fees, medical appraisal costs), strict procedural formalities, and lack of specialized legal aid cause vulnerable citizens to abandon court remedies or fail to enter the judicial sphere altogether.

Structural Asymmetry & Power Imbalance (비대칭적 당사자 관계): Social security administrative agencies possess vast institutional resources, specialized expertise, and legal representation. Unrepresented, low-literacy plaintiffs face severe structural inequality in adversarial litigation.

Inadequate Remedies under Current Administrative Litigation Act:

  • No Action for Mandatory Enforcement (의무이행소송의 부재): Korean courts only recognize revocation actions (거부처분 취소소송) and actions for confirmation of illegal omission (부작위위법확인소송). When a plaintiff wins a revocation judgment, the agency can simply issue a new rejection under a slightly modified rationale, forcing the impoverished plaintiff to file endless iterative lawsuits.
  • Rigid Application of Adversarial Principle (변론주의의 엄격적 적용): Supreme Court precedent treats ex officio investigation (Art. 26) as merely supplementary to the adversarial principle. Impoverished plaintiffs who fail to plead specific legal requirements or afford expert medical appraisals routinely lose due to burden of proof.
  • No Preliminary Injunctions & High Suspensive Effect Bar (가처분 배제 및 집행정지 요건): Korean administrative courts reject civil preliminary injunctions (가처분) and require "irreparable harm" (회복하기 어려운 손해) for suspensive effect. Stoppage or clawback of basic livelihood benefits or medical care during multi-year trials threatens the plaintiff's immediate right to survival.

Legal/Policy Gap
Korea applies general administrative litigation rules indiscriminately to social security disputes, ignoring the inherent public-law, ongoing, and subsistence-oriented nature of social benefit entitlements.

Objectives
Implement structural procedural reforms to guarantee substantive constitutional equality, lower entry barriers for vulnerable citizens, enforce "equality of arms" (무기대등의 원칙), and deliver complete, gapless legal remedies (공백 없는 권리구제).

Implementation & Proposed Statutory Reforms
Enactment of Mandatory Enforcement Lawsuit (의무이행소송 도입): Amend Administrative Litigation Act Articles 4, 38-2 ~ 38-8 to introduce mandatory enforcement lawsuits, empowering courts to directly order administrative agencies to issue specific benefit grants or re-decide applications in compliance with binding judicial findings.

Enhancing Access to Justice (원고 사법접근권 강화):

  • Amend venue rules to permit filing in the court covering the plaintiff's residence, domicile, or place of employment.
  • Expand Justice Access Centers (사법접근센터) with specialized personnel to assist unrepresented plaintiffs in drafting oral/written complaints.
  • Introduce a Public Defender System for Social Security Litigation (사회보장소송 국선변호사제도) under Administrative Judiciary Rules.
  • Make Legal Aid (소송구조) mandatory for recipients of Basic Livelihood, Single-Parent, Basic Pension, and Disability Pension benefits by amending Civil Procedure Act Art. 128 and Court Regulations.

Institutionalizing Ex Officio Investigation (직권심리주의 원칙화): Amend Administrative Litigation Act Article 26 to establish ex officio investigation as the primary fact-finding principle in social security cases, placing responsibility on judges to investigate technical/medical facts and gather evidence independently.

Quasi-Lay Judge System & Expert Participation (준참심제 및 전문가 참여): Introduce a Quasi-Lay Judge System (준참심제) under Court Organization Act amendments where 2 expert lay judges (from welfare, labor, medical, and legal associations) participate in hearings and deliberations with advisory impact. As an immediate administrative measure, designate "Social Security Technical Examiners" (사회보장 전문심리위원) and "Social Security Court Investigators" (사회보장사건조사관).

Interim Relief & Preliminary Injunctions (가처분 및 집행정지 완화):

  • Amend Administrative Litigation Act Article 23(2) to relax the suspensive effect threshold from "irreparable harm" to "serious harm" (중대한 손해).
  • Enact proposed Article 24-2 to establish Preliminary Injunctions (가처분) to grant temporary provisional benefits during pending trials.

Specialized Social Security Divisions & Specialized Court (사회보장 전문재판부 및 사회보장법원): Immediately expand specialized social security divisions (사회보장 전문재판부) in district/administrative courts nationwide (pioneered by Seoul Administrative Court in Feb 2025 with 4 collegiate and 11 single-judge panels), paving the way for a standalone Social Security Court (사회보장법원) or integrated Labor and Social Security Court (노동사회법원).

Evidence & Empirical Data
German Social Court Statistics (2023): Total cases filed in 1st instance Social Courts (Sozialgericht) reached 236,429. Social security cases accounted for 9.75% of ALL court cases in Germany in 2023 (236,429 out of 2,305,454 total cases across civil, criminal, family, admin, tax, labor, and social courts).

German Social Court Judicial Survey: Survey of German Social Court judges revealed that 75.7% identified social security plaintiffs as having below-average income levels, 60.6% viewed plaintiffs as socially disadvantaged, and ~50% evaluated plaintiffs as lacking education/ability to confront administrative agencies without court assistance.

Korean Administrative Court Statistics: 1st instance administrative social security cases totaled ~20,000 across a 10-year period (2011–2020), averaging ~2,000 cases per year — roughly 1/10th of Germany's caseload relative to population.

Korean Welfare Beneficiary Population (Nov 2024): Basic Livelihood recipients: 2,666,032; Disability Pension recipients: 351,386; Basic Senior Pension recipients: 6,684,357; Near-poverty / single-parent recipients: 1,006,834.

OECD Social Expenditure Data (2022/2023): Korea's public social expenditure was 12.3% of GDP in 2023 (Costa Rica level), ranking near the bottom of the OECD (vs OECD average ~21%, France 30.7%, Finland 29.4%, Denmark 28.4%, Germany 25.6%).

6. MediaWiki Output

Research on the German Social Courts is an official judicial policy research report published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of South Korea's Supreme Court.

Summary

This document is an official judicial policy research report published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of South Korea's Supreme Court. It comparatively and comprehensively analyzes the organizational structure, constitutional guarantees, the "plaintiff-friendliness" principle (원고 친화성 원칙 / Klägerfreundlichkeit), and the honorary judge system (명예법관제도 / Ehrenamtliche Richter) of the German Social Courts (Sozialgericht, under the Sozialgerichtsgesetz — SGG 1953). It diagnoses barriers to access to justice for vulnerable citizens in South Korea; compares caseloads (Germany handles 236,429 social cases annually vs. Korea's ~2,000 per year); and formulates legal reforms including a mandatory enforcement lawsuit (의무이행소송), expanded representation and mandatory legal aid (대리인 및 국선변호 확대), the principle of ex officio investigation (직권심리주의 원칙화), quasi-lay judge participation (준참심제), preliminary injunctions (가처분), and specialized social security trial divisions (사회보장 전문재판부).

Legal Basis

The legal frameworks analyzed include:

  • Constitution of the Republic of Korea: Article 11(1) (Equality Principle), Article 27(1) (Right to a Fair Trial), and Article 34 (Right to Social Security & State Duty).
  • German Basic Law (Grundgesetz - GG): Article 1(1) (Human Dignity), Article 2(1) (Free Development of Personality), Article 3(1) (Equality Before the Law), Article 19(4) (Guarantee of Effective Legal Protection / Effektiver Rechtsschutz), Article 20(1) & Article 28(1) (Social State Principle / Sozialstaatsprinzip), Article 92 (Judicial Power), Article 95(1) (Federal Social Court / Bundessozialgericht — BSG), Article 101(1) (Right to a Lawful Judge), and Article 103(1) (Right to Legal Hearing / Recht auf rechtliches Gehör).
  • German Social Court Act (Sozialgerichtsgesetz - SGG 1953): Articles 1, 2, 3, 12, 13, 14, 51 (Subject-Matter Jurisdiction), 54 (Types of Action), 57 (Local Venue at Plaintiff's Residence), 67 (Wiedereinsetzung), 73 (No Mandatory Attorney Representation at 1st & 2nd Instance), 75 (Third-Party Joinder / Beigeladener), 86a (Automatic Suspensive Effect / Aufschiebende Wirkung), 86b (Interim Protection / Einstweilige Anordnung), 88 (Action for Failure to Act / Untätigkeitsklage), 90 (Oral Complaint), 91, 103 (Ex Officio Investigation / Untersuchungsmaxime), 106, 109 (Plaintiff-Selected Medical Expert Evidence), 123, 130 (Interim Judgment on Basis / Grundurteil), 183 (Court Fee Exemption for Citizens / Kostenfreiheit), and 202.
  • German Social Code (Sozialgesetzbuch - SGB I through XII).
  • Korean Administrative Litigation Act (행정소송법): Articles 3, 4, 8, 18, 20, 23, 26, 30, 38, and proposed amendments.

Background

In a modern welfare state, the fulfillment of social security rights must be guaranteed through the judicial system. In Germany, the first-instance Social Courts (Sozialgericht) received 236,429 cases in 2023 (accounting for 9.75% of all national court cases). By contrast, South Korea's administrative courts handled only ~20,000 social security cases over an entire decade (2011–2020). Barriers of cost, legal complexity, and the absence of interim injunctions frequently discourage low-income, elderly, disabled, and unemployed citizens in Korea from pursuing their rights in court.

Objectives

  1. Guarantee substantive access to justice (사법접근권) for socially and economically vulnerable citizens.
  2. Adopt the plaintiff-friendliness principle (원고 친화성 원칙) and the principle of equality of arms (무기대등의 원칙) in social security litigation procedure.
  3. Enact a mandatory enforcement lawsuit (의무이행소송) and preliminary injunctions (가처분) through amendments to the Korean Administrative Litigation Act.
  4. Establish specialized social security trial divisions (사회보장 전문재판부) and lay the groundwork for a future Social Security Court (사회보장법원).

Scope

  • Empirical & Statistical Study: Analysis of 236,429 German SGG cases in 2023, a survey of German Social Court judges (75.7% assessed plaintiffs as below-average income; 60.6% assessed plaintiffs as socially disadvantaged), statistics on 2.66 million Korean Basic Livelihood recipients, and OECD social expenditure data (Korea 12.3% of GDP vs. Germany 25.6% vs. France 30.7%).
  • Comparative Analysis of German Law:
    • Plaintiff-Friendliness Principle: Free court fees (SGG § 183), venue at the plaintiff's domicile (SGG § 57), oral complaints (SGG § 90), and the principle of most-favorable interpretation (Meistbegünstigungsprinzip).
    • Honorary Judge System (Ehrenamtliche Richter): Participation of 2 full-voting lay judges alongside 1 professional judge in each panel (SGG § 12).

Key Provisions

  • Mandatory Enforcement Lawsuit (의무이행소송 — Proposed Article 4(4) & Articles 38-2~38-8 of the Administrative Litigation Act): Allows courts to directly order administrative agencies to issue benefit grant decisions.
  • Ex Officio Investigation (직권심리주의 원칙화 — Proposed Article 26 of the Administrative Litigation Act): Requires judges to independently investigate facts and evidence without placing the burden on unrepresented plaintiffs.
  • Preliminary Injunction (가처분 — Proposed Article 24-2 of the Administrative Litigation Act): Provides temporary benefit payments during ongoing litigation to protect the right to subsistence (생존권).
  • Legal Aid & Mandatory Fee Waivers: Requires legal aid (소송구조) and public defenders (국선변호사) for recipients of basic social assistance.

Implementation

Courts route social security cases to Specialized Social Security Trial Divisions (사회보장 전문재판부), expand Justice Access Centers (사법접근센터), and engage Expert Commissioners (전문심리위원) and Case Investigators (사건조사관) to assist judges.

Policy Issues

  • The practice of ordinary revocation lawsuits (거부처분 취소소송) results in repeated denials by administrative agencies under new rationales (limits of the re-decision obligation, 재처분의무의 한계).
  • The suspensive effect standard ("irreparable harm," 회복하기 어려운 손해) is too strict for livelihood benefit disputes.

Reform / Amendments

  1. Enactment of the Mandatory Enforcement Lawsuit (의무이행소송).
  2. Amendment of Article 23(2) of the Administrative Litigation Act (relaxing the suspensive effect threshold to "serious harm," 중대한 손해).
  3. Enactment of Article 24-2 of the Administrative Litigation Act (the Preliminary Injunction / 가처분 system).
  4. Amendment of Article 26 of the Administrative Litigation Act (formalizing the principle of ex officio investigation, 직권심리주의).
  5. Establishment of the Quasi-Lay Judge System (준참심제) & Specialized Social Security Trial Divisions (사회보장 전문재판부).
