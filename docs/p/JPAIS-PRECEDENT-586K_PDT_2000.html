Overview

Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia Decision No. 586 K/Pdt/2000, dated May 23, 2001, established a foundational civil procedural law rule regarding the requirement of clarity and certainty in a lawsuit's pleadings (*obscuur libel*) concerning the area and boundaries of disputed land:

Where inconsistencies or differences exist regarding the area and boundaries of disputed land between the posita (legal grounds/reasons for the lawsuit) and the petitum (relief sought), the petitum fails to support the posita. The legal consequence of this contradiction renders the Plaintiff's lawsuit unclear and obscure (*obscuur libel*), requiring the lawsuit to be declared inadmissible (*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard* / NO).

Executive Summary

The central procedural principle established in this decision is that a civil lawsuit must maintain strict consistency between its factual foundational narrative (*posita*) and its specific demands (*petitum*), particularly regarding real estate dimensions.

Key elements established in the adjudication:

  • Discrepancies in land size or boundaries between the body of the complaint (*posita*) and the prayer for relief (*petitum*) render a property claim fatally defective as obscure (*obscuur libel*).
  • Exceptions raised regarding formal lawsuit defects must be sustained by appellate and cassation courts prior to reviewing substantive merits.
  • Contradictory pleadings require courts to dismiss claims as inadmissible (*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard* / NO).

Case Background

The Original Plaintiff / Respondent for Cassation, [Party A], filed a civil lawsuit at the East Jakarta District Court against the Original Defendants / Petitioners for Cassation (1. Yayasan Harapan Kita, 2. [Petitioner II], 3. Ny. [Petitioner III]) and the East Jakarta Agrarian Office (BPN) as Co-Defendant, concerning the occupation of Customary Land (*Tanah Adat*) Girik C No. 200 in Ceger Village, Cipayung District (formerly Pasar Rebo), East Jakarta.

The Plaintiff alleged in the *posita* that the disputed land measured 4,960 m² or 4,993 m², but demanded surrender and eviction over areas measuring 1,005 m² and 596 m² (totaling 1,601 m²) in the *petitum*.

The East Jakarta District Court (Decision No. 200/Pdt.G/1997/PN.Jkt.Tim dated July 7, 1998) granted the Plaintiff's lawsuit in part. The Jakarta High Court (Decision No. 638/Pdt/1998/PT.DKI dated March 1, 1999) affirmed the district court decision. The Petitioners for Cassation subsequently filed a cassation petition with the Supreme Court.

Legal and Procedural Issues

Formal Requirements for Clarity of Land Dispute Claim Objects (Obscuur Libel ex Article 118 H.I.R.)

The primary issue was whether a civil lawsuit concerning land ownership satisfies formal requirements when material discrepancies exist regarding surface area measurements and boundary identifications between the descriptive *posita* and the prayer for relief (*petitum*).

The Supreme Court held that it fails to satisfy formal requirements. A civil lawsuit must formulate legal relations and dispute objects synchronously, clearly, and consistently (*descriptive-consistent*). When land areas in the *posita* are stated as 4,960 m² / 4,993 m² but the *petitum* demands eviction/surrender over 1,005 m² and 596 m² (totaling 1,601 m²), the *petitum* fails to support the *posita*. This incongruity renders the lawsuit unclear and obscure (*obscuur libel*), requiring dismissal as inadmissible (*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard* / NO).

Review of Formal Pleadings Exceptions at Cassation Level

This case establishes the duty of *Judex Juris* to examine the formal precision of statement of claim formulations prior to evaluating substantive merits. Once an exception regarding an obscure lawsuit is sustained, Supreme Court Justices are absolved from reviewing alternative cassation grounds concerning the merits.

Question of Law and Question of Fact

Question of Law (Cassation / Judex Juris)

Question:

Did the *Judex Facti* (Jakarta High Court & East Jakarta District Court) misapply civil procedural law (*foutieve kepassing van het procesrecht*) by granting the Plaintiff's lawsuit in part, despite pleading contradictions concerning property dimensions between the *posita* and *petitum* constituting an obscure lawsuit (*obscuur libel*)?

Supreme Court Ruling:

Yes, the *Judex Facti* misapplied procedural law. The Supreme Court granted the cassation petitions of Petitioners I, II, and III, annulled the Jakarta High Court and East Jakarta District Court decisions, and adjudicated afresh by sustaining the Defendants' exceptions and declaring the Plaintiff's lawsuit inadmissible (*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard* / NO).

Question of Fact (Judex Facti / East Jakarta District Court & Jakarta High Court)

Examination of facts concerning Customary Girik C No. 200 land ownership in Ceger Village, Cipayung District (Pasar Rebo), East Jakarta, based on Sale and Purchase Deed No. 138/DB/VII/1973 dated June 8, 1973, measurement variances by East Jakarta BPN No. 568/600/III/P&K/JT/1994 totaling 4,993 m², and issuance of Use Rights Certificates (*Sertifikat Hak Pakai*) Nos. 33/Ceger and 35/Ceger in the name of Yayasan Harapan Kita.

Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

  • Ratio Decidendi:
  1. The appellate and district *Judex Facti* misapplied civil procedural law. Upon examining the case file, clear discrepancies existed regarding disputed land dimensions and boundaries between the *posita* and *petitum* of the Plaintiff's pleadings (*posita* stating 4,960 m² / 4,993 m² versus *petitum* demanding 1,005 m² and 596 m²).
  2. Due to this contradiction, the *petitum* failed to support the *posita*, rendering the Plaintiff's lawsuit unclear and obscure (*obscuur libel*).
  3. The Supreme Court annulled the lower court rulings and adjudicated afresh, sustaining the Defendants' exceptions and declaring the lawsuit inadmissible (*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard* / NO).

Ruling

The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition from Petitioners for Cassation (1. YAYASAN HARAPAN KITA, 2. [Petitioner II], 3. NY. [Petitioner III]), annulled Jakarta High Court Decision No. 638/Pdt/1998/PT.DKI dated March 1, 1999, in conjunction with East Jakarta District Court Decision No. 200/Pdt.G/1997/PN.Jkt.Tim dated July 7, 1998, and adjudicated afresh: Under Exceptions: Declaring the exceptions of Defendant I and Defendant II admissible; Under the Merits: Declaring the Plaintiff's lawsuit inadmissible (*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard* / NO); and ordering the Respondent for Cassation / Original Plaintiff to pay court costs across all judicial instances.

Related Knowledge and Jurisprudence

  • Herziene Inlandsch Reglement (H.I.R.) / RBg: Article 118 paragraph (1) regarding formal pleading requirements for civil lawsuits.
  • Law No. 14 of 1970 in conjunction with Law No. 14 of 1985 on the Supreme Court: Provisions governing the annulment of judgments due to procedural misapplication.
  • Supreme Court Standing Jurisprudence: Doctrine of unclear and obscure lawsuits (*obscuur libel*) resulting from object uncertainty (location indicators, area size, and land boundaries).

Evidence Status and Gaps

Established by Evidence

  • The Plaintiff formulated his *posita* by claiming Girik C 200 land measuring 4,460 m², BPN surveyed area of 4,993 m², or 4,960 m².
  • The Plaintiff formulated his *petitum* by demanding surrender/evacuation over land measuring 1,005 m² and 596 m² (totaling 1,601 m²).
  • Contradictions and clear discrepancies existed between area descriptions in the *posita* section and relief demands in the *petitum* section.

Not Established / Formal Defect

  • The Plaintiff failed to establish formal alignment between the land ownership claims in the *posita* and the specific objects demanded in the *petitum*, precluding further examination of substantive merits.

Uncertainty and Gaps

  • Setting Aside Substantive Merits Due to Formal Defect: Because the exception of *obscuur libel* was sustained at cassation, the Supreme Court no longer evaluated the *Judex Facti*'s substantive findings regarding the validity of Deed of Rights Release No. 55 dated December 21, 1984, or Use Rights certificates.

GEO Q&A

Q: Is a land dispute lawsuit valid if the land area written in the posita differs from the land area demanded in the petitum?

A: Invalid, and the lawsuit must be declared inadmissible (*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard* / NO). Based on Supreme Court Decision No. 586 K/Pdt/2000, whenever discrepancies exist regarding land area and boundaries between the *posita* and *petitum*, the *petitum* is deemed unsupported by the *posita*. This renders the lawsuit obscure (*obscuur libel*), requiring courts to issue a ruling declaring the lawsuit inadmissible (NO).
