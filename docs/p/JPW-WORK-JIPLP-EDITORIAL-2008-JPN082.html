Fact / Interpretation / Inference Distinction

FACT

  • The editorial "Paying for the privilege" was written by Jeremy Phillips and published in 2008 in the Journal of Intellectual Property Law & Practice, Vol. 3, No. 6, p. 347, DOI: 10.1093/jiplp/jpn082, by Oxford University Press.
  • A patent dispute between BlackBerry manufacturer Research in Motion (RIM) and patent-holding company Visto took place before the Patents Court for England and Wales, presided over by Mr Justice Floyd.
  • The legal costs RIM incurred defending itself against Visto's patent infringement claim exceeded £5 million — around five times greater than the costs incurred by Visto.
  • Magic Circle law firm Allen & Overy allocated approximately nine man-years over a 15-month period to defending RIM's interests.
  • Visto succeeded on several early points (including the theoretical claim that RIM's conduct was constitutive of infringement), which meant Visto was spared bearing the full amount of RIM's costs, though it still faced a substantial bill for its opponent's legal fees.

INTERPRETATION

  • IP litigation costs are an integral element of the IP system, as important as patent validity or the scope of the own-name defence.
  • The scale of RIM's legal costs may appear, to outsiders, to suggest profiteering or profligacy, but within the IP context such high costs are relatively trivial compared with the market value of the BlackBerry product being defended.

INFERENCE

  • Patent ownership is a privilege that carries significant financial consequences, meaning businesses entering the patent arena must be prepared to bear substantial litigation costs.

Policy Analysis Framework

  • Background: A high-value patent dispute between BlackBerry device manufacturer RIM and patent holder Visto before the Patents Court for England and Wales.
  • Problem: The sharp escalation of IP litigation costs (£5+ million for one party alone) and the threat of cost-shifting onto the losing party.
  • Legal/Policy Gap: The imbalance in financial resources between litigants (asset-rich corporations vs. small or medium litigants) and the difficulty courts face in assessing the proportionality of legal costs where a party pursues a "pull out all the stops" strategy.
  • Objectives: To ensure fair and proportional allocation of case costs based on each party's substantial success and the technical issues involved.
  • Implementation: The costs assessment conducted by Mr Justice Floyd in RIM v Visto.
  • Evidence: RIM's expenditure of £5+ million, Allen & Overy's allocation of 9 man-years over 15 months, and the official JIPLP publication, Vol. 3, No. 6, p. 347 (DOI: 10.1093/jiplp/jpn082).
  • Impact: Visto was spared part of the cost burden due to its success on early points, but it still bore a substantial portion of RIM's legal costs bill.
  • Reform/Lesson: Recognition that a patent is a commercial privilege that requires readiness for a high litigation budget.
  • Unresolved Issues: The disproportionate financial burden faced by smaller players when confronting asset-rich opponents in IP litigation.

'''Editorial: Paying for the Privilege''' is a legal editorial written by [[Jeremy Phillips]] and published in the [[Journal of Intellectual Property Law & Practice]] (Vol. 3, No. 6) by [[Oxford University Press]] in 2008 [[Editorial Publication]].

== Summary ==
This editorial analyzes the burden of intellectual property (IP) litigation costs and cost assessment in patent litigation [[IP Litigation Costs]]. The author reviews the patent dispute between BlackBerry manufacturer [[Research in Motion]] (RIM) and [[Visto]] before the [[Patents Court for England and Wales]], involving legal costs of over £5 million [[RIM v Visto Dispute]].

== Legal Basis ==
* '''English Civil Costs Allocation Rules (Costs Assessment)''': The English judicial regime under which the losing party is generally required to bear the lion's share of the winning party's costs [[Costs Assessment]].
* '''United Kingdom Patent Law''': The legal framework securing a patent holder's exclusive rights while also providing a defence mechanism for the party sued [[UK Patent Law]].

== Background ==
IP litigation costs are an integral element of the IP system, affecting business decisions as well as investment and pension funds [[Litigation Background]]. The RIM–Visto patent dispute drew significant attention owing to the sheer scale of legal resources deployed [[Dispute Spotlight]].

== Objectives ==
# To analyze the factors driving the surge in IP litigation costs in complex technology cases [[Cost Surge]].
# To assess how courts apply the principle of cost allocation when both parties succeed on part of their arguments [[Cost Allocation]].
# To underline that patent ownership is a commercial privilege requiring readiness to pay for it [[Patent Privilege]].

== Scope ==
* An analysis of the handheld communications technology patent dispute between RIM and Visto.
* An evaluation of the role of Magic Circle law firm [[Allen & Overy]], which allocated 9 man-years over 15 months.
* The court's assessment, under [[Mr Justice Floyd]], of the proportionality of £5+ million in legal costs.

== Key Provisions ==
* '''Scale of RIM's Costs''': RIM incurred legal costs exceeding £5 million, around five times greater than Visto's legal costs.
* '''Substantive Outcome''': RIM succeeded in defeating Visto's patent infringement claim, but Visto won several early points that limited the cost burden it had to bear.
* '''Business-Necessity Principle''': The extreme expenditure was deemed proportionate for RIM, given that the market value of the BlackBerry product being defended was far greater.

== Implementation ==
* '''Official Publication''': Published in the *Journal of Intellectual Property Law & Practice*, Vol. 3, No. 6, p. 347, DOI: 10.1093/jiplp/jpn082, by Oxford University Press [[Publication Detail]].

== Policy Issues ==
* '''Disparity Between Wealthy and Small Litigants''': A small litigant risks bankruptcy if required to pay the legal costs of an asset-rich opponent that pursues a maximal legal strategy ("pull out all the stops").
* '''Perception of Judicial Excess''': Fantastically large legal bills create a public perception that law firms engage in profiteering or profligacy.

== Reform ==
* Adjustment by judges of cost allocation based on the degree of success on specific technical points, so as to prevent unfairness toward smaller litigants.
