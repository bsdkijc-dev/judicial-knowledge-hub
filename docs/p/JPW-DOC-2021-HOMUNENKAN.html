Policy Analysis

  • Background & Policy Scope:
    • FACT: The MOJ Annual Report 2021 provides a comprehensive operational overview of the Ministry of Justice of Japan (internal units, advisory councils, facilities, regional offices, and external agencies) covering January 1 to December 31, 2021.
  • Key Policy Priorities (FY2021):
    • Law Enforcement & Public Safety: Countering special fraud targeting the elderly, juvenile delinquency, cybercrime, narcotics, organized crime, and expanding international investigative cooperation.
    • Substantive & Procedural Reform:
      • Juvenile Act Revision (Act No. 47 of 2021): Enacted May 28, 2021 (effective April 1, 2022) creating the "Specified Juveniles" framework (ages 18–19) and establishing Fifth-Type Juvenile Training Schools.
      • Owner-Unknown Land Legislation (Act Nos. 24 & 25 of 2021): Passed Civil Code and Real Estate Registration amendments mandating inheritance registration and creating the National Treasury Escheatment Act.
    • Judicial Administration Digitalization: Enactment of Digital Government rules, IT integration in civil litigation, standardized name registration (Kanji/Kana), and My Number Card deployment.
    • 14th UN Crime Congress & Judicial Diplomacy: Hosted in Kyoto (March 7–12, 2021) with over 5,000 hybrid participants across 152 nations, adopting the Kyoto Declaration. Follow-ups include the Global Youth Forum and UN Standards for the Prevention of Re-offending.
    • Recidivism Prevention & CFP Framework: Replaced graduated treatment with the Case Formulation in Probation/Parole (CFP) framework evaluating 8 risk domains, alongside the rollout of the H@ portal for Volunteer Probation Officers (Hogoshi).
    • Legal Support & International Arbitration: Japan Legal Support Center (Houterasu) handled 594,300 consultations in FY2021 and introduced 10-language assistance at FRESC. JIDRC Tokyo operated in Toranomon to facilitate cross-border dispute resolution.
  • Interpretations & Structural Inferences:
    • INTERPRETATION: The report reflects a strategic pivot from traditional prosecution toward digital legal infrastructure, global judicial diplomacy, and criminologically targeted rehabilitation.
    • INFERENCE: Despite rising operational complexity from cybercrime and special fraud, integrated technologies (CFP system, H@ portal, digital courts) serve as primary instruments for national legal stability.
