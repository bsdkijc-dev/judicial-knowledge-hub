Definition

An official judicial policy research publication issued by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) under the Supreme Court of Korea (authored by Lead Research Fellow Lim, Sungmin) that analyzes South Korea's transition into a "super-aged society" (초고령사회 — over 20% aged 65+) in late 2024, assesses the legal status, legal needs, and legal vulnerabilities of older adults, examines judicial support systems and comparative international models across 4 jurisdictions (United States, France, Germany, Japan), and formulates comprehensive physical/digital accessibility reforms, procedural judicial support measures (civil, criminal, family/non-litigation), integrated dispute resolution structures (Access to Justice Commission / 사법접근위원회, Access to Justice Centers / 사법접근센터, and specialized Elder Courts / 고령자 법원), and community-based collaborative networks.

4. Policy Analysis

Background
At the end of 2024, South Korea officially entered the status of a "Super-Aged Society" (초고령사회), where the proportion of the population aged 65 and over exceeded 20% of the total national population. Rising life expectancy (reaching 83.5 years in 2020) and expanding social participation among older adults have driven a rapid increase in legal disputes and judicial procedures involving elderly individuals. Despite experiencing physical, mental, and social decline due to the aging process, older adults in South Korea face substantial inequality in realizing their Right to Access to Justice (사법접근권), a right derived from fundamental constitutional guarantees (Articles 10, 11, 27, and 34 of the ROK Constitution).

Policy Problem
Substantial Inequality and Elderly Vulnerability: Older adults face physical mobility barriers in court buildings, declining perceptual/sensory capacity, a severe digital divide (디지털 디바이드 — elderly digital capability stands at only 55.3% of the general population), the highest elderly poverty rate among OECD countries (40.4% in 2020), and a surge in dementia patients (projected to reach 1.42 million by 2030 and 3.15 million by 2050).

Escalation of Crime, Victimization, and Violence Against Older Adults:

  • Elderly Crime Suspects: The number of crime suspects aged 61+ surged from 84,028 (4.2%) in 2007 to 230,857 (17.0%) in 2022.
  • Elderly Crime Victims: Elderly crime victims rose to 173,056 people in 2022; victims of fraud and embezzlement against the elderly surged 3.6 to 4.1 times.
  • Elder Abuse (노인학대): Verified elder abuse cases reached 6,807 in 2022, with 89.3% occurring at home, and cases of spousal abuse among elderly couples increased 2.54-fold over the past decade.

Absence of Specific Rules & Fragmentation of Judicial Services: Existing judicial support is primarily designed for registered persons with disabilities or low-income citizens, not specifically tailored to the diverse characteristics of the elderly. The ROK judiciary has yet to establish specific normative rules (대법원 예규), lacks dedicated elder support officers (사회적 약자 지원관), and shows very low utilization rates of existing legal aid instruments among older adults.

Legal/Policy Gap
The absence of a national steering body (control tower — Access to Justice Commission / 사법접근위원회), the absence of a Specialized Elder Court Act, the lack of elder-specific statement assistant provisions in civil/criminal cases, and insufficient integrated community-based cooperative networks among the judiciary, executive ministries, and social welfare institutions.

Objectives
Realize a user-centered access to justice strategy; guarantee equal physical and digital access; enact a Supreme Court Regulation on Judicial Support for Vulnerable Groups (장애인 등 사법지원 예규); recruit dedicated judicial support officers; expand state-appointed counsel and statement assistants; strengthen Access to Justice Centers (사법접근센터); and establish a Specialized Elder Court (고령자 법원) in the mid-to-long term.

Implementation & Proposed Reforms
Physical & Digital Accessibility of Court Buildings: Improve court building design guidelines (modeled on the U.S. Courts Design Guide); provide assistive devices (bone-conduction headsets), magnifiers, and wheelchairs; launch a Pro Se Litigant portal (나홀로소송); and implement a plain-language AI chatbot (modeled on the US SANDI/JIA systems).

Procedural Reform:

  • General: Enact a Supreme Court Regulation (장애인 등 사법지원 예규); assign Vulnerable Group Support Officers (사회적 약자 지원관); provide real-time text interpretation (real-time CART); and provide training and handling manuals for judges and court staff on elderly cases.
  • Civil Cases: Reform legal aid (소송구조); expand the criteria for Statement Assistants (진술보조인) by amending Article 30-2 of the Civil Procedure Rules to include trusted non-family neighbors/caregivers; and issue judgments in Easy-Read format.
  • Criminal Cases: Expand mandatory state-appointed counsel (필요적 국선변호) for elderly defendants; implement statement assistants/companions for elderly defendants/victims; enact a Special Act on Elder Abuse Crimes; and incorporate victim protection orders (피해자보호명령) into the Senior Citizens Welfare Act.
  • Family & Guardianship Cases: Expand assistants in family litigation procedure; transform the Adult Guardianship System (성년후견제도) from a substitute decision-making model to a supported decision-making model; expand state-appointed guardians (국선후견인); and strengthen court oversight of guardianship.

Integrated Dispute Resolution Structure:

  • Establish an Access to Justice Commission (사법접근위원회) under the Supreme Court as a national policy steering body.
  • Develop Access to Justice Centers (사법접근센터) into a Multi-Door Courthouse model equipped with pre-trial intake screening and referrals to partner institutions.
  • Establish a Specialized Elder Court (고령자 법원 / Elder Court) to handle civil, criminal, and elder abuse protection matters in an integrated manner.

Evidence & Empirical Data
Demographic Context: The population aged 65+ exceeded 20% by the end of 2024; life expectancy reached 83.5 years; the 2018 Legal Needs Survey showed 100% of legal aid visitors aged 60+ had experienced disputes (money 19.9%, housing 17.1%, employment 15.6%, land 13.3%).

Judicial Support Data: In 2023, state-appointed counsel for defendants aged 70+ covered 8,333 cases (8,557 defendants); Access to Justice Centers handled 66,759 consultations in 2023 (18.0% for vulnerable groups, 8.7% elderly).

6. MediaWiki Output

A Study on Enhancing Access to Justice for Older Adults is an official judicial policy research report published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of South Korea's Supreme Court.

Summary

This document is an official judicial policy research report published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of South Korea's Supreme Court. It comprehensively analyzes the Right to Access to Justice (사법접근권) for older adults in South Korea, which officially entered "Super-Aged Society" status (초고령사회 — population aged 65+ exceeding 20%) at the end of 2024. It evaluates the legal status and legal needs of older adults; diagnoses physical, mental, economic, and digital vulnerabilities; examines comparative judicial support systems across 4 countries (US, France, Germany, Japan); and formulates reform recommendations including enacting a Supreme Court Regulation on Judicial Support for Vulnerable Groups (장애인 등 사법지원 예규), assigning Vulnerable Group Support Officers (사회적 약자 지원관), establishing an Access to Justice Commission (사법접근위원회), transforming Access to Justice Centers (사법접근센터) into a Multi-Door Courthouse, and establishing a Specialized Elder Court (고령자 법원) over the mid-to-long term.

Legal Basis

The legal frameworks analyzed include:

  • Constitution of the Republic of Korea: Article 10 (Human Dignity & the Right to Pursue Happiness), Article 11(1) (Right to Equality), Article 27 (Right to a Fair Trial / 재판청구권), Article 34(1), (4), (5) (Right to a Decent Life & State Duty to Care for the Elderly), and Article 36(3) (Right to Health).
  • Domestic Legislation on the Elderly & Vulnerable Groups:
    • The Senior Citizens Welfare Act (노인복지법), Articles 1-2, 4, 25, 26, 28, 33-2, 36, 39-5, 39-6, 39-8, 39-9, 39-19.
    • The Act on Prohibition of Age Discrimination in Employment and Elderly Employment Promotion (고령자고용법), Article 2(1) & Enforcement Decree Article 2(1), (2).
    • The Act on Guarantee of Convenience Promotion of Persons with Disabilities, Senior Citizens, Pregnant Women, etc. (장애인등편의법), Articles 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, 16.
    • The Civil Procedure Act (민사소송법), Article 143-2 (Statement Assistant / 진술보조인) & Article 327-2 (Video Witness Examination).
    • The Criminal Procedure Act (형사소송법), Article 33(1) Item 3 (State-Appointed Counsel for Age 70+), Article 163-2 (Person in a Trust Relationship / 신뢰관계인), Article 165-2, Article 276-2.
    • The Civil Act & the Family Litigation Act (가사소송법) on the Adult Guardianship System (성년후견제도) and Article 7.
  • International Instruments & Conventions: The UN Principles for Older Persons (1991), the Vienna/Madrid International Plan of Action on Ageing (1982, 2002), the UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDG 16.3), and the OECD Equal Access to Justice Guidelines.

Background

South Korea has experienced the fastest population aging rate in the world, reaching Super-Aged Society status at the end of 2024. Growth in the elderly population and their social activity has directly correlated with rising legal disputes. However, older adults face real inequality in accessing justice due to declining physical and cognitive function, a high elderly poverty rate (40.4%), the digital divide, a surge in dementia, and rampant elder abuse. A user-centered access to justice strategy is needed to guarantee equal judicial rights.

Objectives

  1. Diagnose the legal vulnerabilities, constitutional status, and empirical legal needs of older adults in South Korea.
  2. Comparatively examine judicial and legal aid systems for the elderly in the US, France, Germany, and Japan.
  3. Formulate recommendations for physical/digital accessibility reform, civil/criminal/family procedural reform, and integrated dispute resolution institutional structures.

Scope

  • Empirical Legal Needs Study: Analysis of the 2018 Legal Needs Survey data (monetary disputes 19.9%, housing 17.1%, employment 15.6%, land 13.3%).
  • Four-Country Comparative Study: The US (Elder Protection Court & ATJ Commissions), France (Point-Justice & FALC), Germany (Betreuungsgericht & Beratungshilfe), and Japan (Houterasu & Judicial Social Work).
  • Procedural & Institutional Reform: Amendment of procedural rules, assignment of support officers, establishment of the Access to Justice Commission, transformation of Access to Justice Centers into a Multi-Door Courthouse, and establishment of the Specialized Elder Court.

Key Provisions

  • Enactment of a Supreme Court Regulation (장애인 등 사법지원 예규): Establishes standardized obligations for judicial support for the elderly and vulnerable groups across all court levels.
  • Assignment of Vulnerable Group Support Officers (사회적 약자 지원관): Places dedicated officers at every court to identify elderly needs from the case-filing stage onward.
  • Provision of Real-Time Text Interpretation (Real-Time CART): Provides live text interpretation in courtrooms for hearing-impaired elderly litigants.
  • Amendment of Article 30-2 of the Civil Procedure Rules: Expands the criteria for Statement Assistants (진술조력인) to include trusted non-family neighbors or caregivers for elderly individuals living alone.
  • Establishment of the Access to Justice Commission (사법접근위원회): Establishes a steering committee under the Supreme Court to coordinate legal aid policy across agencies.
  • Transformation of Access to Justice Centers (사법접근센터): Adopts a Multi-Door Courthouse model providing pre-trial intake screening and integrated referrals.

Implementation

The Supreme Court adopts a draft Regulation on Judicial Support for Vulnerable Groups, expands accessibility features on the e-Court portal, and integrates cooperative networks with executive ministries and local social welfare institutions.

Policy Issues

  • Unequal access to justice caused by the digital divide and elderly poverty.
  • Rampant elder abuse and financial fraud cases that remain inadequately addressed in an integrated manner.
  • Low utilization of existing legal aid instruments due to the absence of standardized rules and support officers.

Reform / Amendments

  1. Enactment of a Supreme Court Regulation on Judicial Support for Vulnerable Groups (장애인 등 사법지원 예규).
  2. Assignment of Vulnerable Group Support Officers (사회적 약자 지원관) and Real-Time Text Interpreters (CART).
  3. Establishment of the Access to Justice Commission (사법접근위원회) and reform of Access to Justice Centers (사법접근센터).
  4. Enactment of a Special Act on Elder Abuse Crimes and establishment of a Specialized Elder Court (고령자 법원).

3 Most Important Improvements:

  1. Enactment of a Supreme Court Regulation on Judicial Support for Vulnerable Groups (장애인 등 사법지원 예규 법제화): Enact standardized Supreme Court norms requiring the assignment of Vulnerable Group Support Officers (사회적 약자 지원관), the provision of real-time text interpretation (CART), and adjustment of hearing schedules for the elderly across all courts.
  2. Institutionalization of the Access to Justice Commission (사법접근위원회) & Reform of Access to Justice Centers (Multi-Door Model): Establish an independent steering committee under the Supreme Court and restructure Access to Justice Centers into a Multi-Door Courthouse model providing pre-trial intake screening and an integrated referral system to legal aid, health, and welfare institutions.
  3. Drafting a Special Act on Elder Abuse Crimes & Establishing a Specialized Elder Court (고령자 법원): Enact a Special Act on Elder Abuse that incorporates victim protection orders (피해자보호명령) into the Senior Citizens Welfare Act, and establish a Specialized Elder Court (Elder Court) to handle civil, criminal, guardianship, and elder abuse disputes in an integrated manner.

Suggested Next Steps: Two strategic directions for further analysis could be pursued: (1) a detailed comparative matrix of global elder justice frameworks (US Elder Court vs. French Point-Justice vs. German Betreuungsgericht vs. Japanese Judicial Social Work), examining institutional structure, funding models, and the effectiveness of elder legal protection across the four countries; and (2) an analysis of the legal implications and operational workflow design for a Multi-Door Courthouse for the elderly, mapping the process from intake screening at Access to Justice Centers through to the allocation of elderly civil, criminal, and guardianship disputes to mediation, guardianship, or trial tracks.
