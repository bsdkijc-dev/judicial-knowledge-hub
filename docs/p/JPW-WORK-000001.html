4. POLICY ANALYSIS

Background: [FACT] Spain's legal and judicial landscape in 2019 faced a fierce debate between demands for "material justice" (justicia material) in banking disputes and the protection of legal certainty (seguridad jurídica), a pillar of economic development (Art. 9.3 CE). At the same time, EU regulatory harmonization required corporate governance reform (Shareholder Rights Directive II — Directive 2017/828), centralized banking supervision by the ECB through the Single Supervisory Mechanism (SSM), modernization of capital markets (the IBERCLEAR two-tier registration system), the development of venture capital bridge financing instruments, and a four-year review of the implementation of the IRPF exit tax (Art. 95 bis LIRPF).

Policy Problem: [FACT]

  1. Judiciary: The risk of "judicial populism" (populismo judicial), where judges set aside positive law in favor of subjective perceptions of justice.
  2. Corporate Law: The potential for value extraction from companies ("tunneling") through related-party transactions (RPT) lacking transparency and minority shareholder protection.
  3. Litigation & Arbitration: The complexity of calculating default interest, uncertainty over the starting date (dies a quo) upon partial reversal of judgments, and strict limits on interest anatocism.
  4. EU Banking Supervision: The need for a fast, independent objection channel against ECB decisions before filing an annulment action with the EU General Court (Art. 263 TFEU).
  5. Corporate Registration: Technical obstacles in registering proportionally elected directors for listed companies, due to ambiguity in account-blocking certification at the level of IBERCLEAR's participating entities.
  6. Taxation: An explicit contradiction between the IRPF exit tax rules (Art. 95 bis LIRPF) and the corporate restructuring rules under the Corporate Income Tax Law (Arts. 80.4 & 81.3 LIS); unfair tax burdens in cases where a taxpayer dies outside the EU/EEA; and rigid conditions for tax-payment guarantees required by the AEAT (Spanish Tax Agency).

Legal/Policy Gap: [FACT] The Spanish Capital Companies Law framework had not yet fully aligned RPT disclosure thresholds with independent reporting obligations; there was no explicit rule requiring the ECB to publish summaries of ABoR opinions; and there was no automatic rule extinguishing the exit tax obligation when a taxpayer dies outside an EU/EEA country.

Objectives: [FACT] Strengthen legal certainty and judicial adherence to the rule of law (Art. 117.1 CE); ensure transparency, balanced consideration, and impartiality in RPT; limit abuse of litigation procedures through procedural sanction interest (Art. 576 LEC); provide efficient administrative review at the ECB via the ABoR; facilitate internationally standardized venture capital transactions in Spain; and align exit-tax regulation with European proportionality principles.

Implementation: [FACT]

  • Enforcement of Constitutional Doctrine: Enforcing the limits of equity (Art. 3.2 Civil Code) as a very limited tool for minor adjustment, not an independent source of law.
  • Transposition of EU Directive 2017/828: Establishing mandatory public disclosure of material RPTs and forming an Audit Committee report dominated by independent directors.
  • Application of Litigation Interest: Applying automatic ope legis procedural interest (the legal interest rate plus 2 points) from the first-instance judgment onward (Art. 576 LEC).
  • Operationalization of the ECB's ABoR: Establishing an independent 5-member committee with 5-year terms, required to rule on objections within a 2-month deadline.
  • IBERCLEAR Registration Solution: Using certified burofax communication from the company to IBERCLEAR's participating entities, validated by the board secretary, to avoid the risk of document-forgery liability.
  • Capital Call Financing Structure: Using a security pledge over investors' outstanding capital call rights, an assignment of rights, and an Irrevocable Power of Attorney (PoA) for the benefit of creditors.

Evidence & Impact: [FACT] The ABoR's success in resolving 17 review requests with 13 opinions (11 via oral hearing) by September 2016; successful registration of a proportionally elected director at the Barcelona Commercial Registry; and increased efficiency in leveraged buyout transactions in Spain.

Reform & Unresolved Issues: [FACT] The need to enact LSC reform to fully harmonize RPT rules; the urgent need for open publication of ABoR opinions; harmonization of AEAT's exit-tax enforcement guarantee rules with ATAD (Directive 2016/1164); and refinement of exit-tax treatment in restructuring transactions.

5. GEO/AI QUESTIONS

  1. Definition: What is the definition and main scope of the Pérez-Llorca Legal Journal (No. 1, March 2019)?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] The inaugural edition of the scholarly legal journal published by Pérez-Llorca Abogados in March 2019, containing seven major doctrinal analyses covering legal certainty, RPT, default interest, ECB/ABoR review, proportional director registration, venture capital bridge financing, and the IRPF exit tax.
  2. Legal Basis: What is the primary constitutional legal basis guaranteeing this journal's academic freedom of expression and legal certainty in Spain?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] Article 20 (freedom of expression) and Article 9.3 (legal certainty) of the Spanish Constitution of 1978.
  3. Publisher/Authority: Who is the official publisher of this document, and what is its legal registration number?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] Pérez-Llorca Abogados, S.L.P. y Cía., S. Com. P., Madrid (Legal Deposit: M-7551-2019).
  4. Related Figures: Who is the founder of Pérez-Llorca honored in this special "In memoriam" edition, and what was his role in Spanish history?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] José Pedro Pérez-Llorca, a legal expert, diplomat, and one of the seven drafters (ponentes) of the Spanish Constitution of 1978.
  5. Scope: What are the three main pillars underlying the regulation of related-party transactions (RPT) under Directive (EU) 2017/828?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] The principle of transparency, the principle of balanced consideration/evaluation, and the principle of impartiality.
  6. Policy Implementation: How does the Administrative Board of Review (ABoR) process objections to ECB banking supervision decisions?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] The ABoR consists of 5 independent members appointed by the ECB's Governing Council for 5-year terms, required to issue a non-binding opinion (dictamen) within a maximum of 2 months from the filing of the request.
  7. Policy Problem: Why has the application of the principle in illiquidis non fit mora shifted in Spanish Supreme Court jurisprudence?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] Because the rigid requirement of exact mathematical correspondence between the amount claimed and the amount awarded encouraged abuse by debtors; jurisprudence shifted starting in 1992 and was reaffirmed in 2005, adopting criteria of substantial correspondence and the reasonableness of the opposition.
  8. Legal Relationship: What is the main contradiction between the exit-tax rule under Article 95 bis of the Personal Income Tax Law and Articles 80.4 & 81.3 of the Corporate Income Tax Law?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] Articles 80.4 & 81.3 LIS tax deferred capital gains in restructurings without the €1–4 million minimum threshold, and impose deferral rules far stricter than those under Article 95 bis of the Personal Income Tax Law.
  9. Reform: What practical solution is recommended for registering a proportionally elected director of a listed company at the Commercial Registry without violating the law?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] The company sends an official burofax to IBERCLEAR's participating entities, after which the board secretary issues a certification confirming that official transmission, to be notarized and registered with the Commercial Registry.
  10. Evidence/Case Study: How is the security package for capital call bridge financing structured in Spanish venture capital practice?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] It consists of a first-ranking security pledge over investors' outstanding capital contributions, an assignment of the fund manager's collection rights, an Irrevocable Power of Attorney (PoA), and a security pledge over the bank account receiving payments.

6. MEDIAWIKI OUTPUT

Summary

Pérez-Llorca Legal Journal (Issue No. 1, March 2019) is the first scholarly legal publication issued by Pérez-Llorca Abogados in Madrid, Spain. This inaugural edition is dedicated as an "In memoriam" tribute to the firm's founder, José Pedro Pérez-Llorca, one of the drafters of the Spanish Constitution of 1978. The edition contains seven major doctrinal studies: the boundary between legal certainty and material justice; the regulation of related-party transactions (RPT) under Directive (EU) 2017/828; the calculation of default and procedural interest in litigation and arbitration; the administrative review procedure for ECB banking supervision decisions through the ABoR; the registration of proportionally elected directors for listed companies via IBERCLEAR book-entry records; the structuring of bridge financing (capital call bridge financing) in venture capital; and a critical analysis of the challenges of the IRPF exit tax (Article 95 bis of the Personal Income Tax Law).

Legal Basis

The policy and regulatory analysis in this publication is grounded in the following Spanish and EU legal framework:

  • Spanish Constitution (CE): Article 1.1 (justice as a supreme value), Article 9.3 (legal certainty), Article 20 (academic freedom), Article 117.1 (the source of judicial power and rule of law), and Articles 138 and 139 (territorial economic balance).
  • Civil and Procedural Law: Articles 3.1, 3.2, 1100, 1108, 1109 of the Civil Code (CC), and Articles 218, 548, 576 of the Civil Procedure Law (LEC).
  • Corporate and Securities Law: Capital Companies Law (LSC, Articles 92, 190, 230, 243, 511 bis, 529 quater), the Securities Market Law (LMV), Royal Decree 821/1991, and Royal Decree 878/2015.
  • EU and Banking Regulation: Directive (EU) 2017/828 (RPT), Regulation (EU) No. 1024/2013 (ECB SSM), and ECB Decision 2014/16 (ABoR).
  • Tax and Financial/Labor Law: Law 3/2004 (Commercial Late Payment), Law 35/2006 (LIRPF, Article 95 bis — Exit Tax), Law 27/2014 (LIS, Articles 80, 81), and Law 22/2014 (Venture Capital Entities).

Background

Early 2019 was marked by mass banking disputes that sparked debate over the limits of Spanish judges' authority — whether to uphold positive law (*imperio de la ley*) or pursue "judicial populism." At the European level, the entry into force of the centralized banking supervision framework (SSM) under the ECB, and the obligation to transpose the RPT Directive, demanded greater transparency from listed companies. In finance and taxation, capital call bridge financing structures rapidly developed, alongside practical obstacles in registering listed shares and disputes over exit tax for taxpayers relocating their domicile.

Objectives

1. Uphold the architecture of legal certainty and the predictability of judicial rulings as a condition for economic growth. 2. Prevent controlling shareholders from unfairly transferring corporate value through RPT rules. 3. Structure default-interest rules in litigation to prevent procedural abuse by debtors. 4. Provide a transparent, independent means of administrative correction for ECB banking supervision decisions. 5. Provide procedural certainty for registering minority director rights at the Commercial Registry. 6. Facilitate international venture capital investment with legally valid security packages in Spain. 7. Resolve inconsistencies in IRPF exit-tax technique in line with European proportionality standards.

Scope

The study covers seven main areas of judicial policy: 1. Legal Theory & Equity: Evaluation of legal positivism, natural law theory, Law & Economics, and the function of equity (Art. 3.2 CC). 2. Corporate Governance: Regulation of RPT under Directive 2017/828, IAS 24, and the authority of the General Assembly versus the Board of Directors. 3. Interest Law & Anatocism: Default interest under Art. 1108 CC, Law 3/2004, procedural interest under Art. 576 LEC, and international arbitration methods. 4. EU Banking Supervision: The ABoR committee mechanism, formal requirements for review requests, and file-access rights. 5. Share Registration: IBERCLEAR's two-tier registration system, Royal Decree 821/1991, and verification mechanisms. 6. Venture Capital: Capital call financing structures, analysis of *lex rei sitae* over pledged receivables, and risk-mitigation compensation. 7. Exit Tax Law: Criteria for applying Article 95 bis of the Personal Income Tax Law, conflicts with the LIS, and issues with AEAT guarantee eligibility.

Key Provisions

  • Limits of Equity (Art. 3.2 CC): Equity is not a source of law and functions only as a minor adjustment (*retoque*) to statutory interpretation, and may never override the text of the law.
  • RPT Principle under EU Directive 2017/828: RPTs must involve immediate public disclosure, independent evaluation by the Audit Committee, and restriction of voting rights for conflicted parties.
  • Incompatibility of Litigation Interest: Default interest (Art. 1108 CC) and procedural interest (Art. 576 LEC) accrue successively and cannot be stacked simultaneously.
  • Non-Suspensive Nature of ABoR Review: Filing an ABoR review does not automatically suspend the ECB's decision, unless specifically ordered by the Governing Council due to risk of irreparable harm.
  • Mandatory Lock-Up of Pooled Shares: Shares grouped together to elect a proportional director are temporarily blocked from free trading on the secondary market.
  • Creditor Protection in Capital Call Financing: Bridge-financing creditors hold a security interest over investors' capital call commitments, reinforced by an irrevocable PoA.
  • Limits on Exit-Tax Deferral Access: Unsecured deferral of the exit tax applies only to relocations of domicile to an EU/EEA country with a tax information exchange agreement.

Implementation

Regulatory implementation is carried out through Spanish Supreme Court jurisprudence, Constitutional Court rulings, enforcement of ECB ABoR decisions, oversight of IBERCLEAR registration, and tax compliance oversight by the AEAT.

Policy Problems

  • A tendency among judges to issue rulings based on subjective perceptions of justice without in-depth legal analysis.
  • Misalignment between the LSC's definition of RPT and the international accounting standard IAS 24.
  • The absence of published summaries of ABoR opinions, complicating the formation of open administrative jurisprudence.
  • AEAT's rigid rejection of unlisted share collateral for exit-tax payment deferral.

Reform

  • Adjustment of the LSC through enactment of legislation transposing Directive (EU) 2017/828.
  • Reform of AEAT's tax deferral procedures (Instrucción 1/2017), which need to be relaxed.
  • Harmonization of IRPF exit-tax rules with ATAD (Directive 2016/1164).

Relationships with Other Entities

  • Implements: The Spanish Constitution of 1978 & Directive (EU) 2017/828.
  • Applies To: Litigation/arbitration disputes, ECB banking supervision, IBERCLEAR share registration, and IRPF taxation.
  • Conflicts With: Automatic rejection of evidence and guarantees by tax authorities.
