4. POLICY ANALYSIS

Background: [FACT] Spain's legal landscape in late 2023 was marked by regulatory adjustment to international markets and modern economic transformation. Key emerging issues included the need for a specific regime protecting intangible business-secret assets following the transposition of EU Directive 2016/943; traditional banking's retreat from the tertiary property sector, fueling a surge in alternative financing (direct lending) from non-bank lenders ("Mayfair lenders"); enforcement of civil compensation mechanisms (tort liability) for pollution victims, separate from the administrative regime; pushback against false "green" marketing strategies (greenwashing); and simplification of urban planning procedures in the Community of Madrid through the Omnibus Law.

Policy Problem: [FACT]

  1. Business Secrets: The absence of a single legal definition before Law 1/2019, creating ambiguity between corporate secrecy rights and workers' rights to experience and mobility.
  2. Alternative Financing: High cost of capital, uncertain legal status of non-bank lenders under RDL 5/2005, and the prohibition on pacto comisorio in Spanish civil law, which hinders direct security enforcement (appropriation).
  3. Environmental Damage: The complexity of proving causation in cases of continuous or diffuse pollution.
  4. Greenwashing: The proliferation of vague or unverified environmental claims by companies without objective scientific evidence.
  5. Madrid Urban Planning: Slow urban plan approval processes and rigid land-use change rules under the LSCM.

Legal/Policy Gap: [FACT] Conflict between overly generic confidentiality agreements and the right to labor mobility (Art. 1.3 LSE); uncertainty over the acceptance of "double LuxCo" or dematerialized-share structures for sponsorless local Spanish investors; the absence of specific greenwashing sanction regulation by an independent Spanish regulator compared to the UK's ASA; and misalignment of environmental evaluation procedures for minor "Detail Studies" (Estudios de Detalle).

Objectives: [FACT] Provide comprehensive legal protection for business secrets through the exclusive jurisdiction of Commercial Courts; facilitate private capital access to the property sector without sacrificing consumer protection; ensure physical restoration and full compensation for civil pollution victims; require ex-ante scientific verification of all environmental product claims; and accelerate the implementation of Regional Scope Projects and urban permitting efficiency.

Implementation: [FACT]

  • Law 1/2019 on Business Secrets: Application of confidentiality-protection measures in judicial proceedings (document dissociation, closed hearings), and establishment of a damages scheme based on actual loss or hypothetical royalty (Art. 10 LSE).
  • Property Financing Structures: Use of bridge financing, mezzanine financing, super senior tranches, and integration of standard LMA agreements and make-whole clauses.
  • Environmental Litigation: Application of the "adequate causation" doctrine, presumption of negligence, and pro-victim evidentiary rules under Article 1908 of the Civil Code.
  • Greenwashing Enforcement: Application of Articles 5 and 7 of the Unfair Competition Law (LCD) by Spanish courts and adoption of the EU Taxonomy Framework.
  • Madrid Urban Reform: Application of Article 50 LSCM, reformulated by the Omnibus Law, allowing parcel use changes of up to 15% via a Special Plan.

Evidence & Impact: [FACT] Immediate application of the LSE by Spanish courts in ex-employee disputes; growth of the international direct-lending market in Spain; consolidation of Supreme Court jurisprudence confirming the strict-liability nature of pollution liability under Article 1908 CC; a Barcelona Commercial Court ruling (Brita case) annulling water-filtration advertising for misleading environmental claims; and the Constitutional Court's confirmation (STC 86/2019) of the validity of excluding environmental evaluation for Detail Studies.

Reform & Unresolved Issues: [FACT] Final approval of the Proposed Directive on Ecological Transition and the Directive on Environmental Claims in the EU; the need to broaden RDL 5/2005 criteria for enforcing non-bank share collateral in Spain; and legal certainty in applying the right to disconnection and environmental risk-burden evaluation.

  3. Publisher/Authority: Who is the official publisher of this document, and what is its legal registration number?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] Pérez-Llorca Abogados, S.L.P., Madrid (Legal Deposit: M-7551-2019, ISSN: 2792-2871).
  4. Related Figures: Who is the founder of Pérez-Llorca who directly participated in drafting the Spanish Constitution of 1978?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] José Pedro Pérez-Llorca y Rodrigo.
  5. Scope: Which court holds exclusive jurisdiction to hear business-secret infringement claims under Law 1/2019?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] The Commercial Courts.
  6. Policy Implementation: How does Article 10 of Law 1/2019 on Business Secrets govern the two alternative methods for calculating damages?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] The first method is based on calculating total actual loss (lost profits, unjust enrichment, and moral damages), while the alternative method sets compensation based on a hypothetical royalty (license fee).
  7. Policy Problem: Why did traditional banking institutions in Spain reduce property financing, opening space for alternative lending?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] Due to strict regulatory pressure following the 2008/2012 crisis, heavy capital reserve requirements, and instructions under the 2012 Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) to deleverage troubled assets.
  8. Legal Relationship: What is the main difference between environmental damages actions under Article 1908 of the Civil Code and Law 26/2007 on Environmental Liability?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] Article 1908 CC is a civil (tort) action aimed at compensating a victim's individual/patrimonial rights, while Law 26/2007 is a public/administrative mechanism requiring prevention and restoration of the environment as a public good, without requiring an individual victim.
  9. Reform: How does the EU's Proposed Directive on Environmental Claims change companies' obligations when making green product claims?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] It requires companies to conduct ex-ante evaluation and verification based on recognized scientific evidence before publishing environmental statements or using green labels.
  10. Evidence/Case Study: What key change did the Omnibus Law (Law 11/2022) introduce to the function of the "Special Plan" in the Community of Madrid?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] It allows a Special Plan to alter structural determinations (such as changing parcel use by up to 15% or increasing buildability by up to 15%) on consolidated urban land without requiring a full PGOU (General Urban Plan) revision.

6. MEDIAWIKI OUTPUT

Summary

Pérez-Llorca Legal Journal (Issue No. 10, December 2023) is a scholarly legal publication issued by Pérez-Llorca Abogados in Madrid, Spain. This edition includes an in memoriam tribute to Ramón Rodríguez Arribas (former Vice-President of the Constitutional Court), two comprehensive doctrinal studies on business secret protection under Law 1/2019 on Business Secrets (transposing EU Directive 2016/943) and the legal framework and products of alternative/direct real estate financing in Spain, and three practical analyses on civil damages claims for environmental harm under Articles 1902 and 1908 of the Civil Code, EU and Spanish regulation combating greenwashing practices, and analysis of urban planning instruments and the innovations of the Omnibus Law (Law 11/2022) in the Community of Madrid.

Legal Basis

  • Spanish Constitution (CE): Article 20 (freedom of expression and academic freedom), Article 35 (employment), Article 40 (social policy), Article 45 (environment), and Article 148.1.3 (regional land-use planning).
  • Business Secrets and Competition Law: Law 1/2019 on Business Secrets (LSE), Directive (EU) 2016/943, and Law 3/1991 on Unfair Competition (LCD, Arts. 5, 7, 13).
  • Financial and Banking Law: Royal Decree-Law 5/2005, Law 5/2019 on Real Estate Credit, the Venture Capital Law (Art. 7), and the CRR Regulation / CRD IV Directive.
  • Civil and Environmental Law: Civil Code (Arts. 1902, 1908), Law 26/2007 on Environmental Liability, the Proposed Directive on Ecological Transition, and the Proposed Directive on Environmental Claims.
  • Land Use and Urban Planning Law: Law 11/2022 on Urgent Measures (Madrid Omnibus Law), Law 9/2001 on Land Use of the Community of Madrid (LSCM), and Royal Decree 2159/1978.

Background

The December 2023 edition reflects the dynamics of Spanish legal reform in facing modern economic and sustainability challenges. The enactment of Law 1/2019 ended an era of fragmented business-secret regulation. In the property sector, traditional banking's retreat spurred the growth of high-yield private financing structures (direct lending). In the environmental field, civil protection against pollution nuisances was strengthened alongside public regulation, while the EU tightened rules to prevent misleading green claims (greenwashing). In Madrid, the need to accelerate investment drove Omnibus Law reforms to land law.

Objectives

1. Honor the jurisprudential contributions and constitutional legacy of Ramón Rodríguez Arribas. 2. Consolidate a business-secret protection system balanced with labor mobility rights. 3. Provide practical guidance on legal structures for alternative property financing (bridge, mezzanine, super senior, double LuxCo). 4. Clarify the requirements and limits of civil environmental damages claims under Article 1908 CC. 5. Map national and European legal instruments to combat greenwashing. 6. Begin simplification of urban planning instrument approval procedures in Madrid.

Scope

The study covers six main pillars: 1. In Memoriam: The judicial career of Ramón Rodríguez Arribas and the history of drafting the 1978 Constitution. 2. Business Secrets: The LSE's definition, comparison with the LCD, direct/indirect infringement actions, and damages calculation. 3. Alternative Financing: Non-bank lenders, limits under RDL 5/2005, usury analysis, and alternatives to prohibited collateral arrangements. 4. Environmental Damages: Article 1908 CC versus Law 26/2007, the adequate-causation doctrine, and presumption of negligence. 5. Greenwashing: The proposed EU Directive, Articles 5 & 7 LCD, and case studies of UK ASA sanctions (Shell, Repsol, Petronas). 6. Madrid Urban Planning: Classification of POS, PGOU, PAU, Partial Plans, Special Plans, Detail Studies, and the Omnibus Law reform.

Key Provisions

  • Business Secret Protection (Art. 1 LSE): A business secret covers technical, business, or financial information with economic value due to its secrecy, and protected by reasonable measures.
  • Worker Experience Exception (Art. 1.3 LSE): The LSE prohibits restricting labor mobility based on experience and skills honestly acquired during a professional career.
  • Characteristics of Alternative Financing: Non-bank private financing focused on the tertiary segment and high-risk investments (bridge, mezzanine) without drawing on public funds.
  • Strict Liability for Pollution (Art. 1908 CC): Jurisprudence confirms the strict/risk-based nature of liability for damage caused by smoke, explosions, and waste.
  • Greenwashing Verification Test: Environmental statements must be based on objective scientific evidence verified ex-ante.
  • Madrid Special Plan Reform (Art. 50 LSCM): Permits use changes and buildability increases of up to 15% on consolidated urban land without a PGOU revision.

Implementation

Implementation occurs through enforcement at Commercial Courts and Courts of First Instance, AEPD oversight, CNMV regulation, and Community of Madrid urban planning administration.

Policy Problems

  • Overly broad confidentiality clauses hindering labor mobility.
  • Obstacles to enforcing private share collateral in Spain due to rigid interpretation of RDL 5/2005.
  • Difficulty proving causation in cases of diffuse pollution.
  • Proliferation of environmental claims without standardized verification (greenwashing).
  • Risk of annulment of urban planning instruments due to procedural errors or publication defects.

Reform

  • Enactment of Law 1/2019 on Business Secrets.
  • Reform of the Insolvency Law (Law 16/2022) regarding interim financing.
  • Adoption of Law 11/2022 (Omnibus Law) in the Community of Madrid.
  • Projected passage of EU Directives on Ecological Transition and Green Claims.

Relationships with Other Entities

  • Implements: Law 1/2019, Directive (EU) 2016/943, RDL 5/2005, the Civil Code, the LCD, and the Omnibus Law.
  • Applies To: Commercial Courts, businesses holding trade secrets, private property financing, civil pollution actors, advertisers, and Madrid urban developers.
  • Conflicts With: Abusive confidentiality clause practices, prohibited collateral arrangements (pacto comisorio), and misleading environmental advertising.
