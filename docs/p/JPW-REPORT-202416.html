Definition

An official judicial policy research publication issued by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of the Supreme Court of Korea that analyzes the legal, constitutional, and procedural challenges posed by AI-driven automated administrative dispositions under Article 20 of the Administrative Basic Act; evaluates domestic automated systems (e.g., SAFE-i24) and foreign implementations (US COMPAS, Chinese AI surveillance); conducts a comprehensive review of US administrative deference doctrines (Chevron, Skidmore, State Farm, Overton Park); and formulates a novel 3-tier judicial review framework tailored to algorithmic administrative actions.

4. Policy Analysis

Background
Enacted on March 23, 2021, Article 20 of South Korea's Administrative Basic Act established explicit statutory authority for administrative agencies to issue dispositions using fully automated systems, provided the disposition does not involve administrative discretion. This landmark statutory shift catalyzed the deployment of AI in public administration across South Korea.

Policy Problem
The Black Box & Explainability Deficit: Deep learning and complex machine learning algorithms operate with opaque inner layers ("black box" problem), making it difficult or impossible for affected citizens — and reviewing courts — to trace how specific administrative outputs were derived.

Algorithmic Bias & Data Contamination: AI models trained on historical data risk inheriting or amplifying societal biases (e.g., racial bias in US COMPAS recidivism scores, gender bias in Amazon's recruitment AI, or Google Photos' labeling errors).

Constitutional & Statutory Friction: Fully automated AI decisions challenge traditional principles of due process (right to be heard / hearing rights under Administrative Procedure Act Arts. 21–22), non-delegation ("cyber-delegation" and "data-delegation"), clarity (명확성의 원칙), democratic legitimacy, and individual state compensation liability under State Compensation Act Arts. 2 and 5.

Legal/Policy Gap
Traditional judicial review theory in civil law jurisdictions divides administrative actions strictly into bound actions (기속행위) and discretionary actions (재량행위). When administrative agencies deploy AI, reviewing courts lack clear standards on whether to defer to algorithmic outputs, evaluate dataset quality, or conduct de novo review.

Objectives
Reconcile administrative efficiency and speed with the rule of law (법치행정), procedural due process, algorithmic transparency, and effective judicial control over AI administrative dispositions.

Implementation & Proposed Judicial Review Framework
The report formulates a 3-Tier Judicial Review Standard Framework (3단계 사법심사 모델):

  1. Tier 1 (Bound Dispositions / High Deference / 기속행위): Applied when an administrative agency operates within its area of statutory expertise, under clear statutory authority, and uses a transparent, publicly predictable algorithm (e.g., SAFE-i24 for imported food clearance). The court accords high deference to the AI disposition, focusing review on whether statutory requirements were breached and whether the output matched the published algorithm.
  2. Tier 2 (Discretionary Dispositions with Expert Agency / 재량행위): Applied when an agency operates in its expert field but under ambiguous statutory terms (involving administrative discretion or undefined legal concepts) OR where the algorithm structure is opaque. The court reviews the disposition under standard human official scrutiny — evaluating proportionality (비례원칙), equality (평등원칙), and prohibition of abuse of discretion.
  3. Tier 3 (Non-Expert Agency / Direct Review / 재량행위 / De Novo): Applied when the administrative agency lacks domain expertise in the subject matter or lacks technical competence over the deployed AI algorithm. The court accords zero deference and independently determines the legality and reasonableness of the disposition (De Novo review).

Technical Audit Criteria for Courts: When conducting judicial review of AI actions, courts should audit four technical stages: (a) Dataset Verification (data quality, completeness, and bias), (b) Labeling Verification (accuracy in supervised learning), (c) Algorithm Verification (model structure and transparency), and (d) Explanation Quality Verification (reason-giving adequacy).

Evidence & Empirical Case Studies
Domestic SAFE-i24 (전자심사 24): Deployed by MFDS on Sept 14, 2023 under Imported Food Safety Act Art. 20-2, automatically auditing ~260 parameters for repeat food imports, reducing processing time from 48 hours to under 5 minutes.

US Deference Jurisprudence: Reviewed Skidmore (1944), Overton Park (1971), State Farm (1983), Chevron (1984), Mead (2001), Barnhart (2002), and Prometheus Radio (2021) to extract judicial review principles balancing administrative expertise against arbitrary and capricious actions.

US Criminal COMPAS & State v. Loomis (Wis. 2016): Analyzed due process challenges to proprietary risk assessment algorithms; Wisconsin Supreme Court upheld COMPAS sentencing use provided written warnings regarding algorithmic risks are attached for judges.

6. MediaWiki Output

Judicial Review of AI-Based Administrative Decision Making is an official judicial policy research report published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of South Korea's Supreme Court.

Summary

This document is an official judicial policy research report published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of South Korea's Supreme Court. It comparatively and theoretically analyzes judicial review of administrative dispositions generated by artificial intelligence (AI) under Article 20 of the Administrative Basic Act. It formulates a 3-tier judicial review standard framework.

Legal Basis

The legal frameworks analyzed include:

  • Administrative Basic Act (행정기본법): Article 20 (Fully Automated Administrative Actions).
  • Special Act on Safety Management of Imported Food (수입식품안전관리 특별법): Article 20-2 (Automated Reception of Import Declarations / 전자심사 24).
  • Intelligent Informatization Framework Act (지능정보화 기본법): Article 3 and Article 5.
  • Administrative Procedure Act (행정절차법): Articles 21, 22, 23, and 24.
  • State Compensation Act (국가배상법): Article 2 and Article 5.

Background

Article 20 of the Administrative Basic Act, effective March 23, 2021, provided the legal basis for government agencies to issue fully automated administrative decisions. Systems such as SAFE-i24 (전자심사 24) cut food import inspection time from 48 hours to 5 minutes. However, the use of AI raises serious challenges regarding transparency, accountability, and citizens' right to challenge decisions.

Objectives

  1. Address the "black box" (블랙박스) problem and the lack of algorithmic transparency in administrative decisions.
  2. Establish a 3-tier judicial review framework to guide judges in examining the legality of AI-based administrative actions.

Scope

  • Domestic Case Studies: The Electronic Review 24 system (Korea's Ministry of Food and Drug Safety) and the Smart Seoul Welfare Service.
  • Foreign Comparative Study: US administrative law doctrines (Chevron, Skidmore, State Farm, Overton Park, Mead, Prometheus Radio), the COMPAS system (State v. Loomis), and AI surveillance/CCTV systems in China.

Key Provisions

The 3-Tier Judicial Review Standard Framework (3단계 사법심사 모델):

  • Tier 1 (기속행위 / High Deference): The agency has expertise, clear statutory authority, and a transparent/predictable algorithm. The court respects the decision and reviews only for legal violations and algorithm consistency.
  • Tier 2 (재량행위 / Standard Review): The agency has expertise but the statutory basis is ambiguous (discretionary), OR the algorithm is not transparent. The court reviews using standard human-official scrutiny (proportionality and equality principles).
  • Tier 3 (재량행위 / De Novo Review): The agency lacks domain expertise in AI or the subject matter. The court conducts an independent re-examination (De Novo).

Implementation

Courts perform a four-stage technical audit when examining AI decisions: (1) Dataset Verification (data quality & bias), (2) Labeling Verification, (3) Algorithm Verification, and (4) Explanation Quality Verification (explainability).

Policy Issues

  • The deep learning "black box" problem, which complicates compliance with the reason-giving obligation under Article 23 of the Administrative Procedure Act.
  • The risk of historical data bias (such as racial bias in COMPAS or gender bias in recruitment AI).

Reform / Amendments

  • Adoption of the 3-Tier Judicial Review Framework by the judiciary.
  • Amendment of the Administrative Procedure Act and alignment with Algorithmic Accountability rules.
