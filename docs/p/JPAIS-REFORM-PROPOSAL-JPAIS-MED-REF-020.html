Description / Deskripsi

Provides an exhaustive analysis of the international legal framework governing maritime crime, examining codifying conventions like UNCLOS (1982), sector-specific suppression treaties (SUA Convention, SOLAS/ISPS, MARPOL, UNTOC, 1988 UN Drug Convention, UNFSA, and FAO PSMA), mechanics of maritime law enforcement across spatial zones, evidentiary standards, digital forensics, comparative domestic benchmarks (Indonesia, Singapore, Australia, UK, US, Netherlands, Norway), and systemic challenges including the "catch and release" phenomenon, flags of convenience, and shadow fleets.

Problem / Masalah

  • JPAIS-MED-PROB-004: UNCLOS defines piracy through restrictive cumulative criteria (two-ship scenario, private ends, high seas), creating significant jurisdictional vacuums for political violence, single-ship mutinies, and armed robbery in territorial or archipelagic waters[cite: 4].
  • JPAIS-MED-PROB-005: Misalignment between high-seas interdiction authority and domestic prosecutorial capacity leads to the "Catch and Release" phenomenon, where capturing navies release armed suspects due to legislative omissions, human rights constraints, and high logistical costs[cite: 2].
  • JPAIS-MED-PROB-006: Opaque corporate structures and Flags of Convenience (FOCs) enable the operation of "Dark Fleets" or "Shadow Fleets" that engage in sanctions evasion, crude smuggling, and unauthorized transfers outside coastal state supervision[cite: 2].

Current Indonesian Practice / Praktik Indonesia Saat Ini

  • JPAIS-MED-ID-020: Indonesia enforces maritime security via Law No. 45/2009 on Fisheries, Law No. 32/2014 on Ocean Affairs, and the Penal Code (KUHP) through multi-agency commands including BAKAMLA, KKP, and TNI-AL, though inter-agency operational overlaps persist[cite: 4].

Japanese & Comparative Practice / Praktik Perbandingan

  • JPAIS-MED-JP-020: Advanced global jurisdictions (such as Australia under the Maritime Powers Act 2013, the United States via MDLEA, Singapore, and European nations) deploy consolidated statutory enforcement frameworks, universal jurisdiction enforcement, and port state control mechanisms[cite: 3].

Gap / Kesenjangan

  • JPAIS-MED-GAP-003: Areas Beyond National Jurisdiction (ABNJ) cover nearly two-thirds of oceans but suffer from weak flag-state monitoring, limited third-party interdiction powers under RFMO frameworks, and flag-consent delays[cite: 2].

Proposed Adaptation / Adaptasi Usulan

Ratify the 2005 SUA Protocols, establish expedited flag-state boarding request procedures (Article 8bis), bridge the "Catch and Release" gap via regional trial frameworks, strengthen port state enforcement under the FAO PSMA, harmonize digital evidence standards, and eliminate flag of convenience secrecy by mandating public disclosure of ultimate beneficial ownership[cite: 2].

Action / Aksi

  • JPAIS-MED-ACT-004: Implement standardized regional transfer agreements supported by international judicial capacity-building[cite: 2].
  • JPAIS-MED-ACT-005: Enforce strict port entry prohibitions and mandatory inspections for non-compliant foreign vessels under the FAO PSMA.
  • JPAIS-MED-ACT-006: Establish unified protocols for gathering and authenticating digital evidence (AIS, VMS, SAR imagery, body-worn cameras) across common law and civil law jurisdictions.

Pilot / Pilot Proyek

  • JPAIS-MED-PILOT-020: 120-day regional harmonization pilot for digital evidence admissibility and port state verification across strategic maritime transit corridors[cite: 2].

KPI / Indikator Kinerja Utama

  • JPAIS-MED-KPI-020: 100% compliance in digital evidence chain-of-custody logging; $\ge 95\$ successful prosecution conversion rate for interdicted transnational maritime crime suspects; zero uninspected high-risk vessels entering participating port states[cite: 2].

Policy Implication / Implikasi Kebijakan

Promulgate cohesive national and multilateral maritime security policies aligning domestic penal codes with extraterritorial treaty obligations under UNTOC, SUA, and UNCLOS[cite: 2].

Regulatory Implication / Implikasi Regulasi

Strengthen domestic statutory frameworks and port state control regulations to mandate ultimate beneficial ownership transparency and suppress shadow fleet operations[cite: 2].

Evidence / Bukti Empiris

  • UNCLOS Article 101 restricts piracy to high-seas two-ship scenarios, leaving territorial waters exposed to armed robbery[cite: 4].
  • Over 1,300 to 1,481 active shadow fleet vessels operate globally outside regulatory frameworks, utilizing fraudulent registries and unrated P&I insurance[cite: 2].
