The European Cooperation in Court Administration Studies Framework (EGPA Study Group & Swiss Sinergia Project) is a policy framework and international academic-practitioner network that facilitates empirical-interdisciplinary research, judicial public administration cooperation, and court management modernization through the integration of the European Group for Public Administration (EGPA) Permanent Study Group on Justice and Court Administration, the Swiss Sinergia Project (funded by the Swiss National Science Foundation), and the International Association for Court Administration (IACA) [FACT].

Summary

Published in a special issue of the International Journal for Court Administration (IJCA), this research policy framework was formulated by Philip Langbroek, Markus Zimmer, Andreas Lienhard, Luis Palma, Marco Fabri, and Daniel Kettiger. The framework marks a fundamental transition in the management of courts in Europe, moving away from the exclusive domain of rigid formalistic law toward organizational analysis, public management, and open governance (shift from law to organization and governance). Supported by the Swiss Sinergia Project, which commenced on May 1, 2012, and the EGPA network, this framework integrates interdisciplinary approaches (law, sociology, economics, psychology, history, and political science) to generate practical solutions for judicial workloads.

Background

Since the mid-1990s, democracies in Europe have experienced a fundamental shift in which courts are no longer viewed merely as closed legal forums, but rather as public institutions that must be accountable and transparent. However, empirical data-driven research on judicial administration in Europe has lagged significantly behind the United States, Canada, and Australia. In 1999, the EGPA initiated a judicial administration study group in Bergen, which was subsequently strengthened by the disbursement of research funds for the Swiss Sinergia Project by the Swiss National Science Foundation in May 2012.

Objectives

1. Expand the research network of judicial administration academics and practitioners across Europe and internationally. 2. Encourage empirical and interdisciplinary research to comprehend the complexities of court governance. 3. Facilitate a platform for the exchange of experiences among judges, court administrators, and academic researchers through the IACA and EGPA platforms. 4. Develop integrated management models to enhance judicial efficiency without compromising the enforcement of the rule of law.

Scope

This policy and research network encompasses various prominent institutions in Europe:

  • Center of Competence for Public Management (Univ. of Bern, Switzerland): The lead institution for the Sinergia project, working in collaboration with the Universities of Zurich, Lucerne, St. Gallen, and IDHEAP.
  • Montaigne Centre (Utrecht Law School, Netherlands): A primary research partner in European judicial administration.
  • Italian & Spanish Research Centers: IRSIG-CNR (Bologna), Research Centre for Judicial Studies (Bologna), Observatory on Justice (Coimbra, Portugal), and the Institute of Law and Technology (UAB Barcelona).
  • Menu For Justice Project: Involves over 30 institutes researching the training needs of judges and lawyers in Europe.

Key Provisions

  • Two Facets of Judicial Independence: Differentiates between the individual judge's independence in deciding cases without intimidation (adjudicative independence) and the institutional autonomy of the court itself (institutional autonomy).
  • Absolute Interdisciplinary Approach: Studying the judiciary from a purely legal angle is insufficient; it must incorporate sociological, macroeconomic, psychological, historical, and political science perspectives.
  • Courts as Complex Public Organizations: Adopts Willoughby's (1929) concept that courts are complex public organizations that share characteristics with other public institutions.
  • Pragmatic Compromise of Values: Resolves conflicts between management efficiency values and the administration of justice flexibly and pragmatically, avoiding rigid artificial logic.

Implementation

  • EGPA Conference in Bergen (September 2012): Served as a presentation platform for European judicial administration research papers.
  • Publication of the IJCA Special Issue (2013): Presented 10 research articles from authors across 7 countries (Australia, Switzerland, Italy, France, Finland, Norway, USA).
  • Support from International Volunteer Teams: Involved teams of proofreaders and editors from Australia, Ireland, Brazil, the UK, Germany, and the US under the direction of Technical Editor Linda Wade-Bahr.

Policy Problems

  • Isolation of Formalist Legal Traditions: The limitation of judicial studies due to the tendency of legal academics to separate courts from public administration science.
  • Risk of Political Oversight: The potential disruption of judicial independence when executive political authorities hold control over court budgets and management.

Reform / Change

  • Modernization of judicial management practices as an absolute necessity for the future.
  • Integration of academic platforms (EGPA/IJCA) with the operational needs of court practitioners in the field.
