Overview

Supreme Court Decision No. 110 K/AG/2007 dated December 7, 2007, establishes fundamental jurisprudence and legal rules regarding child custody (*hadhanah*): The primary consideration in matters of *hadhanah* is the welfare and best interests of the child, rather than solely determining who is normatively most entitled. Even though a child has not reached the age of 7 (seven) years or *mumayyiz* (which normatively under Article 105 letter a of the KHI falls under the mother's custody), if the mother frequently travels abroad making it unclear who will care for the child, whereas the child has been proven to live peacefully and securely with the father, then for the welfare of the child, the right of *hadhanah* is awarded to the father.

Case Background

The Original Plaintiff / Respondent for Cassation ([Mother]) and Original Defendant / Petitioner for Cassation ([Father]) married on October 23, 1999, in Bandung and were blessed with a daughter named [Child] (born [details removed]). Continuous disputes occurred during roughly the last two years of the marriage. The Plaintiff filed a divorce suit and a claim for child custody (*hadhanah*) at the South Jakarta Religious Court.

The South Jakarta Religious Court (Decision No. 50/Pdt.G/2006/PA.JS dated July 27, 2006) granted the divorce with a single *ba'in shughra* pronouncement and awarded child custody (*hadhanah*) to the Defendant ([Father]). The Jakarta High Religious Court (Decision No. 105/Pdt.G/2006/PTA.JK dated December 20, 2006) annulled the South Jakarta Religious Court decision and designated the Plaintiff ([Mother]) as the holder of child custody. Dissatisfied with the appellate ruling, the Defendant filed a cassation petition to the Supreme Court.

Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

  • Ratio Decidendi:
  1. Regarding child custody, the inquiry is not merely who holds the primary entitlement, but rather whose care minimizes detriment to the child; priority must be given to the child's interests above all else.
  2. Based on facts revealed by the First-Instance Judge, the child would suffer more if placed under the mother's care because the mother frequently travels abroad without a clear arrangement for the child's supervision, whereas the child currently lives calmly and securely with the father (Petitioner for Cassation / Defendant).
  3. Even though the child is placed under the father's custody, this must not sever communication with the mother (Respondent for Cassation / Plaintiff); the mother retains the right to visit, assist in upbringing, and bestow affection upon the child.
  • Ruling: Granting the cassation petition from Petitioner for Cassation [Father]; Annulling the Jakarta High Religious Court decision dated December 20, 2006, No. 105/Pdt.G/2006/PTA.JK, which had annulled the South Jakarta Religious Court decision dated July 27, 2006, No. 50/Pdt.G/2006/PA.JS; and Independently Adjudicating: (1) In Provision: Declaring the Plaintiff's provision request inadmissible; (2) In Convention: In Exception: Rejecting the Defendant's exceptions in their entirety; In the Principal Case: 1. Granting the Plaintiff's lawsuit in part; 2. Pronouncing a single *ba'in shughra* divorce of Defendant ([Father]) against Plaintiff ([Mother]); 3. Declaring the Plaintiff's claim under item 4 concerning child maintenance costs inadmissible; 4. Rejecting the remainder of the Plaintiff's lawsuit; (3) In Reconvention: 1. Granting the Plaintiff-in-reconvention's lawsuit in part; 2. Determining that the child named [Child], born [details removed], is placed under the custody (*hadhanah*) of the Plaintiff-in-reconvention ([Father]); 3. Ordering the Plaintiff-in-reconvention to provide the Defendant-in-reconvention, as her biological mother, the opportunity to meet and be with the child during school holidays or mutually agreed days; 4. Rejecting the remainder of the Plaintiff-in-reconvention's lawsuit; (4) In Convention and Reconvention: Assessing court costs in accordance with judicial level provisions.
