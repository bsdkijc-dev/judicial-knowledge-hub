JPW-POL-ILJ-001

By Cecep Mustafa, Indonesian Judicial College (IJC) · revision 1312, 2026-09-17 · wiki source · Markdown

The Italian Legal Imaginary and Journal Policy Framework refers to the editorial, epistemological, and comparative policy structure developed by The Italian Law Journal (ItALJ) to critically analyze, unpack, and disseminate the Italian legal tradition within global legal discourse 1.

Lead

Founded in 2014 by Camilla Crea and a group of Italian civil and comparative law scholars, The Italian Law Journal serves as an open-access, peer-reviewed e-journal using English as a lingua franca 2. Grounded in the post-WWII constitutionalization of private law under the 1947 Italian Constitution and the rise of comparative legal studies, the framework seeks to liberate legal thought from classic formalism and national narcissism 3.

Summary

The framework addresses two major epistemological turns in Italian legal history:

  1. National Constitutionalization of Private Law: Infusing the 1942 Civil Code with constitutional values—notably human dignity, personalism, substantive equality (Art. 3(2)), and solidarity—transforming private law from economic concerns toward the human person 4.
  2. Rise of Comparative Legal Studies: Breaking the isolation of domestic law through revolutionary methodologies, including Gino Gorla's case method, Mauro Cappelletti's functionalism, and Rodolfo Sacco's theory of legal formants and cryptotypes [^5].

Legal Basis

The policy framework operates at the intersection of:

  • Italian Constitution (1947): Articles 2 (inviolable rights), 3 (formal and substantive equality), and 42 (social function of property) 5.
  • Civil Code of 1942: Re-evaluated through constitutionally-oriented interpretation and general clauses (good faith, social utility, injustice of harm) 6.
  • Ministerial Decrees of 1999 and 2005: Establishing comparative law as an autonomous and mandatory course in Italian law school curricula 7.

Background

Historically, Italian legal scholarship was constrained by a stereotype of rigid positivism and dogmatics, labeled by John Henry Merryman as the "Italian Style" 8. Earlier benchmarks, such as Alessandro Pizzorusso's Italian Studies in Law (1992) and Giacinto della Cananea's Italian Journal of Public Law (2009), paved the way for ItALJ's focus on private law as a social engineering tool 9.

Objectives

  • Make the Italian legal tradition visible and accessible to the international community in English 10.
  • Foster bidirectional critical dialogue without sacred texts or uncritical mythologies 11.
  • Promote interdisciplinary openness connecting private law with history, economics, sociology, and philosophy 12.

Scope

Applies to international legal scholarship, private law reform, comparative jurisprudence, legal education, and judicial interpretation across continental Europe, the Americas, and global legal systems 13.

Key Provisions & Formants

  • Theory of Legal Formants: Dissecting legal systems into statutory, judicial, and doctrinal formants, along with unexpressed "cryptotypes" 14.
  • Indirect Drittwirkung: Applying constitutional fundamental rights directly to horizontal relationships between private parties 15.
  • Alternative Use of Law: Utilizing constitutional interpretation to advance social justice and protect disadvantaged social actors 16.

Implementation

Published bi-annually under the auspices of the Università degli Studi del Sannio and sponsored by the Harvard Italian Law Association (HILA), SISDIC, SIRD, and NIABA 22.

Policy Problems

  • Linguistic Periphery: Esoteric Italian legal terminology and out-of-print, expensive English translations of the Italian Civil Code 23.
  • Disciplinary Silos: Rigid academic fragmentation between public, private, and comparative law departments 24.

Reform

Scholars advocate translating primary Italian judicial decisions into English and expanding trans-systemic legal education programs 25.
