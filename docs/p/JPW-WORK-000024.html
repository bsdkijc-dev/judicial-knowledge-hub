4. POLICY ANALYSIS

  • Background: [FACT] Prior to the enactment of Law 1/2019 (LSE), Spain lacked a dedicated statute defining and comprehensively regulating business secrets. Protection was heterogeneously fragmented across the Unfair Competition Law (Art. 13 LCD), Workers' Statute, Patent Law, and Criminal Code (Arts. 278–280). The absence of a statutory definition generated heavy reliance on civil-commercial case law doctrine and Article 39 of the WTO TRIPS Agreement.
  • Policy Problem: [FACT]
    • Legal Fragmentation & Ambiguity: Marked disparities across EU member states regarding trade secret protection (*secretos comerciales/empresariales*), which discouraged cross-border R&D and innovation investment.
    • Unclear Employee Mobility Boundaries: Overly generic, all-encompassing, and indefinite confidentiality clauses used by employers to restrict labor mobility and forbid workers from utilizing honestly acquired professional experience and know-how.
    • Evidentiary Challenges in Damages: Complexities in measuring actual economic loss, unjust enrichment (*enriquecimiento injusto*), and moral/reputational damage (*daño moral*) resulting from trade secret unlawful acquisition, use, or disclosure.
  • Legal/Policy Gap: [FACT] Absence of specialized procedural rules to prevent trade secrets from being publicly disclosed during litigation, and lack of clarity regarding transitional rules for infringements originating before the LSE's entry into force.
  • Objectives: [FACT] Provide a comprehensive protective regime (*régimen integral*) from both substantive and procedural viewpoints; transpose EU Directive 2016/943; establish the LSE as *lex specialis* over the LCD; strike a balance between employer IP rights and worker mobility rights; and concentrate judicial jurisdiction exclusively within Commercial Courts (*Juzgados de lo Mercantil*).
  • Implementation: [FACT]
    • Trade Secret Criteria (Art. 1 LSE): Information must cumulatively fulfill three requirements: (1) secret/not easily accessible, (2) possess commercial value because it is secret, and (3) subject to reasonable security measures by its holder.
    • Worker Protection (Art. 1.3 LSE): Prohibits invoking the LSE to restrict employee mobility or ban the use of skills and experience honestly acquired during a professional career.
    • Direct vs. Indirect Infringement (Art. 3 LSE): Direct infringement is measured objectively without requiring intent to profit/harm (*ánimo de lucro/perjuicio*). Indirect infringement establishes a cognitive standard (*knew or ought to have known*) regarding the unlawful source from a third party.
    • Fact-Checking Measures (Art. 17 LSE): Application of pre-suit fact-checking proceedings (*diligencias de comprobación de hechos*) referencing procedures under Patent Law 24/2015.
    • Confidentiality in Litigation (Art. 50 LSE): Empowers judges to restrict document access, conduct closed-door hearings, and issue redacted non-confidential versions of rulings.
    • Quantification of Damages (Art. 10 LSE): Trade secret holders can choose between calculating total actual loss (*Differenztheorie*: actual loss, lost profits, unjust enrichment, moral damage, investigation costs) or an alternative hypothetical royalty rate (*daño normativo*).
  • Evidence & Impact: [FACT] Immediate enforcement by Spanish Commercial Courts in resolving trade secret disputes (e.g., rulings by Commercial Court No. 3 Madrid, No. 7 Barcelona, and Provincial Courts of Barcelona/Vizcaya); strict enforcement of the burden of proof regarding specific confidential information; and dismissal of claims where employers rely solely on boilerplate confidentiality clauses without demonstrating concrete secret information.
  • Reform & Unresolved Issues: [FACT] Further unification of case law concerning the exact boundary between employee professional experience and employer trade secrets; procedural coordination regarding joinder of related claims (*acumulación de acciones*) across Civil Courts of First Instance and Commercial Courts following Organic Law 7/2022 reforms; and procedural handling of cross-border ongoing infringements post-LSE transitional provisions.

5. GEO/AI QUESTIONS

  1. Definition: What is the official statutory definition of a business secret (*secreto empresarial*) under Article 1 of Spanish Law 1/2019?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] A business secret is any information or knowledge (including technological, scientific, industrial, commercial, organizational, or financial) that is secret, holds actual or potential commercial value because it is secret, and has been subjected to reasonable measures by its holder to maintain its secrecy.
  2. Legal Basis: Which European Union legal instrument was transposed into Spanish domestic law through the enactment of Law 1/2019 on Business Secrets?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] Directive (EU) 2016/943 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 8 June 2016.
  3. Publisher/Authority: Which judicial bodies hold exclusive objective jurisdiction (*competencia objetiva*) to hear civil claims and applications regarding business secret infringements in Spain?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] Commercial Courts (*Juzgados de lo Mercantil*) pursuant to Articles 86 bis and ter of the LOPJ and Article 14 of the LSE.
  4. Related Figures: Who are the two Pérez-Llorca legal experts who authored this doctrinal analysis on Law 1/2019?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] Antoni Frigola (Of Counsel) and Ana Parés (Associate).
  5. Scope: Can the skills and experience acquired by an employee during their employment be classified as an employer's business secret?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] No. Article 1.3 of the LSE explicitly excludes experience and skills honestly acquired by employees during their professional career from the definition of business secrets to protect labor mobility.
  6. Policy Implementation: What is the main distinction between direct infringement (*infracción directa*) and indirect infringement (*infracción indirecta*) under Article 3 of Law 1/2019?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] Direct infringement occurs when a secret is unlawfully acquired, used, or disclosed directly from the holder on an objective basis without requiring proof of intent to profit or cause harm. Indirect infringement occurs when a secret is acquired via a third party and requires establishing that the infringer knew or ought to have known that the third party acted unlawfully.
  7. Policy Problem: Why do Spanish courts frequently reject claims based on all-encompassing, time-indefinite confidentiality agreements (*pactos de confidencialidad*)?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] Because vague or overly broad clauses fail to specify the precise confidential information being protected, making it impossible to verify the existence of a genuine business secret while unlawfully infringing upon worker mobility rights.
  8. Legal Relationship: What is the legal hierarchy/relationship between Law 1/2019 (LSE) and Unfair Competition Law 3/1991 (LCD)?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] Law 1/2019 acts as a special law (*ley especial*), whereas Law 3/1991 (LCD) functions as a general supplementary law (*ley general y supletoria*).
  9. Reform & Procedure: Which statutory regulation is referenced by Article 17 of the LSE for conducting pre-suit fact-checking proceedings (*diligencias de comprobación de hechos*)?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] Patent Law 24/2015 (specifically Chapter II of Title XII / Article 123 LP).
  10. Evidence/Calculation of Damages: What are the two alternative methods for calculating monetary damages (*indemnización de daños y perjuicios*) under Article 10 of Law 1/2019?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] The first method calculates total economic loss (actual loss, lost profits, unjust enrichment, moral damage, and investigation costs). The alternative method (*daño normativo*) sets compensation as a hypothetical royalty fee (*cánones o derechos de licencia*) that the infringer would have paid under a license agreement.

6. MEDIAWIKI OUTPUT

Summary

Protection of Business Secrets under Law 1/2019 (A propósito de la protección de los secretos empresariales en la Ley 1/2019, de 20 de febrero: aspectos sustantivos y procesales) (Stable ID: JPW-WORK-000024) is a doctrinal legal study authored by Antoni Frigola and Ana Parés of Pérez-Llorca. Published in Revista Jurídica Pérez-Llorca (Issue No. 10, December 2023), the work analyzes the substantive and procedural frameworks of Law 1/2019 on Business Secrets (LSE), which transposed EU Directive 2016/943 into Spanish law. The study details the three cumulative criteria of a trade secret, protections for employee mobility, direct vs. indirect infringement criteria, exclusive jurisdiction of Commercial Courts (*Juzgados de lo Mercantil*), pre-litigation fact-checking measures (*diligencias de comprobación de hechos*), courtroom confidentiality safeguards, and dual methods for quantifying monetary damages.

Legal Basis

The policy and statutory analyses presented in this publication are grounded in Spanish national and European Union legal frameworks:

  • Spanish Constitution (CE): Article 20 (freedom of expression and academic publication).
  • Primary Business Secret Legislation: Law 1/2019 on Business Secrets (LSE) and Directive (EU) 2016/943 of the European Parliament and of the Council.
  • Unfair Competition & Industrial Property Law: Unfair Competition Law 3/1991 (LCD Art. 13), Patent Law 24/2015 (LP Arts. 73 & 123), and WTO TRIPS Agreement (Art. 39).
  • Procedural & Judicial Statutes: Organic Law 6/1985 on the Judiciary (LOPJ Arts. 86 bis & ter), Civil Procedure Law 1/2000 (LEC Arts. 249.1.4º & 283 bis b), and Organic Law 7/2022.
  • Employment & Criminal Law: Recast Workers' Statute (ET Arts. 5 & 20) and Criminal Code (Arts. 278–280).

Background

Prior to the enactment of Law 1/2019, Spain lacked a single unified law governing trade secrets. Statutory provisions were fragmented across unfair competition rules, employment laws, and criminal codes. Transposition of Directive (EU) 2016/943 via the LSE established legal certainty and designated the LSE as *lex specialis* for business secret protection in Spain.

Objectives

1. Map the unified statutory definition and protection criteria for trade secrets in Spain. 2. Safeguard worker mobility rights and prevent the abuse of overly broad confidentiality agreements. 3. Systematize the legal criteria for direct (*infracción directa*) and indirect (*infracción indirecta*) infringements. 4. Confirm the exclusive objective jurisdiction of Commercial Courts (*Juzgados de lo Mercantil*) over trade secret disputes. 5. Provide procedural safeguards to maintain document confidentiality throughout litigation. 6. Detail the dual valuation methodologies for calculating actual damages and hypothetical royalties.

Scope

The analysis covers five core operational areas: 1. Substantive Requirements: Three cumulative criteria for trade secret status (secrecy, commercial value, reasonable security measures) and worker mobility protections under Art. 1.3 LSE. 2. Infringement Typologies: Objective direct infringement vs. cognitive indirect infringement standards (*knew or ought to have known*). 3. Procedural Rules: Commercial Court jurisdiction, fact-checking measures (*diligencias de comprobación de hechos*) under Patent Law, and ordinary civil trials (*juicio ordinario*). 4. Litigation Confidentiality: Article 50 LSE measures (document restrictions, closed hearings, redacted public judgments). 5. Damages Valuation: Actual loss breakdown (*Differenztheorie*) vs. normative hypothetical royalty rate (*daño normativo*).

Key Provisions

  • Three Cumulative Criteria (Art. 1 LSE): A trade secret must be secret, possess actual or potential commercial value, and be subject to reasonable protective measures.
  • Worker Mobility Safeguard (Art. 1.3 LSE): Skills, talent, and professional experience honestly acquired by workers belong to the worker and cannot be restricted as employer trade secrets.
  • Direct Infringement (Arts. 3.1 & 3.2 LSE): Applied on an objective basis without requiring proof of intent to gain profit or cause harm (*ánimo de lucro/perjuicio*).
  • Indirect Infringement (Art. 3.3 LSE): Requires proving that the third-party recipient knew or ought to have known that the trade secret was obtained unlawfully.
  • Fact-Checking Proceedings (Art. 17 LSE): Applicants must provide prima facie evidence of infringement (*presumible infracción*) to inspect an alleged infringer's premises.
  • Hypothetical Royalty (Art. 10.1 LSE): Alternative damages benchmark calculated on the licensing fee that would have been due had authorization been requested.

Implementation

Implementation is observed through judicial enforcement in Commercial Courts, application of ordinary civil trial procedures (*juicio ordinario*), pre-litigation fact-checking orders, and active courtroom confidentiality management under Article 50 of the LSE.

Policy Problems

  • Boilerplate confidentiality clauses in employment contracts failing judicial scrutiny due to lack of specificity.
  • Difficulties faced by employers in distinguishing proprietary company secrets from employee professional experience.
  • Evidentiary hurdles in proving unjust enrichment (*enriquecimiento injusto*) during damages quantification.

Reform

  • Enactment of Law 1/2019 on Business Secrets (LSE).
  • Enactment of Organic Law 7/2022 amending the LOPJ and LEC to streamline joinder of related claims in Commercial Courts.

Relationships with Other Entities

  • Implements: Directive (EU) 2016/943 & Law 1/2019 on Business Secrets.
  • Amends / Acts as Lex Specialis to: Unfair Competition Law 3/1991 (LCD Art. 13).
  • Applies to: Commercial Courts (*Juzgados de lo Mercantil*), business enterprises, trade secret holders, and employees.
