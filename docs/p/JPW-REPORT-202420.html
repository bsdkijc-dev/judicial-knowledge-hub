Definition

An official judicial policy research publication issued by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of the Supreme Court of Korea that conducts an empirical and comparative analysis of criminal service of process (형사송달); diagnoses systemic causes of service failure, trial delays, and illegal public notice reversals (위법한 공시송달); evaluates international models across 5 jurisdictions (US, Germany, Austria, Switzerland, Japan); and formulates comprehensive statutory and administrative reforms, including criminal e-service optimization, mobile notice integration (모바일 전자고지), integrated service (통합송달), court police establishment (법원경찰제도), and reformed service report guidelines.

4. Policy Analysis

Background
Service of process (송달) acts as the foundational wheel of judicial proceedings, guaranteeing litigants' constitutional right to a fair trial by informing them of pending actions and enabling defense rights. In criminal cases, service failure creates severe bottlenecks, forcing trial postponements, enabling in absentia trials (불출석 재판), triggering improper detention or bench warrants, and causing witness non-appearance.

Policy Problem
High Service Failure Rates: Out of 2,690,448 total service attempts in Korean criminal courts in 2023, 452,377 failed (16.8% overall failure rate; rising to 24.1% in summary/약식 cases and 38.3% for witness subpoenas).

Predominance of Closed-Door Absenteeism: 71.1% of all service failures stem from 폐문부재 (door locked / occupant absent during daytime mail delivery), reflecting modern demographic shifts toward single-person households (34.5% in 2022) and daytime employment.

Over-Reliance on Traditional Mail: Mail service accounts for 95.8% of all criminal service attempts, while court bailiff service (집행관 송달) accounts for 2.8%, dispatch service (발송송달) 1.0%, and court constable service (법원경위 송달) only 0.5%.

Rampant Illegal Public Notice Reversals: Because public notice service (공시송달) requirements under Special Expedited Act Article 23 require exhaustive searches of all addresses and phone numbers in the record, lower courts frequently issue invalid public notice orders. An empirical review of 361 Supreme Court decisions identified 161 cases reversed specifically because lower courts failed to call phone numbers or check secondary addresses listed in prosecution records.

Administrative Overload: 42.7% of court clerks (실무관) report spending over 50% of their total working hours on service tasks (printing documents, manual mail envelope stuffing, and address checking).

Legal/Policy Gap
Criminal E-Document Act Constraints: The Criminal E-Document Act (형사절차전자문서법, effective Oct 20, 2024) mandates user consent for e-service. However, consent obtained during police/prosecution investigation does NOT automatically transfer to court proceedings, forcing courts to revert to paper mail for initial indictment service (공소장부본). Furthermore, the statutory 14-day deemed service period (송달간주기간) is 2–3 times longer than paper mail delivery (3.5–5.7 days), risking strategic trial delays by evasive defendants.

Lack of Court Enforcement Power: Courts face uncooperative police support when executing bench warrants (구인장) or location searches (소재탐지촉탁), resulting in months of idle case suspension.

Objectives
Elevate service success rates, eliminate structural trial delays, transition to secure digital delivery while accommodating low-literacy groups, prevent invalid public notice reversals, and empower courts with independent enforcement tools.

Implementation & Proposed Statutory Reforms
Mobile Electronic Notification (모바일 전자고지) & Mobile Location Change Reporting: Implement Connecting Information (CI) code matching via mobile document brokers (Kakao/SMS) to notify defendants of pending service on smartphones before postal dispatch. Introduce a Mobile Location Change Reporting System (모바일 송달장소 신고제도) via court portals or HomeTax-style mobile forms.

Integrated Service (통합송달) Expansion: Extend civil integrated service (통합송달 — combining 1 day, 1 night, and 1 holiday attempt within 20 days) to criminal cases and expand execution authority to court constables (법원경위).

Court Police System (법원경찰제도) Enactment: Amend Article 55-2 of the Court Organization Act to establish an independent Court Police Force (법원경찰대) under judicial command to execute bench warrants, perform location searches (소재탐지), and deliver urgent subpoenas.

Pre-Public Notice Prosecution Location Data Submission: Amend Article 43 of the Criminal Procedure Rules to require prosecutors to submit a final location data report (소재파악 필요자료) from unadmitted investigation files immediately before a judge issues a public notice order.

Reformed Service Report Formats: Modify official service report templates (송달사유통지서/송달사유보고서) to include explicit mandatory checkboxes verifying supplementary service validity (보충송달 적법성 — verifying co-residence and contactability).

Evidence & Empirical Data
Judicial Survey of Judges & Clerks: Nationwide survey of 325 judges and 218 court clerks found:

  • 88.3% of judges supported shortening the 6-month statutory waiting period for public notice service under Special Expedited Act Art. 23 (37.3% favoring 2–3 months, 36.9% favoring 3–4 months).
  • 88.3% of judges supported requiring prosecutors to submit location records prior to public notice orders.
  • 81.8% of judges supported establishing a Court Police Force (법원경찰제도) for bench warrant execution and location searches.
  • 94.5% of clerks reported that service manufacturing (E-Post/mail stuffing) is their most labor-intensive task.

Summary

This document is an official judicial policy research report published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of South Korea's Supreme Court. It empirically and comparatively analyzes criminal service of process (형사송달), diagnoses the main causes of service failure (송달불능) that drive trial delays (재판지연), and formulates reforms covering criminal e-litigation (형사전자소송), mobile electronic notification (모바일 전자고지), integrated service (통합송달), establishment of a Court Police Force (법원경찰제도), and improved service report forms (송달보고서).

Legal Basis

The legal frameworks analyzed include:

  • Criminal Procedure Code of the Republic of Korea (형사소송법): Articles 25, 27, 54, 60, 61, 62, 63 (Public Notice Requirements), 64, 65, 76, 81 (Warrant Execution), 151, 152, 266, 344, 345–348, 365, 448, 452, 458.
  • Civil Procedure Act of the Republic of Korea (민사소송법 — applied mutatis mutandis): Articles 98, 99, 101, 102, 105, 106 (Supplementary Service), 107, 108, 109, 173, 175, 178, 181, 182, 183, 186, 187, 190, 191, 193, 195.
  • Act on Special Cases Concerning Expedition of Legal Proceedings (소송촉진 등에 관한 특례법 / 소촉법): Article 21, Article 23 & Special Rules Articles 18, 19.
  • Act on the Use of Electronic Documents in Criminal Justice Procedures (형사절차전자문서법 — effective October 20, 2024): Articles 2, 6(3), 13, 14, 15 & Supreme Court Rule (형사전자문서규칙) Articles 4, 16, 23, 24, 25, 26, 27.
  • Court Organization Act (법원조직법): Proposed Article 55-2 (Court Police) and Article 64(3) (Court Constables / 법원경위).

Background

In 2023, out of 2,690,448 total service attempts in Korean criminal courts, 452,377 failed (an overall failure rate of 16.8%; 24.1% in summary cases; and 38.3% for witness subpoenas). 71.1% of failures were caused by 폐문부재 (locked doors / occupant absent during the day). Heavy reliance on physical mail (95.8%) and a high rate of Supreme Court reversals due to illegal public notice service (위법한 공시송달 — 161 cases reversed) drove the need for systemic reform.

Objectives

  1. Increase the success rate of criminal service of process and eliminate unnecessary trial delays.
  2. Build mobile notification infrastructure (Mobile CI Notification) and a self-service mobile location-change reporting system (모바일 송달장소 신고제도).
  3. Establish a Court Police Force (법원경찰제도) under judicial authority to execute bench warrants (구인장) and conduct location searches (소재탐지).

Scope

  • Empirical Study: Statistical analysis for 2018–2023, review of 161 Supreme Court reversal cases, and a nationwide survey of 325 judges and 218 court clerks.
  • Five-Country Comparative Study: United States (FRCP Rule 4/49 & Process Server System), Germany (StPO § 37, ZPO § 173 & Mein Justizpostfach), Austria (Ediktsdatei & JustizOnline), Switzerland (justitia.swiss / Justitia 4.0), and Japan (Criminal Procedure Rules & 2023 IT Digitization).

Key Provisions

  • Strengthened Mobile Notification: Integrates SMS/Kakao-CI systems with the court portal and enables emergency address-change registration via mobile phone.
  • Adoption of Integrated Service (통합송달): Extends integrated service (combining daytime, nighttime, and holiday delivery attempts) to criminal cases and grants execution authority to court constables (법원경위).
  • Strict Pre-Public Notice Review: Requires prosecutors to submit defendant location records (수사기록 소재자료) immediately before a judge issues a public notice order (공시송달).
  • New Report Forms: Revises service report forms (송달보고서) to require verification of the validity of supplementary service (보충송달 — confirming co-residence and contactability with family members or coworkers).

Implementation

Courts adopt an automated checklist within the judicial computer system to prevent invalid public notice orders (공시송달), and establish a Court Police unit (법원경찰대) to independently carry out field enforcement.

Policy Issues

  • The 14-day deemed service period (송달간주) under the Criminal E-Document Act is far longer than paper mail delivery (3.5–5.7 days), risking exploitation by defendants seeking to delay proceedings.
  • Difficulty transferring e-service consent from the police/prosecution investigation stage to the court trial stage.

Reform / Amendments

  1. Enactment of Article 55-2 of the Court Organization Act (법원경찰제도).
  2. Amendment of Article 43 of the Criminal Procedure Rules (requiring prosecutors to submit location records before public notice).
  3. Amendment of Article 117 of the Criminal Procedure Rules (requiring defendants' mobile numbers and email addresses to be included in indictments).
  4. Adoption of an e-Post system and integration of Integrated Service (통합송달).
