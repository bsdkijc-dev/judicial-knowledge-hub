Overview

Supreme Court Judicial Review Decision No. 27 PK/Pid/2003 establishes fundamental jurisprudence and legal standards regarding evidentiary standing in criminal charges of unfair competition (*cybersquatting* over domain names) and the limits of evidentiary admissibility. The Supreme Court affirmed that testimony regarding elements of deceiving the public or individuals whose statements were never directly heard before the trial court, but rather acquired secondhand from other witnesses receiving information or faxes, constitutes *Testimonium De Auditu* and consequently cannot be utilized as a valid legal instrument of proof.

Case Background

The Defendant, [Defendant] (General Manager of International Marketing at PT Martina Bertho), was charged with registering the domain name Mustika-Ratu.Com via Network Solutions in the United States on October 7, 1999. This registration was accused of constituting unfair competition (Article 382 bis of the KUHP) and obstructing PT Mustika Ratu Tbk from conducting electronic commerce transactions with prospective overseas partners.

The Central Jakarta District Court (Decision No. 1075/PID.B/2001/PN.JKT.PST dated December 11, 2001) acquitted the Defendant of all charges (*vrijspraak*). However, at the cassation level (Decision No. 1082 K/Pid./2002 dated January 24, 2003), the Supreme Court overturned the district court ruling and sentenced the Defendant to 4 months of imprisonment under Article 382 bis of the KUHP. The Defendant subsequently filed a Petition for Judicial Review (*Peninjauan Kembali / PK*) on the grounds of manifest error by the cassation judges, who improperly relied on secondhand testimony from witnesses who heard rumors from prospective partners in Saudi Arabia and Malaysia (*testimonium de auditu*) alongside unverified fax evidence lacking original documentation.

Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

  • Ratio Decidendi:
  1. The *judex facti* and cassation judgments committed an error in law within their legal reasoning by treating proof regarding the element of deceiving the public or an individual whose statement was never directly heard in court—where testimony was instead heard secondhand from others—as valid evidence. Such testimony must be categorized as *Testimonium De Auditu* and is therefore legally inadmissible as evidence.
  2. Fax communications originating from prospective partners in Saudi Arabia and Malaysia that were never presented as original documents, never confirmed through the Indonesian Embassy or Consulate General, and whose primary witnesses were never presented in court possess no valid probative value. Consequently, a manifest error/mistake (*kekhilafan/kekeliruan yang nyata* pursuant to Article 263 paragraph (2) letter c of the KUHAP) infected the prior cassation decision.
  • Ruling: Granting the Petition for Judicial Review from Petitioner for Judicial Review [Defendant]; Annulling the Supreme Court decision dated January 24, 2003, No. 1082 K/Pid./2002; and Independently Adjudicating: (1) Declaring that the Convict is not legally and convincingly proven guilty of committing the criminal offense as charged by the Prosecutor / Public Prosecutor; (2) Acquitting the Convict of all charges; (3) Restoring the Convict's rights in capacity, position, honor, and dignity (rehabilitation); (4) Charging the procedural costs of the Judicial Review to the State.
