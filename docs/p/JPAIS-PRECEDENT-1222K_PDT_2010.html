Supreme Court Decision No. 1222 K/Pdt/2010 is an Indonesian civil-law precedent concerning the lack of authority of the general courts to examine internal disputes of the Indonesian Journalists' Association (Persatuan Wartawan Indonesia, PWI) and the inadmissibility of a claim for an unlawful act (perbuatan melawan hukum, PMH) under Article 1365 of the Civil Code (KUHPerdata).

The decision establishes that a dispute between a PWI branch board and the PWI Central Board over the dismissal of the branch chair and the suspension of the branch cannot be assessed as an unlawful act, because such matters are regulated autonomously within PWI by its Articles of Association and Bylaws (AD/ART) and the Journalistic Code of Ethics. Such disputes must be resolved through PWI's own mechanisms, and a claim brought before the general courts is inadmissible (Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard / NO).

Executive Summary

The dispute arose between the Board of the PWI West Papua Branch (2006–2010) and the PWI Central Board. The branch board members sued the Central Board before the Manokwari District Court, seeking annulment of PWI Central Board Decree No. 576/PP-PWI/2008 dated 9 May 2008, which dismissed the Chair of PWI West Papua ([Party A]), temporarily suspended the branch board, and appointed a caretaker. They characterized the decree as an unlawful act under Article 1365 of the Civil Code.

The PWI Central Board had dismissed [Party A] because he concurrently served as Head of Public Relations Section (Kabag Humas) of the West Papua Provincial Government, which it considered a violation of the AD/ART. The Manokwari District Court partially granted the claim and annulled the decree, and the Jayapura High Court affirmed the decision with a correction of the operative part.

The Supreme Court held that the Judex Facti had erred in applying the law. The dispute could not be assessed as an unlawful act under Article 1365, because PWI regulates such matters autonomously through its AD/ART and Journalistic Code of Ethics and accounts for them at the PWI Congress. The dispute therefore had to be resolved through PWI's own mechanisms and did not fall within the authority of the general courts. The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition, annulled the lower-court decisions, and, adjudicating the case itself, declared the Plaintiffs' claim inadmissible (NO).

Case Background

The Original Plaintiffs ([Party A], SH; Karsuhan; La Ode Mursidin; and Deminikus Talakua, SH), the Board of the PWI West Papua Branch for 2006–2010, sued the Original Defendants (the PWI Central Board / Drs. Tarman Azzam; Usman Taufiq Divinubun; and Angleng Murcana) before the Manokwari District Court.

The Plaintiffs sought annulment of PWI Central Board Decree No. 576/PP-PWI/2008 dated 9 May 2008 on the Dismissal of the Chair of PWI West Papua ([Party A]), the Temporary Suspension of the Branch Board, and the Appointment of a Caretaker, which they considered an unlawful act under Article 1365 of the Civil Code.

The PWI Central Board had dismissed [Party A] because he concurrently served as Head of Public Relations Section (Kabag Humas) of the West Papua Provincial Government, which was considered a violation of the AD/ART.

The Manokwari District Court partially granted the claim and annulled the decree. The Jayapura High Court affirmed the decision with a correction of the operative part. The PWI Central Board et al. then filed a cassation petition with the Supreme Court.

Procedural History

First Instance: Manokwari District Court

The Manokwari District Court decided Case No. 08/PDT.G/2008/PN.Mkw on 14 November 2008. It partially granted the Plaintiffs' claim and annulled the PWI Central Board decree.

Appeal: Jayapura High Court

The Jayapura High Court decided Appeal No. 25/PDT/2009/PT.Jpr on 11 December 2009. It affirmed the first-instance decision with a correction of the operative part.

Cassation: Supreme Court

The PWI Central Board et al. filed a cassation petition. The Supreme Court granted the petition, annulled the lower-court decisions, adjudicated the case itself, and declared the Plaintiffs' claim inadmissible.

Procedural timeline

Legal Issues

The case raised the following principal legal issues:

  1. Whether a dispute between the PWI West Papua Branch Board and the PWI Central Board over the dismissal of the branch chair and the suspension of the branch can be assessed as an unlawful act (PMH) under Article 1365 of the Civil Code.
  2. Whether the general courts have authority to examine internal disputes of PWI that are governed by its AD/ART and Journalistic Code of Ethics.
  3. Whether the Judex Facti (the Manokwari District Court and the Jayapura High Court) erred in applying the law by granting the claim.

Supreme Court's Reasoning

1. The Dispute Was Not an Unlawful Act under Article 1365

The dispute between the PWI West Papua Branch Board and the PWI Central Board concerning the dismissal and suspension of the branch cannot be assessed as an unlawful act (perbuatan melawan hukum) under Article 1365 of the Civil Code.

2. Internal PWI Matters Are Regulated Autonomously

Within the PWI organization, such matters are regulated autonomously by the Articles of Association and Bylaws (AD/ART) and the Journalistic Code of Ethics, and are accounted for at the PWI Congress.

3. Resolution through PWI's Own Mechanisms; Outside the Authority of the General Courts

Disputes within the PWI organization must be resolved through PWI's own mechanisms and do not fall within the authority of the general courts.

4. The Lower Courts Misapplied the Law

The Judex Facti (the Jayapura High Court and the Manokwari District Court) erred in applying the law by examining and granting the claim.

5. Consequence: Inadmissibility

Adjudicating the case itself, the Supreme Court declared the Plaintiffs' claim inadmissible (Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard / NO).

Ratio Decidendi

The core ratio decidendi may be stated as follows:

A dispute between a PWI branch board and the PWI Central Board over the dismissal and suspension of the branch cannot be assessed as an unlawful act under Article 1365 of the Civil Code, because within PWI such matters are regulated autonomously by its AD/ART and the Journalistic Code of Ethics and accounted for at the PWI Congress; such internal disputes must be resolved through PWI's own mechanisms and do not fall within the authority of the general courts, so that a claim brought before them is inadmissible (NO).

Holding

The Supreme Court:

  1. Granted the cassation petitions of the Cassation Petitioners: (1) the Central Board of the Indonesian Journalists' Association, (2) Usman Taufiq Divinubun, and (3) Angleng Murcana.
  2. Annulled Jayapura High Court Decision No. 25/PDT/2009/PT.Jpr dated 11 December 2009, which had corrected Manokwari District Court Decision No. 08/PDT.G/2008/PN.Mkw dated 14 November 2008.
  3. Annulled Manokwari District Court Decision No. 08/PDT.G/2008/PN.Mkw (see the ratio, which names both lower-court decisions as annulled).
  4. Adjudicated the case itself (mengadili sendiri):
    1. Declared the Plaintiffs' claim inadmissible (Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard / NO).
  5. Ordered the Cassation Respondents to pay court costs at all judicial levels (cassation costs set at IDR 500,000).

Legal Significance

This precedent is significant for Indonesian civil procedure and organizational law because it addresses the reach of the general courts over the internal affairs of a professional organization.

The decision illustrates several principles:

  • A dispute between a branch board and the central board of PWI over dismissal and suspension cannot be assessed as an unlawful act (PMH) under Article 1365 of the Civil Code.
  • Where an organization regulates its internal affairs autonomously through its AD/ART and code of ethics and accounts for them at its congress, internal disputes are to be resolved through the organization's own mechanisms.
  • Such internal disputes do not fall within the authority of the general courts.
  • The Supreme Court may annul Judex Facti decisions that examine such a dispute and declare the claim inadmissible (NO).
