Description / Deskripsi

Presents the complete 14-slide presentation breakdown of the integrated August 2026 Inventory of Issues (DIM) for amending PERMA No. 3 of 2021, fully translated into English and styled under the **Vector 2022 Wiki Architecture** featuring custom **Indonesia Judicial Center (IJC)** branding and the official **Mahkamah Agung Corporate University (BSDK)** institutional color palette (Red Pillar, Blue Pillar, Green Pillar, Gold Crown).

Problem / Masalah

  • JPAIS-MED-PROB-001: Procedural ambiguity in relative competence for foreign respondents, day-count definition clashes, rigid deadlines tied to decision excerpts, and unproportional bank guarantee requirements.
  • JPAIS-MED-PROB-002: Absence of clear judicial review standards for economic analysis and relevant markets, strict prohibitions against new evidence (*novum*), limited file access (*inzage*), and lack of data room protections.
  • JPAIS-MED-PROB-003: Execution hurdles for non-monetary sanctions, dual *inkracht* statuses in joint conduct cases, and a shortage of certified commercial judges trained in economic evidence evaluation.

Current Indonesian Practice / Praktik Indonesia Saat Ini

  • JPAIS-MED-ID-001: Commercial Courts review objections against KPPU decisions under PERMA No. 3 of 2021, facing operational hurdles across administrative, evidentiary, and enforcement stages.

Japanese Practice / Praktik Jepang

  • JPAIS-MED-JP-001: Advanced foreign jurisdictions integrate structured economic analysis, specialized expert tribunals, and flexible procedural rules to ensure substantive fairness in administrative antitrust reviews.

Gap / Kesenjangan

  • JPAIS-MED-GAP-001: 23 items touch 14 existing articles of PERMA 3/2021, while 12 critical issues remain completely unregulated, creating procedural voids during Commercial Court objections.

Proposed Adaptation / Adaptasi Usulan

Execute structural amendments across the 9 issue groups, harmonizing day counts, introducing proportional bank guarantees, clarifying the scope of review over economic evidence, enabling limited reciprocal new evidence, and establishing modern digital and expert examination frameworks.

Action / Aksi

  • JPAIS-MED-ACT-001: Review and consolidate the 35 DIM items from internal court inventories (Jakarta Pusat, Medan, Semarang, Surabaya) and expert papers (ICLA, Prof. Ningrum, Ine Ruky).
  • JPAIS-MED-ACT-002: Finalize draft normative amendments targeting the 14 existing articles and structuring provisions for the 12 new issue areas.
  • JPAIS-MED-ACT-003: Upgrade court IT infrastructure (e-Court, SIPP) to support complex multi-applicant registrations and secure virtual expert hearings.

Pilot / Pilot Proyek

  • JPAIS-MED-PILOT-016: Regional Commercial Court implementation across Jakarta Pusat, Medan, Semarang, Surabaya, and Makassar.

KPI / Indikator Kinerja Utama

  • JPAIS-MED-KPI-016: 100% harmonization of procedural deadlines; zero improper rejections due to unproportional financial guarantees; adoption of standardized economic review templates across all Commercial Courts.

Policy Implication / Implikasi Kebijakan

Provide comprehensive legal certainty, protect the equality of arms, and transform Commercial Court proceedings from formalistic rubber-stamps into rigorous substantive reviews.

Regulatory Implication / Implikasi Regulasi

Promulgate an amended PERMA No. 3 of 2021, issue supplementary Supreme Court guidelines (SK KMA) on economic analysis, and align regulatory frameworks with the New Criminal Code (UU 1/2023).

Evidence / Bukti Empiris

  • Integrated August 2026 DIM compiled from internal court data and expert legal practitioner evaluations across multiple regional jurisdictions.

Slide Presentation Breakdown: Amendment of PERMA No. 3 of 2021 (MA Corporate University Theme Colored Format)

Slide 1

  • Main Title: INVENTORY OF ISSUES (DIM)
  • Subtitle: Key Issues for the Amendment of Supreme Court Regulation (PERMA) Number 3 of 2021
  • Topic: Procedures for Filing and Examining Objections against KPPU Decisions in Commercial Courts
  • Presentation Note: This deck summarizes the integrated August 2026 DIM version containing 35 problem points regarding the application of PERMA 3/2021, grouped into 9 issue categories following the case management lifecycle.

Slide 2

  • Title: EXECUTIVE SUMMARY — Amendment Map on a Single Page
  • Key Statistics:

 * **35** Problem Points
 * **9** Issue Groups
 * **14** Potentially Amended Articles
 * **12** Unregulated New Issues

  • Articles Potentially Amended: Article 1 number 7 (Definition of days), Article 2 paragraph (4) (Inkracht status), Article 3 (Competence and deadlines), Article 4 (Bank guarantee obligation), Article 6 (Guarantee amount), Article 8 (Court appointment), Article 10 (Start of examination), Article 11 (Notification of decision), Article 12 (Mediation and evidence), Article 13 (Scope and evidence), Article 14 (Case duration), Article 15 (Witnesses and experts), Article 16 (Subsequent legal remedies), Article 21 (Standing of parties).
  • Potential New Issues to Include: (1) Legal remedies for partnership decisions; (2) Limits of authority between KPPU and other supervisory bodies; (3) e-Court and SIPP support for multiple Applicants; (4) Master case registry number post-consolidation; (5) Judicial review standards for economic analysis; (6) Confidentiality protection and data room mechanisms; (7) Summoning procedures for witnesses and experts; (8) Expert hot-tubbing; (9) Execution of non-monetary relief and asset data availability; (10) Courtroom facilities and human resource capacity; (11) Criminal law harmonization between Law 5/1999 and Law 1/2023 (New Criminal Code); (12) Socialization, periodic evaluation, and coordination.

Slide 3

  • Title: DIM STRUCTURE — Distribution of 35 Points across 9 Issue Groups
  • Heaviest Burden: Group C (Formal Requirements & Administration) and Group G (Witness & Expert Examination) each contain 5 points.
  • Unregulated Issues: 12 out of 35 points currently have no regulatory basis in PERMA 3/2021.
  • Structuring Flow: Grouping follows case stages, from adjudicating authority up to execution and institutional support.

Slide 4

  • Title: GROUP A — Court Jurisdiction and Competence
  • Point 1: Relative competence for foreign Respondents (Article 3 paragraph 2) — PERMA only regulates registry filings without clarifying forums for Respondents domiciled abroad, risking *niet ontvankelijke verklaard*.
  • Point 2: Legal remedies for partnership decisions (Unregulated) — KPPU Regulation 2/2024 states partnership decisions are final, yet courts continue to accept legal remedies.
  • Point 3: Authority limits between KPPU and other supervisory bodies (Unregulated) — Jurisdictional boundaries between KPPU and APIP, BPKP, LKPP, and State Administrative Courts (PTUN) regarding technical specifications remain unclear.

Slide 5

  • Title: GROUP B — Procedures and Deadlines for Filing Objections
  • Point 1: Conflict in day definitions (Article 1 number 7) — PERMA interprets days as calendar days, whereas Government Regulation 44/2021 mandates working days.
  • Point 2: Deadlines calculated from decision excerpts (Article 3 jo. Article 11) — Complete copies take up to 14 days to arrive, forcing memorial drafting without reviewing full legal reasoning.
  • Point 3: Simultaneous filing of declaration and memorial (Article 3 jo. Article 12) — Both must be filed within 14 calendar days, unlike cassation which separates them, while applicants must simultaneously arrange bank guarantees.

Slide 6

  • Title: GROUP C — Formal Requirements and Case Administration
  • Point 1: Bank guarantees for Respondents without fines (Article 4 paragraph 1 & Article 6) — Guarantees remain mandatory even when no monetary payment needs securing; amounts and refund mechanisms are unregulated.
  • Point 2: Status of Co-Respondents (Article 21 paragraph 2) — To avoid *plurium litis consortium*, other respondents are joined as parties, increasing costs and prolonging summons.
  • Point 3: e-Court and SIPP limitations (Unregulated) — Secondary objection filings for the same decision are frequently rejected; SIPP records only one petition per register.
  • Point 4: Master register number post-consolidation (Unregulated) — No legal basis exists for establishing a master case after multiple petitions are consolidated, risking breach of the 12-month limit.
  • Point 5: Delays in cross-forum court appointments (Article 8 paragraph 1) — Supreme Court Chief Justice appointment orders take too long to arrive, causing concurrent trials across Commercial Courts with risks of conflicting verdicts.

Slide 7

  • Title: GROUP D — Examination Timeframes and Stages
  • Point 1: Uncertainty of start timing (Article 10) — Timeframes are counted from the first hearing and dossier handover, even though parties may be absent or dossiers incomplete.
  • Point 2: Desynced minimum and maximum limits (Article 14 paragraph 1 jo. Article 15) — Cases must finish between 3 and 12 months, but judgments may be pronounced without waiting 3 months, causing ambiguity.
  • Point 3: Objection proceedings without mediation (Article 12) — PERMA omits mediation, whereas PERMA 1/2016 mandates court mediation, creating doctrinal debate.

Slide 8

  • Title: GROUP E — Scope and Standards of Objection Examination
  • Point 1: Commercial Court standing as *judex facti* (Article 13 paragraph 1) — Unclear whether panels must re-evaluate evidentiary sufficiency for every violation element or merely test KPPU reasoning reasonableness.
  • Point 2: Review of relevant market definitions (Article 13 paragraph 1) — No explicit authority to re-examine relevant market methodologies despite direct impacts on market share and dominance.
  • Point 3: Judicial review standards for economic analysis (Unregulated) — Economic analysis is not *self-proving*; tiered testing of methodology, data, assumptions, and alternative explanations is proposed.
  • Point 4: Transparency of fine calculation breakdowns (Article 10) — KPPU does not submit fine calculation details, preventing judicial review of formulas, aggravating, and mitigating factors.

Slide 9

  • Title: GROUP F — Evidence and Dossier Access
  • Point 1: Prohibition of new evidence (Article 13 paragraph 5) — KPPU decisions often contain facts and calculation methods absent from the Initial Report (LDP); limited reciprocal *novum* is proposed.
  • Point 2: Indirect evidence without guidelines (Article 12) — No quality standards or boundaries exist for *indirect evidence* aligned with Article 42 of Law 5/1999 or OECD guidelines.
  • Point 3: Limited access to files and investigation minutes / *inzage* (Article 13 paragraph 3 jo. Article 21 paragraph 2) — File inspection rights lack explicit adoption, causing informational asymmetry violating *audi et alteram partem*.
  • Point 4: Protection of business confidentiality (Unregulated) — Confidential info lacks protection in open hearings, whereas international practice utilizes two-version documents, data rooms, and confidentiality rings.

Slide 10

  • Title: GROUP G — Witness and Expert Examinations
  • Point 1: Criteria for submitting witnesses and experts (Article 13 paragraph 3 jo. Article 15) — Submissions are restricted to specific conditions; panel approval criteria lack clarity and written reasoning mandates.
  • Point 2: New witnesses or experts never previously submitted (Article 13) — Prohibitions lack clarity, causing divergent inter-panel applications and expanding proof beyond KPPU findings.
  • Point 3: Witness/expert summoning mechanism (Unregulated) — Unclear whether attendance is a party burden or court-summoned, leading to inconsistent trial practices.
  • Point 4: Online hearings / teleconferencing (Article 15) — Economic and industry experts often reside out-of-town or abroad, causing repeated adjournments due to lack of teleconferencing rules.
  • Point 5: Scope of expert testimony (Unregulated) — Experts are barred from discussing case merits, yet testimony is essential to test economic models; *hot-tubbing* is proposed.

Slide 11

  • Title: GROUP H — Judgments, Subsequent Legal Remedies, and Execution
  • Point 1: Inkracht status in multi-party cases (Article 2 ayat 4) — Some respondents accept final judgments while others pursue objections, confusing joint settlement remedies.
  • Point 2: Conflicting cassation verdicts on a single KPPU decision (Article 8 jo. Article 16) — Separated examinations have produced contradictory cassation verdicts, halting execution.
  • Point 3: Absence of Reconsideration / Peninjauan Kembali (Article 16 paragraph 2) — New evidence bans and law-only cassation reviews prevent the correction of newly discovered factual errors.
  • Point 4: Execution hurdles (Unregulated details) — Distrainable asset data is unavailable and relief is non-monetary; ordering KPPU to compile asset lists is proposed.

Slide 12

  • Title: GROUP I — Institutional Support, Capacity, and Harmonization
  • Point 1: Shortage of certified judges (Panel appointment) — Certified competition judges are extremely limited and fluctuate due to rotations, even in courts with the highest caseloads.
  • Point 2: Courtroom facilities and infrastructure (Unregulated) — Requires electronic trial facilities, big-file document management, and human resource upskilling.
  • Point 3: Criminal law harmonization (Unregulated) — Penal provisions in Law 5/1999 are misaligned with Law 1/2023 (New Criminal Code), particularly regarding corporate criminal liability and fines.
  • Point 4: Socialization, periodic evaluation, and coordination (Unregulated) — Severe regional caseload disparities require periodic evaluations, publication, and enhanced MA-KPPU coordination.

Slide 13

  • Title: DIRECTION OF REFINEMENT — Emerging Core Proposals
  • 1. Deadline Harmonization: Align days with PP 44/2021 and separate declarations from objection memorials.
  • 2. Proportional Bank Guarantees: Differentiate fined Respondents and clarify guarantee amounts and refunds.
  • 3. Scope Clarity: Affirm Commercial Court standing and relevant market review authority.
  • 4. Economic Review Standards: Establish tiered testing for methodology, data, assumptions, and alternatives.
  • 5. Limited New Evidence: Open reciprocal *novum* with written panel permission and reasoning.
  • 6. Dossier Access & Confidentiality: Adopt *inzage* with dual document versions and closed hearings.
  • 7. Modernization: Regulate online witness/expert hearings and e-Court/SIPP integration.
  • 8. Correction & Execution: Open Reconsideration (PK) under strict conditions and strengthen execution instruments.

Slide 14

  • Title: REFERENCES — Integration Sources for Inventory of Issues (August 2026)
