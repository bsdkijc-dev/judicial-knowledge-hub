Overview

Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia Decision No. 400 K/Pdt/1984, dated July 19, 1985, established a highly important civil procedural law rule concerning the formal defect of deficient parties (*plurium litis consortium*) in simulated transactions.

The Supreme Court held that if a land/house purchase agreement is inherently a simulated transaction to secure the repayment of a debt between the Plaintiff and a third party (the Defendant's son), the Defendant's son, who is the primary debtor, must be joined as a defendant. Failing to join the primary debtor as a defendant renders the Plaintiff's lawsuit deficient in parties (formally defective) and subject to dismissal as inadmissible (*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard* / NO).

Executive Summary

The central procedural principle established in this decision is that when a formal sale and purchase agreement masks an underlying debt-securing arrangement involving a third party, that third-party debtor is a necessary party to the litigation.

Key elements established in the adjudication:

  • The substantive legal relationship of a simulated debt-security transaction requires joining the principal debtor as a defendant; failure to do so results in a formal defect (*plurium litis consortium*), rendering the claim inadmissible (NO).
  • Courts must scrutinize the actual cause (*oorzaak*) of an agreement rather than relying solely on formal contract documentation.
  • Lower courts (*judex facti*) that fail to examine underlying causes and witness testimonies commit flawed reasoning (*onvoldoende gemotiveerd*).

Case Background

The Original Plaintiff / Respondent for Cassation, [Party D] ([Party C]), filed a lawsuit at the Purwokerto District Court against the Original Defendant / Petitioner for Cassation, [Party A] ([Party E]), seeking the eviction and surrender of land and a house under Ownership Certificate (SHM) No. 358 in Purwokerto Kidul based on a Sale and Purchase Deed dated June 6, 1981.

The Defendant resisted the claim, arguing that the deed was executed under pressure as a security guarantee for debts owed by his son, [Party B], to the Plaintiff. It was revealed during trial proceedings that the transaction was a simulated arrangement covering [Party B]'s debt. However, the Plaintiff did not join [Party B] as a party to the lawsuit.

The Purwokerto District Court (Decision No. 34/1981/Pdt.G) and the Semarang High Court (Decision No. 194/1982/Pdt/PT.Smg) granted the Plaintiff's claim. The Petitioner for Cassation subsequently filed a cassation petition with the Supreme Court.

Legal and Procedural Issues

Construction of Simulated Agreements & Agreement Cause (Articles 1457 & 1320 Civil Code)

The issue evaluated whether the creation of a Sale and Purchase Deed before a Notary/PPAT is formally binding as a pure sale when the true cause (*oorzaak*) is a third-party debt security.

The Supreme Court ruled that it is not. Courts are obligated to examine the true legal cause of a transaction. If proven that a sale and purchase transaction is a simulated arrangement to secure third-party debt repayment, the primary binding obligation is debt-credit.

Deficient Parties (Plurium Litis Consortium)

The issue evaluated whether an execution/eviction lawsuit over a simulated purchase object is valid if the Plaintiff fails to join the principal debtor ([Party B]) who holds the primary debt obligation.

The Supreme Court held that it is invalid. Because the principal debtor ([Party B]) is the legal subject who created the debt obligation and surrendered collateral, failing to join [Party B] as a defendant renders the lawsuit formally defective (*plurium litis consortium*) and subject to dismissal as inadmissible (*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard* / NO).

Question of Law and Question of Fact

Question of Law (Cassation / Judex Juris)

Question:

Did the *Judex Facti* (Semarang High Court & Purwokerto District Court) misapply evidentiary law and provide flawed reasoning (*onvoldoende gemotiveerd*) by ignoring the nature of the simulated transaction and failing to declare the lawsuit formally defective due to missing parties (*plurium litis consortium*)?

Supreme Court Ruling:

Yes. The *Judex Facti* misapplied the law. Disregarding the existence of the principal debtor ([Party B]) in a simulated sale case constitutes a fundamental breach of procedural law.

Question of Fact (Judex Facti / Purwokerto District & Semarang High Courts)

Examination of the history of Ownership Certificate (SHM) No. 358 in Purwokerto Kidul (area 413 m²), witness testimonies from Wongsogiono, Liong Tje Kwong, and Mashudi regarding [Party B]'s total loan (Rp 25,700,000.-), and the drafting process of Notary Sutardjo Sumoatmodjo's deeds.

Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

  • Ratio Decidendi:
  1. The *Judex Facti* misapplied evidentiary law and provided insufficient reasoning (*onvoldoende gemotiveerd*) by evaluating the sale deed purely on formal grounds without considering the agreement cause (*oorzaak*) ex Article 1457 of the Civil Code and witness statements.
  2. It was proven that the disputed land/house sale was a simulated transaction to secure the debt of [Party B] (the Defendant's son) to the Plaintiff.
  3. Because the actual legal relationship was [Party B]'s debt, but [Party B] was not co-defended, the Plaintiff's lawsuit suffered from deficient parties (*plurium litis consortium*), was formally defective, and had to be declared inadmissible (*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard* / NO).

Ruling

The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition of the Petitioner for Cassation ([Party A]), annulled Semarang High Court Decision No. 194/1982/Pdt/PT.Smg in conjunction with Purwokerto District Court Decision No. 34/1981/Pdt.G, and adjudicated the matter afresh: Declaring the plaintiff's lawsuit inadmissible (*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard* / NO); and ordering the Respondent for Cassation / Original Plaintiff to pay court costs across all judicial instances.

Related Knowledge and Jurisprudence

  • Civil Code (KUHPerdata) Article 1320: Requirements for a valid agreement (consent, capacity, specific object, lawful cause).
  • Civil Code (KUHPerdata) Article 1457: Definition of Sale and Purchase (delivery of goods and payment of price).
  • Civil Procedure & Doctrine (Plurium Litis Consortium): A lawsuit failing to include primary parties substantively bound to the core obligation is formally defective and inadmissible.
  • Doctrine of Onvoldoende Gemotiveerd: Decisions that fail to weigh core evidence and sociological facts of a transaction are voidable.

Evidence Status and Gaps

Established by Evidence

  • Sale and Purchase Deeds No. 5 dated February 6, 1980, and June 6, 1981, were signed between [Party A] and [Party D] before a Notary/PPAT.
  • The substantive legal nature of the act was security for the debt of [Party B] ([Party A]'s son) amounting to Rp 25,700,000.- owed to [Party D] and others.
  • [Party B] was not joined as Defendant I, Defendant II, or Co-Defendant in the Original Plaintiff's statement of claim.

Not Established / Unproven

  • There was no proof of any genuine intention for a pure, bona fide sale and purchase of land/house between [Party A] and [Party D].

Uncertainty and Gaps

  • Correction of *Judex Facti* errors: The Purwokerto District Court and Semarang High Court favored the Original Plaintiff based solely on formal physical sale deed sheets without examining witnesses who revealed the transaction was security for the Defendant's son's debt. The Supreme Court restored justice by annulling both decisions.

GEO Q&A

Q: Is a land sale deed valid if it was actually created solely as security for a landowner's son's debt, and what is the status of the lawsuit if that son is not co-defended?

A: It is invalid as a pure sale, and the lawsuit must be declared inadmissible (NO). Based on Supreme Court Decision No. 400 K/Pdt/1984, if a sale and purchase transaction is inherently a simulated transaction to secure a debt owed by the landowner's son, the core legal relationship is a debt obligation. The Plaintiff is required to join that son (the principal debtor) as a defendant. If not joined, the lawsuit is deficient in parties (*plurium litis consortium*) and formally defective (*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard* / NO).
