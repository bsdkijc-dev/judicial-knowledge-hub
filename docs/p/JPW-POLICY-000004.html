JPW-POLICY-000004

By Cecep Mustafa, Indonesian Judicial College (IJC) · revision 1328, 2026-09-17 · wiki source · Markdown

The Asian Alternative Dispute Resolution and Judicial Reform Framework (Stable ID: JPW-POLICY-000004) is a regional judicial policy and research initiative compiled under the IDE Asian Law Series No. 11. Formulated following the "Law, Development and Socio-Economic Changes in Asia II" Roundtable Conference in Bangkok (November 2001), the policy outlines structural measures to reduce court backlog, integrate alternative dispute resolution (ADR), separate judicial administration from executive control, and guarantee the rule of law across post-crisis Asian economies. Note: Named entities Cecep Mustafa and Reihan Abdan Syakur are Not found in the available sources.

Summary

The framework synthesizes country-level judicial evaluations across eight Asian nations: Thailand, Philippines, Indonesia, Japan, Malaysia, India, China, and Vietnam. It systematically examines two main pillars: (1) Dispute Resolution Processes (court-annexed conciliation, labor arbitration, environmental adjudication, and Comprehensive Dispute Processing), and (2) Law and Political Development (democratization, constitutional review, local government decentralization, and ombudsman supervision).

Legal Basis

Authority and implementation are grounded in national legislation across participating jurisdictions:

  • Thailand: 1997 Constitution; Supreme Court Practice Guidance Concerning Conciliation (1996); CIPITC Establishment Act B.E. 2539 (1996); Civil Procedure Code (Secs. 19, 20, 193, 210–222).
  • India: Constitution of India (Art. 39-A, Part XIV A); Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996; Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987; Consumer Protection Act, 1986; Industrial Disputes Act, 1947.
  • China: Constitution of the P.R.C.; Civil Procedure Law of 1991 (Arts. 135, 146, 159, 177–186); Arbitration Law of 1994.
  • Philippines: 1987 Constitution (Arts. III, VIII, XIII); Labor Code (P.D. 442); Executive Order No. 180.
  • Malaysia: Industrial Relations Act 1967 (Act 177); Essential Regulations 1965.
  • Indonesia: 1945 Constitution; Presidential Decree No. 44 of 2000; Act No. 35 of 1999 (Judicial Power).
  • Japan: Constitution of Japan (Arts. 12, 13, 22, 29, 81); Code of Civil Procedure 1996; Law concerning the Settlement of Environmental Pollution Disputes (1970).
  • Vietnam: 1992 Constitution; Decree No. 116/CP; Ordinance on Economic Contracts (1989).

Background & Policy Problems

Background

Following the 1997 financial crisis and rapid globalization, Asian legal systems faced acute pressure to modernize. Outdated court structures suffered from severe delays, lack of specialized commercial expertise, corruption, and local protectionism. Reformers sought to transition from "rule by law" to "rule of law".

Policy Problems

  • Extreme trial delays and massive case pendency (e.g., over 20 million pending cases in Indian lower courts in 2001).
  • Lack of specialized commercial, technical, environmental, and intellectual property expertise among judges.
  • Systemic corruption, executive interference, and local protectionism in judicial execution.
  • Absence of total judicial finality (res judicata deficit) due to indefinite retrial/judicial supervision procedures.
  • Lawyer monopolies and artificial restrictions on legal practice creating legal access barriers.

Objectives

1. Eliminate trial backlogs through court-annexed conciliation, Lok Adalats, and Fast Track Courts. 2. Establish specialized judicial and administrative bodies (IP/IT courts, environmental commissions, ombudsmen). 3. Guarantee judicial independence by transferring court administration away from executive ministries. 4. Modernize legal education and lift artificial limits on legal practitioners. 5. Institutionalize constitutional guarantees for human rights, democratic accountability, and local government autonomy.

Scope & Implementation

Scope

Covers commercial, labor, environmental, administrative, and constitutional law sectors in Thailand, Philippines, Indonesia, Japan, Malaysia, India, China, and Vietnam.

Key Provisions

  • Court-Annexed Conciliation: Informal pre-trial conciliation with non-gown attire, non-disclosure rules, judge withdrawal options, and fee refunds.
  • Comprehensive Dispute Processing (CDP): Inverted cone model placing consensus negotiation at the top, mediation in the middle, and litigation/arbitration as back-up at the apex.
  • Judicial Supervision & Retrial (China): Retrial procedures initiated by courts, parties, or prosecutor protests (protest procedure) for misjudged cases.
  • Ombudsman Jurisdiction (Indonesia): Independent oversight investigating maladministration, corruption, and judicial forgery.

Implementation

  • Thailand: Supreme Court Practice Guidance (1996); CIPITC operationalized; 1997 Constitution election reforms.
  • India: Over 108,000 Lok Adalats settling 13 million cases; 459 Fast Track Courts.
  • China: Open live TV court broadcasts across 58 intermediate courts.
  • Indonesia: Presidential Decree 44/2000 creating the National Ombudsman Commission.
  • Japan: Environmental Dispute Coordination Committee handling 730+ cases.

Reform

  • Structural transfer of court supervision to Supreme Courts.
  • Conversion of executive ombudsmen into statutory Parliamentary Ombudsmen.
  • Enactment of standalone local government decentralization plans.
