Policy Analysis

  • Background & Research Objectives (Chapter 1):
    • Examines national statistical data alongside questionnaire surveys administered to juveniles in juvenile training schools, juvenile delinquent probationers, and their guardians.
    • Primary goal is to clarify the living environments of juvenile delinquents to improve correctional treatment and support mechanisms for youth facing difficult life conditions.
  • Statistical Research (Chapter 2):
    • The proportion of juvenile delinquents raised by "birth parents" has decreased, while the "birth mother only" group shows an upward trend.
    • Childhood abuse experiences show an increasing trend over time.
    • 2021 Data: Approximately 70% of juvenile inmates from "poor" households resided with only one parent (birth mother or birth father). Juveniles from poor households exhibited higher high school dropout rates and lower high school graduation rates.
    • Inmates and probationers from impoverished backgrounds demonstrated higher rates of mental disorders, including intellectual disabilities.
  • Questionnaire Survey (Chapter 3):
    • Conducted June–September/November 2021 with 579 valid pair analysis subjects (335 juvenile training school inmates and 244 juvenile probationers) and their guardians.
    • Adverse Childhood Experiences (ACEs): 86.3% of juvenile training school inmates and 56.5% of juvenile probationers presented with at least 1 ACE indicator.
    • Household Economic Conditions: Over 40% of combined households fell under "very poor" or "somewhat poor" categories. Significant correlation identified between household poverty and ACE severity.
    • Guardian Profile: Guardians of juveniles with severe ACEs showed higher rates of personal histories involving physical abuse/neglect by their own parents, mental health consultations, spousal physical abuse, and suicidal ideation.
    • Psychological Traits & Daily Life: Juveniles with severe ACEs tended to attribute current circumstances to external factors (Locus of Control Scale), experienced family relationship strain, and exhibited irregular eating patterns.
  • Field Studies & Recommendations (Chapters 4 & 5):
    • Field research conducted at juvenile training schools, probation offices, juvenile support centers, and children's cafeterias (*kodomo shokudo*).
    • Three Core Recommendations: (1) Implementation of trauma-informed care and medical collaboration for severe trauma cases, (2) provision of financial counseling and institutional alignment to overcome economic educational/employment barriers, and (3) early identification of youth and guardian distress by local communities prior to delinquent turning points.
  • Interpretations & Structural Inferences:
    • Report 65 demonstrates that juvenile delinquency in Japan is a complex outcome of cumulative childhood trauma (ACEs), structural family poverty, and intergenerational violence histories among guardians.
    • The high prevalence of ACEs among inmates (86.3%) and its positive correlation with guardian depression and domestic abuse indicate that juvenile justice interventions cannot succeed if limited strictly to penal measures without accompanying trauma care and household economic support.
