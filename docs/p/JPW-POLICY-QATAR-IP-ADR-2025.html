JPW-POLICY-QATAR-IP-ADR-2025

By Cecep Mustafa, Indonesian Judicial College (IJC) · revision 985, 2026-09-16 · wiki source · Markdown

The Arbitrability of Intellectual Property Disputes in Qatar refers to the judicial policy and legal framework in the State of Qatar governing the resolution of intellectual property (IP) conflicts via arbitration and alternative dispute resolution (ADR) mechanisms Qatar IP Policy [1–81].

Overview

In transitioning toward a knowledge-based economy, Qatar utilizes alternative dispute resolution (ADR) and arbitration to resolve IP disputes efficiently Knowledge Economy [1–81]. This framework aligns with UN Sustainable Development Goal 16.3 (SDG 16.3) to ensure equal access to justice and supports the Qatar Third National Development Strategy (2024–2030) SDG 16.3 [1–81].

Legal Basis

  • Law No. 2 of 2017 (Civil and Commercial Arbitration Law): Standalone arbitration legislation modeled on the UNCITRAL Model Law Law No. 2 of 2017 [1–81].
  • Law No. 21 of 2021: Established the Investment and Commerce Court, conferring exclusive judicial jurisdiction over IP disputes Investment Court [1–81].
  • Emiri Decree No. 29 of 2003: Accession to the 1958 New York Convention on the Recognition and Enforcement of Foreign Arbitral Awards New York Convention [1–81].
  • Qatari Court of Cassation Rulings: Judgment of June 20, 2023 (Cassation No. 359/2023), holding that court jurisdiction does not preclude parties from agreeing to arbitrate IP disputes Court of Cassation [1–81].

Background

Rooted in Arab legal tradition, amicable dispute resolution incorporates principles of sulh (reconciliation), wasta (mediation), and hakam (arbitration) Sulh Tradition [1–81]. Qatar integrates these historical concepts with modern digital procedures to build a secure, arbitration-friendly investment environment Arbitration Modernization [1–81].

Key Objectives

  1. Facilitate effective access to justice in IP disputes under SDG 16.3 Access to Justice [1–81].
  2. Reduce domestic court caseloads and expedite dispute resolution for foreign investors Dispute Efficiency [1–81].
  3. Drive national innovation under the Qatar Third National Development Strategy (2024–2030) Development Strategy [1–81].
  4. Establish Qatar as a regional commercial arbitration hub in the Middle East Regional Hub [1–81].

Core Provisions

  • Arbitrability Criteria: Under Law No. 2 of 2017, arbitration is restricted to commercial matters capable of legal compromise (*reconciliation*) Arbitration Criteria [1–81].
  • Digital Access: The 2024 QICCA Arbitration Rules enable electronic filings and expedited proceedings QICCA 2024 [1–81].
  • Sports IP Enforcement: Specialized legislation such as Law No. 27 of 2004 (2006 Asian Games) and Law Nos. 10 & 11 of 2021 (FIFA World Cup Qatar 2022™) Sports IP [1–81].

Implementation & Administration

  • Role of QICCA: The Qatar International Center for Conciliation and Arbitration (QICCA) handled cases valued at nearly QR 3 billion in 2023 QICCA Caseload [1–81].
  • Investment and Commerce Court: Utilizes a paperless digital system capable of resolving urgent cases within 67 days Digital Court [1–81].
  • International Cooperation: MoU signed between QICCA and the WIPO Arbitration and Mediation Center WIPO MoU [1–81].

Policy Issues & Grey Zones

  • Absence of Explicit Statutory Wording: Law No. 2 of 2017 does not explicitly mention the term "intellectual property," causing public policy ambiguities Statutory Grey Zone [1–81].
  • Specialized Expertise: Ongoing need to expand the pool of technical IP experts and arbitrators in Qatar Expert Capacity [1–81].
