Programma "Diffusione di best practices negli uffici giudiziari italiani" (National Interregional/Transnational Program "Diffusion of Best Practices within the Italian Judicial Offices") is a large-scale judicial administrative reform program in Italy approved in April 2008. The program is designed to re-engineer work processes, enhance operational efficiency, expand the use of information and communication technology (ICT), and strengthen public accountability across 182 judicial offices throughout Italy, supported by funding from the European Social Fund (ESF) 2007–2013 [1, 8, 21, 24–28, 40].

Summary

Initiated based on an agreement between the European Commission (DG Employment), the Italian Ministry of Justice, the Ministry of Labor, the Ministry of Public Administration (Dipartimento della Funzione Pubblica), as well as 19 Regional Governments and 2 Autonomous Provinces (Bolzano and Trento), this program represents the first bottom-up incremental administrative reform approach in the history of the Italian judiciary. The program replicates a successful pilot project developed at the Prosecutor's Office of Bolzano (Procura della Repubblica di Bolzano) under the leadership of Chief Public Prosecutor Cuno Tarfusser between 2004 and 2007.

Background

The Italian civil justice system experienced a severe efficiency crisis with a backlog of 5.6 million civil lawsuits and 3.3 million criminal trials, where the average duration of first instance civil trials reached 845 days, appeals 1,500 days, and a total of 7 years. The Doing Business 2012 report noted that commercial credit recovery time in Italy took 1,210 days compared to the OECD average of 518 days. The Bank of Italy estimated that civil justice delays cost Italy 1 percentage point of annual national GDP growth.

Program Objectives

1. Increase the quality of civil and criminal justice services. 2. Reduce organizational operating costs for courts. 3. Strengthen communication capacity and information technology (e-government). 4. Enhance accountability and transparency in the use of public resources.

Scope & 6 Activity Lines

Each participating judicial office implements 6 main activity lines [25–27]:

  • Line 1: Work process mapping and re-engineering, along with front-office improvements (Customer's Office / sportello unico).
  • Line 2: ICT technology analysis and optimization without purchasing new hardware.
  • Line 3: Drafting and publishing annual Social Budget Reports (Bilancio Sociale).
  • Line 4: Drafting Citizens' Charters (Carta dei Servizi).
  • Line 5: ISO 9001:2000 quality management certification support.
  • Line 6: Interactive judicial office website development.

Implementation & Milan Law Court Case Study

By 2012, the program covered 182 judicial offices (including 66 First Instance Prosecutor Offices, 54 Ordinary Law Courts, and 21 Courts of Appeal) with a total technical assistance budget reaching ~30 million Euros. In a sample of 65 monitored offices, 706 modernization projects were produced [48–50].

The Law Court of Milan (Tribunale di Milano) case study, under President Livia Pomodoro and Claudio Castelli, became an exemplary model through the establishment of the Tavolo della Giustizia di Milano [57–60, 70]. The Milan Court gathered united partnership and funding from the Milan Municipality, the Milan Chamber of Commerce, and the Lawyers' Order to develop the Customer's Office, barcode-labeled case file tracking, digitization of Volontaria Giurisdizione, and the Console del Magistrato for monitoring judges' performance [59–68, 71–75].

Policy Problems & Risks

1. Regional Disparities (North vs. South): Northern regions (such as Lombardia and Emilia-Romagna) possess stronger institutional thickness and local leadership compared to Southern regions constrained by budgets and criminal infiltration risks. 2. Central Coordination Weakness: Turnover of officials at the Ministry of Justice caused centralized technical coordination to weaken, leading the Dipartimento della Funzione Pubblica to take over monitoring. 3. Consultancy Dependency: The risk of innovation sustainability once the contracts for assisting consultants based on ESF funds expire.

Reform / Changes

The program proves that the Italian judiciary, as a professional organization, can be modernized through a neo-Weberian model and New Public Management (NPM) based on local participation, rather than merely "reform by decree" (riforma per decreto).
