JPW-REGULATION-GER-NETZENTGELT-2026

By Cecep Mustafa, Indonesian Judicial College (IJC) · revision 965, 2026-09-15 · wiki source · Markdown

The German Network Access Fee Subsidy Bill 2026 (German: Entwurf eines Gesetzes zur Zuschussgewährung zur Dämpfung der Netzentgelte im Jahr 2026) is a legislative proposal enacted by the Federal Government of Germany to cushion end-user electricity prices by providing a €6.5 billion subsidy for public transmission grid fees in 2026 Network Subsidy.

Overview

The Federal Government approved a draft bill allocating a direct €6.5 billion subsidy from the Climate and Transformation Fund (KTF) to Germany's four primary transmission system operators (TSOs) Climate and Transformation Fund. This measure directly targets transmission network fees, which form a substantial component of total retail electricity bills in Germany Electricity Subsidy.

Legal Basis

  • Section 24c Energy Industry Act (EnWG): A new statutory provision inserted into the EnWG to establish the legal framework for TSOs to receive and account for the subsidy EnWG.
  • Electricity Network Access Fee Ordinance (Stromnetzentgeltverordnung): Framework governing non-discriminatory network access fee calculations Stromnetzentgeltverordnung.
  • Incentive Regulation Ordinance (Anreizregulierung): Regulatory revenue cap framework from which TSOs must deduct received subsidies Incentive Regulation.

Background

Persistently high energy costs in Germany continue to impact industrial competitiveness and consumer purchasing power German Energy Costs. The federal intervention subsidizes upstream transmission network fees to lower electricity bills across all consumer tiers [Energy Background].

Key Objectives

  1. Lower end-user power prices by dampening transmission network access fees Tariff Reduction.
  2. Distribute €6.5 billion in funding proportionally among the four national TSOs TSO Allocation.
  3. Complement relief through electricity tax (Stromsteuer) reductions for manufacturing, agriculture, and forestry sectors Electricity Tax.

Core Provisions

  • KTF Funding Allocation: The €6.5 billion sum originates from the Climate and Transformation Fund (KTF) and is distributed based on each TSO's approved revenue cap KTF Funds.
  • Eligible Recipients: The four recipient TSOs are 50Hertz, Amprion, TenneT, and TransnetBW German TSOs.
  • Fee Deduction Obligation: TSOs and downstream distribution operators must deduct the subsidy from their revenue caps when calculating net fee schedules TSO Obligations.
  • October 15 Deadline: TSOs remain legally obligated to determine and publish next year's network access fees by October 15 annually Publication Deadline.

Implementation & Oversight

  • BNetzA Oversight: The Federal Network Agency (Bundesnetzagentur) supervises compliance and proper accounting of the subsidy funds Bundesnetzagentur.
  • Consumer Pass-Through: Electricity suppliers must pass along lowered network access fees to end consumers upon final parliamentary enactment Pass-Through.

Policy Considerations

  • Tariff Relief Expectations: The estimated reduction of 1.3 cents/kWh falls short of the government Coalition Agreement's broader target of 5 cents/kWh Coalition Targets.
  • Parliamentary Delays: Industry groups express concern over potential delays in parliamentary adoption beyond the October 15 publication cut-off Parliamentary Risks.
  • Single-Year Limitation: The policy applies exclusively to calendar year 2026, prompting calls for long-term structural relief such as an industrial power price cap (Industriestrompreis) Industriestrompreis.
