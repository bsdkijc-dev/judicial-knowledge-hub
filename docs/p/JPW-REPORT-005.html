JPW-REPORT-005

By Cecep Mustafa, Indonesian Judicial College (IJC) · revision 1067, 2026-09-16 · wiki source · Markdown

2021 Report of the Court on Key Performance Indicators is the official ICC report presenting the evaluation of judicial, prosecutorial, international cooperation, complementarity, and organizational performance for the full 2021 calendar year.

Summary

For the first time, the report presents a complete year of performance data (January–December 2021), covering the seven-phase judicial framework, judicial decision timeframes, international cooperation, complementarity, and organizational performance.

Legal Basis

  • Mandate of the Assembly of States Parties: Resolution ICC-ASP/13/Res.5.
  • ICC Strategic Plan 2019–2021 (Presidency, OTP, Registry).
  • Rome Statute: Articles 74, 75, 76, 81, 85, and 93(10).
  • Chambers' Practice Manual (source of judicial decision-timeframe indicators).

Background

Development of the KPI framework began at the ASP's request in 2014. Following reports published in 2015, 2016, 2017, 2019, and 2020, the 2021 report introduces methodological reforms based on Recommendation 146 of the Independent Expert Review (IER).

Objectives

  • Measure the efficiency, expeditiousness, and fairness of judicial proceedings.
  • Assess compliance with judicial decision deadlines under the Chambers' Practice Manual.
  • Measure the effectiveness of international cooperation and mutual legal assistance.
  • Assess the impact of positive complementarity at the national level.

Key Provisions

Judicial performance is measured across 7 phases:

  • Phase 1: Confirmation of Charges.
  • Phase 2: Trial Preparation.
  • Phase 3: Trial.
  • Phase 4: Trial Deliberations.
  • Phase 5: Sentencing.
  • Phase 6: Reparations.
  • Phase 7: Final Appeals.

Implementation

The Registry led data collection in coordination with the Presidency and OTP. In 2021, the OTP sent 338 requests for assistance (RFAs) to 57 partners, with a 57.19% execution rate and average execution time of 58 days. On complementarity, the OTP received 24 incoming RFAs from national authorities under Article 93(10) of the Rome Statute, responding substantively to 100% within less than three months (average 34 days). Transcript and translation service workload rose approximately 400% year-on-year while maintaining a 100% delivery rate. The Court recorded 5,956 new registrations for victim participation/reparations and 1,354 new beneficiaries of collective reparations in the *Lubanga* case. IT security systems handled 40 million threat attempts per month, and staff performance-goal-setting compliance reached 96%.

Policy Issues

  • 25 States Parties remain under-represented and 54 non-represented among Court staff as of 2021.
  • Gender imbalance persists at senior management levels (P-4 and above).
  • COVID-19 travel restrictions continued to affect missions and field cooperation.

Relationships with Other Entities

  • International Criminal Court: Institution that published the report.
  • Assembly of States Parties: Oversight body that mandated the KPI framework.
  • Registry (ICC): Organ coordinating KPI data collection.
  • Study Group on Governance: Working group that approved the shift to full calendar-year reporting in October 2021.
  • Karim A.A. Khan QC: ICC Prosecutor who began his term in 2021 and led strategic complementarity initiatives, including closing the Colombia preliminary examination and signing a Memorandum of Understanding with Venezuela.
  • Yoshimitsu Yamauchi: Special Advisor on Mutual Legal Assistance, appointed by Prosecutor Karim A.A. Khan QC to strengthen judicial cooperation relationships.
  • Thomas Lubanga Dyilo: Convicted in Case ICC-01/04-01/06; Trial Chamber II monitors implementation of the symbolic and service-based collective reparations plan.
  • Germain Katanga: Convicted in Case ICC-01/04-01/06; Trial Chamber II oversees implementation of individual and collective reparations orders.
  • Laurent Gbagbo and Charles Blé Goudé: Defendants whose acquittal was confirmed by the Appeals Chamber on 31 March 2021.
  • Bosco Ntaganda: Convicted defendant whose conviction and sentence were confirmed by the Appeals Chamber on 30 March 2021.
  • Dominic Ongwen: Convicted of 61 crimes against humanity and war crimes in Northern Uganda, sentenced to 25 years' imprisonment on 6 May 2021.
  • Ali Muhammad Ali Abd-Al-Rahman ("Ali Kushayb"): Defendant whose war crimes and crimes against humanity charges were confirmed by Pre-Trial Chamber II on 9 July 2021.
  • Paul Gicheru: Defendant whose charges of offences against the administration of justice were confirmed by Pre-Trial Chamber A on 15 July 2021.
  • Mahamat Said Abdel Kani: Defendant whose war crimes and crimes against humanity charges were confirmed by Pre-Trial Chamber II on 9 December 2021.
  • Cecep Mustafa: Not found in the available sources.
  • Ferdi Raditya Akbar: Not found in the available sources.
