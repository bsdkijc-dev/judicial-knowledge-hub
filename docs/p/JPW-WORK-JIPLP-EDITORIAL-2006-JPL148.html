Editorial: Should forum-shopping be a basket case? is a legal editorial authored by Jeremy Phillips and published in the Journal of Intellectual Property Law & Practice (Vol. 1, No. 11) by Oxford University Press on September 6, 2006 Editorial Publication [1–10].

Executive Summary

This editorial critically evaluates the stance of the European Court of Justice (ECJ) in *Roche Nederland v Primus and Goldenberg*, which rejected patent plaintiffs' practice of forum-shopping under the Brussels Convention Forum Shopping Critique [1–10]. The author argues that forcing separate actions in each defendant's home jurisdiction hinders effective cross-border patent enforcement against coordinated global infringers Patent Enforcement [1–10].

Legal Framework & Doctrines

  • Brussels Convention on Jurisdiction and the Enforcement of Judgments: The primary European treaty governing jurisdiction and enforcement in civil and commercial litigation Brussels Convention [1–10].
  • Abuse of Process Doctrine: Procedural restrictions preventing duplicate litigation over identical issues, as applied in the trade mark dispute *PepsiCo v Coca-Cola* Abuse of Process [1–10].

Background

IP litigation within public court systems frequently imposes substantial financial burdens, extended delay, and commercial uncertainty IP Litigation Cost [1–10]. Consequently, alternative private dispute resolution systems, including arbitration, mediation, and domain name dispute mechanisms under WIPO and EurID, have grown rapidly IP Arbitration [1–10].

Core Objectives

  1. Analyze the legal and practical impact of ECJ Case C-539/03 on patent holders across the European Union ECJ Impact [1–10].
  2. Examine whether forum selection (forum-shopping) serves as a constructive form of competition between court systems Jurisdictional Competition [1–10].
  3. Assess the procedural efficiency of the Brussels Convention in multi-jurisdictional patent enforcement Brussels Convention Efficiency [1–10].

Key Holdings & Observations

  • Roche Nederland v Primus and Goldenberg Holding: The ECJ ruled that patent holders must file nine independent lawsuits across nine separate jurisdictions where affiliated group entities reside Roche Holding [1–10].
  • Forum Selection Options: IP litigants face choices between traditional courts, accelerated forums (such as US "rocket dockets" or the US International Trade Commission), and between national versus Community rights in Europe Forum Selection [1–10].
  • Abuse of Process Limits: *PepsiCo v Coca-Cola* demonstrated that losing a Community trade mark action prevents a subsequent Benelux national trade mark suit targeting the same product PepsiCo Case [1–10].

Policy & Unresolved Issues

  • Enforcement Barrier: Requiring fragmented litigation across multiple jurisdictions acts as a financial deterrent for researchers and investors seeking to protect IP Enforcement Barrier [1–10].
  • Single Market Contradiction: A contradiction exists between encouraging market competition across the EU while rejecting competitive choices in judicial dispute resolution Single Market Contradiction [1–10].
