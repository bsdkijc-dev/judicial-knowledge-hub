Policy Analysis

Background & Research Framework

  • Conducted under the "Comprehensive Study on Trends in Crime Victimization and Crime Victims" by the Research and Training Institute, Ministry of Justice, Japan.
  • Aims to provide baseline data for preventing sexual victimization and evaluating support policies for victims with mental disorders or disabilities (Article 5, Act on Mental Health and Welfare for Persons with Mental Disorders or Disabilities), particularly those vulnerable to unreported crime (dark figure).

Criminal Justice Trends

  • Clearance Rates: Reported penetrative sexual assault cases rose since 2017, but the clearance rate fell to ~70% in 2023 (down from 90% in 2015–2021). Indecent assault cases increased since 2021, with clearance rates near 80% in 2023.
  • Victimization Rates: Female penetrative sexual assault victimization peaked in 2023 at 4.1 per 100,000 (male rate remained below 0.2). Female victims were predominantly aged 20–29, while male victims were mostly under 13. Acquainted victim-perpetrator relationships showed a steady increase.
  • Prosecution: Penetrative sexual assault prosecutions exceeded 600 individuals in 2023 (up from 400–500 historically). Suspensions of prosecution and non-prosecutions due to insufficient evidence increased post-2017. Indecent assault prosecutions reached 1,400 in 2023.

Criminal Case Record Analysis

  • Analyzed 176 victims with mental disorders (conviction cases 2018–2022) and 349 victims without mental disorders (2022 convictions: 335 aged <16, 14 aged >=16).
  • Disability Profiles: Over 70% of victims with mental disorders had intellectual disabilities.
  • Crime Locations: While non-disabled victimization occurred mostly outdoors, disabled victimization occurred primarily in schools, workplaces, care facilities/sanatoriums, or daycare centers.
  • Perpetrator Characteristics: Almost exclusively male. Perpetrators targeting disabled victims had a higher proportion of elderly individuals (65+) and were most frequently support personnel, outnumbering unacquainted offenders.
  • Victimization Awareness: Disabled victims and children under 16 showed low victimization awareness due to inability to comprehend the act, causing repeated incidents before detection.
  • Reporting Routes: Disabled victims typically disclosed incidents first to relatives, care workers, or school staff. Relatives/carers often conducted internal checks and prioritized medical care over police reporting.

Judicial Process & Policy Recommendations

  • High adoption of forensic interviews and witness protection measures (shielding and video-link testimonies).
  • Three Core Recommendations (NOHARA Ichiro):
  1. Sustaining and strengthening sexual crime victim support systems.
  2. Expanding regular forensic interview training for public prosecutors and raising public awareness of trained interviewers.
  3. Refining information delivery and expanding the System for Hearing and Communication of Victims' Sentiments across correctional institutions, probation offices, and regional parole boards for disabled victims and children under 16.

Interpretations & Structural Inferences

  • Demonstrates double vulnerability: exploitation by trusted figures (support personnel) in care/educational settings, combined with cognitive barriers that obscure crime recognition and lead to repeated abuse before discovery.
  • The tendency of relatives and care facilities to prioritize internal medical handling over law enforcement reporting indicates that actual sexual crime volume among disabled groups significantly exceeds official police records (dark figure of crime).
