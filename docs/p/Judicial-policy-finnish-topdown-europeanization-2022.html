Judicial-policy-finnish-topdown-europeanization-2022

By Cecep Mustafa, Indonesian Judicial College (IJC) · revision 1532, 2026-09-18 · wiki source · Markdown

The Europeanization of Finnish Constitutional Culture and Fundamental Rights Framework (Kerangka Kebijakan Europeanisasi Budaya Konstitusi dan Hak Fundamental Finlandia) is a judicial policy and constitutional law analytical framework demonstrating that constitutional culture and fundamental rights in Finland were shaped top-down through the integration of European Union norms and the jurisprudence of the European Court of Human Rights (ECtHR) and the Court of Justice of the European Union (CJEU)1.

Overview

Authored by Tomi Tuominen, Lecturer in Law at the University of Lapland, this paper critiques the bottom-up approach utilized by the European Law Institute (ELI) project in identifying common constitutional traditions (Article 6(3) TEU)2. Tuominen demonstrates that Finland did not export local traditions to the EU, but rather acted as a receiving end, absorbing European legal norms into the deep structure of its constitutional culture following its accession to the Council of Europe (1989) and the EU (1995)3.

Legal Basis

The framework is grounded in key domestic and European legal instruments:

  • Article 6(3) & Article 4(2) TEU: EU Treaty provisions regarding common constitutional traditions and national constitutional identity4.
  • Constitution of Finland 1999 (731/1999): Framework for ex ante constitutional review by the Parliamentary Committee (Section 74) and ex post judicial review by courts (Section 106)5.
  • European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR): Incorporating European fundamental rights standards into domestic law since 19906.
  • EU Charter of Fundamental Rights: Evaluative benchmark in criminal and administrative proceedings7.

Background

Finnish constitutional history evolved through four distinct eras: the Swedish era (since the 13th century), the Grand Duchy of the Russian Empire era (1809–1917), the post-1917 independence and Nordic welfare state era, and the post-1989 Europeanization era8. In 2012, Section 1 of the Constitution was amended to explicitly confirm that "Finland is a Member State of the European Union"9.

Objectives

  • Demonstrate that fundamental rights protection in Finland developed top-down through European jurisprudence integration10.
  • Analyze the operational dynamics of Finland's hybrid constitutional review system11.
  • Evaluate the alignment of Finnish law with European standards regarding free expression, market freedoms, and judicial independence12.

Scope

The scope encompasses Finnish constitutional law, European Union law, ECtHR/CJEU case law, and the institutional frameworks of both general and administrative court systems13.

Key Provisions & Hybrid Review

  • Ex Ante Review by the Parliamentary Committee (Perustuslakivaliokunta): A political committee that consults constitutional law professors to assess the constitutionality of legislative bills before enactment14.
  • Ex Post Judicial Review (Section 106): Courts may disapply an act of Parliament only if it manifests an "evident conflict" with the Constitution15.
  • Harmonized Interpretation (Verfassungskonformen Auslegung): Courts and committees adopt the doctrine of interpreting domestic legislation in conformity with the ECHR and EU law16.

Judicial Evidence (Landmark Case Law)

  • Judgment KKO 2012:58 (Jussi Halla-aho): The Supreme Court convicted politician Halla-aho of blasphemy and ethnic agitation, applying ECtHR hate speech benchmarks17.
  • Judgments KKO 2018:51 & 2018:81: Balancing privacy rights against freedom of expression on social media based on von Hannover v. Germany jurisprudence18.
  • CJEU Judgment Viking Line (Case C-438/05): Reviewing the conflict between EU freedom of establishment and fundamental labor rights within collective bargaining19.

Implementation

Implemented through daily judicial reasoning in the Supreme Court (KKO) and Supreme Administrative Court (KHO), as well as the establishment of the National Courts Administration (Tuomioistuinvirasto) in 2018 to ensure judicial autonomy20.

Policy Challenges

  • Politicization Concerns: Criticisms regarding academic dominance and political dynamics within the Parliamentary Committee21.
  • Fiscal and EU Market Conflicts: Ongoing tension between national vehicle taxation/alcohol monopoly policies and EU internal market freedoms22.

Reforms

The 2012 Constitutional Amendment entrenched EU membership into Section 1 and formally eliminated the practice of using exceptive acts (poikkeuslaki)23.
