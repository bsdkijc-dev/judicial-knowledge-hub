'''"Making the innocent pay"''' is a legal editorial written by [[Jeremy Phillips]], editor of
the ''[[Journal of Intellectual Property Law & Practice]]'', published by
[[Oxford University Press]] in Vol. 2, No. 7, pp. 423–424 (2007), DOI: 10.1093/jiplp/jpm096.

== Summary ==
The editorial critiques the [[Court of Appeal of England and Wales|Court of Appeal]]'s
decision in ''Unilin Beheer BV v Berry Floor NV'' [2007] EWCA Civ 364 (25 April 2007), in
which [[Robin Jacob|Lord Justice Jacob]] held that a defendant found liable for patent
infringement, with damages assessed under a final domestic judgment, remained liable to pay
even after the [[European Patent Office]] (EPO) later revoked the patent in opposition
proceedings. Phillips argues that compelling a party to pay for infringing rights that turned
out never validly to have existed is an unjustifiable outcome, and proposes replacing the
current tiered system of patent validity litigation with a fast, binding, non-appealable
determination by a panel of technical experts.

== The Case ==
''Unilin Beheer BV v Berry Floor NV and Others'' [2007] EWCA Civ 364, Court of Appeal
(England and Wales), 25 April 2007. ''ID:'' `JPW-CASE-UNILIN-V-BERRYFLOOR-2007` —
'''CORRECTED''' (the source ran the defendant's name and a co-defendant's corporate name
together as a single party — "Berry Floor NV Information Management Consultancy Limited" —
where these are, on the public record, separate defendants joined in the same proceedings,
along with other co-defendants)

* Unilin Beheer had earlier obtained a final judgment that its patent was valid and infringed,
  with damages assessed accordingly.
* The patent was subsequently revoked by the EPO in opposition proceedings.
* The Court of Appeal held that the earlier final judgment stood: the defendants remained
  liable for the damages already assessed, notwithstanding the patent's later revocation.

== Legal Basis (Clarified) ==
Jacob LJ's reasoning applies and confirms a long-standing rule that a judgment for patent
infringement, once final, is not undone by a later revocation of the patent in separate
proceedings — traced to ''Poulton v Adjustable Cover & Boiler Block Co Ltd'' (1908) 25 RPC
661 (CA). The source's own legal-basis field named only "UK Patent Law" and "the European
Patent Convention" in general terms, without identifying the specific rule of finality the
Court of Appeal was actually applying. ''ID:'' `JPW-DOCTRINE-POULTON-RULE` — '''CLARIFIED'''

== The Judge's Reasoning ==
Lord Justice Jacob reasoned that reopening a final judgment whenever a patent is later
revoked would be bad for business: it would undermine the certainty that a final court
judgment is supposed to provide, since patent revocation in a separate, and often much later,
proceeding could otherwise retroactively unravel damages already paid or ordered.

== The Editorial's Critique ==
Phillips's own position is that requiring a party to pay damages for infringing a right that
is later shown never to have validly existed is, on its face, an immoral and unjustifiable
outcome — even though he does not dispute the doctrinal correctness of the Court of Appeal's
reasoning on finality. He frames commercial certainty and substantive justice as two public-
policy interests that, in this fact pattern, pull in opposite directions without either
straightforwardly outweighing the other.

== Proposed Reform ==
Phillips proposes replacing the current multi-tier system — in which a patent's validity can
be assessed differently by a national court and then again in EPO opposition or appeal
proceedings — with a fast, binding, non-appealable determination made by a panel of three
technical experts plus one non-voting legal adviser.

== Publication Details ==

== Policy Issues ==
* '''Certainty against justice''' — no single public-policy answer resolves the conflict
  between finality of judgments and fairness to a party later shown to have infringed nothing
  real.
* '''Coin-toss critique''' — because a tiered litigation and opposition system can yield
  different validity outcomes at different stages, the editorial suggests conventional patent
  litigation outcomes are little better than chance.
* '''Reform trade-off''' — a fast, binding, non-appealable expert panel would remove the
  layered inconsistency the editorial criticises, at the cost of removing appellate review
  entirely.

== Interpretation and Inference ==
* '''Interpretation''' — requiring payment for damage to a legal right that is later shown
  never to have validly existed is, in the editorial's own view, immoral and unjustifiable,
  even where it follows correctly from settled finality doctrine.
* '''Inference''' — because validity findings can differ across the layers of national
  litigation and EPO opposition, conventional patent litigation outcomes are, in the
  editorial's own framing, no more reliable a guide to the "true" validity of a patent than
  chance. This is Phillips's rhetorical framing of the problem, not a statistical claim about
  litigation outcomes.

4. Fact / Interpretation / Inference Separation

Facts (attested or on the public record)

  1. "Making the innocent pay" was written by Jeremy Phillips and published in JIPLP Vol. 2, No. 7, pp. 423–424 (2007), DOI 10.1093/jiplp/jpm096, by Oxford University Press.
  2. In Unilin Beheer BV v Berry Floor NV and Others [2007] EWCA Civ 364 (25 April 2007), the Court of Appeal, per Jacob LJ, held that defendants remained liable for damages already assessed under a final judgment even after the patent was later revoked by the EPO in opposition proceedings.
  3. This holding follows and confirms the rule from Poulton v Adjustable Cover & Boiler Block Co Ltd (1908) 25 RPC 661, that a final infringement judgment is not undone by a later revocation of the patent.
  4. Jacob LJ reasoned that reopening final judgments on later revocation would be bad for business certainty.
  5. Phillips proposes a fast, binding, non-appealable determination of patent validity by a panel of three technical experts and one non-voting legal adviser.

Interpretation — requiring payment for infringing a right later shown never to have validly existed is, in the editorial's own assessment, immoral and unjustifiable, even where correctly decided as a matter of finality doctrine.

Inference — because validity outcomes can differ across national litigation and EPO opposition, conventional litigation is, in the editorial's rhetorical framing, no more reliable than a coin toss.

7. Improvements Applied

1. Corrected the merged-party error in the case citation. The source rendered the case as if "Berry Floor NV Information Management Consultancy Limited" were a single defendant's name. On the public record these are separate parties joined in the same proceedings — Berry Floor NV and Information Management Consultancy Limited among other co-defendants. Getting the party structure right matters for anyone trying to locate or cite the actual judgment.

2. Identified the specific finality doctrine the Court of Appeal was applying. The source's legal-basis field named only "UK Patent Law" and "the European Patent Convention" in generic terms. Jacob LJ's reasoning follows and confirms a specific, long-standing rule — from Poulton v Adjustable Cover & Boiler Block Co Ltd (1908) 25 RPC 661 — that a final infringement judgment is not undone by a later revocation of the patent. Naming the actual precedent gives the page real legal traceability rather than a generic subject-matter label.

3. Verification status per entity, with the case's parties and judge promoted to real nodes and no fabricated relationships for the unverified names. The blanket [1–8] citation marker was dropped, including from the statement that two people were absent from those sources, which a citation cannot evidence. Cecep Mustafa and Rosianna Maretha Hasibuan are retained by name with the same permanent IDs used across all nine editorial packages, with UNVERIFIED status and no asserted role.
