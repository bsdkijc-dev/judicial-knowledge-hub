JPW-REPORT-002

By Cecep Mustafa, Indonesian Judicial College (IJC) · revision 1000, 2026-09-16 · wiki source · Markdown

Report of the Court on Key Performance Indicators (2019) is an official interim evaluation document published by the International Criminal Court (ICC) on 25 November 2019.

Summary

The report measures the Court's qualitative and quantitative achievements against the 2019–2021 Strategic Plan, adopted by the Presidency, the Office of the Prosecutor (OTP), and the Registry on 17 July 2019. Indicators are grouped into judicial/prosecutorial performance goals and organizational performance goals.

Legal Basis

  • Mandate of the Assembly of States Parties: Resolution ICC-ASP/13/Res.5, Annex I, paragraph 7(b).
  • ICC Strategic Plan 2019–2021.
  • Rome Statute: Article 74 (Trial Chamber decisions), Article 75 (Reparations), Article 76 (Sentencing), and Article 81 (Appeal).

Background

Development of performance indicators began in 2014 at the ASP's request, to help States Parties strategically assess the ICC's performance. Following reports published in 2015, 2016, and 2017, no report was issued in 2018 due to leadership transitions and harmonization with the new strategic plan.

Objectives

  • Ensure judicial proceedings are expeditious, fair, and transparent.
  • Measure the level of victim access and participation in proceedings.
  • Assess management efficiency, budget execution, gender balance, geographical representation, and physical and IT security.

Key Provisions

Judicial performance is measured across 7 phases:

  • Phase 1: Confirmation of Charges.
  • Phase 2: Trial Preparation.
  • Phase 3: Trial.
  • Phase 4: Chamber Deliberations.
  • Phase 5: Sentencing.
  • Phase 6: Reparations.
  • Phase 7: Final Appeal.

Implementation

The Registry led Court-wide data collection. Data covers case activity from November 2017 through 30 September 2019.

Policy Issues

  • Indicators for Cooperation and Complementarity goals were not yet included in the 2019 report.
  • Need to improve gender balance at senior levels (P-4 and above); at the D-1 level, only 11.11% were women, alongside uneven representation of States Parties.
