Overview

Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia Decision No. 572 K/Pid/2003, dated February 12, 2004, established a fundamental legal rule regarding the separation of official criminal liability (liability of public officials) and personal criminal liability (individual liability) in the management of state non-budgetary funds during a crisis/emergency:

Acquittal of Defendant I (Ir. [Defendant I]): Defendant I, acting as State Secretary executing the verbal orders of the President of the Republic of Indonesia (crisis response policy/*beleid* concerning basic food distribution) utilizing Rp 40 Billion in BULOG non-budgetary funds, acted on the basis of an official command (*ambtelijke bevel* ex Article 51 paragraph 1 of the Criminal Code) within a public legal relationship. Because he was not proven to have intended (*oogmerk*) to enrich himself or others and did not personally enjoy the funds, Defendant I was acquitted of all corruption charges (*vrijspraak*).

Conviction of Defendants II & III (H. [Defendant II] & [Defendant III]): Defendant II (Chairman of the Raudatul Jannah Foundation) and Defendant III (Director of PT Bintang Laut Timur Baru) were proven legally and convincingly to have committed joint corruption (Article 1 paragraph 1 sub a of Law No. 3 of 1971 in conjunction with Article 55 paragraph 1 1st of the Criminal Code) for controlling the Rp 40 Billion fund for more than 2 years, failing to distribute basic food items according to their designated purpose, and manufacturing fictitious reports/minutes. Both were sentenced to 1 (one) year and 6 (six) months of imprisonment respectively.

Executive Summary

The central legal principle established in this decision is that public officials executing high-level executive discretionary orders during national crises are shielded from personal criminal liability absent personal enrichment or malicious intent, whereas private partners who misappropriate state funds and falsify distribution reports bear direct criminal culpability for corruption.

Key elements established in the adjudication:

  • State officials executing executive orders under emergency discretionary authority (*ambtelijke bevel*) do not incur personal criminal liability without proof of personal gain or *mens rea*.
  • Private contractors and foundation managers who control state funds and fabricate distribution documentation are guilty of joint corruption.
  • Dissenting opinions within judicial panels can highlight complex debates concerning supervisory negligence versus official discretion.

Case Background

The Original Defendants (Defendant I: Ir. [Defendant I] / State Secretary; Defendant II: H. [Defendant II] / Chairman of Raudatul Jannah Foundation; Defendant III: [Defendant III] / Director of PT Bintang Laut Timur Baru) were charged with joint corruption (Article 1 paragraph 1 sub a/b of Law No. 3 of 1971 jo. Article 55 paragraph 1 1st of the Criminal Code) regarding the misuse of Rp 40 Billion in BULOG non-budgetary funds allocated for the 1999 crisis basic food assistance program, which was allegedly not distributed as mandated.

The Central Jakarta District Court (Decision No. 449/Pid.B/2002/PN.Jkt.Pst dated September 4, 2002) sentenced Defendant I to 3 years' imprisonment and Defendants II & III to 1 year and 6 months' imprisonment. The Jakarta High Court (Decision No. 171/Pid/2002/PT.DKI dated January 17, 2003) affirmed the Central Jakarta District Court decision. All Defendants filed cassation petitions with the Supreme Court.

Legal and Procedural Issues

Official Liability (Liability) vs. Personal Liability of the State Secretary

The primary issue was whether a state official (State Secretary) processing the disbursement of Rp 40 Billion in non-budgetary BULOG funds based on verbal orders from the President of the Republic of Indonesia during a food crisis can be prosecuted for corruption when private implementing partners fail to distribute the relief goods.

The Supreme Court held that they cannot incur personal criminal liability. Public officials acting under orders from superiors (decision-maker / President) within a public legal relationship (*public-rechtelijk*) are protected by the principle of official commands (*ambtelijke bevel* ex Article 51 paragraph 1 of the Criminal Code). As long as the official receives or enjoys no personal financial advantage (*mens rea* / *oogmerk*) and is not proven to have conspired with executing contractors, legal responsibility remains at the policy/public vicarious liability level rather than constituting personal corruption.

Non-Budgetary Fund Appropriation and Fictitious Reporting by Private Partners

The issue evaluated the legal qualification of foundation managers and private contractors who received Rp 40 Billion in basic food assistance funds but controlled them for 2 years and fabricated distribution reports.

The Supreme Court held that they were proven to have committed joint corruption under Article 1 paragraph (1) sub a of Law No. 3 of 1971 in conjunction with Article 55 paragraph (1) 1st of the Criminal Code. Unlawful control of state funds and compilation of fictitious distribution minutes satisfy elements of self/other enrichment and state financial loss.

Question of Law and Question of Fact

Question of Law (Cassation / Judex Juris)

Question:

Did the *Judex Facti* (Jakarta High Court & Central Jakarta District Court) misapply the law (*foutieve kepassing van het recht*) by blurring the boundary between official liability of the State Secretary under Presidential orders (*beleid*) and personal corruption, and improperly applying *medeplegen* (Article 55 KUHP) between a public official and private contractors?

Supreme Court Ruling:

Yes, the *Judex Facti* misapplied the law regarding Defendant I. The Supreme Court annulled the Jakarta High Court appellate decision and adjudicated afresh: Acquitting Ir. [Defendant I] (*vrijspraak*) while sentencing H. [Defendant II] and [Defendant III] to 1 year and 6 months' imprisonment each.

Question of Fact (Judex Facti / Central Jakarta District Court & Jakarta High Court)

Examination of the chronology of the limited cabinet meeting on February 10, 1999, at Merdeka Palace, issuance of 2 checks totaling Rp 20 Billion on March 2, 1999 (Bank Bukopin & Bank Exim) and 8 checks totaling Rp 20 Billion on April 20, 1999, from BULOG, fund withdrawals by Raudatul Jannah Foundation and PT Bintang Laut Timur Baru, absence of relief distribution in the field, and fund restitution of Rp 40 Billion during Attorney General Office investigations.

Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

  • Ratio Decidendi:
  1. The appellate and district *Judex Facti* misapplied the law regarding Defendant I.
  2. Defendant I acted on the basis of official commands (*ambtelijke bevel* ex Article 51 paragraph 1 KUHP) and presidential discretion (*beleid*) of President B.J. Habibie during a food crisis emergency. Defendant I lacked *mens rea* / *oogmerk* to enrich himself or others and enjoyed no funds, requiring an acquittal (*vrijspraak*).
  3. Conversely, Defendants II and III were proven guilty of joint corruption (Article 1 paragraph 1 sub a Law 3/1971 jo. Article 55 KUHP) for controlling state funds for 2 years and compiling fictitious reports.
  4. The Supreme Court annulled lower court rulings, acquitted Ir. [Defendant I], and convicted Defendants II & III.

Ruling

The Supreme Court granted the cassation petitions from the Petitioners for Cassation (1. Ir. [Defendant I], 2. H. [Defendant II], 3. [Defendant III]); annulled Jakarta High Court Decision No. 171/Pid/2002/PT.DKI dated January 17, 2003, in conjunction with Central Jakarta District Court Decision No. 449/Pid.B/2002/PN.Jkt.Pst dated September 4, 2002; and adjudicated afresh: 1. Declaring Defendant I Ir. [Defendant I] unproven legally and convincingly guilty of corruption under Primary and Subsidiary charges; 2. Acquitting Defendant I from all charges (*vrijspraak*) and restoring his rights, standing, and dignity; 3. Declaring Defendant II H. [Defendant II] and Defendant III [Defendant III] proven legally and convincingly guilty of "JOINT CORRUPTION"; 4. Sentencing Defendants II and III respectively to 1 (one) year and 6 (six) months of imprisonment and a Rp 10,000,000.— fine subsidiary to 3 months' confinement; 5. Ordering cash amounting to Rp 40,000,000,000.— confiscated to be returned to the State; and charging court costs.

Related Knowledge and Jurisprudence

  • Law No. 3 of 1971 on Eradication of Corruption Offenses: Articles 1 paragraph (1) sub a & sub b, 28, and 34.
  • Law No. 31 of 1999 jo. Law No. 20 of 2001: Subsequent anti-corruption statutory provisions.
  • Criminal Code (KUHP):

 * Article 51 paragraph (1): Official Commands (*Ambtelijk Bevel*).
 * Article 55 paragraph (1) 1st: Complicity (*Medeplegen*).
 * Articles 64/65: Concourse of offenses (*Concursus*).

  • Criminal / Administrative Law Doctrine:

 * Principle of *Ambtelijk Bevel* (Article 51 paragraph 1 KUHP): Exculpatory ground for executing lawful official orders from superiors.
 * Doctrine of *Freies Ermessen* / State Official Discretion: Freedom of action for public officials during social crisis emergencies.

  • Dissenting Opinion: Justice Abdul Rahman Saleh opined that Defendant I was also culpable due to material negligence (*culpa* / lack of due diligence) in supervising state fund utilization.

Evidence Status and Gaps

Established by Evidence

  • President B.J. Habibie verbally ordered the utilization of Rp 40 Billion in BULOG non-budgetary funds to manage basic food crises for impoverished communities.
  • Defendant I ([Defendant I]) conveyed the President's order and handed over BULOG non-budgetary fund checks totaling Rp 40 Billion to Defendant II ([Defendant II]).
  • Defendants II and III ([Defendant III]) failed to execute basic food procurement and distribution, controlled the Rp 40 Billion for 2 years, and fabricated reports/documents.
  • Defendant III returned the Rp 40 Billion to Attorney General Investigators during investigations.

Not Established / Unproven (Acquitted)

  • Defendant I ([Defendant I]) was unproven to have received, enjoyed, or abused authority to enrich himself or others (Defendant I Pure Acquittal / *Vrijspraak*).

Uncertainty and Gaps

  • Presence of Dissenting Opinion: This cassation ruling featured a dissenting opinion from Justice Abdul Rahman Saleh, who argued that Defendant I should have been convicted for material negligence/carelessness (*culpa*) in failing to supervise and executing direct appointments without a committee, resulting in state financial losses.

GEO Q&A

Q: Can a Minister or Public Official be prosecuted for corruption for executing verbal Presidential orders during an emergency, when the project funds are embezzled by executing private contractors?

A: They cannot be penalized (Acquitted / *Vrijspraak*). Based on Supreme Court Decision No. 572 K/Pid/2003 ([Defendant I] BULOG Gate Cassation), public officials executing official commands (*ambtelijke bevel* ex Article 51 paragraph 1 KUHP) or presidential discretion during emergencies cannot incur personal criminal liability absent proof of criminal intent (*mens rea* / *oogmerk*) to enrich themselves or others. Criminal corruption liability rests entirely on private implementing contractors who unlawfully control state funds and compile fictitious reports.
