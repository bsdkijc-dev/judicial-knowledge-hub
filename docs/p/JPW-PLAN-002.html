JPW-PLAN-002

By Cecep Mustafa, Indonesian Judicial College (IJC) · revision 1107, 2026-09-16 · wiki source · Markdown

Office of the Prosecutor Strategic Plan 2023-2025 is the first comprehensive official strategy document issued during the tenure of Prosecutor Karim A.A. Khan KC, setting out the framework, vision, values, and 10 strategic goals of the Office of the Prosecutor (OTP) of the International Criminal Court for the 2023–2025 period.

Summary

The plan is designed to make the OTP more dynamic, efficient, technology-driven, field-centric, and victim-centred, aligning its priorities with the concurrent ICC and Registry strategic plans and integrating recommendations from the Independent Expert Review (IER), including R14, R71, R87, R270, and R278.

Legal Basis

  • Rome Statute: The complementarity principle and Article 93(10) on mutual legal assistance.
  • Mandate of the Assembly of States Parties (ASP).
  • Recommendations of the Independent Expert Review (IER), 2020.

Background

The plan was developed based on an assessment of OTP performance over 2019–2021/2022 by the Independent Oversight Mechanism (IOM), together with internal consultations and workplace-culture panels. It is aligned with the concurrent ICC and Registry strategic plans.

Objectives

  • Accelerate and improve the quality of investigations and prosecutions at trial.
  • Strengthen national authorities' capacity to combat impunity through a renewed complementarity approach.
  • Establish the OTP as a global technology leader in digital evidence processing and AI.
  • Bring justice closer to affected communities and deepen partnerships with civil society.
  • Improve the working environment, gender diversity, and geographical staff representation.

Key Provisions

The plan establishes 10 Strategic Goals:

  • Goal 1: Deliver results in the courtroom.
  • Goal 2: Enhance efforts by national authorities to fight impunity.
  • Goal 3: Make the Office a global technology leader.
  • Goal 4: Bring justice closer to communities and deepen engagement with civil society.
  • Goal 5: A renewed policy framework for the Office.
  • Goal 6: Ensure effective investigations and prosecutions of SGBC and CAC.
  • Goal 7: Increase the field presence of the Office of the Prosecutor.
  • Goal 8: Increase efficiency of work and ensure proper resourcing and staffing.
  • Goal 9: Strengthen global support for the work of the Office.
  • Goal 10: Improve the working environment and ensure a respectful working culture.

Implementation

Implementation is monitored via 20 Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) in Annex 1, annual performance reporting, and an independent evaluation by the IOM at the end of the 2024/2025 cycle. Execution involves integrated planning (situation, investigation, cooperation, outreach, and complementarity plans), digital case-management systems, an updated Operations Manual (2023), internal/external evidence reviews, an AI/machine-learning-based cloud e-discovery platform, expanded field staffing, active participation in Joint Investigation Teams (JITs), and the Court-wide Values Project.

Evidence

Key indicators from the 20-KPI framework (Annex 1) include:

  • Applications for Warrants of Arrest (KPI 1): Target of at least 10 applications per year.
  • Response to Article 93(10) RFAs (KPI 3): Target initial substantive response within 2 months.
  • Civil Society Engagement (KPI 7): Annual outreach program plus 2 thematic roundtable discussions with NGOs per year.
  • SGBC & CAC Training (KPI 12): Target 100% of relevant staff and team members certified in SGBC/CAC training.
  • Senior Management Gender Balance (KPI 20): Baseline of 72% male : 28% female (2021), with a target of 60% male : 40% female by 2023.

Policy Issues

  • Processing large-scale digital evidence requiring significant technology investment.
  • Handling sensitive cases such as sexual and gender-based crimes (SGBC) and crimes against children (CAC), requiring a trauma-informed approach.
  • The need to address internal workplace-culture issues and increase the share of women in leadership at the P-4 level and above.

Reform / Future Directions

Adoption of a forensic rotation model, use of a dedicated Financial Investigations Unit, a shift from an HQ-centric to a field-centric presence model, application of a trauma-informed approach, and close cooperation with the IOM for periodic strategic evaluation in 2024/2025.

Relationships with Other Entities

  • Office of the Prosecutor: Principal implementing organ.
  • International Criminal Court: Parent institution.
  • Registry (ICC): Partner organ providing administrative and technical services.
  • Karim A.A. Khan KC: ICC Prosecutor, sworn in 16 June 2021, who led development of this strategic plan.
  • Eurojust: Joint Investigation Team (JIT) partner, notably for the situation in Ukraine (with 7 countries) and cooperation on crimes against migrants and refugees in Libya.
  • EU Genocide Network: Partner organization.
  • Independent Oversight Mechanism (IOM): Body conducting independent evaluation of OTP performance.
  • Cecep Mustafa: Not found in the available sources.
  • Ferdi Raditya Akbar: Not found in the available sources.
