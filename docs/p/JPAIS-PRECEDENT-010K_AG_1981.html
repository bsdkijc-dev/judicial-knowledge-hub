Overview

Supreme Court Decision No. 10 K/AG/1981 dated January 20, 1982, establishes an important legal principle regarding the absolute jurisdictional limits of Religious Courts in disputes involving joint marital property. The Supreme Court outlined that matters concerning the division of gono-gini goods (joint marital property) fall under the authority of the District Court (General Judiciary) rather than the Religious Courts.

Case Background

The Petitioner / Defendant for Cassation ([Party A]) filed a petition for divorce (talak) against his wife, the Respondent / Plaintiff for Cassation ([Party B]) at the Wates Religious Court, citing domestic disputes and alleged disobedience by the wife. The Wates Religious Court (Determination No. 169/1979 dated June 13, 1979) rejected the Petitioner's divorce petition. On appeal, the Surakarta Islamic High Court (Decision No. 03/1980 dated April 14, 1980) annulled the Wates Religious Court determination and granted [Party A]'s request to pronounce talak. [Party B] filed a cassation petition to the Supreme Court, raising objections concerning household disputes, maintenance claims, and requesting that jointly acquired marital property be divided equally.

Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

  • Ratio Decidendi: Matters concerning the division of gono-gini goods fall within the competence of the District Court; therefore, the Islamic High Court committed no error in law by omitting the division of marital property from its ruling on the divorce petition. Objections raised by the Petitioner for Cassation regarding the evidentiary evaluation of facts cannot be reviewed at the cassation level.
  • Ruling: Rejecting the cassation petition filed by the Plaintiff for Cassation ([Party B]) with a modification to the dispositive portion of the Surakarta Islamic High Court Decision No. 03/1980, such that the full order reads: (1) Accepting the appellant's appeal; (2) Annulment of Wates Religious Court Determination No. 169/1979; (3) Independently Adjudicating: (a) Granting the petitioner's request to pronounce talak; (b) Ordering the Wates Religious Court to reopen sessions to witness [Party A]'s talak pronouncement to [Party B]; (4) Ordering the Plaintiff for Cassation / Respondent to bear procedural costs.
