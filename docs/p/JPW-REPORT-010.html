JPW-REPORT-010

By Cecep Mustafa, Indonesian Judicial College (IJC) · revision 1098, 2026-09-16 · wiki source · Markdown

Twenty-Sixth Report of the Prosecutor of the International Criminal Court to the United Nations Security Council on the Situation in Libya is the 26th periodic report submitted by the Office of the Prosecutor (OTP) to the UN Security Council on 8 November 2023, pursuant to UNSC Resolution 1970 (2011), presenting progress on the updated investigative strategy, the near-conclusion of the 2014–2020 military operations line of inquiry, and a roadmap toward completion of OTP activities in Libya.

Summary

This is the third periodic report since the launch of the updated strategy and roadmap in the April 2022 report (23rd Report). The report opens with condolences for the flooding disaster caused by Storm Daniel, which struck Derna in September 2023.

Legal Basis

  • UN Security Council Resolution 1970 (2011), invoking Chapter VII of the UN Charter.
  • Rome Statute: Article 15 (Communication of Information) and Article 42(6) (Prosecutor Self-Recusal).

Background

Since receiving the UNSC referral in 2011, the OTP has investigated international crimes in Libya. The 26th report is the third periodic update measuring progress against the benchmarks set in the April 2022 roadmap.

Objectives

  • Optimize allocation of regular-budget resources and seconded expert staff.
  • Strengthen empowerment of victims, witnesses, and NGOs through evidence-documentation Guidelines training.
  • Advance direct complementarity partnerships with Libyan law enforcement authorities.
  • Expand cooperation with the Joint Team, Europol, and third states.

Key Provisions

The four lines of inquiry include:

  • 2011 Violence: Focused on executing the outstanding public arrest warrant against Saif Al-Islam Gaddafi.
  • Detention Facilities: Investigation of torture and violence in detention facilities, supported by clinical forensic medical capacity.
  • 2014–2020 Military Operations: This line of inquiry is approaching completion.
  • Crimes Against Migrants: Investigation of human trafficking, enslavement, and extortion in cooperation with the Joint Team.

Implementation

The OTP launched the OTPLink portal, migrated evidence to an AI/machine-learning-based cloud system (Project Harmony), and recruited 7 psychosocial support specialists. The Prosecutor, Karim A.A. Khan QC, voluntarily recused himself under Article 42(6) of the Rome Statute from lines of inquiry posing a potential conflict of interest arising from his prior role as defense counsel; Deputy Prosecutor Nazhat Shameem Khan assumed full oversight of the affected lines of inquiry.

The Unified Libya Team conducted over 15 missions to three regions, collected over 4,000 items of evidence (audio, video, forensic materials, satellite imagery), conducted 20+ witness interviews, screened 30+ potential witnesses, and held training on evidence-documentation Guidelines together with the International Commission of Jurists.

Evidence

  • Collection of over 4,000 new items of evidence.
  • Analysis of hundreds of witness statements in cooperation with the Joint Team.
  • Use of forensic clinical medical capacity to assess torture-related injuries.
  • Recording of over 12,000 migrants intercepted at sea and returned to Libya in 2023.
  • More than 2,000 migrants reported dead or missing along the Central Mediterranean Route.

Policy Issues

  • Delays in visa issuance by local Libyan authorities for operational-level investigators.
  • Regular-budget constraints affecting the OTP's capacity to analyze financial flows and proceeds of crime.
  • Psychological risks and re-traumatization concerns for victims and witnesses.
  • Humanitarian crisis following the Storm Daniel flooding disaster in Derna in September 2023.

Relationships with Other Entities

  • Office of the Prosecutor (ICC): Organ that published the report.
  • United Nations Security Council: Recipient of the periodic report.
  • Joint Team: Joint investigative team on crimes against migrants.
  • OTPLink: Digital platform for receiving new evidence and Article 15 communications.
  • Karim A.A. Khan QC: ICC Prosecutor who voluntarily recused himself under Article 42(6) from any line of inquiry posing a potential conflict of interest arising from his prior role as defense counsel.
  • Nazhat Shameem Khan: ICC Deputy Prosecutor who assumed full oversight responsibility for investigations in the lines of inquiry affected by the Prosecutor's recusal.
  • Zeiad S. S. Daghim: Ambassador of Libya to the Netherlands, who confirmed the Presidential Council's directive to expand judicial cooperation with the OTP, including technical visas and forensic support.
  • Mohamed al-Menfi: President of the Presidential Council of Libya, who instructed the Libyan Embassy in the Netherlands to provide full cooperation to the OTP.
  • Saif Al-Islam Gaddafi: Sole subject of an outstanding public arrest warrant related to crimes committed against protesters and opponents of the Gaddafi regime in 2011.
  • Cecep Mustafa: Not found in the available sources.
  • Ferdi Raditya Akbar: Not found in the available sources.
