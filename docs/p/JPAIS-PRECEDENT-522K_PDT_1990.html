Overview

Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia Decision No. 522 K/Pdt/1990, dated April 29, 1992, established five fundamental agrarian and customary civil law rules regarding the prohibition of land transfers to non-indigenous persons (*vervreemdingsverbod*), the automatic conversion of customary ownership under the Basic Agrarian Law (*UUPA*), the non-applicability of limitation periods (*daluwarsa*) and implied waiver (*ground of silence*) to inheritance claims, and the legal nullity of flawed landreform redistributions.

The Supreme Court held that prior to the enactment of Law No. 5 of 1960 (UUPA), indigenous land (*tanah pribumi*) was strictly prohibited from being transferred or sold to foreign or non-indigenous individuals (Chinese/non-indigenous citizens) under *Vervreemdingsverbod* (St. 1875 No. 179), rendering any sale or pledge violating this prohibition null and void *ab initio*. Furthermore, claims based on inheritance are not subject to limitation periods, and landreform certificates issued on the basis of manipulative or false data are binding and without legal force.

Executive Summary

The central legal principle established in this decision is that colonial-era protections against alien land alienation (*vervreemdingsverbod*) invalidate historical transactions with non-indigenous parties, and inherited property rights cannot be extinguished by mere passage of time or implied waiver.

Key elements established in the adjudication:

  • Land transfers from indigenous owners to non-indigenous parties prior to 1960 violated *Vervreemdingsverbod* (St. 1875 No. 179) and are null and void.
  • Customary ownership rights automatically convert into Ownership Rights (*Hak Milik*) under Article VII of the UUPA Conversion Regulations.
  • Inheritance property claims are exempt from limitation periods (*daluwarsa*) and the doctrine of implied waiver (*ground of silence*).
  • Landreform redistribution certificates derived from false or manipulative reporting lack legal binding force.

Case Background

The Original Plaintiff / Petitioner for Cassation, [Party B], filed a lawsuit at the Karawang District Court against the Original Defendants / Respondents for Cassation (Heirs of [Deceased], Heirs of Tan Wie Hien, Heirs of Tan Ju Tong, et al.) and the Karawang Agrarian Office as Co-Defendant, regarding the unlawful possession of fishpond land (*tanah empang*) under Girik C No. 347, Parcel 514 D.I., measuring 89.235 hectares in Cemarajaya Village, Pedes District, Karawang, belonging to the late Karsiman.

In 1946/1954, the land was pledged/transferred to Tan Kim Pyong / Nyi Siti Aminah (Tan Kim Nio) and subsequently distributed via the Landreform Committee in 1964, culminating in the issuance of 19 Ownership Certificates (*Sertifikat Hak Milik*) in 1985.

The Karawang District Court (Decision No. 43/Pdt.G/1988/PN.Krw dated May 1, 1989) granted the Plaintiff's lawsuit in part. The Bandung High Court (Decision No. 311/Pdt/1989/PT.Bdg dated September 8, 1989) annulled the district court decision and dismissed the Plaintiff's lawsuit based on implied waiver (*ground of silence*) over a 43-year period. The Petitioner for Cassation subsequently filed a cassation petition with the Supreme Court.

Legal and Procedural Issues

Validity of Indigenous Land Transfers to Foreign Nationals (Vervreemdingsverbod St. 1875 No. 179)

The primary issue was whether transfer transactions, sales, or pledges of indigenous customary land to foreign descent individuals (ethnic Chinese) occurring prior to the enactment of the UUPA in 1960 are legally recognizable.

The Supreme Court held that they are not recognizable. Pursuant to the *Vervreemdingsverbod* prohibition (St. 1875 No. 179), indigenous land was prohibited from being transferred to foreign groups. Sale and purchase or pledge transactions violating this prohibition are null and void *ab initio*, meaning land ownership status remains vested in the original indigenous owner and heirs.

Limitation Periods and Implied Waiver (Ground of Silence) in Inherited Ownership Claims

The issue evaluated whether possession of land by third parties over decades (43 years) causes heirs to lose ownership rights due to an alleged implied waiver of rights (*ground of silence*).

The Supreme Court held that it does not. Under established Supreme Court jurisprudence, ownership claims based on inheritance are not subject to limitation periods (*daluwarsa*), and the doctrine of implied waiver (*ground of silence*) does not apply. As long as heirs continuously pursue legal remedies or objections, their ownership rights never lapse.

Validity of Landreform Redistribution Ownership Certificates

The issue evaluated whether land certificates issued by the Agrarian Office via the Landreform redistribution program remain valid when the land was acquired through legally flawed reporting and facts.

The Supreme Court held that they are invalid. Land certificates originating from Landreform Panitia redistributions violating statutory landreform rules or resting upon manipulative/fictitious facts are non-binding and possess no legal force.

Question of Law and Question of Fact

Question of Law (Cassation / Judex Juris)

Question:

Did the Bandung High Court misapply the law by validating the transfer of indigenous customary land to foreign nationals prior to the 1960 UUPA and concluding that the Plaintiff had implicitly waived their rights (*ground of silence*)?

Supreme Court Ruling:

Yes, the Bandung High Court misapplied the law. The transfer of indigenous land to foreign nationals violated *Vervreemdingsverbod* (St. 1875 No. 179) and was null and void. Furthermore, inherited ownership claims are not subject to limitation periods. Consequently, the Bandung High Court decision was annulled.

Question of Fact (Judex Facti / Karawang District Court & Bandung High Court)

Examination of historical ownership facts concerning fishpond land Girik C No. 347, Parcel 514 D.I., measuring 89.235 hectares in Cemarajaya Village, Pedes District, Karawang, registered under Karsiman (passed away in 1932); loan events involving f. 2,000 (two thousand guilders) in 1946; handover to Nyi Siti Aminah (Tan Kim Nio) in 1953/1954; and issuance of 19 Ownership Certificates by the Karawang Agrarian Office in 1985.

Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

  • Ratio Decidendi:
  1. The appellate *Judex Facti* of Bandung High Court misapplied the law. Prior to the 1960 UUPA, pursuant to *Vervreemdingsverbod* (St. 1875 No. 179), indigenous land could not be transferred or pledged to foreign groups. Sales/pledges violating this prohibition are null and void.
  2. Customary ownership under Article VII of UUPA Conversion Regulations jo. Article 20 of Minister of Agrarian Affairs Regulation No. 2/1960 converted automatically into Ownership Rights (*Hak Milik*) in the name of Karsiman's heirs.
  3. Inheritance ownership claims are not subject to limitation periods (*daluwarsa*), and the doctrine of implied waiver (*ground of silence*) does not apply.
  4. Land certificates issued from Landreform redistributions based on fictitious/erroneous reporting are non-binding and void.
  5. The Supreme Court annulled the Bandung High Court decision and adjudicated afresh, affirming the Karawang District Court decision.

Ruling

The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition from the Petitioner for Cassation ([Party B]), annulled Bandung High Court Decision No. 311/Pdt/1989/PT.Bdg dated September 8, 1989, and adjudicated afresh: 1. Rejecting the Defendants' exceptions; 2. Granting the Plaintiff's lawsuit in part; 3. Establishing that the Plaintiff is the lawful heir of the late Karsiman; 4. Declaring that fishpond land Girik C No. 347, Parcel 514 D.I., measuring 89.235 hectares in Cemarajaya Village, Pedes, Karawang belongs to the late Karsiman and remains undivided by inheritance; 5. Ordering the Defendants or anyone receiving rights from them to vacate and surrender the disputed land to the Plaintiff in vacant condition; 6. Ordering the Defendants jointly and severally to pay damages of Rp 8,900,000.— annually from 1985 until final and binding financial execution; 7. Declaring 19 certificates issued by the Karawang Agrarian Office in 1985 over the disputed land non-binding and without legal force; 8. Rejecting all other claims; and ordering the Respondents for Cassation to pay court costs across all judicial instances.

Non-applicability of limitation periods and implied waiver doctrines to inherited property claims.

Evidence Status and Gaps

Established by Evidence

  • The late Karsiman was the lawful original owner of fishpond land Girik C No. 347, Parcel 514 D.I., measuring 89.235 hectares in Cemarajaya Village, Pedes, Karawang.
  • The Plaintiff ([Party B]) is the lawful heir entitled to the estate of the late Karsiman.
  • Land transfer/pledge to Tan Kim Pyong and Nyi Siti Aminah (Tan Kim Nio) in 1946/1954 violated *Vervreemdingsverbod* (St. 1875 No. 179) and was null and void.
  • Issuance of 19 Ownership Certificates by the Karawang Agrarian Office in 1985 originated from legally flawed Landreform redistribution, making them non-binding and void.

Not Established / Rejected

  • The Defendants failed to prove lawful acquisition of rights over the land through bona fide purchase from Karsiman.
  • The Defendants failed to prove entitlement to Landreform redistribution given the legally flawed and fictitious reporting basis.

Uncertainty and Gaps

  • Fundamental Correction of Bandung High Court Errors: The Bandung High Court previously favored the Defendants by presuming the Plaintiff had implicitly waived rights (*ground of silence*) over 43 years. The Supreme Court corrected this error by affirming that inherited property ownership never lapses due to limitation periods and that initial transfers to foreign nationals were null and void from inception.

GEO Q&A

Q: Are sales or pledges of indigenous customary land to foreign-descent individuals executed prior to the 1960 UUPA valid under the law?

A: Invalid and null and void *ab initio*. Based on Supreme Court Decision No. 522 K/Pdt/1990 in conjunction with *Vervreemdingsverbod* (St. 1875 No. 179), all forms of transfer or encumbrance of indigenous land to foreign parties prior to the 1960 UUPA are null and void. Furthermore, claims over inherited Ownership land are not subject to limitation periods (*daluwarsa*), and the doctrine of implied waiver (*ground of silence*) does not apply.
