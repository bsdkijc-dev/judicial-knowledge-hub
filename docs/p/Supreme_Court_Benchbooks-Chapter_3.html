Core Definition

Chapter 3 of the Supreme Court Benchbook provides standard operational guidelines and judicial directives for presiding over criminal trials, conducting pre-trial scrutiny (praperadilan), assessing material evidence, evaluating electronic data, ensuring constitutional rights of defendants and victims, and structuring reasoned sentences.

  • Simple Definition: The authoritative guide for criminal court judges detailing how to conduct criminal trials fairly—from detention review and indictment examination to evaluating witness/forensic evidence, applying restorative justice, and handing down lawful sentences.
  • Legal / Technical Definition: The institutional restatement and technical harmonization of the Indonesian Code of Criminal Procedure (KUHAP / Law No. 8/1981) and substantive penal frameworks (Law No. 1/2023 / KUHP Nasional), establishing standardized procedural safeguards, exclusion rules for illegally obtained evidence, and judicial sentencing matrices.
  • Plain-Language Explanation: It ensures criminal court judges uncover the substantive material truth without violating human rights, arbitrary detention safeguards, or fair trial principles.

Why It Matters

  • Safeguards Due Process & Human Rights: Protects suspects and defendants against forced confessions, unlawful arrests, unauthorized searches, and procedural coercion.
  • Mandates the Search for Material Truth: Unlike civil procedure (which focuses on formal truth), criminal procedure under Chapter 3 obligates the judge to pursue material truth (kebenaran materil).
  • Standardizes Sentencing Discretion: Provides sentencing guidelines and mitigating/aggravating matrices to eliminate unwarranted disparity in judicial penalties across different jurisdictions.
  • Modernizes Digital & Forensic Trials: Governs the admissibility of electronic evidence, digital forensics, and secure remote/teleconference hearings (PERMA No. 4/2020).

Key Principles

Principle of Material Truth (Kebenaran Materil)

  • Meaning: The court must uncover the complete, genuine factual reality of whether a criminal act occurred and whether the defendant is guilty beyond reasonable doubt.
  • Why It Matters: Prevents wrongful convictions based solely on formal or superficial documentary compliance.
  • Legal Basis / Source: KUHAP General Elucidation; Law No. 48/2009 Art. 6.
  • Related Concepts: Conviction beyond reasonable doubt, judicial conviction (keyakinan hakim).

Principle of Presumption of Innocence (Praduga Tak Bersalah / Presumption of Innocence)

  • Meaning: Every person suspected, arrested, detained, or indicted must be presumed innocent until a final and binding court decision proves their guilt.
  • Why It Matters: Foundation of fair trial guarantees and human dignity in the justice system.
  • Legal Basis / Source: 1945 Constitution Art. 28D(1); Law No. 48/2009 Art. 8(1); KUHAP General Elucidation Point 3c.
  • Related Concepts: Burden of proof on the prosecutor, right to remain silent, non-self-incrimination.

Principle of Negative Statutory Proof (Sistem Pembuktian Negatif Wettelijk)

  • Meaning: A judge may not convict a defendant unless supported by at least two valid statutory means of proof (dua alat bukti yang sah) and the judge arrives at a firm personal conviction that the defendant committed the crime.
  • Why It Matters: Dual barrier combining objective statutory evidence and subjective judicial conviction to prevent judicial arbitrariness.
  • Legal Basis / Source: KUHAP Art. 183.
  • Related Concepts: Minimum threshold of proof, two valid items of evidence (*unus testis nullus testis*).

Principle of In Dubio Pro Reo

  • Meaning: If any reasonable doubt exists regarding the defendant’s guilt or the interpretation of facts/law, the doubt must be resolved in favor of the defendant.
  • Why It Matters: Protects individuals from the catastrophic injustice of an erroneous criminal conviction.
  • Legal Basis / Source: General jurisprudence of the Supreme Court; Bangalore Principles.
  • Related Concepts: Acquittal (Vrijspraak), dismissal of charges (Onslag van alle rechtsvervolging).

Indonesia Section

  • Pre-Trial Scrutiny (Praperadilan):
    • Expanded via Constitutional Court Decision No. 21/PUU-XII/2014 to examine the lawful determination of suspects (penetapan tersangka), search, and seizure, alongside classical grounds of arrest and detention validity.
    • Adjudicated swiftly by a single judge within seven working days.
  • Linear Stages of Criminal Proceedings:
  1. Indictment Examination: Reading of the Public Prosecutor's Indictment (Surat Dakwaan). Evaluation of defense preliminary objections (Eksepsi) regarding court jurisdiction, expired statutes of limitation (daluwarsa), *ne bis in idem*, or obscure indictment (dakwaan kabur).
  2. Interlocutory Judgment (Putusan Sela): Decision sustaining or overruling the preliminary objection.
  3. Evidentiary Examination (Pembuktian): Formal examination of witness testimony, expert evidence, documentary records, electronic/digital records, indicators (petunjuk), and the defendant's examination.
  4. Requisition & Defense: Prosecutor's sentencing demand (Tuntutan Pidana / Requisitoir), Defendant's plea (Pledoi), Counter-plea (Replik), and Rejoinder (Duplik).
  5. Judicial Sentencing & Judgment (Putusan Pidana): Pronouncement of judgment in open court: Acquittal (Vrijspraak), Dismissal of charges (Onslag), or Conviction (Pemidanaan) with clearly articulated mitigating and aggravating factors.
  • Alternative Sanctions & Restorative Justice: Guided by PERMA No. 1/2024 and modern penal guidelines, encouraging social work, restitution, and compensation for minor offenses to alleviate prison overcrowding.

Emerging doctrine: confessions or evidence obtained under unlawful duress/torture are invalid. Judicial discretion guided by statutory limits, chamber circulars, and KUHP sentencing criteria.

Strict Fourth/Fifth Amendment Exclusionary Rule (Fruit of the Poisonous Tree doctrine). Federal Sentencing Guidelines with structured offense levels and criminal history categories.
Japan Free evaluation of evidence subject to judicial conviction beyond reasonable doubt (Code of Criminal Procedure Art. 317). District Court Judges issue warrants; pre-trial conference proceedings (Kōhan-zen-seiri-tetsuzuki) narrow points of dispute. Strict exclusion of involuntary confessions (*Kenkyu*) and unconstitutionally seized evidence. Lay Judge System (Saiban-in) for serious crimes; heavy reliance on sentencing tables and confession remorse.

Investigating Judge (*Ermittlungsrichter*) oversees searches, telephone wiretaps, and pre-trial detention. Structured statutory evaluation prohibitions (*Beweisverwertungsverbote*). Individualized proportionality-based sentencing governed by § 46 StGB (Penal Code).

Important Decisions & Chamber Plenary Guidance

Decision
Constitutional Review on Suspect Determination as a Object of Praperadilan

  • Issuing Body: Constitutional Court of Indonesia (Mahkamah Konstitusi)
  • Case Reference: Decision No. 21/PUU-XII/2014
  • Legal Principle: Suspect status determination (penetapan tersangka) must be based on at least two valid items of preliminary evidence (dua alat bukti permulaan) and is subject to pre-trial judicial scrutiny.
  • Why It Matters: Eliminated unchecked investigator discretion in naming criminal suspects.
  • Official Source: Constitutional Court Official Reports.

Decision
Precedential Guidance on Involuntary Confessions

  • Issuing Body: Mahkamah Agung RI (Criminal Chamber Plenary)
  • Instrument / SEMA Ref: SEMA No. 1/2017 jo. SEMA No. 3/2023
  • Legal Principle: Police investigation reports (BAP) containing confessions repudiated in open court cannot serve as valid evidence unless corroborated by independent material evidence.
  • Why It Matters: Protects defendants from forced confessions and upholds the constitutional ban on torture.
  • Official Source: Supreme Court Chamber Reports.

Debates & Controversies

Debate
Judicial Assessment of Living Law (Hukum Adat) in Criminal Sentencing

  • Position A (Strict Legality Principle / *Lex Scripta*): Punishments must strictly follow clear written statutes to prevent subjective local biases and maintain nationwide legal certainty.
  • Position B (Substantive Justice / Living Law Recognition): As mandated by Law No. 48/2009 Art. 5(1) and Law No. 1/2023 Art. 2, judges must recognize unwritten customary law (*hukum adat*) and community sanctions to deliver contextual justice.
  • Evidence: Regional judicial practices handling customary dispute resolutions and restorative agreements in customary communities.
  • Current Status: Harmonized under Law No. 1/2023: Customary offenses are recognized subject to regional regulation compliance and fundamental constitutional human rights baselines.

Rule-of-Law Connection

  • Decisional Independence: Criminal judges must remain insulated from public trial-by-media pressure or executive law-enforcement expectations.
  • Proportionality in Sentencing: Punishments must fit the gravity of the offense, culpability of the offender, and the rights/restitution of the victim.
  • Protection Against Arbitrary Detention: Strict enforcement of statutory detention caps (KUHAP Art. 24–29) ensures no individual is detained without valid, timely court orders.

AI & Future of Law

  • Digital & Teleconference Integrity: Chapter 3 provides safeguards for virtual witness cross-examination, verifying witness identity, preventing offline witness coercion during remote feeds, and securing electronic transmission lines.
  • Digital Forensic Evidence Standards: Standards for chain-of-custody verification (hash verification, timestamping) of electronic documents, surveillance logs, and metadata presented under Law No. 1/2024 (ITE).
  • Predictive Sentencing Ethics: AI sentencing prediction tools must remain purely analytical references for judicial research; automated algorithm-driven sentencing is strictly prohibited.

Common Misconceptions

Myth
A defendant's confession is sufficient on its own to hand down a criminal conviction.

  • Reality: Under KUHAP Art. 189(4), a defendant's confession alone is insufficient to prove guilt; it must be corroborated by at least one other valid statutory item of evidence and supported by the judge's conviction.
  • Source: KUHAP Art. 183 & 189; Supreme Court Benchbook Chapter 3.

Myth
An acquittal (Vrijspraak) and a dismissal of charges (Onslag) are legally identical outcomes.

  • Reality: An acquittal (*Vrijspraak*) means the alleged act was not legally proven to have occurred. A dismissal of charges (*Onslag van alle rechtsvervolging*) means the act was proven, but the act does not constitute a criminal offense (e.g., it is a civil matter, or justified by self-defense/necessity).
  • Source: KUHAP Art. 191; Supreme Court Jurisprudence.

Practical Example

  • Scenario: A defendant is indicted for grand theft. The sole evidence presented by the prosecution is the testimony of a single eyewitness (*unus testis*) and an unsworn written statement without corroborating physical exhibits, forensic matches, or defendant admission.
  • Legal Question: Can the judicial panel convict the defendant?
  • Applicable Principle: Minimum Proof Standard (*Unus Testis Nullus Testis* & Negative Statutory Proof / KUHAP Art. 183, 185(2)).
  • Possible Analysis: The testimony of a single witness does not satisfy the statutory threshold of two valid items of evidence (*dua alat bukti yang sah*). In the absence of corroborating documentary evidence, indicators, or admissions, the panel must acquit the defendant (*Vrijspraak*).
  • Limitation: If independent circumstantial physical indicators (*alat bukti petunjuk*) are legally constructed from valid sources, the threshold may be satisfied.

Key Terms

Term Definition Related Term
Vrijspraak (Acquittal) A judgment delivered when the court finds the criminal charges unproven based on the statutory evidence threshold. Onslag, Putusan Bebas
Onslag van alle rechtsvervolging A judgment declaring that while the act was committed, it does not constitute a punishable crime due to legal justification or civil nature. Putusan Lepas, Alasan Pemaaf
Praperadilan An expedited court mechanism to evaluate the lawfulness of arrests, detentions, terminations of investigation, or suspect determinations. Habeas Corpus, Due Process
Alat Bukti Petunjuk Circumstantial indicators derived by the judge from witness testimonies, written documents, or defendant statements demonstrating commission of the crime. KUHAP Art. 188, Evidence Hierarchy
