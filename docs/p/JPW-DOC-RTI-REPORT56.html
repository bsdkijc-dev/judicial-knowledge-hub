Policy Analysis

  • Background & Core Facts:
    • Over the past 20 years, Japan experienced a significant rise in elderly (aged 65+) and mentally disordered offenders across all criminal justice stages (police, prosecution, corrections, and rehabilitation).
    • High re-offending rates stem from a lack of independent living capacity, stable housing, medical care, or family support post-release.
    • Policy Framework & Interventions:
      • Grounded in the July 2011 Ministerial Meeting on Measures Against Crime decision on "Comprehensive Measures for the Prevention of Repeat Offenses."
      • Evaluates the Special Coordination (特別調整) system, a pre-release mechanism securing social welfare placement, healthcare access, and pensions for elderly or disabled inmates lacking appropriate residence upon release.
    • 20-Year Statistical Trends:
      • Elderly Offenders: Elderly non-traffic Penal Code offenders cleared increased 3.8-fold, prosecutor clearances rose 6.1-fold, indictments rose 6.9-fold, and newly admitted elderly inmates grew 4.5-fold. Re-imprisonment rate for male elderly inmates reached 73.0% as of 2015.
      • Mentally Disordered Offenders: Cleared offenders doubled (1.7% of all cases in 2015). Proportion of mentally disordered inmates rose to 13.1% (exceeding 20% among female inmates). Female inmates with neurotic disorders jumped 28.8-fold.
      • Special Coordination Efficacy: Tracking study of inmates released between February 1 and March 14, 2014, proved that those receiving Special Coordination had significantly lower re-imprisonment rates than those who refused or did not receive assistance.
    • Operational Barriers & Comparative Legal Models:
      • Analyzes Italy's misure alternative alla detenzione as a reference model for community-based non-custodial sanctions.
      • Primary hurdles in Japan include inmate refusal of welfare services due to lack of understanding and gaps in multi-agency networking among prosecutors, prisons, probation offices, and public welfare bodies.
  • Interpretations & Structural Inferences:
    • Demonstrates a paradigm shift in Japanese criminal justice from purely retributive punishment to a justice-welfare crossover model addressing demographic aging and mental health criminological drivers.
    • Failure to integrate social welfare safeguards early in prosecution risks overburdening penal facilities with elderly repeat minor offenders as Japan's elderly population peaks.
