'Virtual Court Proceedings (VCP) Framework in Nigeria' (NJC, 2020)

Wiki Entity Page

The Virtual Court Proceedings (VCP) Framework in Nigeria is a judicial administration policy and legal technology operational framework in the Federal Republic of Nigeria that permits the remote conduct of civil and criminal court proceedings through the utilization of audio-visual links (AVL) and digital communication platforms.

Summary

Initiated in April 2020 by the National Judicial Council (NJC) as a response to the Covid-19 pandemic crisis, the VCP evolved from a mere emergency instrument into a permanent judicial modernization strategy. Although it has received broad support from legal practitioners for increasing efficiency and saving time, the implementation of VCP in Nigeria still faces serious challenges in the form of a national electricity crisis, uneven internet access, technophobia, and technical conflicts with public document evidentiary rules.

Legal Basis

The implementation of VCP in Nigeria is based on a series of circulars, practice directions, and national legislation:

  • NJC Circular No. NJC/CIR/HOC/II/656 (April 6, 2020) regarding the prevention of the spread of Covid-19 and the use of VCP.
  • Practice Direction for Remote Hearing of Cases in the Lagos State Judiciary 2020.
  • High Court of the Federal Capital Territory (FCT) Abuja Covid-19 Practice Direction 2020.
  • Federal High Court of Nigeria Practice Direction for Sitting During Covid-19 Period 2020.
  • National Industrial Court of Nigeria (NICN) Practice Direction 2020.
  • Administration of Criminal Justice (Amendment) Law of Lagos State 2021 (Section 12(2) which recognizes VCP in criminal trials).
  • Court of Appeal of Nigeria (Civil Procedure) Rules 2021.
  • Evidence (Amendment) Act of Nigeria 2023 (Sections 1–10 regarding the recognition of digital oaths, computer evidence, and electronic signatures).
  • Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria 1999 (Section 36 regarding the right to a fair and public hearing).

Background

Prior to 2020, the application of technology in the Nigerian judiciary only covered e-filing and e-service in certain state courts. The outbreak of the Covid-19 pandemic forced the cessation of physical court operations. To prevent case backlogs, the NJC issued a national instruction for judicial bodies to utilize audio-visual networks to hold remote hearings.

Objectives

1. Ensure business continuity of judicial functions under both emergency and normal conditions. 2. Increase the efficiency and speed of legal dispute resolution in courts. 3. Cut travel costs and risks for advocates, witnesses, and litigants. 4. Modernize the Nigerian judiciary to align with international judicial standards (such as in Singapore, Australia, and the UK) [31–37].

Scope

The VCP policy applies across all Federal and State judicial jurisdictions in Nigeria, with trial focus points in two main legal hubs:

  • Lagos State: The national commercial center with the highest case volume.
  • Federal Capital Territory (FCT) Abuja: The center for high-level judicial institutions (Supreme Court, Court of Appeal, Federal High Court, NICN).

Key Provisions

  • Conduct of Digital Hearings: The use of video conferencing platforms (such as Zoom and WebEx) to hear statements from parties, witnesses, and legal arguments.
  • Digital Oaths and Access: Recognition of oaths taken via video link and the electronic transmission of trial files.
  • Ethics and Courtroom Decorum: The obligation to maintain lawyer-client confidentiality, official advocate attire, and the orderliness of the virtual courtroom.

Implementation

  • Participation Levels: Based on a 2024 empirical study of 353 advocates, 46.5% of respondents stated they had direct experience leading/participating in VCP hearings [48–51].
  • Advanced Regulatory Phase: The passage of the Evidence (Amendment) Act 2023 strengthened the legal legitimacy of electronic evidence and digital signatures used in the VCP.

Policy Problems

1. Basic Infrastructure: The electricity supply crisis (where 92 million citizens lack stable electricity) and weak internet networks are the biggest barriers to VCP (agreed upon by 85% of respondents). 2. Constitutional Debate: Claims emerged that virtual trials violate the requirement for a public hearing under Section 36 of the 1999 Constitution. An attempted constitutional amendment failed in the Senate in March 2022. 3. Evidentiary Procedure Conflicts: Section 90(1)(c) of the Evidence Act 2011 still requires the submission of physical Certified True Copies (CTC) of public documents, complicating document tendering in virtual trials. 4. Technophobia & Skills: 45.5% of advocates/judges experience fear of new technology (technophobia) and 34.9% lack IT skills.

Reform / Change

  • The need for comprehensive harmonization between VCP Practice Directions and the Civil Procedure Rules of each respective court.
  • Recommendation to amend Section 90(1)(c) of the Evidence Act so that electronic copies of public document CTCs can be legally admitted as secondary evidence.
  • The need for planned investment from judicial institutions and the Nigerian Bar Association (NBA) in IT training and the provision of hardware facilities in court buildings.

Relationships with Other Entities

  • National Judicial Council (NJC): The oversight body and primary catalyst for the VCP policy in Nigeria.
  • Lagos State Judiciary & High Court FCT Abuja: Key pioneers in the trials and issuance of VCP Practice Directions.
  • Babcock University School of Law: The academic institution conducting the empirical evaluation study of VCP.
