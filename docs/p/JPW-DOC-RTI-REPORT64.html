Policy Analysis

  • Background & Special Fraud Trends (Chapter 2):
    • Special fraud cases surged sharply around 2003, peaking in 2004 with ~25,700 cases and total financial damages of 28.4 billion JPY. In 2021, reported cases stood at ~15,000 with damages of 28.2 billion JPY.
    • "It's me" fraud (Ore-ore sagi) accounted for the largest share of cases and losses throughout most of the period.
    • Victim Profile: Females accounted for 75% of total victims in 2021, and elderly individuals aged 65+ accounted for nearly 90% of all victims. Victims aged 75+ comprised over half of all cases.
    • Communication Channels: Landline phones were used in >80% of cases, rising to >90% when combined with mobile phones.
  • Judicial Sentencing Tracking Research (Chapter 3):
    • Analysis of a dataset of 1,343 fraud offenders convicted in District Courts (Jan 1–Mar 31, 2016), of which 408 (one-third) were special fraud offenders.
    • Special Fraud Offender Profile: Young average age (<30 years), predominantly male, unemployed, higher proportion of first-time offenders, and sentenced to imprisonment without suspension at higher rates.
    • Role Division (Tokyo Metropolitan Area, N=202): Categorized into masterminds, callers, receivers/drawers (nearly half), and logistics. Masterminds had strong ties to Boryokudan organizations, received the largest payouts, and received the harshest sentences (>80% unsuspended imprisonment with longer terms).
    • Recidivism Rate (N=1,231): Estimated 4-year repeat offense rate based on Kaplan-Meier analysis was 15.0%. The repeat offender group included no females or elderly individuals aged 50+.
  • First-Time Inmate Survey (Chapter 4):
    • Questionnaire survey (Jul 1–Aug 31, 2018) of 393 male inmates without prior imprisonment history (94 special fraud, 81 other fraud, 218 theft).
    • 97.7% of special fraud offenders operated with accomplices, and ~60% were enticed by peer recruitment (3 to 5 times higher than other fraud or theft groups). Only 1.1% initiated the fraud idea independently. Peer recruitment frequently occurred via high-paying job ads on social media or the dark web.
    • Psychological Characteristics: 80% expressed guilt. Based on the TIPI-J scale, special fraud offenders exhibited lower conscientiousness compared to other fraud offenders. Motivation for rehabilitation at the maintenance stage was also lower than among theft offenders.
  • Policy Recommendations (Chapter 5):
    • Special fraud is systematically executed with organized division of labor under organized crime leadership (Boryokudan).
    • The most effective strategy to prevent recidivism is severing ties with delinquent peers and exiting criminal organizations.
    • The psychological profile of special fraud offenders suggests that structured guidance in penal and probation facilities can significantly lower repeat offense probability.
  • Interpretations & Structural Inferences:
    • Special fraud in Japan represents industrial-scale organized crime exploiting youth economic vulnerability via social media recruitment while targeting elderly women via landlines.
    • Because 97.7% of offenders operate in syndicates and 60% are recruited by peers, correctional policies focused on severing delinquent ties and blocking access to organized networks are far more critical than individual moral instruction.
