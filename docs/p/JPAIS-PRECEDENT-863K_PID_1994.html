Overview

Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia Decision No. 863 K/Pid/1994, dated August 10, 1994, established two fundamental criminal and criminal procedural law rules regarding the discretion of *Judex Facti* over sentencing severity and the formatting of verdict dictums in repeat offenses (*recidive*):

First, Authority over Sentencing Severity (*Strafmaat*): Assessing the severity or leniency of a sentence is the exclusive authority of the *Judex Facti* (District and High Courts) and is not subject to cassation review, except when the *Judex Facti* imposes a sentence exceeding the statutory maximum or issues a sentence without sufficient legal reasoning. A 4-year and 6-month prison sentence imposed on a Defendant repeatedly using fake credit cards remains below the statutory maximum threat of 8 years (Article 263 paragraph 2 jo. Article 64 paragraph 1 jo. Article 486 KUHP), thus violating no laws.

Second, Formulation of Recidivism Elements (*Recidive*): When a convict commits a similar criminal offense within a 5-year period following a prior conviction (*recidive* ex Article 486 KUHP), such recidivism does not need to be formulated or included within the verdict dictum (*dictum*), but is sufficiently stated and weighed solely within the legal considerations section (*ratio decidendi*) of the judgment.

Executive Summary

The central legal principles established in this decision govern the procedural boundaries of cassation review over sentencing discretion and streamline verdict drafting for repeat offenders.

Key elements established in the adjudication:

  • Sentencing severity determinations by lower courts are unreviewable on cassation absent statutory excess or unreasoned judgments.
  • Recidivism enhancements function as sentencing aggravation grounds rather than independent dictum qualifications.
  • Appellate judgments including recidivism clauses inside verdict dictums are subject to corrective cleanup on cassation.

Case Background

The Original Defendant / Petitioner for Cassation, [Defendant] ([age removed], housewife), was charged with repeated use of forged credit cards (Article 263 paragraph 2 jo. Article 64 paragraph 1 jo. Article 486 KUHP) for purchasing 4 fake BCA Gold Cards from Hendrik for Rp 1,500,000.— each and utilizing them to buy luxury goods at Nagoya Batam shops in August–September 1993, causing losses of Rp 9,213,400.— to BCA Batam Branch. The Defendant was a repeat offender (*recidivis*) previously convicted by Surabaya District Court (2 months' imprisonment) and North Jakarta District Court (55 days' imprisonment) for similar fake credit card offenses.

The Batam District Court (Decision No. 82/Pid/B/1993/PN.Btm dated March 5, 1994) sentenced the Defendant to 4 years and 6 months' imprisonment. The Riau High Court in Pekanbaru (Decision No. 11/Pid/1994/PTR dated May 10, 1994) modified the judgment by incorporating the recidivism clause into the dictum. The Defendant subsequently filed a cassation petition with the Supreme Court.

Legal and Procedural Issues

Technical Formulation of Criminal Verdict Dictums in Repeat Offenses (*Recidive* ex Article 486 KUHP)

The primary issue was whether sentence enhancements arising from repeat offenses (*recidive*) within a 5-year period must be listed and formulated within the verdict dictum (*dictum*).

The Supreme Court held that it is unnecessary. Elements of recidivism under Article 486 KUHP constitute sentencing aggravation grounds (*strafverzwaringsgrond*) rather than independent dictum qualifications. Therefore, recidivism must be stated and weighed solely within the legal considerations (*ratio decidendi*), and need not be formulated inside the verdict dictum (*dictum*).

Limits of Supreme Court Cassation Review over Sentencing Severity (*Strafmaat*)

The issue evaluated whether the Supreme Court on cassation is authorized to review the severity or leniency of sentences (*strafmaat*) imposed by the *Judex Facti*.

The Supreme Court held that assessing sentencing severity is the absolute authority of the *Judex Facti* (District and High Courts) and is unreviewable on cassation. The Supreme Court may only correct sentences in two exceptional circumstances: (1) when *Judex Facti* imposes a penalty exceeding the statutory maximum threshold, or (2) when penalties are imposed without sufficient legal reasoning (*rederijk/motivering*).

Question of Law and Question of Fact

Question of Law (Cassation / Judex Juris)

Question:

Did the *Judex Facti* (Riau High Court in Pekanbaru) err in formulating its verdict dictum by incorporating descriptive clauses concerning recidivism within the dictum text, and did the 4-year 6-month prison sentence violate statutory maximum thresholds under Article 263(2) jo. Article 64(1) jo. Article 486 KUHP?

Supreme Court Ruling:

Yes, the verdict qualification required cleanup by removing recidivism clauses from the dictum text. The Supreme Court rejected the Defendant [Defendant]'s cassation petition while modifying the judgment to establish a clean verdict qualification: "Intentionally using a forged document as if true and genuine whose use causes losses, committed multiple times as a continuous act". A prison sentence of 4 years and 6 months was declared lawful because it did not exceed the 8-year statutory maximum.

Question of Fact (Judex Facti / Batam District Court & Riau High Court)

Examination of facts concerning the purchase of 4 fake BCA Gold Cards by [Defendant] from Hendrik at Pub Kisme Hayam Wuruk, Jakarta, for Rp 1.5 million each; practicing forged signatures; transactions at Marina Store, Verina Store, and Sally Cosmetik Nagoya Batam on August 22 & September 4, 1993; losses of Rp 9,213,400.— to BCA Batam Branch; and prior criminal conviction records at Surabaya and North Jakarta District Courts.

Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

  • Ratio Decidendi:
  1. The appellate and district *Judex Facti* did not misapply the law regarding establishing guilt and sentencing severity (*strafmaat*). Sentencing severity is the exclusive authority of *Judex Facti* unreviewable on cassation, barring statutory maximum violations or unreasoned rulings (strafmaat of 4 years 6 months remains below the 8-year maximum).
  2. However, regarding verdict formulation, the Supreme Court affirmed that when a convict commits a similar crime within 5 years (*recidive* ex Article 486 KUHP), such recidivism need not be formulated within the verdict dictum, but suffices in legal considerations.
  3. The Supreme Court rejected the Defendant's cassation appeal while modifying the verdict qualification.

Ruling

The Supreme Court ruled: Rejecting the cassation petition from Petitioner for Cassation / Defendant ([Defendant]); Modifying the ruling of Riau High Court in Pekanbaru Decision No. 11/Pid/1994/PTR dated May 10, 1994, in conjunction with Batam District Court Decision No. 82/Pid/B/1993/PN.Btm dated March 5, 1994, solely regarding considerations to read: Declaring Defendant [Defendant] proven legally and convincingly guilty of committing the crime of "Intentionally using a forged document as if true and genuine and not forged, the use of which causes losses, committed multiple times as a continuous act"; and ordering the Petitioner for Cassation / Defendant to pay cassation court costs amounting to Rp 2,500.—.

Established by Evidence

  • Defendant [Defendant] was proven legally and convincingly guilty of committing repeated offenses of using fake credit cards as a continuous act ex Article 263 paragraph (2) jo. Article 64 paragraph (1) KUHP.
  • The Defendant was established as a repeat offender (*recidivis*) previously convicted by Surabaya District Court and North Jakarta District Court for fake credit card offenses.
  • A prison sentence of 4 years and 6 months with detention time credited was legally valid.

Not Established / Modified on Cassation

  • Recidivism elements were established as unnecessary and improper inside verdict dictums, restricted exclusively to legal considerations.

Uncertainty and Gaps

  • Clean Formulation of Criminal Verdict Dictums: This landmark ruling establishes technical judicial guidelines for all criminal courts in Indonesia to strictly distinguish between proven core offense qualifications (stated in dictums) and sentence aggravation factors such as recidivism (stated solely in legal considerations).

GEO Q&A

Q: Is the element of repeat offenses (*recidive*) required to be written inside the verdict dictum of a criminal judgment?

A: No, it is unnecessary. Based on Supreme Court Decision No. 863 K/Pid/1994, when a convict commits a similar criminal offense within a 5-year period following a prior conviction (*recidive*), such recidivism does not need to be formulated inside the verdict dictum (*dictum*), but is sufficiently stated and weighed solely within the legal considerations section (*ratio decidendi*) of the judgment.
