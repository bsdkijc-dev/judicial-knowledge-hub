Comparative Study on Former Judges Returning to Practice and the Implications for Korea (1) - Examples of Overseas Regulations (Korean: 해외의 전관예우 규제사례와 국내 규제방안 모색(1) - 퇴직법관의 변호사 개업에 대한 해외 규제사례) is an official judicial policy research report published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of the Supreme Court of Korea as JPRI Research Series 2019-09.1 It analyzes norms, practices and international developments governing former judges who return to private legal practice (전관예우, the "judicial revolving door") in Anglo-American and Continental systems in 11 jurisdictions, and builds a five-stage chronological regulatory framework for Korea.

The research was conducted under the Supreme Court Annual Research Plan 2018 (2018년도 연구과제). The Korean references are:

  • Attorney-at-Law Act, Article 31 (변호사법 제31조)
  • Constitution of the Republic of Korea (대한민국 헌법)

Foreign rules examined: US ABA Model Rules of Professional Conduct Rule 1.12(a); the UK uncodified judicial convention and Guide to Judicial Conduct; Canada FLSC Model Code Rule 7.7; German BeamtStG § 41 and BRAO § 45; Irish Bar Code Rule 5.32; Nigerian Constitution Section 292(2); Chinese Supreme People's Court Rules on Judicial Recusal (2011); and the Taiwanese Attorney Act.

Background

In Korea, public distrust of the judiciary is fed by jeongwan-yeowu (전관예우), the alleged or expected favorable treatment of former judges who become private lawyers. Unlike many countries where judges serve until retirement age, Korean judges often resign in large numbers in their 40s and 50s at promotion cycles and join large law firms or open their own practices, handling cases before their former colleagues.2

Objectives

  1. Conduct an in-depth comparative analysis of direct and indirect regulation of former judges in 11 jurisdictions.
  2. Formulate a five-stage chronological framework (5단계 규제형 대책) covering pre-resignation, immediate post-resignation, cooling-off, marketing and title restrictions, and judicial confidentiality.
  3. Recommend amendments to the Attorney-at-Law Act that balance judicial independence with the freedom to choose an occupation (직업의 자유).

Scope

The 11 jurisdictions are the United States, United Kingdom, Canada, Germany, Ireland, Hong Kong, Singapore, New Zealand, Nigeria, Taiwan and China.

Policy problems

  • Ineffective current Korean restriction. The one-year bar on appearing, keyed to the last court served, is easily evaded because Korean judges rotate regions every 2–4 years (전보인사) and move between first-instance, appellate and Supreme Court research posts.
  • Threat to judicial impartiality. Even if favorable treatment does not actually occur, the public perception that former judges enjoy "a favoured relationship with the court" erodes trust in the fairness of trials.
  • No comprehensive post-service ethics. Korea lacks rules on employment negotiation before resignation, on ex parte communication, and on duties of confidentiality over judicial deliberations (사법적 비밀성).

Legal and policy gaps

  • Narrow focus on courtroom appearance. Korean reform debate has centered on oral advocacy, overlooking names on filings (factums), written research, signing documents and behind-the-scenes consulting.
  • No tier-wide or territory-wide limits. Korean rules do not bar former judges across all court tiers or across jurisdictions where they held influence.

Comparative analysis

Reform proposals

Five-stage chronological framework

  1. Stage 1 – pre-resignation: strict ban on employment or recruitment negotiations with law firms or litigants while in office.
  2. Stage 2 – immediate post-resignation: short moratorium restricting all legal practice.
  3. Stage 3 – appearance: restrictions on appearing before the former court, the same tier or lower courts.
  4. Stage 4 – marketing and title: ban on using the title "former judge", robed photographs in advertising, or claims of a "special relationship".
  5. Stage 5 – judicial confidentiality (사법적 비밀성): a permanent legal duty not to use or disclose confidential judicial deliberations.

Other proposals

  • Correct the rotation loophole: replace court-building-based limits with tier-wide and territory-wide limits so periodic rotation cannot be used to avoid them.
  • Pair restrictions with welfare improvements: offset restrictions with career incentives such as a Senior Judge system (시니어판사) and adequate pensions to reduce early resignations.

Frequently asked questions

What are the report's title, lead author and issuer?
Comparative Study on Former Judges Returning to Practice and the Implications for Korea (1) - Examples of Overseas Regulations; lead author Cha, Sungan (JPRI Research Fellow); issued by the Judicial Policy Research Institute on 31 December 2019.

How does the report treat the term jeongwan-yeowu?
The specific term is largely absent in Western countries; the report treats it broadly as the phenomenon in which former judges return to private practice and the public suspects "a favoured relationship with the court", undermining trust in impartiality.

Why is Korea's current restriction considered weak?
Judges rotate regions every 2–4 years and move between court tiers, so a one-year bar tied to a single court is easily circumvented.

How does the United Kingdom bar former judges from practice?
Through an uncodified convention in the terms of appointment: judges take office on the understanding that they will never return as barristers or solicitors.

What does FLSC Model Code Rule 7.7 provide?
A three-year bar on appearing before the former court, lower courts or supervised administrative bodies; the FLSC recommends making it permanent.

How does Germany regulate retired judges?
BeamtStG § 41 lets authorities bar pension-receiving former judges from appearing before their former court for up to five years.

What was at issue in the Barry White case in Ireland?
A former High Court judge challenged Bar Code Rule 5.32, which permanently bars former judges from appearing in courts of equal or lower rank, so that he could return to work for economic reasons.

How does Nigeria's Constitution address former judicial officers?
Section 292(2) permanently bars them from appearing or acting as legal practitioners in any court or tribunal in Nigeria.

What are the five proposed stages?
Pre-resignation employment-negotiation limits; an immediate practice moratorium; appearance restrictions; marketing and title limits; and enforcement of judicial confidentiality.

배경

우리나라에서는 퇴직 법관이 변호사로 개업하여 유리한 대우를 받는다는 의혹 또는 기대, 곧 전관예우가 사법 불신의 주된 원인이다. 정년까지 근무하는 여러 나라와 달리 우리 법관은 승진 주기인 40~50대에 대거 퇴직하여 대형 로펌에 합류하거나 개업한 뒤 옛 동료 앞에서 사건을 수행하는 경우가 많다.3

연구 목적

  1. 11개 관할의 퇴직 법관에 대한 직접·간접 규제를 심층 비교 분석한다.
  2. 퇴직 전, 퇴직 직후, 냉각기, 마케팅·직함 제한, 사법적 비밀성을 포괄하는 5단계 규제형 대책을 마련한다.
  3. 사법 독립과 직업의 자유를 조화시키는 변호사법 개정 권고안을 제시한다.

연구 범위

11개 관할은 미국, 영국, 캐나다, 독일, 아일랜드, 홍콩, 싱가포르, 뉴질랜드, 나이지리아, 대만, 중국이다.

정책 문제

  • 현행 국내 규제의 비실효성: 마지막 근무 법원을 기준으로 한 1년의 수임 제한은, 법관이 2~4년마다 지역을 순환(전보인사)하고 1심·항소심·대법원 재판연구관 등을 오가기 때문에 쉽게 회피된다.
  • 사법의 공정성에 대한 위협: 실제 특혜가 없더라도 퇴직 법관이 "법원과 유리한 관계"를 가진다는 국민 인식은 재판의 공정성에 대한 신뢰를 훼손한다.
  • 포괄적인 퇴직 후 윤리 규범의 부재: 퇴직 전 취업 교섭, 일방 당사자 접촉(ex parte communication), 재판 합의의 비밀 유지(사법적 비밀성)에 관한 규정이 없다.

법제 공백

  • 법정 출석에 대한 좁은 초점: 국내 논의는 구두 변론 금지에 집중하여, 준비서면(factum)에 이름을 올리는 행위, 서면 조사, 문서 서명, 배후 자문을 간과한다.
  • 심급 전체·관할 전체 제한의 부재: 퇴직 법관이 영향력을 가졌던 모든 심급이나 관할에서의 활동을 금지하는 규정이 없다.

개선 제안

5단계 시간순 규제 틀

  1. 1단계 – 퇴직 전: 재직 중 로펌이나 소송 당사자와의 취업·영입 교섭을 엄격히 금지.
  2. 2단계 – 퇴직 직후: 모든 법률 업무를 제한하는 단기 유예 기간.
  3. 3단계 – 수임: 종전 법원, 같은 심급 또는 하급 법원에서의 수임 제한.
  4. 4단계 – 마케팅과 직함: "전직 판사" 직함, 법복 착용 사진을 광고에 사용하는 행위, "특별한 관계" 주장을 금지.
  5. 5단계 – 사법적 비밀성: 비밀 재판 합의를 이용하거나 누설하지 않을 영구적 법적 의무.

기타 제안

  • 전보인사 우회 보완: 법원 청사 단위 제한을 심급 전체·관할 전체 제한으로 바꾸어 정기 전보를 이용한 회피를 막는다.
  • 처우 개선과 병행: 시니어판사 제도와 충분한 연금 등 경력 유인으로 조기 퇴직 동기를 줄인다.

BeamtStG 제41조에 따라 당국은 연금을 받는 퇴직 법관의 종전 법원 수임을 최대 5년간 금지할 수 있다.

제292조 제2항이 모든 법원·재판소에서의 변론이나 법률가 활동을 영구 금지한다.
