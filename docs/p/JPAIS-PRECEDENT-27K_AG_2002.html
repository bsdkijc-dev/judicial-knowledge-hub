Overview

Supreme Court Decision No. 27 K/AG/2002 dated February 26, 2004, establishes an important legal principle regarding the proof of land grants (*hibah*). The Supreme Court affirmed that anyone claiming rights over land based on a grant must be able to prove ownership of that grant pursuant to Article 210 paragraph (1) of the Compilation of Islamic Law (KHI). If land is acquired through a grant, the land title should be promptly transferred (*balik nama*) into the name of the grantee. If no title transfer takes place and a dispute subsequently arises, the legal status of the land remains unchanged (registered under the name of the grantor) unless a change in ownership status is conclusively proven.

Case Background

The Original Plaintiff (Sahudin / Sanudin bin Taslim) filed a grant lawsuit at the Giri Menang Religious Court (Decision No. 76/Pdt.G/2000/PA.GM) against the Original Defendants ([Defendant I], [Defendant II], [Defendant III], and [Defendant IV]). The Plaintiff claimed ownership of an agricultural plot measuring approximately 17 *are* located in Subak Bali, Karang Petak Hamlet, East Pemenang Village, West Lombok, allegedly acquired via a grant from his uncle (Amaq Mali alias H. Abdul Hamid) approximately 40 years prior. Because the Plaintiff was illiterate and unsophisticated, the land title was never transferred and remained registered under Amaq Mali's name.

In 1999, the Defendants took over possession of the land. The Giri Menang Religious Court (Decision No. 76/Pdt.G/2000/PA.GM dated December 6, 2000) and the Mataram High Religious Court (Decision No. 24/Pdt.G/2000/PTA.Mtr dated July 9, 2001) granted the Plaintiff's claim in part and declared the grant legally valid. Dissatisfied with these rulings, the Defendants filed a cassation petition to the Supreme Court.

Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

  • Ratio Decidendi: Anyone who asserts a right to land based on a Grant must prove ownership of that Grant as intended by Article 210 paragraph (1) of the KHI, and if acquired via grant, the land title should immediately be transferred (*balik nama*) to the recipient. If this is not done and a dispute arises later, the land's status remains as it originally was unless a change in ownership status is definitively proven.
  • The Plaintiff, as the party asserting rights to the land based on a grant from the late Amaq Mali alias H. [Defendant II's parent], failed to prove ownership of the grant under Article 210(1) of the KHI.
  • If the late Amaq Mali had truly granted the land 40 years ago, the granted land should have been transferred into the Plaintiff's name. In reality, the land remains registered under Amaq Mali alias H. [Defendant II's parent] to this day.
  • Ruling: Granting the cassation petition from the Petitioners for Cassation ([Defendant I], [Defendant II], [Defendant III], and [Defendant IV]); Annulling the Mataram High Religious Court Decision No. 24/Pdt.G/2000/PTA.Mtr dated July 9, 2001, and the Giri Menang Religious Court Decision No. 76/Pdt.G/2000/PA.GM dated December 6, 2000; and Independently Adjudicating: (1) Rejecting the Plaintiff's (Sahudin / Sanudin bin Taslim) lawsuit; (2) Ordering the Plaintiff to pay procedural costs across all levels of jurisdiction and in cassation amounting to Rp 200,000 (two hundred thousand rupiahs).
