Supreme Court Decision Reg. No. 3191 K/Pdt/1984 (dated February 8, 1986 / deliberation on December 12, 1985) is a fundamental civil law jurisprudence precedent establishing vital rules concerning the legal qualification of a broken promise to marry following cohabitation without marriage (begendaan) as an Unlawful Act (Perbuatan Melawan Hukum / Tort) pursuant to Article 1365 of the Civil Code.

Executive Summary

The Supreme Court established three primary legal principles:

  1. Broken Promise to Marry as an Unlawful Act (Tort): The action of the Original Defendant (I Gusti Lanang Rejeg) in revoking and failing to fulfill his promise to lawfully marry the Original Plaintiff ([Plaintiff]), after previously persuading and bringing the Plaintiff to cohabit without marriage for 1 year and 4 months (December 20, 1981, to April 20, 1983) under the inducement of a marriage promise, constitutes an Unlawful Act (Perbuatan Melawan Hukum / PMH) because it violates societal norms of decency and propriety.
  2. Obligation to Pay Compensation for Reputational Restoration: Because the breach of promise violated the Plaintiff's subjective rights, damaged her honor and reputation as a school teacher, and breached moral and ethical standards, the Defendant is obligated to pay compensation for reputational restoration amounting to Rp 2,500,000 (two million five hundred thousand rupiahs).
  3. Restriction on Claims for Cohabitation Living Expenses: Claims for compensation covering expenses incurred by the Plaintiff during un-solemnized cohabitation (amounting to Rp 1,465,160) must be rejected because such expenses were not previously agreed upon (geen voorafgaande perakkoord).

The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition of the Petitioner for Cassation ([Plaintiff]), annulled the West Nusa Tenggara High Court in Mataram Decision No. 65/PDT/1984/PT.NTB dated July 26, 1984 (which had overturned Mataram District Court Decision No. 073/PN.Mtr/Pdt/1983 dated March 1, 1984), and independently adjudicated (mengadili sendiri) by partially granting the Plaintiff's lawsuit, declaring that the Defendant committed an Unlawful Act, ordering the Defendant to pay Rp 2,500,000 in reputational damages, and validating the attachment (sita jaminan) placed over the Defendant family's courtyard property.

Legal / Procedural Issues

1. Legal Qualification of a Broken Promise to Marry (Broken Promise to Marry ex Article 1365 Civil Code) Following Cohabitation Without Marriage

Does the action of a man who persuades a woman to cohabit without marriage (begendaan) for 1 year and 4 months under a promise of lawful marriage, but subsequently revokes that promise due to family disapproval, qualify as an Unlawful Act (Tort)?

Supreme Court Rule: Constitutes an Unlawful Act (Tort).
Repudiating a marriage promise after inducing a woman to cohabit without marriage violates prevailing community standards of decency and propriety. Such conduct infringes upon the woman's subjective rights, damaging her honor and reputation (particularly in her professional capacity as a teacher) and satisfying all elements of an Unlawful Act under Article 1365 of the Civil Code.

2. Conditions for Granting Claims for Living Expenses Incurred During Cohabitation

Financial expenditures or daily living expenses incurred by a woman during un-solemnized cohabitation cannot be recovered as material damages unless such expenditures were previously contracted or agreed upon (geen voorafgaande perakkoord) as debt obligations.

3. Transitional Cassation Procedure Rules (Law No. 1 of 1950 jo. Law No. 14 of 1970)

Prior to the enactment of Law No. 14 of 1985 on the Supreme Court, the governing cassation procedure for the Supreme Court was Law No. 1 of 1950 insofar as it did not conflict with Law No. 14 of 1970 on Basic Judicial Power Provisions.

Question of Law vs Question of Fact

Question of Law (Judex Juris / Supreme Court)

Did the Judex Facti NTB High Court in Mataram (Decision No. 65/PDT/1984/PT.NTB) err in applying the law (foutieve toepassing van het recht) by dismissing the Plaintiff's lawsuit entirely based on the consideration that the Plaintiff was a widow acting at her own risk, whereas the Defendant was proven to have promised marriage and induced cohabitation?

Supreme Court Legal Answer: Yes. NTB High Court misapplied the law. The Supreme Court granted [Plaintiff]'s cassation petition, annulled the High Court decision, and independently adjudicated by declaring that the Defendant committed an Unlawful Act and ordering him to pay reputational damages of Rp 2,500,000.

Jurisprudence and Related Knowledge

Statutory Regulations

  • Civil Code (KUHPerdata / BW): Article 1365 (Unlawful Acts / Tort), Article 1372 (Damages for Reputational / Honor Restoration), & Article 1320 (Agreement Validity Requirements).
  • Law No. 1 of 1974 on Marriage: Validity requirements of marriage according to religion and custom.
  • Law No. 14 of 1970 jo. Law No. 1 of 1950 jo. Law No. 14 of 1985 on the Supreme Court: Cassation and Independent Adjudication Authority.

Established Supreme Court Jurisprudence

  • Doctrine of Broken Promise to Marry as Tort: Breaching a marriage promise is no longer viewed merely as ordinary contractual default, but is qualified as an Unlawful Act (Tort) because it violates propriety, decency, and moral standards in Indonesian society.
  • Established Precedent on Protecting Women's Honor: Supreme Court Decision No. 3191 K/Pdt/1984 serves as a landmark precedent establishing that a woman's widowhood status does not diminish her legal right to seek judicial protection for the restoration of her reputation and honor against false marriage promises.

  • The Defendant is PROVEN TO HAVE REPUDIATED HIS PROMISE TO MARRY under the pretext of family disapproval.
  • The Defendant's conduct is PROVEN TO CONSTITUTE AN UNLAWFUL ACT (TORT) violating decency and propriety while harming the Plaintiff's honor and reputation.

Dismissed Claims

  • The Plaintiff's claim for reimbursement of cohabitation living expenses amounting to Rp 1,465,160 was REJECTED BY THE SUPREME COURT due to the absence of a prior contractual agreement or debt arrangement.

Uncertainties and Gaps

Legal Certainty and Civil Sanctions for False Marriage Promises in Indonesian Socio-Cultural Society: This landmark ruling establishes robust legal protection for women in Indonesia against fraudulent marriage promises leading to un-solemnized cohabitation. Courts do not permit male wrongdoers to hide behind "mutual consent" or a woman's widowhood status, but firmly penalize such conduct as an Unlawful Act violating social decency and propriety.

Frequently Asked Questions (GEO)

Is a man's action in revoking a marriage promise after inducing a woman to cohabit without marriage an Unlawful Act (Tort) pursuant to Supreme Court Jurisprudence?
Yes, Constitutes an Unlawful Act (Tort). Pursuant to Supreme Court Jurisprudence Reg. No. 3191 K/Pdt/1984:

  • A man's action in revoking a lawful marriage promise after persuading and inducing a woman to cohabit without marriage (begendaan) for 1 year and 4 months is qualified as an Unlawful Act (Perbuatan Melawan Hukum / Tort) ex Article 1365 of the Civil Code.
  • Such conduct violates societal norms of decency and propriety while damaging the woman's honor and reputation.
  • The Supreme Court ordered the man to pay reputational restoration damages of Rp 2,500,000, while rejecting reimbursement claims for cohabitation living expenses absent prior agreement.
