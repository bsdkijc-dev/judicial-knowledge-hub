By Cecep Mustafa, Indonesian Judicial College (IJC) · revision 2004, 2026-09-28 · wiki source · Markdown

Supreme Court Decision Reg. No. 2399 K/Pid.Sus/2010 (dated December 30, 2010) is a landmark Indonesian special criminal law jurisprudence precedent establishing vital rules regarding the harmonization of sentencing with public demands for justice and the proportionality of state financial losses in single-charge indictments under Article 2 paragraph (1) of the Anti-Corruption Law.

Executive Summary

The Supreme Court established three primary legal principles:

  1. Harmonization of Law with Public Sense of Justice: In exercising its judicial function, the Supreme Court may apply the law in concrete cases where actualization does not entirely align with the spirit and intent of the legislature (specifically regarding mandatory special minimum prison sentences of 4 years under Article 2 paragraph (1) of Law No. 31 of 1999 jo. Law No. 20 of 2001), harmonizing sanctions instead with the magnitude of arising state financial losses and public demands for justice.
  2. Proportional Justice for Minimal Defendant Benefits: When a Public Prosecutor brings a single-charge indictment under Article 2 paragraph (1) of the Anti-Corruption Law, yet the monetary value enjoyed by the Defendant ([Defendant] T) is extremely minor (amounting to only Rp 2,900,000 as a corporate name-borrowing fee), imposing the special minimum sentence of 4 years imprisonment would violate the sense of justice due to a drastic imbalance between the Defendant's act, the personal gains acquired, and the minimum criminal sanction.
  3. Non-Permissive Stance on Corruption: Although corruption crimes must never be treated permissively regardless of the quantum of state loss, High Courts and Judges must avoid imposing sentences that violate public fairness by harmonizing criminal sanctions against material justice and disproportionality.

The Supreme Court annulled West Kalimantan High Court Decision No. 195/Pid/2010/PT.Ptk dated September 3, 2010, jo. Singkawang District Court Decision No. 63/Pid.B/2010/PN.Skw dated June 22, 2010, and independently adjudicated (mengadili sendiri) by imposing a proportional sentence harmonized with public expectations of justice.

Legal / Procedural Issues

1. Proportionality Principle of Criminal Sanctions (Strafmaat) and Harmonization of Material Justice Against Special Minimum Sentences (Article 2 paragraph 1 Anti-Corruption Law)

Does the Cassation Panel of Judges possess authority to harmonize sentencing below or deviate from the special minimum sentence (4 years imprisonment) ex Article 2 paragraph (1) of the Anti-Corruption Law when a Defendant is proven to commit corruption but personal gains acquired are extremely small (Rp 2.9 Million)?

Supreme Court Rule: Authorized and Obligated to Harmonize. The Supreme Court emphasized that rigid application of the 4-year special minimum sentence against a Defendant who merely enjoyed a Rp 2.9 Million fee from a failed procurement violates public justice (disproportionality of punishment vs. culpability/gains). Judges possess authority to harmonize criminal sanctions by reconciling concrete state losses and public fairness.

2. Juridical Dilemma of Single Indictments in Corruption Cases

When a Public Prosecutor brings a single indictment under Article 2 paragraph (1) of the Anti-Corruption Law without including subsidiary charges under Article 3, the judge's position becomes dilemmatic. Cassation judges must not permit material injustice simply due to rigid framing by prosecutors.

Question of Law vs Question of Fact

Question of Law (Judex Juris / Supreme Court)

Is the rigid application of the 4-year special minimum sentence ex Article 2 paragraph (1) of the Anti-Corruption Law against Defendant [Defendant] T (who received a fee of only Rp 2,900,000) legally correct, and what are the bounds of judicial discretion in harmonizing law with public justice?

Supreme Court Legal Answer: Incorrect because it violates justice. The Supreme Court annulled West Kalimantan High Court Decision No. 195/Pid/2010/PT.Ptk jo. Singkawang District Court Decision No. 63/Pid.B/2010/PN.Skw and independently adjudicated by harmonizing a proportional sentence aligned with the Defendant's gains and public demands for fairness.

Question of Fact (Judex Facti / Singkawang District Court & West Kalimantan High Court)

Examination of facts regarding secretariat DPRD Singkawang clothing procurement for FY 2007 with a budget ceiling of Rp 64,948,000; appointment of [Co-defendant] as Procurement Committee Chairman; corporate name-borrowing by Defendant [Defendant] T and withdrawal of disbursed funds amounting to Rp 58,157,981; fee deduction of Rp 2,900,000 by [Defendant] T and remittance of Rp 55,257,981 to [Co-defendant] on January 2, 2008; total failure of uniform procurement; and BPK RI Pontianak Report No. 12.B/HP/XIX.PNK/05/2008 dated May 19, 2008, establishing fines of Rp 3,247,400 and total state loss of Rp 61,405,381.

Jurisprudence and Related Knowledge

Statutory Regulations

  • Law No. 31 of 1999 jo. Law No. 20 of 2001 on Eradication of Corruption Crimes: Article 2 paragraph (1) & Article 18 (Special Minimum Penalty & State Loss Restitution).
  • Criminal Code (KUHP): Article 55 paragraph (1) 1st (Complicity / Medeplegen).
  • Law No. 48 of 2009 on Judicial Power: Article 5 paragraph (1) (Judge's Duty to Explore Values of Public Justice).

Established Supreme Court Jurisprudence

  • Doctrine of Sentencing Harmonization (Strafmaat Aequitas): The law must not be applied with rigid mechanicalism producing summum ius summa iniuria (rigid justice spawning extreme injustice).
  • Jurisprudence on Small-Scale Corruption Proportionality: Sentencing must account for fault gradations, individual roles, and the real economic gains acquired by defendants.

Uncertainties and Gaps

Enforcing Small-Scale Corruption vs. Minimum Sentence Legal Certainty: This landmark ruling establishes a courageous judicial precedent in Indonesia: the Supreme Court affirms that the judiciary's function is upholding material justice, not merely serving as a mechanical translator of statutory text. In corruption cases charged under a single indictment of Article 2 paragraph (1) carrying a minimum 4-year threat, Cassation Judges possess the right to harmonize penalties to prevent tragic injustice for small-scale defendants.
