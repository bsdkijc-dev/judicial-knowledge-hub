Knowledge Graph

A knowledge graph represents entities and the relationships between them in a structured form.

Within the Judicial Knowledge Wiki, the concept provides a model for connecting legal, institutional, procedural, empirical, technological, and educational knowledge.

Basic Model

The fundamental structure is:

ENTITY → RELATIONSHIP → ENTITY

Example:

Court → decides → Case

Judicial Example

A possible judicial knowledge graph may contain:

  • Court
  • Judge
  • Case
  • Decision
  • Law
  • Regulation
  • Procedure
  • Right
  • Policy
  • Dataset
  • Technology
  • Research
  • Institution

Relationship Examples

Subject Relationship Object
Court decides Case
Decision interprets Law
Law regulates Procedure
Policy implemented by Institution
Technology supports Process
AI System affects Right
Research evaluates Policy

These relationships are illustrative and require evidence when used to represent factual relationships.

Benefits

A structured graph can support:

  • Discovery
  • Navigation
  • Research
  • Comparative analysis
  • Knowledge retrieval
  • AI-assisted reasoning
  • Education
  • Impact analysis

Ontology

The ontology defines the types of entities and relationships recognized by the knowledge system.

See:

Project:Ontology

AI Readiness

Knowledge graphs can increase machine readability by making entities and relationships explicit.

However, machine-readable information is not necessarily correct information.

Therefore:

STRUCTURE ≠ TRUTH

Evidence and verification remain necessary.
