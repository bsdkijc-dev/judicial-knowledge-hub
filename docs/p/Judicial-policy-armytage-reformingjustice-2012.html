'Reforming Justice: A Journey to Fairness in Asia' (Armytage, 2012)

Wiki Entity Page

Reforming Justice: A Journey to Fairness in Asia is an academic judicial policy monograph authored by Dr. Livingston Armytage (published by Cambridge University Press, 2012, ISBN 978-1-107-01382), which was scientifically reviewed by Sandra Taal (PhD Candidate at the Law Department, Utrecht University).

Summary

This document reviews a critical analysis of the failure of international judicial reform programs in development assistance over the past decades. The book critiques the dominant economic growth-based "institutionalist" approach to legal reform because it has demonstrably failed to alleviate poverty and has not created equitable growth. Armytage reformulates the primary goal of judicial reform from a mere economic instrument to a constitutive theory of justice centered on fairness and equity and human rights [1–4].

Legal Basis

  • Not found in the available sources: This document is an academic research monograph and scientific book review, not a specific statutory or judicial legislative instrument [1–6].

Background

International development assistance has allocated massive funds for judicial reform worldwide, but the performance has generally been disappointing. This is due to the dominance of the market economy paradigm that views courts instrumentally only as supporters of economic growth, without considering the distributive dimension of justice for the poor.

Objectives

1. Position justice centrally in the concept of equitable development. 2. Shift the reform paradigm from economic/political justification to a humanistic justification that views poverty as a deprivation of opportunity and human rights. 3. Build a performance evaluation framework for judicial reform based on international normative standards.

Scope & 3 Asian Case Studies

The book evaluates reform practices through three main case studies in the Asia-Pacific region: 1. Judicial reform experience at the Asian Development Bank (ADB). 2. AusAID's Law and Justice Sector Program in Papua New Guinea. 3. The experience of reform practitioners across the Asia-Pacific region.

Key Provisions & Evaluation Framework

  • Failure of the Economic Institutionalist Model: [FACT] There is no empirical evidence showing that economic growth-based judicial reform successfully alleviates poverty.
  • Humanistic Justification: [FACT] Judicial reform must focus on promoting equity, fairness, and access to justice for the poor.
  • Human Rights Evaluation Framework: [FACT] Evaluating the effectiveness of judicial reform must be based on the principal treaties of international human rights law. These treaties provide a universal normative standard recognized by the majority of UN member states.

Policy Problems

1. A lack of judicial reform evaluations conducted methodologically and independently. 2. The failure of international donors to agree on a standardized orthodoxy regarding the measurement of legal development effectiveness.

Reform / Change

Realigns the policy direction of global judicial reform to center on human rights enforcement, distributive equity, and evaluation accountability [3–6].
