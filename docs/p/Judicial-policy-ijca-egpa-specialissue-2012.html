'International Journal for Court Administration, Special Issue: Research in Judicial Administration' (IACA & EGPA, 2012)

Wiki Entity Page

International Journal for Court Administration (Special Issue 2012, Source File: 14-78-PB.pdf) is the official peer-reviewed electronic periodical publication of the International Association for Court Administration (IACA) published in cooperation with the European Group for Public Administration (EGPA) Study Group on "Justice and Court Administration". This issue features 10 international comparative research articles examining European judicial efficiency (CEPEJ), multirational court management, French judicial governance, the Sinergia research project on Swiss court management, judicial specialization in Australia, court performance measurement in Italy (CPMS), backlog reduction projects in Finland, cross-border e-justice in Europe (e-CODEX), sentencing disparities in Switzerland (BADAC), and judicial communication in France.

Summary

This special issue is led by Markus B. Zimmer (Executive Editor), Prof. Dr. Philip M. Langbroek (Managing Editor), Prof. Dr. Andreas Lienhard, and Luis Maria Palma (Journal Editors), alongside Dr. Marco Fabri and Mag. rer. publ. Daniel Kettiger (Guest Editors). The journal presents the paradigm shift from formalistic legalism to modern public administration and organizational governance within courts.

Background & Key Article Summaries

1. CEPEJ Judicial Evaluation (Jon T. Johnsen - Norway)

  • Analyzes CEPEJ statistical surveys across 46 Council of Europe member states, SATURN time indicators (Clearance Rate, Disposition Time, Backlog Capacity), and critiques the lack of attention given to legal aid for the poor.

2. Multirational Management in Courts (Angela Eicher & Kuno Schedler - Switzerland)

  • Examines courts as multirational organizations that combine legal, economic, and political rationality. Identifies 4 management practices: Isolation, Avoidance, Tolerance, and Competence.

3. MAJICE Project: Administration & Judgment (Loïc Cadiet et al. - France)

  • Compares judicial governance in France (culture of honor / centralist), the United Kingdom (culture of contract / NPM), and the Netherlands (culture of consensus / RechtspraaQ).

4. Sinergia Research on Swiss Court Management (Lienhard, Kettiger, & Winkler)

  • A 39-question survey across 32 cantonal appeal courts and 3 federal courts regarding 10 management elements, finding that NPM-based courts with global budgets exhibit highly organized performance.

5. Specialization & Caseload Allocation in Australia (Kathy Mack et al.)

  • Analyzes case allocation in Australian magistrates' courts, balancing the general principle of "everyone does everything" with the specialized "horses for courses" approach.

6. Italian Court Performance Measurement / CPMS (Lepore, Metallo, & Agrifoglio)

  • Develops a 5-perspective Balanced Scorecard framework (CPMS) combining DeLone & McLean's IS Success model and the NCSC's CCAI culture instrument at the Bari and Naples trial courts.

7. Delay Reduction Projects in Finland (Petra Pekkanen et al.)

  • Case studies of 5 Finnish courts applying process control points, a 3-stage time-frame alarm system (exclamation mark icons), and complex case labels.

8. e-CODEX Cross-Border Infrastructure (Nadia Carboni & Marco Velicogna)

  • Connects the national ICT systems of 15 countries through a "Circle of Trust" architecture to support European Payment Order and Small Claims procedures.

9. Swiss Cantonal Sentencing Disparities & Recidivism Paradox (Daniel Fink & Christophe Koller)

  • Utilizes the BADAC database to prove that extreme disparities in the use of pre-trial detention and short prison sentences across cantons have no impact on recidivism rates, making monetary fines far more efficient.

10. The Role of Judicial Communication in France (Jeuland & Sotiropoulou)

  • Analyzes the impact of the Outreau case, the role of the magistrat délégué à la communication, supreme court press releases (communiqués), and internal software systems (WinCi TGI, Cassiopée, Pharos, Pilote).

Policy Problems

1. Conflicts between legal rationality (judges) and economic rationality (court managers). 2. The risk of quantitative performance measurements threatening judicial independence. 3. Cantonal sentencing disparities that fail to effectively reduce recidivism. 4. Technical and legal interoperability challenges in cross-border e-justice.

Reform / Change

1. Adoption of integrated performance indicator systems (CEPEJ SATURN, CPMS, CourTools). 2. Implementation of time-limit alarm systems and automated caseflow management. 3. Transition toward integrated e-delivery based on the Circle of Trust (e-CODEX).
