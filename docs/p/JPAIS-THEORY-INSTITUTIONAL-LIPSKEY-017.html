1. Regulatory Framework & Institutional Isomorphism

[SOURCE EVIDENCE]: In Japan, litigation settlement (Wakai) is regulated under a simple 2-article framework in the Code of Civil Procedure, granting settlement protocols (Berita Acara Wakai) immediate executorial force (kekuatan eksekutorial BHT) without requiring a separate judicial decree. Civil Conciliation (Chōtei Perdata) is governed by the 1951 Civil Conciliation Act (UU Chōtei Perdata), while family disputes are governed by the Family Adjudication Act (UU Acara Perkara Keluarga). Key statutory innovations include:

  • Chōtei Officer (Chōtei-kan) System (2004): Amending UUCP Art. 23-2 to grant experienced advocates part-time judicial authority (2-year public servant terms) to lead conciliation panels in Summary and Family Courts.
  • Ex-Officio Orders in Lieu of Conciliation (UUCP Art. 17 / Family Act Art. 284): Authorizing courts to issue provisional evaluative settlement orders upon impasse, which automatically gain final executorial force if no party files an objection within 2 weeks (14 calendar days, Art. 18 / 287).
  • Evidence Remand Utilization (UUCP Art. 20): Allowing expert conciliation opinions and Chōtei evidence to be submitted directly into subsequent trial hearings if conciliation fails, avoiding wasteful re-litigation.

[THEORETICAL INFERENCE]: In Institutional Theory, this regulatory structure exerts coercive isomorphism over litigating parties while avoiding institutional decoupling. In Indonesia, PERMA No. 1 of 2003, 2008, and 2016 established court-annexed mediation, but created decoupling: mediation is mandatory for all civil suits, yet failed mediation records must be destroyed, forcing judges to restart trials from zero. Adapting Japanese statutory mechanisms (Chōtei-kan, Article 17 orders, evidence remand) into Indonesia's RaPerMA provides coercive structural incentives for genuine pre-trial settlement.

2. Judicial / Institutional Structure & Organizational Capacity (5M Framework)

Applying the 5M Framework to Attorney Kusano Yoshiro's presentation illuminates the structural mechanics of Japanese conciliation capacity:

Man (Human Capital & Professional Roles)

[SOURCE EVIDENCE]: Japan deploys 7,729 Civil Chōtei Commissioners and 11,483 Family Chōtei Commissioners (2025 roster). Family Chōtei panels mandate gender balance (pairing male and female commissioners, including experienced housewives) to alleviate psychological anxiety for female litigating parties. Furthermore, Family Court Investigating Officers (Petugas Penyelidik Court / Family Court Investigating Officer) under Court Organization Act Art. 61-2 conduct fact investigations, child interviews, and duty performance monitoring (Family Act Art. 58, 65, 289).

[THEORETICAL INFERENCE]: In Organizational Sociology, integrating non-judge experts and investigating officers expands judicial capacity, allowing specialized social and financial evaluation that career judges cannot perform alone.

Method (Procedural Methodology & Teori Teknik Wakai)

[SOURCE EVIDENCE]: Attorney Kusano formulates Teori Teknik Wakai to transform tacit judicial settlement skills into codifiable, transferable techniques:

  • Negotiation-Focused Approach Supported by Conviction: Prioritizes aligning party arguments via negotiation technique over imposing rigid judicial winning probability views.
  • Confirmatory vs. Constitutive Disclosure: Confirmatory disclosure (revealing judge views when parties implicitly agree) is safe and effective; Constitutive disclosure (revealing views during sharp conflict) requires extreme caution to avoid process disruption.
  • Nominal Settlement Engineering:
    • Debt Forgiveness after Partial Installment Payment: Resolves nominal deadlocks (e.g., Claim ¥800k vs Offer ¥600k) by agreeing to ¥800k total liability, paying ¥600k in installments, and forgiving the remaining ¥200k upon completed payment.
    • Geometric Mean $\sqrt{AB}$: Applied instead of arithmetic mean $\frac{A+B}{2}$ when party nominal offers differ by more than 2x.
  • Future-Oriented Philosophy: Adjudication looks backward to past facts; Wakai looks forward to create a shared future for both parties ("Wakai menciptakan masa depan").
Material (Case Files & Protocol Execution)

[SOURCE EVIDENCE]: Berita Acara Wakai / Chōtei are drafted directly by court clerks (panitera) and stamped by judges (cap hakim). Parties do not draft or sign the protocol, streamlining administrative execution.

Money (State Budget & APBN Parity)

[SOURCE EVIDENCE]: In Japan, Chōtei application fees are minimal (half litigation fee / flat ¥1,200 for family cases), and mediator honoraria are fully funded by the state treasury (kas negara / APBN). In Indonesia, parties must pay external mediator fees, creating a financial barrier to non-judge mediation.

Machine / Technology (Digital SIPP Roster Integration)

[SOURCE EVIDENCE]: Modern 1998 CCP rules allow written Wakai acceptance and judge-adjudicated Wakai clauses, providing the legal foundation for digital e-Court integration.

Legal Culture & Social Structure

[SOURCE EVIDENCE]: Historically, Japanese judges before 1980 were taught "not to be a Wakai judge" ("jangan menjadi hakim Wakai"), maintaining a "poker face" and deciding cases solely through written judgments. Post-1980, active judicial engagement in Wakai became the professional norm. Furthermore, Chōtei was created in 1922 specifically to align with Japanese social culture, which traditionally disfavors formal public courtroom litigation.

Street-Level Bureaucracy vs. Judicial Discretion

[THEORETICAL INFERENCE]: Lipsky's Street-Level Bureaucracy framework requires distinguishing frontline court staff from adjudicators:

  • Court Clerks (Panitera) & Investigating Officers: Clerks handle protocol drafting and stamping under judge supervision. Family Court Investigating Officers (Petugas Penyelidik) exercise frontline administrative discretion when interviewing children and investigating family backgrounds, filing objective reports to guide judges.
  • Judges & Chōtei Officers (Chōtei-kan): Possess curial discretion and judicial independence. Judges exercise discretion when choosing between caucuses (metode kaukus) and joint meetings, timing conviction disclosures, issuing Article 17 orders upon impasse, and applying Kusano's Teori Teknik Wakai to engineer sustainable, forward-looking settlements.

4. Implementation & Business Experience

[SOURCE EVIDENCE]: In commercial, property, and debt disputes, unmanaged litigation creates severe business disruption. By offering low-cost pre-litigation Chōtei, utilizing state-funded expert commissioners (Chōtei-kan), enforcing Article 17 orders subject to a 14-day passive consent window, and applying geometric mean nominal adjustments, the Japanese civil justice system provides high regulatory predictability, minimal financial burden, and rapid contract enforcement certainty.

C. Synthesis of Evidence vs. Epistemological Uncertainty

What is Firmly Supported by Source Evidence

  • Wakai protocols are drafted by clerks and stamped by judges, carrying immediate executorial force (BHT) without party signatures.
  • Civil Chōtei (UUCP 1951) is a standalone pre-litigation route, funded by state honoraria (APBN), with 7,729 civil and 11,483 family commissioners in 2025.
  • The 2004 Chōtei-kan system (UUCP Art. 23-2) appoints experienced advocates as part-time judges for 2-year terms.
  • Article 17 UUCP and Article 284 Family Act authorize ex-officio Orders in Lieu of Conciliation, becoming final if no objection is filed within 2 weeks (14 days).
  • Family Chōtei mandates gender balance and utilizes Family Court Investigating Officers (Petugas Penyelidik) under Court Organization Act Art. 61-2.
  • Attorney Kusano formulates Teori Teknik Wakai: negotiation-focused approach, confirmatory vs. constitutive disclosure, caucus preference, debt forgiveness upon installment completion, and geometric mean $\sqrt{AB}$ for nominal gaps > 2x.
  • If Chōtei fails upon remand, Chōtei expert written opinions can be used as evidence in subsequent litigation trials.

D. Policy Recommendations for Indonesian Civil Procedure Reform (RaPerMA)

Based on Attorney Kusano Yoshiro's presentation on Japanese practice and Teori Teknik Wakai:

  1. Institutionalize State-Funded Non-Judge Commissioners (Chōtei-kan): Amend Supreme Court Regulations (RaPerMA) to establish state-funded honoraria (APBN) for non-judge expert commissioners and accredited advocates, achieving cost parity with judge-led mediation.
  2. Adopt Orders in Lieu of Settlement (Penetapan Pengganti Perdamaian): Codify an equivalent to Article 17 UUCP, empowering panels to issue provisional evaluative settlement orders upon impasse, which convert into binding Settlement Deeds (Akta Perdamaian) if no party objects within 14 calendar days.
  3. Permit Evidence Remand from Failed Mediation: Amend court rules so that expert written opinions and non-confidential factual findings from failed conciliation sessions can be submitted as written evidence in subsequent trial hearings, preventing wasteful re-litigation.
  4. Train Judges in Kusano's Settlement Techniques: Integrate Teori Teknik Wakai into BSDK Kumdil judicial training, teaching judges negotiation-focused caucusing, confirmatory conviction disclosure, and nominal adjustment methods (installment debt forgiveness, geometric mean calculation).
  5. Establish Family Court Investigating Officers: Create dedicated court investigating officer positions (Petugas Penyelidik) within Religious Courts (Pengadilan Agama) and District Courts to conduct objective social and child welfare investigations.
