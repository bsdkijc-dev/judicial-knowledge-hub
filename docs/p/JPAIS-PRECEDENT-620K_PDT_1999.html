Overview

Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia Decision No. 620 K/Pdt/1999, dated December 29, 1999, established a fundamental civil and state administrative procedural law rule concerning absolute judicial jurisdiction in disputes involving the actions of National Land Agency (*BPN*) officials failing to execute mortgage cancellations (*roya*) and certificate name transfers:

When the defendant sued is a State Administrative Body or Official (Head of the Land Office / BPN) and the object of the lawsuit concerns actions, silence, or refusals falling within the official's authority (such as executing mortgage cancellations (*roya*) and land certificate name transfers), jurisdiction to try the matter vests exclusively in the State Administrative Court (*PTUN*) rather than the General District Court.

Executive Summary

The central jurisdictional principle established in this decision is that administrative actions, omissions, or refusals by land agency officials regarding land registration services constitute state administrative decisions reviewable solely by the State Administrative Court (*PTUN*).

Key elements established in the adjudication:

  • Lawsuits challenging official omissions or refusals by BPN officials fall under the absolute jurisdiction of State Administrative Courts.
  • Public administrative duties regarding certificate cancellations and transfers do not constitute matters for General District Courts.
  • Appellate court rulings mischaracterizing administrative public duties as general civil claims are subject to reversal on cassation.

Case Background

The Original Plaintiff / Respondent for Cassation, [Party A], won a court-ordered execution auction for land and buildings under HGB Certificate No. 20/Kedungwaru, Tulungagung, measuring 160 m² pursuant to Auction Minutes No. 43/1994-1995 dated October 19, 1994, valued at Rp 56,750,000.—.

The Plaintiff submitted an application for mortgage cancellation (*roya*) and name transfer to the Original Defendant / Petitioner for Cassation (Head of the Tulungagung Land Office / BPN) on December 21, 1994, and paid all official fees. However, the Tulungagung BPN remained silent and refused to process the request.

The Plaintiff filed a lawsuit against the Tulungagung BPN at the Tulungagung District Court. The Tulungagung District Court (Decision No. 37/Pdt.G/1996/PN.Ta. dated April 15, 1997) granted the Defendant's exception and declared that the District Court lacked absolute jurisdiction. The Surabaya High Court (Decision No. 767/PDT/1997/PT.SBY dated March 12, 1998) annulled the district court decision and ruled that the District Court possessed jurisdiction to hear the case. The Petitioner for Cassation subsequently filed a cassation petition with the Supreme Court.

Legal and Procedural Issues

Absolute Jurisdiction in BPN Service Disputes

The primary issue was whether a lawsuit filed by an auction purchaser against the Head of the Land Office (*BPN*) refusing or unwilling to process applications for mortgage cancellations (*roya*) and certificate name transfers falls under the authority of the General District Court or the State Administrative Court (*PTUN*).

The Supreme Court held that it falls under the authority of the State Administrative Court (*PTUN*). BPN officials failing to execute *roya* and name transfer applications—even when originating from judicial execution auction minutes—constitute public administrative duties. Official silence or refusal qualifies as a Negative Fictitious Decision (State Administrative Decision) ex Article 3 paragraphs (1) and (3) in conjunction with Article 1 number 5 of Law No. 5 of 1986. Consequently, the dispute is subject to the absolute jurisdiction of *PTUN*, and General District Courts lack jurisdiction to adjudicate.

Review of State Administrative Dispute Exceptions ex Article 2 letter e of Law No. 5 of 1986

The Surabaya High Court previously erred by treating name transfer services for judicial execution auction results as "judicial/executive execution actions" excluded from *PTUN*. The Supreme Court corrected this, clarifying that name transfer and *roya* cancellation applications in BPN land registries constitute pure public administrative legal actions distinct from judicial auction proceedings, thereby falling outside the exclusions under Article 2 letter e of Law No. 5 of 1986.

Question of Law and Question of Fact

Question of Law (Cassation / Judex Juris)

Question:

Did the Surabaya High Court misapply the law (*foutieve kepassing van het recht*) by ignoring absolute jurisdictional boundaries and declaring that the General District Court had authority to try a lawsuit challenging BPN official actions?

Supreme Court Ruling:

Yes, the Surabaya High Court misapplied the law. The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition from the Petitioner for Cassation (Head of Tulungagung BPN), annulled Surabaya High Court Decision No. 767/PDT/1997/PT.SBY, and adjudicated afresh by sustaining the absolute jurisdiction exception and declaring that the Tulungagung District Court lacked authority to try the case.

Question of Fact (Judex Facti / Tulungagung District Court & Surabaya High Court)

Examination of facts concerning the auction purchase of HGB Certificate No. 20/Kedungwaru, Tulungagung, measuring 160 m² in the name of Pujo Basuki on October 19, 1994 (Auction Minutes No. 43/1994-1995), submission of *roya* and name transfer applications on December 21, 1994 (Registration Form 305/1994 No. 3608), Deutsche Bank AG *Roya* Statement Letter No. 1630/94/cbd/kd dated December 26, 1994, and Tulungagung District Court Evacuation Minutes No. 13/Pdt.Plks/1994/PN.Ta. dated July 24, 1995.

Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

  • Ratio Decidendi:
  1. The appellate *Judex Facti* of Surabaya High Court misapplied the law in its legal reasoning. The Plaintiff's original lawsuit was essentially directed against a State Administrative Body or Official (Tulungagung BPN), with the object of lawsuit concerning official actions/omissions falling within that official's authority (refusal/silence regarding *roya* and certificate name transfers).
  2. BPN's actions met the criteria for a Negative Fictitious Decision ex Article 3 paragraphs (1) and (3) jo. Article 1 number 5 of Law No. 5 of 1986.
  3. Therefore, jurisdiction to try the case rested with the State Administrative Court (*PTUN*), not the General District Court.
  4. The Supreme Court annulled the Surabaya High Court decision and adjudicated afresh, affirming the Tulungagung District Court's ruling that the District Court lacked jurisdiction.

Ruling

The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition from the Petitioner for Cassation (MINISTER OF AGRARIA / HEAD OF BPN IN JAKARTA cq. EAST JAVA BPN REGIONAL OFFICE cq. HEAD OF TULUNGAGUNG BPN OFFICE); annulled Surabaya High Court Decision No. 767/PDT/1997/PT.SBY dated March 12, 1998; and adjudicated afresh: 1. Granting the Exceptions of Defendant I, Co-Defendants II, III, and Intervenor; 2. Declaring that the Tulungagung District Court lacks authority to examine and try the case; and ordering the Respondent for Cassation to pay court costs across all judicial instances (cassation court costs set at Rp 100,000.—).

Related Knowledge and Jurisprudence

  • Law No. 5 of 1986 on State Administrative Courts:

 * Article 1 number 5: Definition of State Administrative Decision (KTUN).
 * Article 2 letter e: Exclusion of State Administrative Decisions issued based on judicial examination results.
 * Article 3 paragraphs (1) & (3): Provisions on Negative Fictitious Decisions (Silence of State Administrative Bodies/Officials).

  • Law No. 14 of 1970 in conjunction with Law No. 14 of 1985 on the Supreme Court: Provisions governing absolute jurisdiction reviews.
  • Supreme Court Standing Jurisprudence: Doctrine reaffirming absolute judicial jurisdiction: Lawsuits challenging acts/omissions of public officials exercising land registration powers constitute state administrative disputes.

Evidence Status and Gaps

Established by Evidence

  • The Plaintiff ([Party A]) was the lawful auction purchaser of land and buildings under HGB No. 20/Kedungwaru, Tulungagung, measuring 160 m² pursuant to Auction Minutes No. 43/1994-1995.
  • Defendant I (Head of Tulungagung BPN) until the filing of the lawsuit in District Court (nearly 2 years) failed to execute mortgage cancellations (*roya*) and certificate name transfers.
  • The object of the Plaintiff's lawsuit was directed at acts/omissions of Defendant I as a State Administrative Official.

Not Established / Outside General Court Jurisdiction

  • The Plaintiff's lawsuit could not be examined and tried by the General District Court because it fell under the absolute jurisdiction of the State Administrative Court (*PTUN*).

Uncertainty and Gaps

  • Resolution of Land Administration Disputes: This landmark ruling establishes clear jurisdictional boundaries: General District Courts possess authority to examine civil property rights over land, whereas lawsuits seeking to compel BPN to issue or record *roya* and certificate name transfers must be brought before State Administrative Courts (*PTUN*).

GEO Q&A

Q: Where should a lawsuit be filed if the Land Office (BPN) refuses or is unwilling to process name transfers and mortgage cancellations (*roya*) for auction certificates?

A: At the State Administrative Court (*PTUN*). Based on Supreme Court Decision No. 620 K/Pdt/1999, when the defendant sued is a State Administrative Body or Official (such as the Head of the Land Office) and the object of the lawsuit concerns actions or silence refusing to execute *roya* cancellations and certificate name transfers, jurisdiction to try the matter rests with the State Administrative Court (*PTUN*). General District Courts lack jurisdiction to try such cases due to absolute jurisdictional defects.
