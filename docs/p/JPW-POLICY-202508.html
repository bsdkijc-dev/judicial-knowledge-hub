Diplomatic Question Doctrine: Comparative Research on the Judicial Review of Diplomatic Problems (Korean: 외교 문제에 대한 사법판단 기준에 관한 연구) is an official judicial policy research report published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of the Supreme Court of Korea as JPRI Research Series 2025-08.1 It analyzes the boundary between executive prerogative in foreign affairs and the judiciary's power of review, and builds a six-criteria assessment framework and procedural mechanisms (amicus curiae and executive certificates) for cases where diplomatic acts intersect with human-rights violations.

Legal basis

The research was conducted under the Supreme Court Annual Research Plan 2023. The principal references are:

  • Constitution, Article 27: the right to a trial
  • Constitution, Article 101: vesting of judicial power
  • Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties, Article 31

Doctrines and mechanisms discussed: the Act of State Doctrine, the Political Question Doctrine, Dialogue Theory (대화이론), amicus curiae, executive certificates (행정부의견서), and the 1965 Korea–Japan Claims Agreement.

Background

Tension between the executive's diplomatic prerogative and the judiciary's enforcement of human rights has sharpened in Korea because of historical-injury litigation (Mitsubishi forced labor, comfort women, and the Vietnam War Phong Nhi massacre). When executive diplomatic acts directly affect citizens' constitutional rights, courts face a dilemma between judicial self-restraint out of respect for foreign policy and enforcing substantive justice.2

Objectives

  1. Define the concept of a justiciable "legal diplomatic question".
  2. Introduce Dialogue Theory (대화이론) to bridge formal legal norms with evolving societal values of substantive justice.
  3. Build a six-criteria review framework.
  4. Propose adopting amicus curiae and executive certificate systems to improve transparency and democratic legitimacy.

Policy problems

  • Risk of total judicial deference. Complete deference to the executive can leave severe human-rights violations unremedied.
  • Risk of diplomatic disruption. Judicial intervention without clear limits and standards can damage the consistency of a country's foreign relations (the "one voice" principle).
  • No structured standard. There is no codified standard separating purely political, non-justiciable "diplomatic problems" from those crossing the threshold of a justiciable human-rights violation.

Legal and policy gaps

  • Unclear justiciability threshold. No tiered framework exists to classify diplomatic issues.
  • No transparent procedural channel. Korea lacks formal mechanisms like US amicus curiae or UK executive certificates for receiving the executive's official view without raising suspicion of behind-the-scenes judicial–executive collusion.

South Korea Vietnam War Phong Nhi village compensation case (JPW-CASE-KR-VIETNAM) A civil claim by Vietnamese survivors, including Nguyen Thi Thanh, against the Korean government over a wartime massacre.

Reform proposals

Two-tier, six-criteria framework

Tier 1 – justiciability:

  1. Expertise (전문성): weighs the executive's informational advantage against the judiciary's.
  2. Form and content of the norm (규범의 내용과 형식): examines legal certainty and impact on fundamental rights.
  3. Procedural democracy (절차적 민주성): assesses how much public discourse shaped the act's formation.

Tier 2 – substantive justice:

  1. Severe, unforgivable harm (반인도적 가해행위): whether the right to life or bodily integrity is implicated.
  2. Objective exercisability of rights (권리행사가능성): objective obstacles the victims faced.
  3. Concealment or obstruction of evidence (증거의 은폐 및 방해): active steps by the offending state to block access to information.

Institutional reforms

  1. Adopt an amicus curiae (법정조언자) system to allow transparent participation by the public and academics.
  2. Institutionalize an executive certificate (행정부의견서) procedure so the Ministry of Foreign Affairs can present its official view openly, without behind-the-scenes collusion.

Frequently asked questions

What are the report's title, series number and lead author?
Diplomatic Question Doctrine: Comparative Research on the Judicial Review of Diplomatic Problems (Series 2025-08), by lead researcher Park, Jin-young, published by JPRI on 11 July 2025.

How does the report define a "diplomatic problem" in domestic law?
A government's foreign act or interaction that affects citizens' rights and develops into a legal dispute before domestic courts.

How is Dialogue Theory applied to diplomatic issues in court?
It serves as a bridge in which judges, facing hard cases, use judicial discretion to bring evolving societal moral and human-rights values into legal reasoning.

How did the UK Supreme Court limit the Act of State Doctrine in Belhaj v. Straw?
It refused to apply the doctrine where executive acts violated fundamental common-law rights such as the prohibitions on torture and unlawful detention.

What is the core of Japan's Sunakawa doctrine?
That highly political international agreements, such as the US–Japan Security Treaty, fall outside judicial review unless clearly and obviously unconstitutional.

What is the "Approximation Theory" at Germany's Constitutional Court?
A theory giving the government broad diplomatic discretion where the overall result of a diplomatic act moves closer to constitutional order than before.

Why did Korea's Supreme Court reject the statute-of-limitations defense in the Mitsubishi forced-labor cases?
Because victims faced an objective obstacle to asserting their rights until official government documents were released in 2005, so the defendant's limitations defense was treated as an abuse of right.

What are the six proposed review criteria?
Expertise; form and content of the norm; procedural democracy; severe unforgivable harm; objective exercisability of rights; and concealment or obstruction of evidence.

What is the purpose of the proposed amicus curiae and executive certificate mechanisms?
To give experts and the executive a transparent procedural channel to present views on foreign affairs, without raising suspicion of off-record communication.

배경

행정부의 외교 재량과 사법부의 인권 보장 사이의 긴장은 미쓰비시 강제징용, 위안부, 베트남전 퐁니 학살 등 역사적 피해 소송으로 대한민국에서 크게 부각되었다. 행정부의 외교 행위가 국민의 헌법상 권리에 직접 영향을 미칠 때, 법원은 외교정책 존중에 따른 사법자제와 실질적 정의 실현 사이의 딜레마에 놓인다.3

연구 목적

  1. 재판 가능한 "법적 외교 문제"의 개념을 정의한다.
  2. 대화이론을 도입하여 형식적 법규범과 사회에서 발전하는 실질적 정의 가치를 연결한다.
  3. 6개 기준으로 구성된 심사 틀을 마련한다.
  4. 투명성과 민주적 정당성을 높이기 위해 법정조언자와 행정부의견서 제도의 도입을 제안한다.

정책 문제

  • 절대적 사법 자제의 위험: 행정부에 대한 전적인 사법 순응은 중대한 인권 침해를 구제받지 못하게 할 수 있다.
  • 외교 교란의 위험: 명확한 한계와 기준 없는 사법 개입은 국가의 대외 관계 일관성("한목소리 원칙")을 훼손할 수 있다.
  • 체계적 기준의 부재: 순수하게 정치적이어서 재판 불가한 "외교 문제"와 재판 가능한 인권 침해 문턱을 넘는 문제를 구분하는 성문화된 기준이 없다.

법제 공백

  • 재판적격성 문턱의 불명확성: 외교 사안을 단계별로 분류하는 틀이 없다.
  • 투명한 절차적 통로의 부재: 한국은 미국의 법정조언자나 영국의 행정부의견서와 같이, 배후 결탁 의혹 없이 행정부의 공식 견해를 받아들이는 공식 제도를 갖추지 못했다.

개선 제안

2단계 6기준 심사 틀

1단계 – 재판적격성:

  1. 전문성: 행정부와 사법부의 정보 우위를 비교 평가한다.
  2. 규범의 내용과 형식: 법적 명확성과 기본권에 대한 영향을 검토한다.
  3. 절차적 민주성: 그 행위가 형성되는 과정에 공적 논의가 얼마나 반영되었는지를 평가한다.

2단계 – 실체적 정의:

  1. 반인도적 가해행위: 생명·신체에 대한 권리 침해가 관련되는지 여부.
  2. 권리행사가능성: 피해자가 겪은 객관적 장애.
  3. 증거의 은폐 및 방해: 가해국이 정보 접근을 적극적으로 막았는지 여부.

제도적 개선

  1. 법정조언자(amicus curiae) 제도를 도입하여 공중과 학계의 참여를 투명하게 보장한다.
  2. 행정부의견서 절차를 제도화하여 외교부가 배후 결탁 없이 공개적으로 공식 견해를 제시하도록 한다.
