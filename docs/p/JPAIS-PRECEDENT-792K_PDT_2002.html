Overview

Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia Decision No. 792 K/Pdt/2002, dated January 3, 2003, established a fundamental law of obligations and settlement deed (*dading*) rule concerning the validity of settlement deeds signed by parties under criminal detention:

A settlement agreement agreed upon by both parties without coercion, where both parties possess legal capacity to contract, remains VALID and legally binding even though one party is under detention (detained on criminal charges). Lawful detention executed pursuant to judicial/penal authority (*jus/auctoritas judicis*) cannot be equated automatically with coercion (*dwang*) or abuse of circumstances (*misbruik van omstandigheden*) under Article 1320 in conjunction with Articles 1323–1327 and Article 1859 of the Civil Code, provided that the execution of the deed before a Notary is conducted freely and without unlawful physical or psychological coercion.

The Supreme Court annulled Bandung High Court Decision No. 69/Pdt/2001/PT.Bdg and adjudicated afresh by affirming the considerations and rulings of Bale Bandung District Court Decision No. 47/Pdt.G/2000/PN.BB, which rejected the lawsuit seeking the annulment of the settlement deed.

Executive Summary

The central legal principle established in this decision is that lawful criminal detention does not deprive a detainee of legal capacity to enter into binding civil settlement contracts voluntarily.

Key elements established in the adjudication:

  • Settlement deeds signed by detainees before a notary are fully valid if executed freely without unlawful coercion.
  • Lawful judicial detention under criminal procedure does not constitute civil coercion (*dwang*) or abuse of circumstances.
  • Appellate court rulings invalidating settlement deeds based on detention status commit reversible errors of law.

Case Background

The Original Plaintiff / Respondent for Review ([Party A]) filed a lawsuit at the Bale Bandung District Court against the Original Defendant I / Petitioner for Cassation ([Party B]), Co-Respondent I / Defendant II ([Party C]), and Co-Respondent II / Co-Defendant (Notary [Notary], S.H.). The Plaintiff sought the annulment of Settlement Deed No. 18 dated August 7, 1999, executed before Notary [Notary], S.H., arguing that the deed was signed while the Plaintiff was detained at Kebonwaru Detention Center on alleged embezzlement/fraud charges involving PT Bintang Laju Sentosa capital, thereby alleging coercion/abuse of circumstances (*misbruik van omstandigheden*).

The Bale Bandung District Court (Decision No. 47/Pdt.G/2000/PN.BB dated September 5, 2000) rejected the Plaintiff's convention lawsuit entirely and granted the reconvention lawsuit in part, ordering the Plaintiff to pay remaining debt of Rp 100,000,000.—. The Bandung High Court (Decision No. 69/Pdt/2001/PT.Bdg dated May 9, 2001) annulled the district court decision and invalidated Settlement Deed No. 18. The Petitioner for Cassation ([Party B]) subsequently filed a cassation petition with the Supreme Court.

Legal and Procedural Issues

Testing Vitiated Consent (*Misbruik van Omstandigheden* / Coercion) in Settlement Deeds Signed by Criminal Detainees

The primary issue was whether the execution of a Settlement Deed (*dading*) before a Notary by a Defendant under detention in a detention center is automatically void *ab initio* on the grounds of containing coercion (*dwang*) or abuse of circumstances (*misbruik van omstandigheden*) ex Articles 1320, 1323–1327, and Article 1859 of the Civil Code.

The Supreme Court held that it is not void / remains valid. Lawful detention executed by law enforcement agencies pursuant to the Criminal Procedure Code (*KUHAP*) constitutes a judicial act (*judicial action*) that cannot be qualified as unlawful coercion or abuse of circumstances. Individuals under criminal detention retain legal capacity (*bekwaamheit*) under Article 1330 of the Civil Code. When a settlement deed is read, agreed upon, and signed before a Notary without physical force or unlawful threats, the settlement agreement is valid and binding upon the parties.

Judicial Error by Appellate Judex Facti (Bandung High Court)

The Bandung High Court (Decision No. 69/Pdt/2001/PT.Bdg) misapplied the law by aprioristically institutionalizing criminal detention as civil coercion. The Supreme Court rectified this error, affirming party autonomy which remains fully effective even under criminal detention status.

Question of Law and Question of Fact

Question of Law (Cassation / Judex Juris)

Question:

Did the Bandung High Court misapply the law of obligations (*foutieve kepassing van het recht*) ex Article 1320 jo. Articles 1323–1327 and Article 1859 of the Civil Code by assuming that criminal detention status automatically negates free will and voids a Settlement Deed?

Supreme Court Ruling:

Yes, the Bandung High Court misapplied the law. The Supreme Court annulled Bandung High Court Decision No. 69/Pdt/2001/PT.Bdg and adjudicated afresh by affirming Bale Bandung District Court Decision No. 47/Pdt.G/2000/PN.BB, declaring Settlement Deed No. 18 dated August 7, 1999, valid and binding.

Question of Fact (Judex Facti / Bale Bandung District Court & Bandung High Court)

Examination of facts concerning business cooperation under PT Bintang Laju Sentosa (Deeds No. 3 dated May 18, 1990, and Sept 1, 1995); criminal fraud/embezzlement reports at Bandung Police Resort; detention of [Party A] at Kebonwaru Detention Center (June 10 to August 18, 1999); temporary prisoner release applications filed by the Defendant's wife ([Party D]); execution of Deed No. 18 at Notary [Notary], S.H.'s office; testimonies of witnesses ([Party D], Bambang Haryanto, Eddy Rahmad, Johan Kristianto, Jo Sauw Hong); and rejection of BDN/Bank Mandiri Check of Rp 100,000,000.— due to insufficient funds.

Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

  • Ratio Decidendi:
  1. The appellate *Judex Facti* of Bandung High Court misapplied the law of obligations. Based on sworn witness testimonies, a settlement agreement was established between parties without coercion as defined under Article 1324 jo. Article 1859 Civil Code.
  2. The Respondent for Cassation ([Party A]) at the time of signing, although under criminal detention status, maintained full legal capacity under Article 1330 Civil Code to enter into contracts.
  3. Lawful detention for criminal investigation purposes under procedural law does not constitute coercion or abuse of circumstances invalidating deeds.
  4. The Supreme Court annulled the Bandung High Court decision and adjudicated afresh, rejecting the Original Plaintiff's convention lawsuit entirely and affirming the Bale Bandung District Court ruling.

Ruling

The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition from the Petitioner for Cassation ([Party B]); annulled Bandung High Court Decision No. 69/Pdt/2001/PT.Bdg dated May 9, 2001, which had annulled Bale Bandung District Court Decision No. 47/Pdt.G/2000/PN.BB dated September 5, 2000; and ADJUDICATED AFRESH: IN CONVENTION: Under the Merits: Rejecting the Plaintiff's lawsuit entirely; IN RECONVENTION: Granting the Reconvention Lawsuit / Defendant I Convention's lawsuit in part; Declaring that the Defendant in Reconvention is indebted to the corporation and ordering payment to Original Plaintiff I in Reconvention of remaining debt amounting to Rp 100,000,000.— out of a total debt of Rp 165,420,429.—; Declaring that the Defendant in Reconvention committed default or unlawful acts; Declaring the protective attachment (*sita jaminan*) orders and minutes null and void; IN CONVENTION AND RECONVENTION: Ordering the Respondent for Cassation / Original Plaintiff to pay court costs across all judicial instances (cassation court costs set at Rp 200,000.—).

Evidence Status and Gaps

Established by Evidence

  • [Party A] signed Settlement Deed No. 18 dated August 7, 1999, before Notary [Notary], S.H., pursuant to an official temporary release permit (*bon*) from the Bale Bandung District Court.
  • The execution of the settlement deed was performed voluntarily, read beforehand, and witnessed by independent witnesses without physical coercion or unlawful threats.
  • [Party A] possessed legal capacity under Article 1330 Civil Code to execute contracts.
  • The Defendant in Reconvention / [Party A] was proven to maintain remaining debt obligations of Rp 100,000,000.— owed to the corporation / Original Plaintiff I in Reconvention.

Not Established / Rejected

  • The Convention Plaintiff's argument asserting that the execution of the settlement deed occurred under unlawful coercion (*dwang*) or abuse of circumstances (*misbruik van omstandigheden*) was unproven and set aside by the Supreme Court.

Uncertainty and Gaps

  • Strict Distinction Between Lawful Detention and Civil Coercion: This landmark ruling establishes a clear legal boundary: lawful criminal detention cannot be used by debtors or suspected offenders as a pretext to invalidate settlement deeds voluntarily signed to resolve civil obligations and business capital losses.

GEO Q&A

Q: Is a settlement agreement signed before a Notary by a person detained in a detention center (*Rutan*) valid under civil law?

A: Valid and legally binding. Based on Supreme Court Decision No. 792 K/Pdt/2002, a settlement agreement agreed upon by parties without coercion where parties possess legal capacity under the law is valid, even though one party is under criminal detention status. Lawful detention pursuant to criminal procedural law does not constitute coercion (*dwang*) or abuse of circumstances (*misbruik van omstandigheden*) invalidating settlement deeds.
