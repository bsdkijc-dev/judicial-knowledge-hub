Description / Deskripsi

Provides an exhaustive legal analysis of Indonesia's maritime security regime, evaluating structural, statutory, and institutional dynamics under Law No. 1 of 2023 (New KUHP), Constitutional Court Decision No. 15/PUU-XIX/2021, and sector-specific legislation. It examines international treaty compliance (UNCLOS, UNTOC, SUA), institutional coordination challenges between Bakamla, TNI AL, PSDKP KKP, Polairud, and KPLP, and outlines a legislative reform roadmap featuring the proposed Maritime Security Bill (*RUU Keamanan Laut*).

Problem / Masalah

  • JPAIS-MED-PROB-034: Co-existence of multiple agencies with overlapping mandates under Law No. 17 of 2008 on Shipping and Law No. 32 of 2014 on Maritime Affairs creates institutional friction that compromises state efficacy at sea.
  • JPAIS-MED-PROB-035: Bakamla lacks independent prosecution and comprehensive investigative authority under Law No. 32 of 2014, limiting its enforcement capacity despite its role as central patrol coordinator under PP No. 13 of 2022.
  • JPAIS-MED-PROB-036: Transnational syndicates exploit regulatory fragmentation and short investigation windows to execute IUU fishing, maritime smuggling, and environmental crimes.

Current Indonesian Practice / Praktik Indonesia Saat Ini

  • JPAIS-MED-ID-030: Indonesia enforces maritime security through a fragmented multi-agency model involving Bakamla, TNI AL, Polairud, PSDKP KKP, KPLP, and Customs, recently modernized by the New KUHP and Constitutional Court asset recovery expansions.

Japanese & Comparative Practice / Praktik Perbandingan

  • JPAIS-MED-JP-030: Unified coast guard models in Australia, Malaysia, and the United States consolidate civilian maritime law enforcement under a single agency to eliminate jurisdictional overlaps.

Gap / Kesenjangan

  • JPAIS-MED-GAP-011: Horizontal conflicts of norms between shipping and maritime affairs statutes prevent the realization of a cohesive single-agency multi-task enforcement framework.

Proposed Adaptation / Adaptasi Usulan

Transition from a fragmented multi-agency model to a cohesive single-agency multi-task framework via the promulgation of the Maritime Security Bill (*RUU Keamanan Laut*), granting Bakamla autonomous investigative and prosecutive powers.

Action / Aksi

  • JPAIS-MED-ACT-034: Promulgate the Maritime Security Bill to establish Bakamla as the sole national coast guard and amalgamate KPLP assets.
  • JPAIS-MED-ACT-035: Operationalize PP No. 13 of 2022 and integrate financial intelligence networks following Constitutional Court Decision No. 15/PUU-XIX/2021.
  • JPAIS-MED-ACT-036: Establish an integrated National Maritime Domain Awareness (MDA) platform consolidating satellite AIS/VMS tracking and coastal radar feeds.

Pilot / Pilot Proyek

  • JPAIS-MED-PILOT-030: 120-year transitional integration pilot for regional maritime intelligence fusion centers and joint investigative teams targeting maritime money laundering.

KPI / Indikator Kinerja Utama

  • JPAIS-MED-KPI-030: Reduction of regulatory fragmentation factor ($R_f$) in the enforcement efficiency index; 100% inter-agency data sharing across maritime fusion nodes; increased successful prosecutions of corporate beneficial owners.

Policy Implication / Implikasi Kebijakan

Harmonization of national maritime security policy through statutory consolidation, eliminating horizontal norm conflicts and strengthening state sovereignty in strategic sea lines of communication.

Regulatory Implication / Implikasi Regulasi

Enactment of the Maritime Security Bill and harmonization of criminal procedure codes to extend investigation windows for complex corporate and financial maritime crimes.

Evidence / Bukti Empiris

  • Landmark case law, including *State v. Yotin Kuarabiab (MV Silver Sea 2)* and Constitutional Court Decision No. 15/PUU-XIX/2021, demonstrates the legal efficacy of DNA species profiling and PPNS money laundering investigations.
  • Mathematical modeling of the enforcement efficiency index ($E_{eff}$) proves that regulatory fragmentation ($R_f$) directly degrades operational outcomes regardless of operational capacity ($O_c$).
