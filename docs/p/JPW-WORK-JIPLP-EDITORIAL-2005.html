JPW-WORK-JIPLP-EDITORIAL-2005

By Cecep Mustafa, Indonesian Judicial College (IJC) · revision 988, 2026-09-16 · wiki source · Markdown

Editorial: Play the game, kill the competition is a legal editorial authored by Jeremy Phillips and published in the Journal of Intellectual Property Law & Practice (Vol. 1, No. 2) by Oxford University Press on December 5, 2005 Editorial Publication.

Summary

This editorial critiques the regulatory preparations leading up to the London 2012 Olympic Games that provided excessive commercial protections to official sponsors Olympic Preparations. The author challenges the tendency of lawmakers to restrict legitimate business competition through the creation of special anti-ambush marketing laws Competition Critique.

Legal Basis

  • Formal Statutory Basis: Not found in the available sources Legal Basis [1–9].
  • IP vs. Competition Principles: The text emphasizes that Olympic sponsor protections extend far beyond reasonable limits established under standard intellectual property law Competition Restrictions.

Background

Staging the London 2012 Olympic and Paralympic Games required substantial corporate sponsorship revenue Olympic Sponsorship. To command high sponsorship fees, organizers offered special legal protections against marketing activities by competitors Exclusive Protections.

Objectives

  1. Highlight the adverse impacts of anti-ambush marketing regulations on fair competition Competition Impact.
  2. Address the disregard for consumer interests in receiving diverse commercial messaging Consumer Interests.
  3. Request a transparent explanation from the European Commission regarding competition-based justifications for such laws European Commission Explanation.

Key Provisions

  • Circular Protection Pattern: Higher sponsorship fees drive demands for broader legal protections, which in turn artificially inflate the market value of future sponsorship packages Rights Circularity.
  • Stigmatization of Competition: Applying the pejorative label ambush marketing to outlaw creative competitor marketing strategies that do not infringe on standard IP rights Marketing Stigmatization.
  • Illustrative Comparison: The use of a hypothetical bus advertisement scenario (*Acme vs. Zenith*) to contrast legitimate commercial ingenuity with actual IP infringement Acme Zenith Scenario.

Implementation

  • Final Publication: Published via *Advance Access* on December 5, 2005, and appearing in Vol. 1, No. 2, p. 79 Advance Access.
  • Referenced Works: Cites an article by London solicitor Abida Chaudri titled *"London Olympics 2012: the Race is On"* Abida Chaudri Article.

Policy Issues

  • Overprotection: Granting exclusive rights to major corporations (such as Nike or Adidas) that exclude competitors without robust competition law justifications Overprotection.
  • Consumer Harm: Restricting choices and information available to consumers purchasing athletic goods post-Olympics Consumer Disadvantage.
