The Dieselgate Automotive and Consumer Law Coordination Framework (officially titled New Solutions and Critical Remarks from Squaring the Circle of Dieselgate: Towards a Fruitful Coordination Between Automotive and Consumer Law?) is a European-Italian civil law, consumer protection, and environmental law policy framework mapping consumer remedies for emission defeat devices and greenwashing practices resulting from the Dieselgate scandal.

Lead

Formulated by Emanuele Tuccari, Assistant Professor of Private Law at the University of Pavia, this framework analyzes how the manipulation of emission software (*defeat devices*) and false sustainability claims (*greenwashing*) by automotive manufacturers are addressed through the interplay of public enforcement and private law remedies. Interpreted through the jurisprudence of the Court of Justice of the European Union (CJEU), emission declarations set out in the certificate of conformity are constructed as objective requirements for conformity of goods, entitling consumers to remedies such as contract termination or price reduction.

Overview

The Dieselgate scandal unfolded in 2014 when independent testing by West Virginia University's Center for Alternative Fuels Engines and Emissions (CAFEE) and the California Air Resources Board (CARB) discovered that Volkswagen diesel vehicles emitted nitrogen oxide ($\text{NO}_x$) on the road at levels far exceeding legal certification limits. Manufacturers installed prohibited software that recognized type-approval testing conditions to suppress emissions, while disabling emission controls under normal driving conditions. In Europe, updated software ("thermo-windows") reduced emissions only within a narrow temperature range (15–33°C). The CJEU affirmed that this practice violates Regulation (EC) No. 715/2007 and constitutes a major lack of conformity under EU consumer law.

Legal Basis

This policy framework is rooted in core statutory and regulatory instruments:

  • Regulation (EC) No. 715/2007: Article 3(10) (definition of defeat device) and Article 5(2) (prohibition of defeat devices and engine protection exceptions).
  • Directive 1999/44/EC & Directive (EU) 2019/771: Governing consumer sales guarantees, subjective and objective conformity requirements, and the hierarchy of consumer remedies.
  • Directive 2005/29/EC & Directive (EU) 2019/2161: Unfair commercial practices and remedies for misleading actions, including Article 11a concerning consumer rights to compensation and contract termination.
  • Regulation (EU) 2018/858: Motor vehicle type-approval and market surveillance framework.
  • Italian Consumer Code (Decreto Legislativo 206/2005): Article 27 (administrative enforcement) and Articles 128–135-septies (legal warranty rights; as amended by Leg. Dec. 26/2023 and Leg. Dec. 170/2021).

Background

The framework's development traces back to administrative enforcement by the Italian Antitrust Authority (AGCM) in 2016, which imposed sanctions for misleading commercial practices. Litigation subsequently shifted to civil courts and individual consumer rights enforcement. The CJEU landmark ruling in Case C-693/18 (*CLCV*) alongside the trilogy of cases C-128/20 (*GSMB Invest*), C-134/20 (*Volkswagen*), and C-145/20 (*Porsche Inter Auto*) on July 14, 2022, directly linked public regulatory non-compliance with private consumer law remedies.

Objectives

  • Harmonize public enforcement (administrative sanctions) and private enforcement (individual civil remedies) in automotive emission compliance.
  • Combat greenwashing by establishing that environmental performance claims in certificates of conformity are legally binding conditions of sale.
  • Ensure effective access to proportionate consumer remedies, including repair and full contract termination.

Scope

The scope covers competition law, vehicle type-approval standards, consumer warranty law, civil remedies, and the emerging "Right to Repair" policy landscape across the European Union and member states.

Key Provisions & Jurisprudential Interpretations

  • Broad Interpretation of Defeat Devices: Any software design that senses parameters (e.g., temperature, speed) to alter emission control systems during standard vehicle operation is classified as an unlawful defeat device under Article 5(2) of Regulation 715/2007.
  • Narrow Engine Protection Exception: The exception under Article 5(2)(a) applies strictly when the device is necessary to protect the engine against sudden and catastrophic danger or damage threatening operational safety.
  • Major Lack of Conformity: Vehicles equipped with prohibited defeat devices suffer a non-minor lack of conformity. Consequently, the consumer's right to request contract termination (*rescission*) cannot be excluded *ex ante*, even if the consumer might still have purchased the vehicle had they known of the defect.

Implementation

Implemented through CJEU jurisprudence, decisions of Italian national courts (TAR Lazio, Consiglio di Stato, Corte di Cassazione, Corte d'Appello di Venezia, Tribunale di Venezia), and the Federal Court of Justice of Germany (BGH).

Policy Issues

  • Minimalist Transposition Limitations: Minimalist transposition approaches (e.g., in Italy regarding Directive 2019/2161/EU) introduce regulatory uncertainty in civil damage determinations and judicial discretion.
  • Remedy Hierarchy Friction: Tensions exist between a consumer's freedom to choose contract termination versus legal systems prioritizing repair or replacement to preserve economic efficiency for manufacturers.

Proposed Reforms

EU legislative initiatives (such as the EU Proposal for a Directive on Common Rules Promoting the Repair of Goods, March 23, 2023) seek to strengthen the "Right to Repair" by imposing obligations on producers for free/affordable repair, introducing a European Repair Information Form, and emphasizing product durability.
