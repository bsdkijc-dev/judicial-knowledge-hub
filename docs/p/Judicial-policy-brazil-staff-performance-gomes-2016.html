The Relationship Between Judicial Staff and Court Performance Policy & Econometric Framework is a judicial human resources governance policy and econometric performance evaluation model in Brazil. Formulated by Adalmir de Oliveira Gomes, Tomas de Aquino Guimaraes, and Luiz Akutsu (University of Brasilia) in the International Journal for Court Administration (IJCA), Vol. 8 No. 1 (October 2016), it analyzes the impact of the number of judges and legal/support staff (judicial assistants) on court productivity (resolved cases per judge/year). It also examines the moderating effect of staffing on the relationship between caseloads and productivity across 27 Brazilian State Courts from 2003 to 2012 [1–25].

Summary

Published by Gomes, Guimaraes, and Akutsu in the IJCA (October 2016), this policy framework evaluates strategies for increasing judicial staff to overcome the crisis of surging caseloads in Brazil [1–3]. Analyzing 10-year panel data (2003–2012) from 27 Brazilian State Courts (Justiça Estadual) sourced from the National Council of Justice's (CNJ) Justiça em Números reports, the study tests four econometric hypotheses using Fixed Effects panel regression with Panel-Corrected Standard Errors (PCSE) [15, 18–20]. The research proves that hiring additional judicial assistants has a direct, positive impact on court productivity (H2 Confirmed), where adding 1 assistant per judge increases productivity by approximately 1%. Furthermore, the number of judicial assistants mitigates the intense pressure that high caseloads place on productivity (H4 Confirmed) [23–24]. Conversely, the proportional number of judges per capita was not proven to significantly impact productivity (H1 & H3 Rejected), effectively rejecting the "Exogenous Productivity of Judges Hypothesis" [21–24].

Background

Between 2003 and 2012, the number of pending cases in Brazil surged by 57%, while the number of judges only increased by 20% and judicial assistants by 28%. Consequently, state courts faced the threat of severe congestion and operational collapse.

Objectives

1. Analyze the direct impact of adding judges and judicial assistants on case resolution productivity (resolved cases per judge/year). 2. Examine the moderating role of judicial staff in the relationship between caseload and productivity. 3. Provide evidence-based econometric guidance for the CNJ and court managers regarding human resources budget allocation.

Scope

This policy covers all 27 Brazilian State Courts (Justiça Estadual), encompassing 11,300 judges and 180,000 judicial assistants across 2,760 districts.

Key Provisions

  • Positive Impact of Judicial Assistants (H2 Confirmed):
    • Adding 1 assistant per judge increases court productivity by approximately 1% annually.
  • Moderating Effect of Judicial Assistants (H4 Confirmed):
    • The number of judicial assistants mitigates or weakens the extreme pressure of high workloads on court productivity.
  • Rejection of the Exogenous Productivity Hypothesis (H1 & H3 Rejected):
    • The proportion of judges per 100,000 inhabitants does not have a direct, statistically significant impact on increasing average court productivity.

Implementation

  • PCSE Panel Data Operationalization (2003–2012): The processing of 270 court-year observations to isolate heterogeneity effects among different courts.
  • Optimization of Judicial-Administrative Division of Labor: The full transfer of administrative duties (scheduling, file management, communications) to judicial assistants.

Policy Problems

  • Budgetary Constraints on Hiring Assistants: Adding 50% more judicial assistants (a massive financial investment) only yields a productivity increase of roughly 10%.
  • Disproportionate Allocation of Judges: The deployment of judges to various districts does not consistently account for actual real-world caseload volumes.

Reform / Change

  • Relieving judges from non-judicial administrative tasks to allow them to focus 100% of their time on substantive adjudication.
  • Formulating a caseload-based staffing model to allocate judges and assistants based on actual workload analyses.

3 Most Important Improvements

Caseload-Based Staffing Allocation for Judges and Assistants: Shift the deployment of judges and judicial staff from a static administrative approach to a dynamic model based strictly on real-time caseload ratio analyses. 2.
