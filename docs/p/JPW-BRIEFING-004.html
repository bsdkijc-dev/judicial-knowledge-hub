JPW-BRIEFING-004

By Cecep Mustafa, Indonesian Judicial College (IJC) · revision 1171, 2026-09-16 · wiki source · Markdown

Remarks of the ICC President to the 23rd Diplomatic Briefing (Pernyataan Presiden Mahkamah Pidana Internasional pada Taklimat Diplomatik Ke-23) is an official policy speech delivered on 29 May 2013 in The Hague, Netherlands.

Summary

This speech contains reports on judicial developments in the Pre-Trial, Trial, and Appeals Divisions regarding situations in the DRC, Côte d’Ivoire, Mali, Libya, Sudan, and Kenya. In addition, the speech details the implementation of the judicial efficiency lessons-learned project, budget management, and progress on the construction of the ICC permanent premises [1–14].

Legal Basis

  • Rome Statute: Article 17 regarding the assessment of case admissibility (*admissibility*) in Libya's complementarity challenges [5–6].

Background

The 23rd Diplomatic Briefing was held on 29 May 2013 to provide transparent updates to representatives of the diplomatic corps regarding the operational developments and resource needs of the ICC [1, 14–16].

Objectives

  • Report on the progress of active cases across all Judicial Divisions [3–9].
  • Explain the cooperation between the Presidency and the ASP in the lessons-learned project for judicial efficiency [10–11].
  • Report on the status of the construction of the new ICC permanent premises and preparations for the migration in late 2015 [12–14].

Key Provisions

  • **Pre-Trial Division**: Surrender of Bosco Ntaganda; confirmation of Laurent Gbagbo's charges; unsealing of Simone Gbagbo's arrest warrant; Mali investigations; and admissibility challenges for Saif Al-Islam Gaddafi and Abdullah Al-Senussi [3–6].
  • **Trial Division**: Acquittal of Mathieu Ngudjolo Chui (18 December 2012); scheduling of the Banda & Jerbo trial; reports regarding Saleh Jerbo's death; suspension of the Ruto & Sang trial; and withdrawal of charges against Francis Muthaura (11 March 2013) [6–9].
  • **Appeals Division**: Substantive appellate review concerning convictions and victim reparations in the Thomas Lubanga Dyilo case.
  • **Efficiency & Budget Projects**: Cooperation with the ASP Study Group on Governance and the Permanent Premises Oversight Committee (*ASP Oversight Committee*) [10–13].

Implementation

Executed through close coordination among the Presidency, Judicial Chambers, Office of the Prosecutor, Registry, and ASP oversight bodies [10–13].

Policy Issues

  • Management of transition costs for the permanent premises migration and long-term cost structure modifications [13–14].
  • Balancing budget tightening by States Parties against surging judicial workloads [14–16].
