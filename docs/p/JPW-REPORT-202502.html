Definition

An official judicial policy research publication issued by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of the Supreme Court of Korea that conducts an empirical and comparative analysis of criminal proceedings and sentencing for technology theft crimes (기술 유출 범죄); evaluates protected technical assets across trade secrets, industrial technologies, and national core technologies; diagnoses systemic causes of trial delays and high acquittal rates; analyzes legal frameworks in the US, Japan, Germany, and France; and formulates statutory and administrative reforms, including jurisdiction concentration (관할집중), expansion of technical investigators, criminal secrecy orders (비밀유지명령), victim participation (피해자참가제도), detention period extension, and loss-based sentencing guidelines.

4. Policy Analysis

Background
Advanced technology forms the backbone of South Korea's national competitiveness. As global technology competition intensifies, technology theft targeting Korean semiconductor, display, battery, and automotive innovations has risen sharply — prosecutions increased from 40 cases in 2018 to 73 in 2020 and 53 in 2022. On November 8, 2023, the government launched the inter-agency "Joint Technology Divulgence Response Team" (범정부 기술 유출 합동대응단). However, criminal courts face severe procedural bottlenecks and technical complexities when trying technology theft cases.

Policy Problem
Severe Trial Delays & Chronic Backlog: 1st instance trial duration for Industrial Technology Protection Act cases averages 23.6 months (unbound) and 21.8 months (bound), compared to 6.2 months for general criminal cases. Over 40.62% of industrial tech cases take over 2 years in 1st instance (vs 3.82% overall criminal average). Average court trial dates reach 12.3 sessions per case.

High Acquittal Rates (29.8%): The 1st instance acquittal rate for Industrial Technology Protection Act cases reached 29.8% between 2018 and 2022 — nearly 10 times the overall criminal acquittal rate of 3.1%. This stems from judges' difficulty in comprehending complex technical evidence without dedicated technical support.

Inadequate Detention Limits: Under CPC Article 92, the 6-month 1st instance detention limit is far too short for complex tech theft trials involving multiple co-defendants, massive digital forensic discovery, and uncooperative overseas discovery. Defendants frequently obtain bail upon 6-month expiration, creating risks of secondary technology leakage.

Secondary Leakage Risks during Trial: Defendants and defense counsel demand full unredacted discovery (열람·등사) of technical files, risking secondary exposure of non-leaked master R&D databases or national core tech parameters.

Leniency Criticism & Disconnect in Sentencing: Sentencing guidelines historically failed to reflect the strategic economic loss of stolen technologies, leading to public criticism of "slapped wrist" (솜방망이 처벌) suspended sentences.

Legal/Policy Gap
Overlapping Legislation ("Roof-on-Roof" Overlap): Protection of technology is fragmented across the Unfair Competition Prevention Act (MOTIE/KIPO), Industrial Technology Protection Act (MOTIE), Defense Technology Protection Act (DAPA/DND), and National High-Tech Strategic Industries Act. This causes prosecutorial confusion and potential double jeopardy or inconsistent statutory application.

Snooze-and-Lose Secret Management Requirement: While 2015 and 2019 Civil Act/Unfair Competition Act amendments relaxed secret management (비밀관리성) from "substantial effort" (상당한 노력) to "reasonable effort" (합리적인 노력) and "managed as secret" (비밀로 관리된), lower courts still apply overly rigid physical access control standards to small-and-medium enterprises (SMEs).

Objectives
Elevate judicial technical expertise; streamline pre-trial discovery; establish criminal jurisdiction concentration; prevent secondary trial leakage; empower victims in court; extend detention limits; and align sentencing with economic loss.

Implementation & Proposed Reforms
Criminal Jurisdiction Concentration (형사소송 관할집중): Enact proposed Article 4-2 of the Criminal Procedure Code. Grant concurrent 1st instance jurisdiction over trade secret/industrial tech crimes to Seoul Central District Court, Suwon District Court, and Daejeon District Court, and exclusive 2nd instance appellate jurisdiction to the Patent Court (특허법원) in Daejeon.

Re-Assignment to Three-Judge Panel (재정합의): Re-assign single-judge technology theft cases to three-judge panels (합의부) via formal re-assignment decisions to ensure panel-level focus and speed.

Expansion of Technical Experts: Expand Technical Investigators (기술조사관) from Seoul Central District Court to Suwon and Daejeon; actively appoint Expert Commissioners (전문심리위원 under CPC Art. 279-2) to assist judges during hearings.

Issue-by-Issue Evidence Examination & Self-Authenticating Official Documents: Implement mandatory pre-trial preparation hearings (공판준비절차 under CPC Art. 266-5); examine evidence grouped strictly by technical issue rather than document-by-document; treat official National Core Technology Determination Applications as self-authenticating business records under CPC Article 315(3).

Criminal Secrecy Orders & Non-Public Trials: Amend Unfair Competition Act (proposed Arts. 14-4 & 18-4) and Industrial Tech Act to extend civil Secrecy Orders (비밀유지명령) to criminal trial participants (defendants, attorneys, experts) with 5-year imprisonment penalties for breach; enact proposed Art. 14-8 allowing judges to close courtroom hearings (심리의 비공개) during technical evidence presentations.

Victim Procedural Participation (피해자참가제도): Introduce victim participation rules in the Unfair Competition and Industrial Tech Acts (modeled after German Nebenklage & Japanese CPC Art. 316-33), permitting corporate victim attorneys to attend pre-trial/trial dates, inspect court files, ask questions via judges, and make closing statements.

Extension of Detention Period Limits (구속기간 완화): Amend CPC Article 92 to extend 1st and 2nd instance detention period limits from 6 months up to 1 year (in 3-month renewal increments) for complex technology theft cases.

Loss-Based Sentencing Reform: Evaluate the 2024 Revised Sentencing Guidelines (effective July 1, 2024, raising national core tech export sentences up to 12 years); recommend long-term structural adoption of USSG § 2B1.1 loss/intended loss valuation tiers (피해액/손실액 기준 유형 분류) supported by accredited technical valuation institutes.

6. MediaWiki Output

A Study on Judicial Procedures and Sentencing in Technology Theft Crimes is an official judicial policy research report published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of South Korea's Supreme Court.

Summary

This document is an official judicial policy research report published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of South Korea's Supreme Court. It empirically and comparatively analyzes trial procedures and sentencing guidelines (양형) in technology theft crime (기술 유출 범죄) cases. It evaluates trade secrets (영업비밀), industrial technology (산업기술), and national core technology (국가핵심기술); diagnoses the causes of trial delays (재판지연) and a high acquittal rate (29.8%); analyzes US, Japanese, German, and French law; and formulates reforms covering jurisdiction concentration (관할집중), criminal secrecy orders (비밀유지명령), victim participation (피해자참가제도), extension of detention periods (구속기간 완화), and loss-based sentencing guideline realignment.

Legal Basis

The legal frameworks analyzed include:

  • Unfair Competition Prevention and Trade Secret Protection Act (부정경쟁방지법): Article 2(2), Article 18(1)–(2), Article 18-2, Article 18-3.
  • Act on Prevention of Divulgence and Protection of Industrial Technology (산업기술보호법): Article 2(1), Article 9 (National Core Technology), Article 14, Article 36(1)–(8), Article 36-2, Article 37, Article 38.
  • Act on Special Measures for National High-Tech Strategic Industries (국가첨단전략산업법): Article 2, Article 15, Article 50(1)–(4).
  • Defense Technology Protection Act (방산기술보호법): Article 2, Article 10, Article 21.
  • Criminal Act of the Republic of Korea (형법): Article 355(2) & Article 356 (업무상배임 / Business Breach of Trust).
  • Criminal Procedure Code of the Republic of Korea (형사소송법): Articles 4, 30, 40, 254(4), 266-5, 279-2, 294-2, 294-4, 315(3), and proposed Article 4-2.
  • Court Organization Act (법원조직법): Article 32(1) and Article 54-2.

Background

Advanced technology is a core pillar of South Korea's national competitiveness. Cases of technology leakage abroad have continued to rise (from 40 cases in 2018 to 73 in 2020). First-instance examination of industrial technology cases takes an average of 23.6 months, with an acquittal rate (무죄율) reaching 29.8% (compared to 3.1% for general criminal cases). The limited detention period (6 months) and the risk of secondary leakage during the evidence discovery process have driven the need for comprehensive judicial reform.

Objectives

  1. Improve judges' technical understanding through the assignment of Technical Investigators (기술조사관) and Expert Commissioners (전문심리위원).
  2. Establish jurisdiction concentration (관할집중) for technology crime cases at the Seoul Central, Suwon, and Daejeon District Courts, and the Patent Court (특허법원).
  3. Prevent secondary leakage of trade secrets through Secrecy Orders (비밀유지명령) and closed hearings (심리의 비공개).
  4. Guarantee victim participation rights (피해자참가제도) and extend the defendant detention period limit (구속기간 완화).
  5. Align sentencing guidelines (양형기준) with the scale of economic loss (intended loss).

Scope

  • Legal & Empirical Analysis: Supreme Court precedent 2024Do4098 (the KAIST Professor case), case statistics from 2018–2023, and interviews with judges, prosecutors, and legal directors of major corporations (Samsung, LG, KT).
  • Four-Country Comparative Study:
    • United States: the EEA (18 U.S.C. § 1831/1832), the DTSA, USSG § 2B1.1, and case studies including U.S. v. You, U.S. v. Hao Zhang, U.S. v. Hongjin Tan, and U.S. v. Yanjun Xu.
    • Japan: Special Criminal Procedure Rules for Trade Secrets (Articles 23–31 of the Unfair Competition Prevention Act) and case studies involving Nissan, Toshiba/SK Hynix, and Yamazaki Mazak.
    • Germany: the 2019 GeschGehG (§ 23) and the Nebenklage (auxiliary prosecution/공소참가) system.
    • France: the EU Trade Secret Directive (2016/943) and the application of vol (theft) and abus de confiance (breach of trust).

Key Provisions

  • Jurisdiction Concentration (Proposed CPC Article 4-2): Grants concurrent first-instance jurisdiction to the Seoul Central, Suwon, and Daejeon District Courts, and exclusive appellate jurisdiction to the Patent Court (특허법원).
  • Criminal Secrecy Orders (Proposed Articles 14-4 & 18-4): Extends secrecy orders (비밀유지명령) to criminal cases, with penalties of up to 5 years imprisonment for violators.
  • Victim Participation (피해자참가제도): Allows victim companies' attorneys to attend hearings, examine case files, and deliver closing statements.
  • Automatic Evidentiary Admissibility (CPC Article 315(3)): Recognizes National Core Technology determination applications as official business records with automatic evidentiary validity.

Implementation

Courts transfer technology cases from single judges (단독) to three-judge panels (합의부) through a re-assignment decision (재정합의), and conduct evidence examination in stages organized by technical issue (쟁점별 증거조사).

Policy Issues

  • Overlapping regulation ("roof-on-roof," 옥상옥) among the Trade Secret Act, Industrial Technology Act, Defense Technology Act, and Strategic Industries Act.
  • Difficulty in determining economic loss value (기술가치평가) for sentencing purposes.

Reform / Amendments

  1. Enactment of Criminal Jurisdiction Concentration (형사소송 관할집중).
  2. Enactment of Secrecy Orders and Closed Hearings (비밀유지명령 및 심리의 비공개).
  3. Enactment of the Victim Participation System (피해자참가제도).
  4. Amendment of Article 92 of the Criminal Procedure Code extending the defendant detention period up to 1 year.
  5. Reform of sentencing guidelines based on economic loss value (modeled on USSG § 2B1.1).
