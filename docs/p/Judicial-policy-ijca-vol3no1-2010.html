'International Journal for Court Administration, Vol. 3, No. 1' (IACA, 2010)

Wiki Entity Page

International Journal for Court Administration (Vol. 3, No. 1) is the official peer-reviewed electronic periodical publication of the International Association for Court Administration (IACA) featuring comparative scientific studies on the High Performance Court Framework, communitative and therapeutic justice, weighted caseload management, and differentiated electronic case management systems (e-courts) [1–5, 152].

Summary

This fifth issue is led by Markus B. Zimmer (Executive Editor) and Prof. Dr. Philip M. Langbroek (Managing Editor). The publication contains five main articles presenting judicial management innovations from the United States, Australia, Switzerland, the Netherlands, and Cambodia.

Background & Key Article Summaries

1. High Performance Court Framework (Hanson, Ostrom, & Kleiman - NCSC, USA)

  • 4 Administrative Principles: (1) Every case receives individual attention; (2) Individual attention is proportional to need; (3) Decisions demonstrate procedural justice; (4) Judges control the legal process [13–15].
  • 4 Court Culture Types: Classified based on Solidarity (shared goals) and Sociability (mutual cordiality): Communal, Networked, Autonomous, and Hierarchical.
  • 10 CourTools Measures: (M1) Access and Fairness; (M2) Clearance Rates; (M3) Time to Disposition; (M4) Age of Active Pending Caseload; (M5) Trial Date Certainty; (M6) Reliability and Integrity of Case Files; (M7) Collection of Monetary Penalties; (M8) Effective Use of Jurors; (M9) Court Employee Satisfaction; (M10) Cost Per Case [19–22].
  • International Application: Distinguishes implementation strategies between transitional and consolidated judicial systems.

2. System Dynamics Modeling at NJC Yarra (Haslett, Ballenden, Godbole, & Walker - Australia)

  • Established under the Courts Legislation (NJC) Act 2006 in Collingwood (Victoria) based on therapeutic and restorative justice.
  • Uses 23 process maps, 5 Causal Loop Diagrams (CLDs), and simulation modeling to balance court caseloads with 3 core goals: "A Fair Go for All", "Reducing Re-Offending", and "Taking the Law Back to the Village".

3. Weighted Caseload Management in Switzerland (Lienhard & Kettiger - Switzerland)

  • First study in Switzerland at the Administrative and Social Insurance Courts using electronic surveys across 19 case categories.
  • Findings: An average administrative case requires 1.89% to 2.07% of the total annual working hours of a full-time staff member (approximately 38–42 net working hours).

4. Differentiated Electronic Case Management Systems / e-Court (Rooze - Netherlands)

  • 4 e-Case Management Components: Administrative, Logistics, Procedural, and Content.
  • 3 Sophistication Levels: Basic, Medium, and Advanced (e-Court).
  • 3 Caseflow Tracks: Mass flow (→ automated e-court), Complex flow (→ digital content management), and Regular flow (→ deadline tracking) [177–183].

Policy Problems

1. An autonomous court culture that triggers judicial isolation and case backlogs. 2. The risk of a court-centric tendency in community courts that shifts focus away from crime prevention. 3. ICT implementation failures due to technology incompatibility with caseflow characteristics.

Reform / Change

1. Systematic adoption of the High Performance Court Framework and CourTools indicators. 2. Establishment of the Neighbourhood Justice Centre based on system dynamics. 3. Transition towards differentiated e-courts based on contingency theory.
