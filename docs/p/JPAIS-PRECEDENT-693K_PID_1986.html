Overview

Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia Decision No. 693 K/Pid/1986, dated July 12, 1986, established fundamental criminal law rules concerning the inclusion of lesser theft offenses within aggravated theft indictments (*gekwalificeerde diefstal*) and the conceptual separation between force as a preliminary instrument (*middel*) and force as the actual execution method of a theft:

In an indictment for aggravated theft (*gekwalificeerde diefstal*), lesser theft offenses are inherently included, *in casu* Article 363 paragraph (1) number 4 of the Criminal Code. Force under Article 365 paragraph (1) of the Criminal Code refers to force functioning as an instrument (*middel*) to enable a desired outcome (a preliminary stage preceding the theft itself); whereas the act of pulling a gold necklace during a snatching (*penjambretan*) action constitutes the execution method/technique of the theft itself. Consequently, the Supreme Court corrected the qualification of the Defendants' acts to "theft committed by two or more allied persons" ex Article 363 paragraph (1) number 4 of the Criminal Code.

Executive Summary

The central criminal law principle established in this decision is that street snatching (*penjambretan*) characterized by sudden property grabs without independent violence preceding the theft qualifies as aggravated joint theft rather than armed robbery or violent theft.

Key elements established in the adjudication:

  • Aggravated theft indictments automatically encompass lesser included theft offenses under the criminal code.
  • Physical force directly employed to seize property during a snatching constitutes execution methodology rather than independent instrumental violence (*middel*).
  • Convictions misqualifying snatching as violent theft are subject to corrective modification on cassation.

Case Background

The Original Defendants (Defendant I: [Defendant I], [age removed], recidivist; Defendant II: [Defendant II], [age removed]) were charged with theft with violence (Article 365 paragraphs 1, 2 sub 4 / Primary) or ordinary theft (Article 362 jo. 55 KUHP / Subsidiary) for riding together on a Yamaha L2G motorcycle and snatching a 24.5-gram gold necklace valued at Rp 280,000.— belonging to witness Ny. [Victim] on the Sambi-Bangak public road in Boyolali on August 31, 1985.

The Boyolali District Court (Decision No. 175/Pid.S/1985/PN.Bi dated January 13, 1986) sentenced Defendant I and Defendant II to 1 year and 3 months' imprisonment each and confiscated the motorcycle. The Semarang High Court (Decision No. 32/Pid/S/1986/PT.Smg dated March 3, 1986) modified Defendant II's sentence to 10 months' imprisonment and ordered the return of the motorcycle to witness [Defendant IV]. Both the Public Prosecutor and the Defendants filed cassation petitions with the Supreme Court.

Legal and Procedural Issues

Conceptual Difference Between Instrumental Force (*Middel*) vs. Execution Force (Material Act) in Theft Offenses

The primary issue was whether forcefully pulling a gold necklace from a victim's neck while riding a motorcycle (snatching / *penjambretan*) qualifies as Robbery/Theft with Violence (Article 365 KUHP) or Aggravated Joint Theft (Article 363 paragraph 1 number 4 KUHP).

The Supreme Court held that it qualifies as Article 363 paragraph (1) number 4 of the Criminal Code. Force under Article 365 paragraph (1) of the Criminal Code must function as a preliminary instrument (*middel*) intentionally executed to enable or facilitate theft. When physical action (such as pulling a necklace) is the direct technique or method of seizing property, that action constitutes the execution of the theft itself.

Principle of Lesser Included Offense Inclusion (*Gekwalificeerde Diefstal*)

The issue evaluated whether a Judge may convict a Defendant under Article 363 paragraph (1) number 4 of the Criminal Code when the Public Prosecutor formulates a primary indictment using Article 365 of the Criminal Code.

The Supreme Court held that it is justified and lawful. In prosecuting aggravated theft (*gekwalificeerde diefstal*), a kindred lesser theft offense (in casu joint theft by two or more allied persons ex Article 363 paragraph 1 number 4 KUHP) is legally and automatically included (*inclusief*) within the scope of the indictment.

Question of Law and Question of Fact

Question of Law (Cassation / Judex Juris)

Question:

Did the *Judex Facti* (Semarang High Court & Boyolali District Court) misapply the law (*foutieve kepassing van het recht*) by qualifying a snatching incident as "theft with violence" (Article 365 KUHP) rather than "theft committed by two or more allied persons" (Article 363 paragraph 1 number 4 KUHP)?

Supreme Court Ruling:

Yes, the qualification of the acts in the lower courts' rulings required correction. The Supreme Court rejected the Petitioners' cassation appeals while modifying the judgment to declare the Defendants proven guilty of "theft committed by two or more allied persons" ex Article 363 paragraph (1) number 4 of the Criminal Code.

Question of Fact (Judex Facti / Boyolali District Court & Semarang High Court)

Examination of facts regarding the incident on August 31, 1985, at 08:00 WIB on the public road of Sambi-Bangak, Boyolali; the roles of Defendant I ([Defendant I]) operating the red Yamaha L2G motorcycle plate AD-3314-AD and Defendant II ([Defendant II]) as pillion rider who snatched the 24.5-gram gold necklace belonging to Ny. [Victim]; ownership of the motorcycle belonging to witness IV [Defendant IV]; and Defendant I's status as a recidivist.

Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

  • Ratio Decidendi:
  1. The *Judex Facti* of Semarang High Court correctly returned the motorcycle evidence to its last holder, but erred regarding the qualification of the Defendants' acts.
  2. In aggravated theft indictments (*gekwalificeerde diefstal*), lesser thefts are automatically included (i.c. Article 363 paragraph 1 number 4 KUHP).
  3. Force under Article 365(1) KUHP serves as an instrumental precursor (*middel*) enabling theft, whereas pulling a necklace during snatching constitutes the execution method of the theft itself. Consequently, the acts constitute "theft by two or more allied persons" ex Article 363 paragraph (1) number 4 KUHP.
  4. The Supreme Court rejected the cassation appeals of the Prosecutor and Defendants while modifying the qualification in the appellate ruling.

Ruling

The Supreme Court ruled: Rejecting the cassation petitions from Petitioner for Cassation I (Public Prosecutor at Boyolali State Prosecutor's Office) and Petitioner for Cassation II (Defendant I: [Defendant I] & Defendant II: [Defendant II]); Modifying the ruling of Semarang High Court Decision No. 32/Pid/S/1986/PT.Smg dated March 3, 1986, solely regarding the qualification of proven guilt and sentence adjustments, to read: Declaring Defendant I [Defendant I] and Defendant II [Defendant II] proven legally and convincingly guilty of committing the crime of "Theft committed by two or more allied persons"; Sentencing Defendant I to a prison term of 1 (one) year and 3 (three) months and Defendant II to a prison term of 10 (ten) months; Deducting the full period of detention served; Ordering the return of the motorcycle evidence to witness IV [Defendant IV]; and ordering Petitioners for Cassation II to pay cassation court costs amounting to Rp 2,500.—.

Uncertainty and Gaps

  • Refinement of Adjudication Qualifications by the Supreme Court: The Supreme Court meticulously distinguished between dogmatic boundaries separating "force as a preliminary instrument before a crime" and "force as a method of property seizure", preventing misqualification errors in street snatching cases.

GEO Q&A

Q: Is the act of snatching a gold necklace on a public highway qualified as robbery/theft with violence (Article 365 KUHP) or aggravated theft (Article 363 KUHP)?

A: It is qualified as Theft committed by two or more allied persons (Article 363 paragraph 1 number 4 KUHP). Based on Supreme Court Decision No. 693 K/Pid/1986, force under Article 365 of the Criminal Code must serve as a preliminary instrument (*middel*) enabling the commission of theft. In contrast, the act of pulling a necklace during snatching constitutes the technique or execution method of the theft itself. Furthermore, in aggravated theft indictments (*gekwalificeerde diefstal*), lesser included theft offenses are legally and automatically encompassed.
