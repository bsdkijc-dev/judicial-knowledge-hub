JPW-POLICY-000014

By Cecep Mustafa, Indonesian Judicial College (IJC) · revision 1348, 2026-09-17 · wiki source · Markdown

The Vietnamese Alternative Dispute Resolution and Judicial System Framework (Stable ID: JPW-POLICY-000014) is a national statutory, judicial, and out-of-court dispute resolution policy framework evaluating the 3-tiered court hierarchy, judge appointment mechanisms, non-governmental arbitration, procedural conciliation, consumer protection, labor dispute adjudication, and environmental dispute resolution in the Socialist Republic of Vietnam. Documented under IDE Asian Law Series No. 20 by a research team from the Institute of State and Law led by Prof. Dr. Dao Tri Uc in collaboration with IDE-JETRO, the policy analyzes court reforms, the dual-track arbitration system (VIAC vs. Decree 116/CP centres), mandatory labor conciliation, and environmental damage compensation models. Note: Named entities Cecep Mustafa and Reihan Abdan Syakur are Not found in the available sources.

Summary

The framework examines judicial and ADR reforms following Vietnam's transition to a market-oriented economy under Doi Moi. It outlines six core structural pillars: (1) Three-Tiered Judicial Structure, comprising District People's Courts, Provincial People's Courts, and the Supreme People's Court, featuring specialized Economic, Labour, and Administrative Chambers; (2) Judicial Professionalization, introducing 5-year judge appointment terms and establishing the Judicial Training School; (3) Non-Governmental Commercial Arbitration, creating a dual arbitration framework under VIAC (Prime Minister Decision 204/114-TTg) and provincial economic arbitration centres (Government Decree 116/CP); (4) Labour Dispute Adjudication, establishing mandatory 7-day grass-root labor conciliation and 61 Provincial Labour Arbitration Councils under the 1994 Labour Code; (5) Consumer Protection Mechanisms, enforcing six statutory consumer rights through multi-agency state administration; and (6) Environmental Dispute Resolution, executing non-contractual strict liability and administrative-executive mediation via MOSTE inspectorates.

Legal Basis

Authority and execution derive from constitutional provisions and statutory enactments:

  • Constitutional Charter: 1992 Constitution of the Socialist Republic of Vietnam (Arts. 12, 23, 26, 29, 35, 59–62, 68, 73–74, 127).
  • Judicial & Procedural Codes: 1992 Law on Organization of People's Courts (as amended 1993, 1995); 1994 Ordinance on Procedures for Settlement of Economic Cases; 1995 Civil Code of Vietnam (Arts. 7, 11, 268, 628); Ordinance on Procedures for Resolution of Administrative Cases (1996, as amended 1998).
  • Arbitration & International Decrees: Government Decree No. 116/CP dated 5 September 1994; Prime Minister Decision No. 204-TTg (1993) & Decision No. 114-TTg (1996); Ordinance on Recognition and Enforcement of Foreign Arbitral Decisions (1995) & 1958 New York Convention (Presidential Decision No. 453/QD-CTN).
  • Labour & Environmental Statutes: 1994 Labour Code of Vietnam (Arts. 157, 163–173); 1993 Law on Protection of the Environment (Arts. 7, 29, 30, 52) & Decree No. 175-CP; Law on Complaint and Denunciation (1998).

Background & Policy Problems

Background

Economic liberalization under Doi Moi created diverse business ownership types and complex commercial relationships. To resolve mounting economic disputes and replace the administrative State Economic Arbitration system, the National Assembly created Economic, Labour, and Administrative Courts in 1994 and authorized non-governmental arbitration centres.

Policy Problems

  • Judge Qualification Gaps: Severe shortage of judges with formal university law degrees, particularly at the district court level where many held only short-course training qualifications.
  • Inconsistent Arbitration Frameworks: Conflict and regulatory duplication between Decision 204/114-TTg (VIAC) and Decree 116/CP provincial economic arbitration centres.
  • Unlawful Labour Strikes: Widespread spontaneous strikes occurring without trade union conciliation due to missing grass-root conciliation councils in private/FDI firms.
  • Short Economic Limitation Window: A rigid 6-month statutory limitation period for filing economic court actions under Article 31 of the 1994 Ordinance that prematurely foreclosed private negotiations.
  • Environmental Compensation Deficit: Local communities facing immense scientific difficulties in proving non-contractual causation, with mediated settlements recovering only 20% to 30% of actual damage.

Objectives

1. Modernize state judicial administration and establish specialized court chambers. 2. Expand non-governmental arbitration and court-annexed conciliation. 3. Guarantee basic consumer safety, quality, and compensation rights. 4. Provide fast, zero-cost grass-root conciliation for workplace labor disputes. 5. Enforce non-contractual strict liability for environmental pollution.

Scope & Implementation

Scope

Applies across all regular courts, specialized chambers, arbitration bodies, labor councils, consumer agencies, and environmental inspectorates in Vietnam.

Key Provisions

  • Specialized Court Jurisdiction: Economic Court jurisdiction over business disputes, share purchases, and company dissolution.
  • Dual-Track Arbitration Structure: Independent operation of VIAC under VCCI and Decree 116/CP provincial economic arbitration centres.
  • Mandatory Grass-Root Labour Conciliation: Requirement for a 7-day conciliation process before filing court lawsuits.
  • MOSTE Environmental Mediation Model: Executive mediation led by environmental inspectorates to evaluate damage and determine compensation.

Implementation

  • Judicial Output: Vietnamese courts tried 165,048 cases in 2000 (86.05% trial disposal rate).
  • Court Conciliation Ratio: Achieving 44%–54% settlement rates in civil/economic disputes.
  • Environmental Compensation Impact: Settling 51 major environmental compensation cases across 30 provinces via MOSTE mediation.

Policy Problems

  • Judge Qualification Gaps: High proportion of district judges lacking regular university law degrees.
  • Dual Regulatory Frameworks in Arbitration: Friction between Decision 204/114-TTg and Decree 116/CP.
  • Unlawful Labour Strikes: Widespread strikes occurring without trade union conciliation in private/FDI firms.

Reform

  • Establishing a dedicated Judges Training Academy.
  • Enacting a unified national Arbitration Law.
  • Extending the economic lawsuit limitation period beyond 6 months.
