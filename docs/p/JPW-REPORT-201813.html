Definition

An official judicial policy research publication issued by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of the Supreme Court of Korea that comprehensively analyzes the methodologies, procedures, characteristics, and limitations of international indices measuring public trust in the judiciary and court systems; evaluates indices directly measuring judicial trust (OECD "Government at a Glance" & World Values Survey - WVS) as well as indices measuring underlying performance components of judicial trust (WJP Rule of Law Index, World Bank Doing Business / WGI, WEF GCI, IMD WCY); diagnoses why South Korea exhibits extreme discrepancies across different international metrics; and formulates policy recommendations and survey design improvements to systematically enhance public trust in the Korean judiciary.

4. Policy Analysis

Background
Public trust in the judiciary is a core form of institutional trust (제도신뢰) and social capital (사회적 자본), functioning as an indispensable prerequisite for democratic governance, the rule of law, and fair economic competition. In South Korea, while the judiciary has implemented extensive institutional reforms to enhance public trust, public perception of judicial trust has often been evaluated as low or unstable. To formulate effective trust-building policies, a rigorous diagnostic assessment of how judicial trust is measured globally is required.

Policy Problem
Glaring Contradictions Across International Indices: South Korea presents a paradoxical profile across global metrics. In objective performance and efficiency metrics — such as World Bank Doing Business "Enforcing Contracts" where Korea ranked 1st–2nd globally, and CEPEJ/OECD 1st instance trial duration averaging 144 days (5th fastest among 33 OECD nations, vs OECD average 242 days and Italy 550+ days) — Korea achieves world-leading results. However, in direct perception surveys published in OECD's Government at a Glance (via Gallup World Poll), Korea ranked near the bottom among OECD nations (22%–29% trust, ranking 33rd–34th out of 34 OECD countries). Concurrently, WVS Wave 6 showed a 66.7% trust rate for Korea's courts (ranking 5th out of 15 OECD countries).

Flaws in Single-Item Binary Survey Instruments: Direct trust surveys often rely on a single, ambiguous question ("Do you have confidence in the judicial system and courts?") with a binary "Yes/No" scale (e.g., OECD/Gallup). This binary format fails to capture nuanced public attitudes, introduces acquiescence bias (묵종 편향), and forces respondents into a binary choice without defining whether "judicial system" includes courts, prosecution, police, or the Ministry of Justice.

Over-Concentration on Technical Competence: Historically, Korean judicial policies focused heavily on case processing speed, IT e-court infrastructure, and technical competence (능력), while neglecting qualitative trust factors such as benevolence/procedural respect (호의), integrity and fairness (성실성·일관성), and institutional openness/transparency (개방성).

Legal/Policy Gap
Korean judicial policy lacked a multi-dimensional framework capable of distinguishing between objective operational performance and subjective trust perceptions, and lacked standardized survey instruments aligned with the OECD 2017 Trust Guidelines and TCPS standards.

Objectives
Diagnose methodological causes of index discrepancies; adopt the OECD 2017 Trust Guidelines for survey design; apply the TCPS multi-dimensional framework (Ability, Benevolence, Integrity/Consistency, Openness); and design balanced short- and long-term judicial trust enhancement policies.

Implementation & Proposed Statutory & Administrative Reforms
Methodological Survey Design Reform (OECD 2017 Guidelines Adoption):

  • Replace binary Yes/No scales with 0–10 point Likert numerical scales anchored by clear verbal descriptors to eliminate acquiescence bias and allow nuanced statistical variance.
  • Utilize computer-assisted self-interviewing (CASI/CAPI) to minimize social desirability bias (사회적 바람직성 편향).
  • Precise item phrasing separating "courts" (법원) from 수사기관 (prosecution/police) to prevent conflation.

Multi-Dimensional Trust Enhancement Strategy:

  • Shift from Speed to Procedural Respect (호의·윤리성·헌신 / TCPS Area 1-4 & 3-1): Reorient courtroom management from pure disposition speed toward active listening, courteous treatment (친절·응답성·존경), and robust procedural participation.
  • Strengthening Judicial Integrity & Independence (성실성·일관성 / TCPS Area 3 & 4-1): Enhance judicial ethics, transparency in case assignment, and independence from improper external or governmental influence.
  • Expanding Openness & Information Transparency (개방성 / TCPS Area 1-1, 1-5, 4-4): Ensure open court proceedings, public disclosure of court decisions, clear judgment writing, public legal education, and affordable access to justice.

Conceptual Separation of Trust (신뢰) and Distrust (불신): Treat trust and distrust as conceptually independent and co-existing dimensions. Policy must actively target and eliminate specific drivers of distrust (e.g., perceived opacity, corruption) rather than assuming that increasing administrative efficiency will automatically diminish distrust.

Establishment of a Domestic Composite Judicial Trust Index: Combine objective performance metrics (disposition times, cost ratios) with multi-item survey data across all TCPS performance areas to produce a balanced, scientifically valid domestic index.

Evidence & Empirical Data
OECD vs. WVS Judicial Trust Discrepancy (South Korea):

  • OECD (Government at a Glance via Gallup World Poll, 2-point Y/N scale): 2007 = 29% (ranked 33rd/34 OECD nations), 2012 = 22% (ranked 34th/34), 2014 = 27% (ranked 33rd/34).
  • WVS (4-point Likert scale): Wave 1 (1982) = 78.0% (3rd/8 OECD nations), Wave 2 (1990) = 66.1% (1st/8), Wave 3 (1996) = 58.4% (7th/17), Wave 5 (2005) = 50.8% (14th/21), Wave 6 (2010) = 66.7% (5th/15).

WJP Rule of Law Index (South Korea 2010–2017):

  • Overall Global Rank (2017): 20th out of 113 nations (Score 0.72).
  • Civil Justice Rank (2017): 15th out of 113 (Score 0.74); Criminal Justice Rank (2017): 17th out of 113 (Score 0.70).
  • Sub-factor strengths: Absence of corruption in civil justice (high score), effective enforcement, trial delay control. Sub-factor weaknesses: Perceived attorney fee burdens.

World Bank Doing Business "Enforcing Contracts" (South Korea): Ranked 1st globally in 2017 (2018 report) and 2nd globally in 2012–2014 and 2016–2018 (2019 report) among 190 economies.

CEPEJ / OECD Disposition Time: 1st instance civil disposition time in South Korea averaged 144 days (5th fastest among 33 OECD countries, vs OECD average 242 days and Italy 550+ days).

6. MediaWiki Output

A Comparative Study on Methods of Measuring Public Trust in the Judicial Systems and Courts is an official judicial policy research report published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of South Korea's Supreme Court.

Summary

This document is an official judicial policy research report published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of South Korea's Supreme Court. It comparatively and methodologically analyzes various international indices measuring public trust in the judiciary (사법신뢰). It diagnoses a fundamental contradiction in South Korea's scores (which rank 1st–2nd in the world for civil efficiency under the World Bank Doing Business index, yet fall near the bottom in binary OECD Gallup surveys); evaluates direct trust indices (the OECD's Government at a Glance & the World Values Survey) as well as performance-component indices (the WJP Rule of Law Index, World Bank WGI/Doing Business, WEF GCI, and IMD WCY); and formulates survey methodology reforms based on the 2017 OECD Guidelines and a multidimensional TCPS-based policy strategy (Ability, Benevolence, Integrity/Consistency, Openness).

Legal Basis

The legal and methodological frameworks analyzed include:

  • Court Organization Act of South Korea (법원조직법): The legal basis for establishing the Judicial Policy Research Institute under the Supreme Court of Korea on January 1, 2014.
  • OECD Guidelines on Measuring Trust (2017): An international standard framework for national statistical agencies and researchers to measure interpersonal and institutional trust.
  • Trial Court Performance Standards (TCPS): A US NCSC/BJA framework establishing 5 performance areas (Access to Justice, Expedition/Timeliness, Equality/Fairness/Integrity, Independence/Accountability, Public Trust & Confidence) and 22 measurable standards.

Background

Public trust in the judiciary is a form of institutional trust (제도신뢰) and social capital (사회적 자본) that is essential to a democratic rule-of-law state. Although the Supreme Court of Korea has implemented various judicial reforms, international trust surveys show confusing and extreme variation. South Korea shows outstanding objective performance in trial speed and efficiency (averaging 144 days for first-instance civil cases), yet scores low on OECD Gallup binary Yes/No surveys (22%–29%). Meanwhile, the WVS Wave 6 survey recorded a 66.7% trust level (ranking 5th among OECD countries).

Objectives

  1. Diagnose the methodological causes behind the divergence in South Korea's international trust index scores.
  2. Adopt the 2017 OECD Guidelines to improve judicial survey instrument design.
  3. Apply the multidimensional TCPS framework, covering Ability (능력), Benevolence (호의), Integrity/Consistency (성실성·일관성), and Openness (개방성).
  4. Formulate a balanced short- and long-term judicial policy strategy.

Scope

  • Methodological Analysis of International Indices:
    • Direct Trust Indices: The OECD's Government at a Glance (Gallup World Poll) and the World Values Survey (WVS Waves 1–6).
    • Performance Component Indices: The World Justice Project (WJP) Rule of Law Index, World Bank Doing Business & WGI, the WEF Global Competitiveness Index, and the IMD World Competitiveness Ranking.
  • TCPS Indicator Mapping: Classifies the 22 TCPS performance standards into the 4 core factors underlying judicial trust.

Key Provisions

  • Survey Design Reform (OECD 2017 Guidelines):
    • Replaces the binary Yes/No scale with a 0–10 point Likert scale.
    • Uses CAPI/CASI modes to reduce social desirability bias.
    • Clarifies the survey's target object by separating the term "court" (법원) from other law enforcement institutions.
  • Separation of the Concepts of Trust (신뢰) and Distrust (불신): Recognizes trust and distrust as two independent dimensions that can coexist.
  • Multidimensional Policy Framework:
    • Shifts focus from mere trial speed (expedition) toward procedural fairness, attentiveness to litigants, and judicial ethics.
    • Improves judicial information transparency and affordable legal access.

Implementation

Courts adopt a multidimensional 0–10 point Likert-based survey instrument, integrate TCPS indicators into an integrated Domestic Judicial Trust Index, and conduct public education programs on judicial performance.

Policy Issues

  • The limitations of single-item binary surveys (the Gallup World Poll), which produce acquiescence bias and blur the boundaries between judicial institutions.
  • A historical policy imbalance overly focused on technical efficiency (ability) while neglecting qualitative factors (benevolence, integrity, openness).

Reform / Amendments

  1. Adoption of the 2017 OECD Guidelines on Measuring Trust in national judicial surveys.
  2. Formulation of a Domestic Composite Judicial Trust Index based on TCPS.
  3. Reorientation of judicial policy from pure efficiency toward procedural fairness and judicial ethics.
  4. Independent management of distrust drivers.
