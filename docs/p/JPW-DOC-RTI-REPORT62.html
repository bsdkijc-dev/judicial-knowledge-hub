Policy Analysis

  • Background & Survey Methodology (Chapter 2):
    • Survey conducted July to August 2017 (extended to November for female inmates) covering 699 newly admitted inmates (462 males, 237 females) sentenced to imprisonment with labor for Stimulants Control Act violations across Japan.
    • Combined questionnaire responses with official correctional records, evaluating DAST-20 scores, drug use histories, emotional triggers, alcohol/gambling dependencies, mental health issues, and adverse childhood experiences.
  • Main Questionnaire Findings (Chapter 2):
    • Stimulant offenders show a high tendency toward repeat imprisonment compared to other crime categories.
    • Approximately 40% initiated stimulant use while underage, with most having prior histories of abusing organic solvents, cannabis, or prescription drugs.
    • According to the DAST-20 screening, over 40% fell into the "substantial" or "severe" problem categories requiring intensive treatment.
    • Primary relapse triggers included contact with user peers and negative emotions (frustration, depression, loneliness). Job stability and healthy social relationships proved key to maintaining abstinence.
    • Female offenders exhibited significantly higher rates of psychiatric issues (eating disorders, self-harm, suicidal ideation), domestic violence history, adverse childhood experiences, and drug-using partners compared to male counterparts.
    • Low utilization of support networks: only 10% to 20% had ever accessed specialized medical services, addiction recovery facilities, or self-help groups, primarily due to the false belief that they could quit on their own.
  • Statistical Trends & Treatment Practices in Japan (Chapter 3):
    • Annual cleared cases under the Stimulants Control Act consistently remained above 10,000, while Cannabis Control Act enforcement steadily grew since 2014.
    • Prosecution rate for stimulant cases remained high, exceeding 70%.
    • Newly admitted stimulant offenders accounted for over 20% of total new inmates (30%–40% among female inmates).
    • Partial suspension of execution of sentence was granted to over 1,000 stimulant offenders annually, with the majority placed under probation supervision.
  • Comparative Studies & Policy Discussion (Chapters 4 & 5):
    • Evaluated UNODC and WHO recommendations regarding the Health Care Model (medical/health alternatives to imprisonment), alongside US Drug Courts and Therapeutic Communities.
    • Applying the Health Care Model in Japan requires comprehensive consideration of local conditions: low lifetime drug prevalence, resolved prison overcrowding, and strict absence of drug access within penal facilities.
    • Primary Policy Recommendations:
      • Strengthen community reintegration support networks via partial suspension frameworks.
      • Provide early guidance and motivational intervention during initial criminal justice processing.
      • Encourage offenders to connect with support networks rather than attempting unassisted recovery.
  • Interpretations & Structural Inferences:
    • Drug addiction in Japan represents a complex issue intertwined with childhood trauma, psychiatric disorders, and social isolation, making purely retributive approaches inadequate without sustainable recovery support networks.
    • Offenders' persistent belief that they can quit independently (the main barrier to seeking help) indicates that correctional interventions must focus on motivational interviewing to shift this perception early in release or probation phases.
