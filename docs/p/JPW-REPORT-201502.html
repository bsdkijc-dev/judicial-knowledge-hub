4. Policy Analysis

Background
Encouraging public participation and openness in judicial administration (사법행정 참여) is a foundational element for strengthening public trust in the courts. In South Korea, the judiciary has developed two main mechanisms for citizen participation: the Citizen Judicial Council (시민사법위원회) and the Citizen Judicial Participation Group (시민사법참여단), alongside online and offline communication mechanisms.

Policy Problem
Operational Limitations of the Citizen Judicial Council (시민사법위원회): The Council often operates passively, focusing solely on plenary meetings (총회), and lacks subcommittees (분과위원회) or a steering committee (운영위원회) to sustain the practical implementation of its agenda.

Fragmented Participation in the Citizen Judicial Participation Group (시민사법참여단): This group, composed of over 100 ordinary citizens, lacks an independent governing body (자체 운영기구) and is entirely dependent on court management, limiting its function to online monitoring or blog commentary without a deeper consultative process.

Lack of Structured Dialogue and Feedback: The dialogue mechanism between courts and local communities has not been systematically structured, and lacks standardized management guidelines, orientation programs, and in-depth interview-based methods for identifying issues.

Legal/Policy Gap
The absence of standardized management guidelines for public participation administration and an integrated online/offline consultation platform to bridge judicial governance and the public.

Objectives
Strengthen the systematization, implementation, specialization, and consolidation of public participation in Korea's judicial administration; activate subcommittees and steering committees; diversify member representation; build an online consultation/debate portal; and implement structured feedback methods (in-depth interviews, focus groups, orientation programs).

Implementation & Proposed Reforms
Activation of Subcommittees & Steering Committee (분과위원회 및 운영위원회 활성화): Establish an executive steering committee and practical subcommittees within the Citizen Judicial Council to complement the plenary meeting function and realize judicial policy proposals.

Diversification & Representation of Membership (구성의 다변화): Expand external membership beyond well-known figures to include representatives from legal organizations, NGOs, arts/culture, religion, media, academia, women's organizations, and disability associations (10–30 members, 2-year terms; 1 year at Seoul Central District Court).

Digital Participation & Online Platforms (온라인 자문 및 커뮤니티 구축): Develop a dedicated website for online consultation, e-panels, and deliberative e-polling (숙의형 e-여론조사) to reach a broader range of stakeholders.

Issue Identification & Capacity-Building Methods: Conduct in-depth interviews and focus groups with court users, staff, and participants; develop management guidelines; hold orientation programs; and establish online communities.

6. MediaWiki Output

A Research on Measures for Encouraging Participation of the Public in Judicial Administration is an official judicial policy research report published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of South Korea's Supreme Court.

Summary

This document is an official judicial policy research report published by the Judicial Policy Research Institute (JPRI) of South Korea's Supreme Court. It evaluates the development and operational status of public participation in South Korea's judicial administration — specifically the Citizen Judicial Council (시민사법위원회) and the Citizen Judicial Participation Group (시민사법참여단) — and examines court and community collaboration practices in the United States. It formulates structured reform proposals to strengthen representative and comprehensive dialogue between the judiciary and the public.

Legal Basis

The legal and operational frameworks analyzed include:

  • Court Internal Regulations (법원 내규) & Council Bylaws (위원회 회칙): Internal rules underlying the establishment and operation of Citizen Judicial Councils at various courts (such as the Seoul High Court, Daejeon High Court, Seoul Administrative Court, and Seoul Southern District Court).

Background

Strengthening public participation in judicial administration is a strategic step toward improving transparency and public trust in the courts. In South Korea, courts operate two main channels: the Citizen Judicial Council, which focuses on policy advice, and the Citizen Judicial Participation Group, which focuses on online monitoring and publication. However, both bodies face challenges from passive plenary meetings and a lack of structured participation.

Objectives

  1. Diagnose the operational status of the Citizen Judicial Council and the Citizen Judicial Participation Group.
  2. Study court-community collaboration systems in the United States.
  3. Formulate policy proposals to improve the systematization, implementation, specialization, and consolidation of public participation.

Scope

  • Domestic Operational Study: Comparative evaluation of the Citizen Judicial Council (10–30 experts/figures) and the Citizen Judicial Participation Group (100+ ordinary citizens).
  • US Comparative Study: Review of court and community collaboration and judicial outreach.
  • Participation Method Development: Use of in-depth interviews, focus groups, deliberative e-polling, and online consultation platforms.

Key Provisions

  • Citizen Judicial Council Structure: Composed of 10–30 members (external members from NGOs, law, culture, religion, media, academia, disability advocacy, plus around 5 internal court members). Term of 2 years (1 year at Seoul Central District Court).
  • Citizen Judicial Participation Group Structure: Composed of 100+ ordinary citizens with no independent governing body, directly managed by the courts for online monitoring and blog activities.
  • Activation of Sub-Bodies: Establishment of a Steering Committee (운영위원회) and Subcommittees (분과위원회) to advance the practical agenda.

Implementation

Courts implement public participation management guidelines, conduct orientation programs for staff and participants, and integrate website-based consultation platforms and online communities.

Policy Issues

  • The passive role of the Citizen Judicial Council's plenary meetings without active subcommittee support.
  • The lack of an independent governing body within the Citizen Judicial Participation Group.

Reform / Amendments

  1. Activation of a Steering Committee and Subcommittees within the Citizen Judicial Council.
  2. Diversification of external membership representation across various sectors of society.
  3. Launch of an online consultation platform and deliberative e-polling system (숙의형 e-여론조사).
  4. Development of management guidelines and structured orientation programs.

Relationship to Other Entities

  • Lead Researcher: Rhee, Jae-Seok (이재석).
