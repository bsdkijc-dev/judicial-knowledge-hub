'Understanding IT for Dispute Resolution' (Reiling, 2009/2013)

Wiki Entity Page

The Matrix of Judicial Roles and IT Functional Requirements Framework is a judicial ICT policy model and court information processing research framework developed by Dr. Dory Reiling (Judge at the First Instance Court of Amsterdam) based on her dissertation Technology for Justice (2009). This framework adapts Erhard Blankenburg's matrix of judicial roles to map court caseflow based on two main dimensions—outcome uncertainty (certain vs. uncertain) and party relationship dynamics (zero-sum vs. win-win)—in order to categorize the judicial caseload into four functional groups (Title Provision, Notarial Declaration, Settlement Support, and Judicial Judgment) to determine the most optimal ICT functionalities, accelerate case resolution, and expand access to justice.

Summary

Reiling asserts that the primary obstacle to technology adoption in courts is not the "judges' lack of IT understanding", but rather a lack of understanding regarding how courts process information. Using data from over 900,000 first instance civil cases in the Netherlands (2007), Reiling proves that the work of adjudicating contested cases (Group 4 - Judgment) only covers 8% of the total court caseload. Most court operations consist of registering undefended claims (Group 1 - Title, 35%), formal declarations (Group 2 - Notarial, 30%), and withdrawals/settlements (Group 3 - Settlement, 9%). By identifying the information characteristics in each group, courts can implement automated electronic filing for Title Cases, provide web forms and standard formulas for Notarial Cases, online negotiation platforms for Settlement Cases, and electronic case files (e-files) and decision support systems for Judgment Cases [12–17, 20–25].

Role Group Case Characteristics Workload Proportion Primary Information Technology (IT) Solution Real-World Implementation Examples
Group 1: Title (Title Provision) Certain Outcome / Zero-Sum (Undefended money claims) 35% Full internal processing automation & independent electronic data filing Money Claim Online (MCOL, UK) & Mahnverfahren (Germany - >90% of 9.5 million claims processed automatically in 2003) [12–15].

Caseflow Re-engineering Strategy ("Shifting Left and Down")

  • Simplification (Shifting Left): Creating routines and standard legal formulas reduces case uncertainty, moving cases from Group 4 (Judgment) to Group 2 (Notarial) or Group 1 (Title).
  • Early Intervention (Shifting Down): Facilitating early mediation and providing golden legal nuggets (practical, jargon-free legal guidance - Susskind) encourages parties to settle disputes out of court.

Application of Party Litigation Theory (Galanter: One-Shotters vs. Repeat Players)

  • Repeat Players: Financial institutions or corporations that frequently act as plaintiffs in Group 1 (Title). Courts benefit by opening channels for bulk electronic data entry.
  • One-Shotters: Ordinary citizens who rarely litigate in court. Courts are obligated to provide clear web portals to improve procedural justice for them.
