Policy Analysis

  • Background & Policy Problem:
    • FACT: The previous English land registration regime relied on the Land Registration Act 1925 (LRA 1925) for nearly 80 years, exhibiting procedural deficiencies and a lack of support for digital legal frameworks.
    • FACT: Core problems under the legacy regime included complex title deed verification for unregistered land (requiring title history tracing back at least 15 years), high risk of hidden third-party interests, and substantial legal fees for conveyancing.
  • Policy Objectives:
    • FACT: Achieve comprehensive land registration in England and Wales (aiming for 100% registered land by 2030).
    • FACT: Accelerate modernization and transition to paperless electronic conveyancing (e-conveyancing).
    • FACT: Ensure legal certainty, transparency, and a state-backed title guarantee.
  • Key Provisions & Reforms:
    • FACT: Three Core Registration Principles: Mirror Principle (register accurately reflects legal state), Curtain Principle (register hides behind-the-scenes trust details), and Insurance Principle (state guarantees accuracy and compensates errors).
    • FACT: Compulsory Registration Triggers: Transfers of freehold, creation of leaseholds over 7 years, and first legal mortgages mandatorily trigger initial registration under Section 4.
    • FACT: Restriction of Overriding Interests: Narrows overriding interests binding buyers without registration under Schedules 1 and 3 (e.g., leases <= 7 years, actual occupation).
    • FACT: Adverse Possession Reform: Reconciles adverse possession for registered land under Schedule 6; squatters after 10 years must apply to the registrar, allowing registered owners notice to object.
    • FACT: e-Conveyancing Framework: Sections 91–95 and Schedule 5 establish a network-based electronic land transaction system.
  • Dispute Resolution:
    • FACT: Land registration disputes were reassigned to the specialized First-tier Tribunal (Property Chamber).
