Policy Analysis

  • Background & Policy Problem:
    • FACT: The Japanese government previously established the Comprehensive Measures for Acceptance and Coexistence of Foreign Nationals on December 25, 2018, revised annually. To address social isolation among foreign residents during COVID-19 and establish a long-term framework, the Ministry of Justice formed an Advisory Panel of Experts on January 29, 2021. Based on its November 29, 2021 report, the Ministerial Conference adopted the Roadmap for Realizing a Society of Harmonious Coexistence with Foreign Nationals on June 14, 2022.
  • Planning Period & Three Visions:
    • FACT: The Roadmap covers a 5-year execution period through FY 2026, with annual progress evaluations conducted with experts.
    • Three Visions:
      • A society where foreign residents are included as members of Japan's future, where everyone can live safely and comfortably.
      • A diverse and dynamic society where all individuals can participate and demonstrate their abilities regardless of background.
      • A society respecting individual dignity and human rights without discrimination or prejudice.
  • Four Key Strategic Pillars:
    • Japanese Language Education: Curriculum development aligned with the Framework of Reference for Japanese Language Education, expanding ICT materials, overseas language education, and teacher qualifications.
    • Information Dissemination & Consultation Systems: Publication of the Guidebook on Living and Working (16 languages), Mynaportal integration, local One-Stop consultation subsidies (228 municipalities in FY2022), 15-language simultaneous translation, and Plain Japanese Guidelines.
    • Life-Cycle Support: Linking foreign children's school enrollment data with Basic Resident Registration, public high school admission quotas, Hello Work career counseling, and pension system orientation.
    • Coexistence Infrastructure: Establishing Coexistence Awareness Month, utilizing the Basic Survey on Foreign Residents database, integrating Residence Cards with Individual Number Cards (My Number Card), and centralizing residency management at ISA.
  • Related Policy Frameworks:
    • Specified Skilled Worker & Technical Intern Programs: Oversight of Specified Skilled Workers (87,472 individuals as of June 2022) and OTIT technical intern training.
    • ISA Ethical Reform: Implementation of "Mission and Principles of ISA Staff" (January 14, 2022) defining 8 core work ethics principles following the Nagoya Immigration Bureau detainee death incident.
    • Humanitarian Support: Assistance for Ukrainian evacuees (Designated Activities status, certification, FRESC Help Desk) and emergency evacuation measures for Myanmar nationals.
  • Interpretations & Structural Inferences:
    • INTERPRETATION: The Roadmap marks a transition in Japanese immigration policy from pure border control to long-term social integration, framing foreign residents as key stakeholders in Japan's future.
    • INFERENCE: Successfully executing the 218 action items requires reliable digital integration (My Number Cards, e-Notification) and sustained subsidies for local One-Stop consultation centers.
