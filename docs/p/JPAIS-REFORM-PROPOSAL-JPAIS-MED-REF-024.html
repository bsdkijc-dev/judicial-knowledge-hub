Description / Deskripsi

Provides an exhaustive analysis of Intelligence-Led Maritime Policing, examining the transition from reactive patrol-dense paradigms to proactive risk-based frameworks using the National Intelligence Model and the ACTION Risk Analysis Model[cite: 6]. It details the maritime intelligence cycle, multi-source intelligence (Multi-INT) synergy, maritime domain awareness (MDA) fusion architectures, the UNODC "Legal Finish" operational framework, and the Universal Maritime Intelligence-Led Policing Model[cite: 6].

Problem / Masalah

  • JPAIS-MED-PROB-016: Traditional reactive maritime policing relies on persistent physical patrols across vast ocean spaces, rendering pure patrol models economically and logistically unsustainable[cite: 6].
  • JPAIS-MED-PROB-017: High-tech surveillance platforms frequently collect actionable operational intelligence that fails to meet legal standards for court admissibility, causing case dismissals[cite: 6].
  • JPAIS-MED-PROB-018: Interagency data siloing and restrictive "need-to-know" security protocols prevent seamless intelligence sharing among naval forces, coast guards, customs, and fisheries agencies[cite: 6].

Current Indonesian Practice / Praktik Indonesia Saat Ini

  • JPAIS-MED-ID-024: Indonesian maritime enforcement agencies coordinate interagency responses across archipelagic waters, integrating surface assets with coastal surveillance infrastructure[cite: 6].

Japanese & Comparative Practice / Praktik Perbandingan

  • JPAIS-MED-JP-024: Advanced global maritime jurisdictions utilize integrated fusion centers, automated anomaly detection algorithms, and multi-domain intelligence fusion to target shadow fleets and dark activity[cite: 6].

Gap / Kesenjangan

  • JPAIS-MED-GAP-007: Disconnections between high-seas intelligence gathering and judicial chain-of-custody protocols undermine successful prosecutions of transnational maritime criminals[cite: 6].

Proposed Adaptation / Adaptasi Usulan

Operationalize the Universal Maritime Intelligence-Led Policing Model by formalizing interagency fusion architectures, embedding legal advisors directly into interdiction planning via UNODC "Legal Finish" standards, and investing in non-cooperative tracking technologies[cite: 6].

Action / Aksi

  • JPAIS-MED-ACT-016: Formalize integrated maritime intelligence fusion centers empowered by binding legal agreements and unified Common Operational Picture software[cite: 6].
  • JPAIS-MED-ACT-017: Embed prosecutors and legal officers directly into interdiction planning teams to standardize VBSS evidentiary protocols[cite: 6].
  • JPAIS-MED-ACT-018: Prioritize funding for non-cooperative tracking assets, including space-borne Synthetic Aperture Radar and commercial radio frequency geolocation[cite: 6].

Pilot / Pilot Proyek

  • JPAIS-MED-PILOT-024: 180-day regional integration pilot deploying automated algorithmic anomaly detection and multi-INT sensor cueing across strategic maritime transit chokepoints[cite: 6].

KPI / Indikator Kinerja Utama

  • JPAIS-MED-KPI-024: 100% integration of legal advisors in interdiction planning; zero case dismissals due to evidentiary chain-of-custody gaps; measurable increase in successful judicial prosecutions resulting from intelligence-led interdictions[cite: 6].

Policy Implication / Implikasi Kebijakanلاء

Transition of national maritime security strategy from reactive surface patrols to proactive, multi-domain intelligence-led policing architectures[cite: 6].

Regulatory Implication / Implikasi Regulasi

Enactment of binding interagency data-sharing protocols and formal legal frameworks supporting UNODC "Legal Finish" standards across national jurisdictions[cite: 6].

Evidence / Bukti Empiris

  • Automated platforms like Project Air CHAN compress intelligence production from weeks into minutes, enabling near-real-time dissemination across joint operational command networks[cite: 6].
  • Multi-INT sensor tipping and queuing successfully overcome gaps caused by vessel transponder disablement and dark fleet AIS spoofing[cite: 6].
