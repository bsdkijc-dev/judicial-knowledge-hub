JPW-REGULATION-UK-CMA-TALENT-2025

By Cecep Mustafa, Indonesian Judicial College (IJC) · revision 905, 2026-09-15 · wiki source · Markdown

CMA Guidance on Competing for Talent is official regulatory guidance issued by the Competition and Markets Authority (CMA) on September 9, 2025, regarding the application of competition law to employee recruitment and retention Passage 7.

Executive Summary

The guidance assists HR professionals, internal recruiters, and employers in understanding and complying with competition law within employment markets Passage 7. It establishes that businesses competing to recruit or retain workers are competitors in the labor market subject to competition law, regardless of whether they compete for the same consumer client base Passage 7.

Legal Basis

  • UK Competition Law, which prohibits agreements or concerted practices that restrict or distort fair competition Passage 7.
  • CMA Labor Cartel Infringement Decision (March 2025), establishing that labor market restrictions are treated as hardcore cartel behavior Passage 7.

Background

Publication of this guidance follows intensified regulatory enforcement against anti-competitive labor behavior globally, particularly across the technology, media, and sports sectors Passage 7. Many organizations were unaware that informal agreements regarding wages or hiring between employers constitute cartel infringements Passage 7.

Objectives

  1. Assist HR personnel and recruiters in avoiding competition law breaches Passage 7.
  2. Safeguard labor mobility and workers' ability to negotiate fair remuneration Passage 7.
  3. Identify and eliminate the three main types of labor cartels Passage 7.
  4. Foster a culture of competition compliance within corporate organizational structures Passage 7.

Key Provisions

The CMA defines three primary prohibited behaviors:

  • No-Poaching Agreements: Agreements between businesses not to hire or recruit each other's employees Passage 7.
  • Wage-Fixing Agreements: Agreements between employers to fix or cap wages, bonuses, or employment benefits Passage 7.
  • Information Sharing & Benchmarking: Exchanging non-public, sensitive details on salaries, bonuses, and terms of employment Passage 7.

Implementation

Employer compliance measures include:

  • HR Training: Elevating HR departments into a frontline compliance role (*frontline compliance function*) Passage 7.
  • Employment Audits: Reviewing hiring policies and recruitment workflows to ensure alignment with competition rules Passage 7.
  • Regulatory Cooperation: Participating fully in CMA investigations to qualify for leniency or fine reductions where breaches occur Passage 7.

Policy & Compliance Issues

  • Industry Communication Boundaries: Drawing clear lines between permissible economic trend discussions and prohibited wage data exchanges Passage 7.
  • Non-Solicitation vs. No-Poaching: Ensuring commercial non-solicitation clauses remain proportionate and limited in duration, scope, and geography so as not to be reclassified as unlawful no-poaching deals Passage 7.
