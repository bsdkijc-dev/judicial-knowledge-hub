Problems & Gaps

  • KCCP-PROB-001 / KCCP-GAP-001 — JPAIS-DISP-PROB-001 / GAP-001: Uniform 5-month deadline forces conflict between speed and thoroughness, ignoring case complexity, evidentiary volume, or geographic summons delays.
  • KCCP-PROB-002 / KCCP-GAP-002 — JPAIS-LOAD-PROB-001 / GAP-001: Unweighted raw docket allocations (Supreme Court Justices handle 2,076–2,373 cases/year; lower judges handle 1,186/year including traffic infractions) causing severe workload imbalance.
  • KCCP-PROB-003 / KCCP-GAP-003 — JPAIS-QUAL-PROB-001 / GAP-001: Fast minutation rules and speed metrics incentivize premature procedural dismissals (putusan NO) and thin legal reasoning (onvoldoende gemotiveerd).
  • KCCP-PROB-004 / KCCP-GAP-004 — JPAIS-ACCS-PROB-001 / GAP-001: Gross e-Court metrics (410,754 cases in 2024) mask procedural barriers and exclusion of indigent, self-represented (pengguna lain), or digitally illiterate litigants.
  • KCCP-PROB-005 / KCCP-GAP-005 — JPAIS-BNCH-PROB-001 / GAP-001: Counter-side lobby survey steering (SKM/SPAK) creates social pressure, generating inflated integrity scores that mask administrative bottlenecks.

Reform Proposals

  • KCCP-REFORM-001 — JPAIS-DISP-REF-001: Differentiated Case Management (DCM) in SIPP — Track A (Summary: 60 days), Track B (Standard: 150 days under SEMA 2/2014), Track C (Complex: 270 days).
  • KCCP-REFORM-002 — JPAIS-LOAD-REF-001: Weighted Caseload Allocation Module in SIPP (scoring 0.1 for traffic infractions to 5.0 for multi-party/land disputes; ceiling of 40 active weighted points per judge).
  • KCCP-REFORM-003 — JPAIS-QUAL-REF-001: Appellate Durability Module in AMPUH & EIS — tracking Appellate Affirmance Rate (AAR), reasoning defect ratio, and procedural dismissal (putusan NO) ratio.
  • KCCP-REFORM-004 — JPAIS-ACCS-REF-001: Net Procedural Accessibility Score (NPAS) in EIS/AMPUH — conditioning top scores on verified PTSP legal aid onboarding, prodeo speed, and disability standards.
  • KCCP-REFORM-005 — JPAIS-BNCH-REF-001: Independent Court User Experience (ICUX) blind survey platform managed directly by Bawas via WhatsApp/SMS 48 hours post-disposition.

3. Japan → Indonesia Co-Creation Pipeline

Japanese Practice → Purpose → Indonesian Practice → Gap → Adaptation → Risk → Pilot → KPI

Pipeline 1: Statutory Macro-Targets vs. Uniform Monthly Deadlines

  • Japanese Practice: Act on Expediting Trials (Act No. 107 of 2003) establishing a flexible 2-year statutory macro-target verified via biannual reviews (Saiban no Jinsokuka ni Kakawaru Kenshō Kekka no Kōhyō).
  • Purpose: Avoid transactional speed mandates on judges while maintaining long-term systemic clearance.
  • Indonesian Current Practice: Uniform 5-month first-instance disposition deadline under SEMA No. 2 of 2014 tracked real-time in EIS v3.
  • Gap: JPAIS-DISP-GAP-001 — Failure to calibrate disposition velocity against case complexity, evidentiary volume, or geographic summons challenges.
  • Indonesia-Adapted Option: SIPP Differentiated Case Management (DCM) assigning Track A (Summary: 60 days), Track B (Standard: 150 days under SEMA 2/2014), and Track C (Complex: 270 days) at registration.
  • Risk: Misclassification of standard cases into Track C by litigants or clerks to gain extended deadlines.
  • Pilot: KCCP-PILOT-001 (PN Jakarta Pusat, PN Sleman, PN Kupang).
  • KPI: KCCP-KPI-001 (Milestone Timeliness Index MTI ≥ 95%; Adjournment Interval Ratio ≤ 10 calendar days; DCM Clearance Rate 98–102%).

Pipeline 2: Pre-Trial Filtering & Workload Balance

  • Japanese Practice: Extensive pre-trial dispute filtering via prosecutorial screening, court-annexed conciliation (minji chōtei), judicial settlements (wakai), and 3-year judicial rotation via Jimusōkyoku.
  • Purpose: Prevent judicial dockets from becoming overwhelmed by low-complexity disputes and balance institutional pressure.
  • Indonesian Current Practice: Unweighted raw docket allocations where summary traffic infractions (tilang) and complex land disputes count equally.
  • Gap: JPAIS-LOAD-GAP-001 — Absence of a Weighted Caseload System (WCS) to measure actual judicial effort.
  • Indonesia-Adapted Option: SIPP Weighted Caseload Allocation Module assigning weighted points (0.1 for tilang to 5.0 for multi-party/land disputes) with a ceiling of 40 active weighted points per judge.
  • Risk: Judicial pushback regarding point weight assignments across specialized divisions.
  • Pilot: KCCP-PILOT-002 (PN Surabaya, PN Klaten).
  • KPI: KCCP-KPI-002 (Caseload Variance Coefficient CVC < 15%; Weighted Clearance Ratio 97–102%; Over-Threshold Pending Rate < 5%).

Pipeline 3: Substantive Reasoning & Appellate Reversal Tracking

  • Japanese Practice: Rigorous judgment drafting (hanketsu riyū), peer deliberations (gōgisei), and appellate reversal (haki) tracking in career portfolios by Jimusōkyoku / Jinji-kyoku.
  • Purpose: Ensure judges do not sacrifice legal analysis or thorough factual findings for administrative speed.
  • Indonesian Current Practice: Fast minutation rules (7–14 days under SEMA 1/2017) and EIS speed rankings evaluating document presence rather than legal reasoning depth.
  • Gap: JPAIS-QUAL-GAP-001 — Absence of qualitative metrics evaluating substantive legal reasoning and appellate durability.
  • Indonesia-Adapted Option: Appellate Durability Module in AMPUH & EIS tracking Appellate Affirmance Rate (AAR), reasoning defect ratio (onvoldoende gemotiveerd), and procedural dismissal (putusan NO) ratio.
  • Risk: High Court review delays in returning appellate durability audit data.
  • Pilot: KCCP-PILOT-003 (5 District Courts under PT Semarang).
  • KPI: KCCP-KPI-003 (Appellate Affirmance Rate AAR ≥ 85%; Reasoning Defect Ratio < 5%; Procedural Dismissal Index ≤ 5%).

Pipeline 4: User-Centric Digitization & Parallel Paper Channels

  • Japanese Practice: Phased civil procedure digitization allowing parallel paper tracks for self-represented litigants (honnin soshō) and legal aid via Hōterasu.
  • Purpose: Protect constitutional due process and prevent technology from excluding digitally illiterate citizens.
  • Indonesian Current Practice: Rapid e-Court expansion (410,754 cases in 2024) where gross adoption percentages drive administrative scoring.
  • Gap: JPAIS-ACCS-GAP-001 — Digital KPIs mask procedural barriers for unrepresented (pengguna lain) or indigent litigants.
  • Indonesia-Adapted Option: Net Procedural Accessibility Score (NPAS) conditioning top AMPUH scores on PTSP legal aid kiosks, prodeo speed, and disability standards.
  • Risk: PTSP kiosk congestion during peak filing hours.
  • Pilot: KCCP-PILOT-004 (PN Jakarta Barat, PN Makassar, PN Jayapura, PN Muaro).
  • KPI: KCCP-KPI-004 (Prodeo Processing Velocity ≤ 3 working days; Posbakum Conversion Efficacy ≥ 80%; Vulnerable Group Accommodation Index 100%).

Pipeline 5: Independent External Oversight vs. Counter-Side Surveys

  • Japanese Practice: Institutional oversight via Jimusōkyoku and biannual bar association consultations (jitsujō chōsa) across regional court districts.
  • Purpose: Identify operational barriers and conduct issues without relying on formulaic counter-side surveys.
  • Indonesian Current Practice: Counter-side lobby survey collection (SKM/SPAK) at PTSP counters tied to WBK/WBBM accreditation.
  • Gap: JPAIS-BNCH-GAP-001 — Lack of independent, blind sampling for integrity assessments.
  • Indonesia-Adapted Option: Independent Court User Experience (ICUX) blind survey platform managed directly by Bawas via SMS/WhatsApp 48 hours post-disposition.
  • Risk: Low response rates on blind digital surveys.
  • Pilot: KCCP-PILOT-005 (4 District Courts & 4 Religious Courts in West Java & NTT).
  • KPI: KCCP-KPI-005 (Externalized Integrity Index EII ≥ 85%; zero local IP survey entries; SIWAS complaint resolution 100% investigated within 30 days).

4. 30–60–90 Days Phased Operational Plan

DAY 1–30: DIAGNOSE → DAY 31–60: CO-DESIGN → DAY 61–90: PILOT

DAY 1–30 — DIAGNOSE (Diagnostic Baseline & Algorithmic Calibration)

  • Technical Deployment: Badilum IT Bureau deploys SIPP sub-routines for Differentiated Case Management (DCM) and Case Weighting Matrix (CWM) within staging databases of pilot courts.
  • Baseline Audit: Extract 12-month historical disposition times, procedural dismissal (putusan NO) ratios, and adjournment durations across pilot courts to establish control benchmarks.
  • Survey Decoupling: Deactivate in-person PTSP lobby survey terminals and establish automated API link between SIPP and Bawas for ICUX blind survey distribution via SMS/WhatsApp 48 hours post-disposition.
  • Governance Workshops: Conduct briefings for Chief Judges, panel chairs, and substitute clerks (Panitera Pengganti) explaining DCM track assignment and legacy EIS speed ranking suspension during pilot.

DAY 31–60 — CO-DESIGN (Parallel Orchestration & Milestone Tracking)

  • Active Track Processing: Process all incoming civil, commercial, and criminal cases through active SIPP-DCM modules, tracking milestone compliance (mediation completion, document exchange, witness hearings).
  • Weighted Workload Balancing: Chief Judges allocate newly registered cases using SIPP Weighted Caseload Module, enforcing the operational ceiling of 40 active weighted points per judge.
  • Bi-Weekly Review Sessions: Hold bi-weekly case management meetings chaired by Chief Judges using SIPP exception logs to address summons or continuance bottlenecks.
  • Blind Survey Monitoring: Bawas monitors real-time ICUX survey incoming data, cross-referencing user feedback against court registries to isolate service bottlenecks.

DAY 61–90 — PILOT (Empirical Validation, Quality Audit & Policy Drafting)

  • Econometric Data Audit: Compare pilot performance metrics against baseline figures across clearance rates, real disposition times, adjournment lengths, and satisfaction ratings.
  • Appellate Quality Audit: High Court appellate review panels conduct substantive audits of all procedural dismissals (putusan NO) issued during the 90-day window to verify legal justification versus speed-driven dismissals.
  • Algorithmic Refinement: Stress-test classification rules to prevent misclassification of standard cases into complex tracks and refine point weight formulas.
  • National Policy Submission: Consolidate findings into a formal report for the Chief Justice of Mahkamah Agung, submitting draft text for a new PERMA on Case Management and operational updates to AMPUH guidelines.

== Description ==
Reforms Indonesian judicial performance evaluation by integrating Differentiated Case Management (DCM) into SIPP and upgrading EIS to version 4. The system classifies incoming cases into Track A (Summary: 60-day target), Track B (Standard: 150-day target), and Track C (Complex: 270-day target), measuring compliance against track-specific procedural milestones rather than a single 5-month cutoff.

== Problem ==
* [[KCCP-PROB-001]]: The uniform 5-month first-instance disposition deadline mandated by SEMA No. 2 of 2014 creates a structural conflict between administrative speed and adjudicative thoroughness, driving premature procedural dismissals (''putusan NO'') and delayed register entries.

== Current Indonesian Practice ==
[[KCCP-PRACT-ID-001]] & [[KCCP-PRACT-ID-002]]: EIS v3 real-time speed rankings enforce a rigid 5-month disposition deadline for all first-instance civil cases regardless of complexity.

== Japanese Practice ==
[[KCCP-PRACT-JP-001]]: Under the Act on Expediting Trials (Act No. 107 of 2003), Japan applies a flexible 2-year statutory macro-target verified through biannual reviews by the Supreme Court General Secretariat (''Jimusōkyoku''), with regular civil cases averaging 12.3–12.4 months.

== Gap ==
[[KCCP-GAP-001]]: Existing clearance metrics treat all cases as functionally homogeneous, failing to calibrate velocity against case complexity, evidentiary volume, or summons delays across island geographies.

== Proposed Adaptation ==
Implement SIPP Differentiated Case Management (DCM) establishing Tracks A, B, and C at registration, tracking compliance through customized procedural milestones.

== Action ==
* [[KCCP-ACT-001]]: Phase 1 (Days 1–30)—Deploy DCM SIPP code, extract historical baseline, and decouple lobby survey kiosks.
* [[KCCP-ACT-002]]: Phase 2 (Days 31–60)—Active DCM track management, weighted case assignment, and bi-weekly milestone reviews.
* [[KCCP-ACT-003]]: Phase 3 (Days 61–90)—Appellate quality audit of ''putusan NO'', econometric analysis, and draft PERMA approval.

== Pilot ==
[[KCCP-PILOT-001]]: 90-Day DCM Pilot across Pengadilan Negeri Jakarta Pusat, Pengadilan Negeri Sleman, and Pengadilan Negeri Kupang.

== KPI ==
* [[KCCP-KPI-001]]: Milestone Timeliness Index (MTI $\ge 95\%$); Adjournment Interval Ratio ($\le 10$ calendar days); DCM Clearance Rate ($98\%-102\%$).

== Policy Implication ==
Promulgate a new Supreme Court Regulation (PERMA) on Case Management and Operational Timelines, formally superseding SEMA No. 2 of 2014.

== Regulatory Implication ==
Revision of SEMA No. 2 Year 2014 and upgrade of Badilum EIS platform to version 4.

== Evidence ==
* SEMA No. 2/2014 mandates 5-month disposition; Supreme Court reports productivity ratios above 99% in 2023–2025.
* Japanese civil cases average 12.3–12.4 months with 2.4 oral argument sessions per case.
