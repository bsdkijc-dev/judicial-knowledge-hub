Supreme Court Landmark Decisions Compendium (2014 Part 2) (encompassing Supreme Court Decisions Reg. Nos. 39 K/AG/2013, 676 K/AG/2012, 724 K/AG/2012, 509 K/TUN/2013, 450 K/TUN/2013, and 97 K/MIL/2011) represents a pivotal compilation of Supreme Court jurisprudence established under Chief Justice Decree No. 45/KMA/SK/III/2014. It consolidates strategic landmark rulings across Religious Family Law, State Administrative Law (TUN), and Military Criminal Law.

Executive Summary

The Supreme Court established six primary legal principles across distinct chambers:

  1. Right to Sue for Inheritance by Estate Controllers & the Principle of Ijbary (Asas Ijbary): "A Plaintiff who controls the entirety of the inheritance estate may file an inheritance lawsuit against Defendants who do not control the estate due to their unwillingness to divide the inheritance (apathy), in accordance with the principle of Ijbary in Islamic inheritance law, whereby upon the moment of the Deceased's passing, ownership of the inheritance estate automatically transfers to the Legal Heirs".
  2. Validity of Grandchild Substitute Heirs (Article 185 Compilation of Islamic Law / KHI): "The status of substitute heirs based on the Compilation of Islamic Law does not contradict Islamic law or Indonesian positive law... Courts must not ignore the Compilation of Islamic Law in adjudicating cases by rejecting substitute heirs, which would result in grandchildren being deprived of inheritance from their grandparents due to their parents having predeceased them".
  3. Divorce Grounded in Marital Strife vs. Fasakh due to Apostasy: "A divorce sought through a petition for permission to pronounce talak based on grounds of marital strife may be granted if the Petitioner's (husband's) arguments align with statutory law provisions, even though the Respondent (wife) stated she practiced Christianity since childhood and practiced Islam solely at the time of marriage. If a divorce is petitioned based on apostasy and such apostasy constitutes a proven fact, the legal consequence is fasakh".
  4. Legal Standing in Public Information Disclosure Disputes: "With the enactment of Law No. 14 of 2008 on Public Information Disclosure, anyone may submit a request for public information (actio popularis). However, in examining public information disclosure disputes, the presence or absence of an interest implying the Plaintiff's legal standing (point d'interest point d'action ex Article 53 paragraph (1) of the Administrative Court Law) must be taken into account".
  5. Administrative Officials' Duty to Correct (Erga Omnes Principle): "Even if a Defendant was not a party to the case in a quo, whenever they become aware of such a ruling, State Administrative Officials are obligated to respond by correcting erroneous state administrative products, in the interest of enforcing public law with binding general applicability (the erga omnes principle)".
  6. Commander's Responsibility for Subordinate Assault Orders (Superior Responsibility): "The victim's death was not a direct act by the Defendant, but the victim's death resulting from the Defendant's order directed to members of the Military District Command (Kodim) falls under the responsibility of the Defendant as the Commander issuing the order".

The Supreme Court granted the cassation petitions in each respective case, annulled erroneous lower court rulings (judex facti), and reinforced legal certainty and substantive justice.

Legal / Procedural Issues

1. Construction of the Ijbary Principle in Islamic Inheritance Law (Articles 171 & 188 Compilation of Islamic Law / KHI)

Does an heir holding physical control over a decedent's estate possess legal standing to file an inheritance partition lawsuit against other passive/apathetic heirs?

Supreme Court Rule: Lawful / Entitled to Sue. Pursuant to the Principle of Ijbary (automatic transfer of ownership by operation of law upon the decedent's death), ownership of the inheritance estate immediately vests in all legal heirs. Apathetic or uncooperative behavior by certain heirs gives controlling heirs the standing to file an inheritance partition lawsuit in Religious Courts ex Article 188 KHI.

2. Protection of Grandchildren's Civil Rights via Substitute Heirs (Ahli Waris Pengganti ex Article 185 KHI)

Are grandchildren whose parents predeceased their grandparents entitled to inherit from their grandparents through substitute heir provisions?

Supreme Court Rule: Entitled to Inheritance Shares. Religious Courts are barred from ignoring the Compilation of Islamic Law or bypassing grandchildren's rights. Grandchildren are entitled to step into their parents' shoes (plaatsvervulling) as substitute heirs with a portion not exceeding the share of primary equal heirs ex Article 185 KHI.

3. Legal Standing in Public Information Disclosure Disputes (Article 53(1) Law No. 5 of 1986 jo. Law No. 14 of 2008)

Although the Public Information Disclosure Law adopts actio popularis, in Administrative Court proceedings, Plaintiffs remain obligated to prove direct infringed interest (legal standing / point d'interest point d'action). Internal financial accountability documents (SPJ) of government bodies that do not constitute public financial reports need not be disclosed without direct legal standing.

4. Erga Omnes Binding Effect of Administrative Court Rulings (Principles of Good Governance / AUPB)

State Administrative Court rulings possessing permanent legal force (inkracht) carry erga omnes applicability (binding generally). Land Office Officials discovering flawed administrative decrees are obligated to execute spontaneous corrections (spontaneous correction) over overlapping certificates.

5. Superior Responsibility in Military Law (ex Article 127 KUHPM jo. Article 338 Criminal Code)

A mid-ranking TNI officer (Dandim) who abuses positional authority by giving verbal orders to subordinates to eliminate and dispose of a victim's corpse bears full criminal responsibility for the victim's death, even if physical execution was carried out by subordinate members.

Question of Law vs Question of Fact

Question of Law (Judex Juris / Supreme Court)

  • Inheritance Case: Did Makassar Religious High Court (Decision No. 74/Pdt.G/2012/PTA.Mks) err in applying the law by dismissing the Plaintiff's lawsuit as premature and finding no dispute?

    Supreme Court Answer: Yes. Makassar Religious High Court misapplied the law. Refusing to partition inheritance constitutes a justiciable dispute ex Article 188 KHI. The Supreme Court annulled the appellate ruling and independently adjudicated the estate partition.

  • Public Information Case: Did Bandung Administrative Court (Decision No. 35/G/2013/PTUN-BDG) err in law by ordering Bekasi Youth & Sports Agency head to disclose internal SPJ financial files?

    Supreme Court Answer: Yes. Bandung Administrative Court misapplied public information law and administrative court procedure. Applicants lacked legal standing over internal SPJ files. The Supreme Court annulled the Bandung Administrative Court ruling and dismissed the information request.

  • Military Criminal Case: Did the Military Appeals Court (Decision No. PUT/26-K/PMU/BDG/AD/XI/2010) correctly affirm imprisonment and discharge for Dandim Lt. Col. Inf. Untung Sunanto?

    Supreme Court Answer: Yes. Commanders bear full criminal responsibility for subordinates' assault orders. The Supreme Court rejected the Defendant's cassation petition.

Established Supreme Court Jurisprudence

  • Principle of Ijbary in Islamic Inheritance: Transfer of inheritance ownership occurs automatically by operation of law (ipso jure) upon the decedent's death without requiring manual co-heir consent.
  • Erga Omnes Doctrine in Administrative Law: Annulment of administrative decisions based on permanent court rulings binds all governmental organs to adjust administrative acts.
  • Command / Superior Responsibility Doctrine: Military superiors issuing unlawful functional orders (unlawful orders) bear full criminal responsibility for lethal consequences executed by subordinates.

Uncertainties and Gaps

Integrity of Islamic Inheritance Enforcement, Public Transparency, & Military Command Discipline: Landmark rulings in this compendium affirm five pillars of legal certainty in Indonesia:

  1. Islamic inheritance guarantees absolute heir rights unimpeded by relative apathy;
  2. KHI strictly protects grandchildren through substitute heir institutions;
  3. Public information rights must not be abused to access internal technical files without legal standing;
  4. Administrative officials must comply with erga omnes principles to rectify flawed certificates; and
  5. Military officers enjoy no immunity from command authority abuses causing civilian deaths.

  • Heirs controlling the entirety of an inheritance estate are entitled to sue passive/apathetic heirs refusing to divide estate assets pursuant to the Principle of Ijbary in Islamic inheritance law.
  • Military commanders bear full criminal responsibility for a victim's death resulting from orders issued to subordinates (superior responsibility), even if physical execution was carried out by subordinate members.
  • The Supreme Court annulled erroneous religious appellate rulings and rejected military commander cassation appeals.
