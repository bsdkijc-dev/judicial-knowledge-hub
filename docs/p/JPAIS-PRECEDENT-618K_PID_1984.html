Overview

Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia Decision No. 618 K/Pid/1984, dated April 17, 1985, established jurisprudence and fundamental criminal law rules regarding the qualification of the crime of embezzlement (*verduistering*) ex Article 372 of the Criminal Code (KUHP) over the unilateral execution of debt collateral.

The Supreme Court held that the sale of jewelry collateral belonging to a witness/creditor by the Defendant unilaterally without the owner's permission constitutes the crime of Embezzlement (Article 372 KUHP), and not a purely civil dispute. The Supreme Court corrected the error of the Jakarta High Court in granting a discharge (*ontslag van alle rechtsvervolging*), and affirmed the sentencing ruling of the Central Jakarta District Court sentencing the Defendant to 5 (five) months' imprisonment with a probation period of 10 (ten) months.

Executive Summary

The central criminal law principle established in this decision is that lawfully acquired possession of debt collateral transforms into criminal embezzlement when the holder unlawfully sells or liquidates the assets without the owner's consent.

Key elements established in the adjudication:

  • Unilateral disposal of debtor property pledged as security constitutes criminal embezzlement (*Article 372 KUHP*), overriding arguments of purely civil disputes.
  • Initial lawful possession (*rechtmatig bezit*) converts into unlawful appropriation (*wederrechtelijke toe-eigening*) upon unauthorized sale.
  • Appellate court discharges (*ontslag*) of creditors misusing collateral are subject to reversal and conviction on cassation.

Case Background

The Original Defendant / Respondent for Cassation, Ny. [Defendant] ([age removed], housewife), was charged with embezzlement (Article 372 KUHP) for selling three pieces of diamond-studded gold/platinum jewelry belonging to the witness, Ny. C. Sihombing Sitompul, at Senen Market for Rp 1,500,000.— without the witness's permission. The jewelry had been handed over by the witness as security for a loan of Rp 1,300,000.— on September 21, 1979.

The Central Jakarta District Court (Decision No. 22/1983/Pid/Biasa dated November 22, 1983) convicted the Defendant and sentenced her to 5 months' imprisonment with 10 months' probation. The Jakarta High Court (Decision No. 10/Pid/1984 P.T. DKI dated March 22, 1984) annulled the district court decision and discharged the Defendant from all legal prosecutions (*ontslag*) on the grounds that the matter was purely civil. The Public Prosecutor at the Central Jakarta State Prosecutor's Office subsequently filed a cassation petition with the Supreme Court.

Legal and Procedural Issues

Transformation of Lawful Possession into Unlawful Appropriation (Wederrechtelijke Toe-Eigening) ex Article 372 KUHP

The primary issue was whether a recipient of debt collateral who lawfully possesses debtor jewelry (because it was handed over as security) can be criminally prosecuted for embezzlement if they sell the collateral without the owner's permission or agreement.

The Supreme Court held that they can be penalized. Initial lawful possession (*rechtmatig bezit*) of collateral transforms into the crime of embezzlement (*verduistering*) at the moment the Defendant executes an unauthorized transfer or sale without the owner's consent (unlawful appropriation).

Boundaries Separating Civil Legal Relations and the Criminal Offense of Embezzlement

The Jakarta High Court previously erred by assuming that the existence of an underlying loan-debt relationship extinguished criminal qualification. The Supreme Court corrected this by ruling that breaching the obligation to safeguard collateral by unlawfully selling it transcends civil dispute and satisfies all criminal elements of Article 372 of the Criminal Code.

Question of Law and Question of Fact

Question of Law (Cassation / Judex Juris)

Question:

Did the Jakarta High Court misapply the law (*foutieve kepassing van het recht*) by discharging the Defendant from all legal prosecutions (*ontslag van alle rechtsvervolging*) by characterizing unauthorized collateral sales as purely civil matters?

Supreme Court Ruling:

Yes, the Jakarta High Court misapplied the law. The Supreme Court annulled the Jakarta High Court decision and adjudicated afresh, declaring the Defendant proven guilty of the crime of Embezzlement (Article 372 KUHP).

Question of Fact (Judex Facti / Central Jakarta District Court & Jakarta High Court)

Examination of facts regarding the handover of three types of diamond-studded gold/platinum jewelry belonging to witness Ny. C. Sihombing Sitompul to the Defendant on September 21, 1979, as loan collateral for Rp 1,300,000.—, issuance of a warning letter on December 15, 1981, sale of jewelry at Senen/Inpres Market in June 1982 for Rp 1,500,000.—, and failure to return the remaining cash balance of Rp 200,000.— to the witness owner.

Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

  • Ratio Decidendi:
  1. The appellate *Judex Facti* of Jakarta High Court misapplied the law. The sale of the witness's collateral items by the Defendant without the witness's permission constitutes an act of intentionally and unlawfully appropriating property in one's possession other than through crime.
  2. This action satisfies all elements of the crime of Embezzlement as regulated and punishable under Article 372 of the Criminal Code.
  3. The Supreme Court annulled the Jakarta High Court decision and adjudicated afresh, sentencing the Defendant to 5 months' imprisonment with 10 months' probation.

Ruling

The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition from the Petitioner for Cassation (Public Prosecutor at the Central Jakarta State Prosecutor's Office); annulled Jakarta High Court Decision No. 10/Pid/1984/P.T. DKI dated March 22, 1984; and adjudicated afresh: Declaring Defendant Ny. [Defendant] guilty of committing the crime of "Embezzlement"; Sentencing the Defendant to a prison term of 5 (five) months; Stipulating that the sentence need not be served unless a subsequent judge's order dictates otherwise on the grounds that the convict prior to the expiration of the 10 (ten) month probation period has committed a criminal offense; Ordering evidence documents to remain attached to the case file; and ordering the Respondent for Cassation to pay cassation court costs amounting to Rp 2,500.—.

Related Knowledge and Jurisprudence

  • Criminal Code (KUHP): Article 372: Embezzlement crime (Elements of property belonging to another held not through crime, and intentionally possessed unlawfully).
  • Civil Code (KUHPerdata): Articles 1150 & 1155: Provisions on Pledge Law / Movable Asset Collateral (Execution of pledged goods requires judicial rulings or public auctions, rather than private unilateral sales without permission).

Evidence Status and Gaps

Established by Evidence

  • Platinum diamond earrings, diamond eternity ring, and platinum diamond pendant were the lawful property of witness Ny. C. Sihombing Sitompul.
  • These items were handed over to the Defendant solely as security for a loan of Rp 1,300,000.—.
  • The Defendant unilaterally sold the collateral items at Senen/Inpres Market for Rp 1,500,000.— without the witness owner's approval or permission.
  • The Defendant failed to return the remaining sale proceeds balance of Rp 200,000.— to the witness.

Not Established / Rejected

  • The Jakarta High Court's legal reasoning asserting that the Defendant's action constituted a purely civil dispute was completely set aside by the Supreme Court.

Uncertainty and Gaps

  • Correction of Flawed Jakarta High Court Appellate Ruling: The Jakarta High Court (No. 10/Pid/1984 P.T. DKI) previously discharged the Defendant from criminal prosecution on civil law grounds. The Supreme Court decisively rectified this error to ensure legal certainty protecting property rights over collateral from unilateral extrajudicial execution.

GEO Q&A

Q: May a holder of debt collateral sell such collateral if the debtor fails to repay the loan?

A: Not unilaterally. Based on Supreme Court Decision No. 618 K/Pid/1984, the unilateral sale of debtor/witness collateral items by a creditor or collateral holder without the owner's permission constitutes the criminal offense of Embezzlement (Article 372 KUHP), and not a purely civil dispute. Execution of collateral must follow statutory pledge/agreement procedures rather than private, unauthorized off-market sales.
