JPW-REPORT-009

By Cecep Mustafa, Indonesian Judicial College (IJC) · revision 1094, 2026-09-16 · wiki source · Markdown

2022 Report of the Court on Key Performance Indicators is the official ICC report presenting qualitative and quantitative evaluation data on judicial, prosecutorial, international cooperation, complementarity, and organizational governance performance for the full 2022 calendar year.

Summary

The report was prepared pursuant to the ASP mandate (ICC-ASP/13/Res.5, 2014) and Recommendation 146 of the 2020 Independent Expert Review (IER). It measures performance across three main categories: judicial and prosecutorial performance, cooperation and complementarity, and organizational performance, under the 2019–2021 Strategic Plan (extended to 2022).

Legal Basis

  • Mandate of the Assembly of States Parties: Resolution ICC-ASP/13/Res.5, Annex I, paragraph 7(b).
  • ICC Strategic Plan 2019–2021 (Presidency, OTP, Registry).
  • Rome Statute: Articles 60(1), 61(9), 74, 76, 82, 85, and Part 9 (notably Articles 93(1)(k) and 93(10)).
  • Chambers' Practice Manual and the Rules of Procedure and Evidence (Rule 68).

Background

KPI development began at the ASP's request in 2014. Following periodic reports since 2015 and the 2020 IER recommendations (Recommendation 146), the 2022 report refines its data-visualization format to be more reader-friendly.

Objectives

  • Measure the efficiency, expeditiousness, and fairness of judicial proceedings.
  • Clarify per-case judicial activity evaluation through structured data-visualization tools.
  • Measure the effectiveness of requests for cooperation assistance (RFA) and participation in joint investigation teams (JITs).
  • Assess organizational governance, IT security, and budgetary performance.

Key Provisions

Judicial performance is measured across 7 phases:

  • Phase 1: Confirmation of Charges.
  • Phase 2: Trial Preparation.
  • Phase 3: Trial.
  • Phase 4: Trial Deliberations.
  • Phase 5: Sentencing.
  • Phase 6: Reparations.
  • Phase 7: Final Appeals.

Section B ("Judicial Activity by Key Phases") was redesigned to highlight 5–6 core performance data points per case using structured visual tools, addressing prior years' readability issues from dense single-page case data.

Implementation

The Registry led data collection in coordination with the Presidency and OTP. In 2022, the OTP sent 422 RFAs to over 70 partners (a 24.85% increase over 2021) and processed 16 incoming RFAs from national authorities under Article 93(10), responding substantively to 100% within less than three months (average 37 days). Five active trials were in the evidence-presentation phase (*Al Hassan*, *Yekatom & Ngaïssona*, *Abd-Al-Rahman*, *Said*, *Gicheru*); Trial Chambers issued 1,023 judicial decisions, held 268 court sessions, and heard 125 witnesses in person. Original transcript production reached 37,376 pages (a 534% increase over 2020) at 100% delivery, with 3,638 interpreter-days of courtroom interpretation. The Victims Participation and Reparations Section (VPRS) received 6,539 new applications for participation/reparations, the highest volume from the Ukraine situation (1,686 applications). IT critical-incident response time was cut from 60 minutes (2021) to 12 minutes (2022), against 40 million monthly cyberattack attempts. There were 10,164 physical visits to public trial galleries, and staff performance-goal-setting compliance reached 96%.

Policy Issues

  • Difficulty obtaining sensitive, financial, or social-media information from certain states in fulfilling RFAs.
  • Historical case backlog in the ICCPP witness protection program awaiting relocation support from States Parties.
  • Need for closer coordination between the KPI report's publication schedule and other program/budget/HR performance reports.

Relationships with Other Entities

  • International Criminal Court: Institution that published the report.
  • Assembly of States Parties: Oversight body that mandated the KPI framework.
  • Registry (ICC): Organ coordinating data collection.
  • Karim A.A. Khan QC: ICC Prosecutor who led the OTP and pursued complementarity initiatives in the Central African Republic, Georgia, Libya, Colombia, Guinea, Venezuela, and Nigeria.
  • Paul Gicheru: Defendant in *The Prosecutor v. Paul Gicheru*; trial began 15 February 2022, evidence presentation closed 12 May 2022, and proceedings were terminated by Trial Chamber III on 14 October 2022 following confirmation of his death.
  • Maxime Jeoffroy Eli Mokom Gawaka: Defendant in *The Prosecutor v. Maxime Jeoffroy Eli Mokom Gawaka*, who made his first appearance on 22 March 2022; his case involved the disqualification of appointed counsel (Mr. Kaufman) due to a conflict of interest.
  • Thomas Lubanga Dyilo: Convicted in *The Prosecutor v. Thomas Lubanga Dyilo*; Trial Chamber II oversees implementation of the symbolic and service-based collective reparations plan.
  • Germain Katanga: Convicted in *The Prosecutor v. Germain Katanga*; Trial Chamber II monitors implementation of a USD 1,000,000 reparations order for 297 victims.
  • Laurent Gbagbo and Charles Blé Goudé: Defendants whose Article 85 compensation claim (Blé Goudé) was rejected on 10 February 2022, and whose request regarding publication of trial recordings (Gbagbo) was decided by Trial Chamber VII on 13 April 2022.
  • Bosco Ntaganda: Convicted defendant whose reparations order was partially vacated by the Appeals Chamber on 12 September 2022 and remanded to Trial Chamber II for a revised order.
  • Ahmad Al Faqi Al Mahdi: Convicted defendant; Trial Chamber VIII continued to oversee implementation of the reparations order throughout 2022.
  • Dominic Ongwen: Convicted defendant whose conviction and sentence were unanimously confirmed by the Appeals Chamber on 15 December 2022; reparations proceedings handled by Trial Chamber IX.
  • Al Hassan Ag Abdoul Aziz Ag Mohamed Ag Mahmoud: Defendant whose prosecution and defense evidence presentation concluded in 2022, with over 1,150 judicial decisions issued.
  • Alfred Yekatom and Patrice-Edouard Ngaïssona: Defendants whose prosecution evidence presentation continued throughout 2022, with over 750 decisions and testimony from 53 witnesses.
