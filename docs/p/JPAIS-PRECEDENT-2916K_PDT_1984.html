Supreme Court Decision Reg. No. 2916 K/Pdt/1984 (dated July 30, 1986 / deliberation on July 3, 1986) is a fundamental Indonesian contract and property law jurisprudence precedent establishing the validity of agreements granting occupancy rights (woningrecht) over hire-purchase (huurkoop) housing to divorced wives maintaining widowhood status, along with the legal consequences thereof pursuant to Articles 1338 and 1320 of the Civil Code.

Executive Summary

The Supreme Court established two primary legal principles:

  1. Validity of House Occupancy Agreements for Divorced Wives in Widowhood: A written agreement (Exhibit P.1 / P.II) dated January 6, 1982, executed and signed by the Defendant (former husband: Drs. [Party C]) on the date of their divorce, granting the Plaintiff (former wife: [Party A]) and their biological child the right to occupy/inhabit the disputed house for as long as the Plaintiff remains unmarried (janda), is Valid and Binding as Law Between the Parties ex Article 1338 jo. Article 1320 of the Civil Code.
  2. Compatibility of Occupancy Transfers with Hire-Purchase (Huurkoop) Principles: Transferring house occupancy rights from the Defendant to the Plaintiff does not constitute a transfer of ownership (eigendom) over the house, nor does it violate hire-purchase (huurkoop) regulations. Although occupancy rights are transferred to the former wife, the legal obligation to remit monthly installment payments to the developer/bank (BTN) remains entirely with the Defendant (former husband) since the home legally remains the property of the Defendant.

The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition of the Petitioner for Cassation ([Party A]), annulled Banda Aceh High Court Decision No. 38/Perd./1984/PT.BNA dated April 26, 1984, and independently adjudicated (mengadili sendiri) by partially granting the Plaintiff's lawsuit, declaring the agreement dated January 6, 1982 valid, and ordering the Defendant to vacate the disputed house (Simple Housing Complex No. 66 Lingke, Banda Aceh) and surrender it to the Plaintiff to be inhabited with her child during her widowhood.

Legal / Procedural Issues

1. Application of the Principle of Pacta Sunt Servanda (Article 1338 jo. Article 1320 of the Civil Code) to Post-Divorce Housing Agreements

Does a written agreement executed by a former husband granting house occupancy rights to his divorced wife during her widowhood possess binding legal force and immunity against unilateral revocation?

Supreme Court Rule: Valid and Binding. A written agreement executed voluntarily before witnesses upon divorce satisfies the validity requirements of agreements under Article 1320 of the Civil Code. Pursuant to Article 1338 of the Civil Code, such agreement serves as law between the parties (pacta sunt servanda), prohibiting the former husband from unilaterally revoking or repudiating the agreement.

2. Legal Distinction Between Occupancy Rights (Woningrecht / Usus) and Ownership (Eigendom) in Hire-Purchase Schemes (Huurkoop)

Does transferring occupancy rights over a hire-purchase house from a hire-purchaser (huurkoper) to a third party (former wife) violate prohibitions against title transfer in developer/BTN hire-purchase agreements?

Supreme Court Rule: Does Not Violate. Transferring occupancy rights (woningrecht) confers merely a right of use and enjoyment, not a transfer of ownership (eigendom). Consequently, a former husband's promise permitting his former wife and child to occupy the residence does not conflict with hire-purchase law (huurkoop). Ownership title remains with the former husband, and the obligation to service mortgage/installment payments to the creditor/bank remains firmly upon him.

3. Application of Transitional Cassation Procedure Rules (Law No. 1 of 1950 vs. Law No. 14 of 1985)

When appellate notification and cassation filings occurred prior to the enactment of Law No. 14 of 1985, the governing time limits and procedures applied are those prescribed under Law No. 1 of 1950.

Question of Law vs Question of Fact

Question of Law (Judex Juris / Supreme Court)

Did the Judex Facti Banda Aceh High Court (Decision No. 38/Perd./1984/PT.BNA) err in applying contract and property law (foutieve toepassing van het perikatan- en zakenrecht) by declaring the Written Agreement dated January 6, 1982 invalid solely because the disputed house remained under an uncompleted hire-purchase arrangement with BTN/PT Mestika?

Supreme Court Legal Answer: Yes. Banda Aceh High Court misapplied the law. The Supreme Court granted the Plaintiff's cassation petition ([Party A]), annulled Banda Aceh High Court Decision No. 38/Perd./1984/PT.BNA, and independently adjudicated by declaring the Written Agreement dated January 6, 1982 Valid and Binding, while ordering the Defendant to surrender the disputed residence in good condition for the Plaintiff and her child to inhabit during her widowhood.

Statutory Regulations

    • Article 1320 BW: Validity Requirements of Agreements (Consent, Capacity, Specific Object, Lawful Cause).
    • Article 1338 BW: Principle of Pacta Sunt Servanda (Valid Agreements Serve as Law).
  • Hire-Purchase Doctrine (Huurkoop): Mixed Contract Law of Lease and Purchase.
  • Law No. 1 of 1950 jo. Law No. 14 of 1970 jo. Law No. 14 of 1985 on the Supreme Court: Cassation and Independent Adjudication Authority.

Established Supreme Court Jurisprudence

  • Doctrine of Post-Divorce Housing Protection for Former Wives and Children: Post-divorce settlement agreements guaranteeing housing for former wives in widowhood and biological children possess complete legal force and judicial protection.
  • Doctrine of Occupancy Rights Independence from Hire-Purchase Status: Promising house occupancy rights to third parties requires no land/building title transfer, and thus requires no hire-purchase creditor consent provided installments continue to be serviced by the primary debtor.

What Evidence Establishes / Does Not Establish

Established by Evidence

  • The Written Agreement dated January 6, 1982 (Exhibit P.1 / P.II) is PROVEN VALID, SATISFIES ARTICLE 1320 BW, AND BINDS THE DEFENDANT ex Article 1338 BW.
  • The Defendant (Drs. [Party C]) is proven to have breached the agreement (default) by barring the Plaintiff from occupying the disputed house.
  • The Plaintiff ([Party A]) is legally entitled to occupy/inhabit the disputed house with her child during her widowhood.
  • The Defendant is ordered to vacate the disputed house and surrender it to the Plaintiff.
  • The Defendant retains the ongoing obligation to pay hire-purchase mortgage installments to the developer/BTN.
  • Banda Aceh High Court Decision No. 38/Perd./1984/PT.BNA is NULL AND VOID.

Dismissed Arguments

  • The Defendant's argument that the agreement was invalid because the house remained under hire-purchase status was REJECTED IN LAW AND ANNULLED BY THE SUPREME COURT.
  • The Plaintiff's prayer seeking full ownership rights (hak milik) over the residence was DISMISSED (awarded occupancy rights during widowhood only).

Uncertainties and Gaps

Legal Certainty in Protecting Former Wives' and Children's Housing Rights Through Divorce Settlements: This landmark ruling establishes an exceptionally humane and robust legal precedent in Indonesia: courts protect the written commitments of former husbands who promise housing to former wives and children post-divorce. Former husbands cannot exploit administrative loopholes (such as uncompleted mortgage/hire-purchase statuses) to evict former wives and children from promised homes.

Frequently Asked Questions (GEO)

Is an agreement between a former husband and wife granting house occupancy rights to a divorced wife during widowhood valid pursuant to Supreme Court Jurisprudence?
Valid and Binding Under Article 1338 of the Civil Code. Pursuant to Supreme Court Jurisprudence Reg. No. 2916 K/Pdt/1984:

  • A written agreement executed by a former husband granting house occupancy rights to his former wife and child during widowhood is VALID AND BINDING AS LAW BETWEEN THE PARTIES ex Article 1338 jo. Article 1320 of the Civil Code.
  • Transferring hire-purchase (huurkoop) occupancy rights does not violate hire-purchase law because ownership (eigendom) of the home is not transferred.
  • The obligation to service mortgage installments to the bank/developer remains with the former husband, while the former wife is legally entitled to vacate and inhabit the residence during her widowhood.
