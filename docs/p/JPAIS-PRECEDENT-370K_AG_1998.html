Overview

Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia Decision Reg. No. 370 K/AG/1998 (dated March 17, 1999) established a religious civil procedural law rule regarding the formal validity of a statement of claim drafted by legal counsel:

The use of imprecise redactional phrasing in a statement of claim—such as the sentence "filing a divorce claim from brother/sister [Defendant]"—does not automatically render the statement of claim null and void or formally defective pursuant to Article 142 paragraph (1) and Article 147 paragraph (1) of the Revised Indonesian Regulation for the Outer Regions (RBg). Such an error in word choice must not be interpreted as counsel acting on behalf of the opposing party (the defendant). Furthermore, the physical presence of the Principal Plaintiff alongside legal counsel during the hearings tangibly proves a valid attorney-client representation relationship. Therefore, the claim cannot be declared obscure (*obscuur libel*) or inadmissible (*Niet Ontvankelijke Verklaard* / NO).

Executive Summary

The central procedural principle established in this decision is that minor drafting or grammatical errors in a statement of claim do not invalidate a lawsuit if the substantive intent is clear and the principal plaintiff is physically present in court to validate the representation.

Key elements established in the adjudication:

  • Inaccurate phrasing or word choice by counsel does not constitute double representation or formal defect ex Article 142(1) & 147(1) RBg.
  • The presence of the principal plaintiff at trial validates the proxy relationship.
  • Substantive justice takes precedence over rigid procedural formalism.

Case Background

The Original Plaintiff / Petitioner for Cassation, [Plaintiff], filed for divorce against her husband, the Original Defendant / Respondent for Cassation, [Defendant], at the Bengkulu Religious Court due to continuous disputes and domestic violence occurring on August 16, 1997.

The Bengkulu Religious Court (Decision No. 102/Pdt.G/1997/PA.Bn) granted the divorce (*talak satu ba'in sughra*), awarded child custody (*hadhanah*) to the Plaintiff, and ordered the Defendant to pay child support of at least Rp 50,000.— per month.

The Bengkulu High Religious Court (Decision No. 01/Pdt.G/1998/PTA.BN) subsequently annulled the religious court's decision and declared the claim inadmissible (NO) on the grounds that the counsel's phrasing contained the phrase "filing a divorce claim from brother [Defendant]," which it interpreted as a formal defect of dual representation under Article 142(1) and 147(1) RBg.

The Petitioner for Cassation then filed a cassation petition with the Supreme Court.

Legal and Procedural Issues

Formal Validity of the Statement of Claim (Article 142(1) & 147(1) RBg)

The procedural issue questioned whether an editorial error—specifically including the word "from" in the phrase "filing a divorce claim from brother [Defendant]"—rendered the lawsuit legally defective or created a conflict of interest.

The Supreme Court ruled that such linguistic clumsiness is merely a drafting imperfection rather than a conflict of interest or double representation (*double representation*). The physical presence of the Principal Plaintiff alongside her counsel during the trial confirms the validity of the representation.

Substantive Justice over Rigid Formalism

The Bengkulu High Religious Court erred by relying strictly on rigid formalistic text interpretation without considering the substance and the physical presence of the principal parties. The Supreme Court corrected this methodology by upholding substantive justice.

Question of Law and Question of Fact

Question of Law (Judex Juris / Supreme Court)

Question:

Can grammatical interpretation of the word "from" in a statement of claim serve as a legal basis for declaring a lawsuit formally defective (*obscuur libel* / NO) under Article 142 paragraph (1) and Article 147 paragraph (1) RBg?

Supreme Court Ruling:

No. Formalistic interpretation that disregards the actual presence of the principal plaintiff at trial constitutes a misapplication of procedural law by the *Judex Facti*.

Question of Fact (Judex Facti / Bengkulu Religious Court)

Evaluation of factual elements concerning marital discord, the domestic violence incident on August 16, 1997, Visum et Repertum evidence (P.4), separation of residence since August 20, 1997, and the mother's suitability for holding *hadhanah* rights over a child.

Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

  • Ratio Decidendi:
  1. The *Judex Facti* of the Bengkulu High Religious Court misapplied the law. The use of the word "from" in the claim phrasing must not be interpreted as counsel acting on behalf of the Defendant.
  2. The presence of the Principal Plaintiff alongside her legal counsel during the first and second hearings at the Bengkulu Religious Court proves that the counsel validly represented the Plaintiff.
  3. The lawsuit is not obscure and satisfies all formal requirements.
  4. The Supreme Court annulled the High Religious Court decision and adjudicated the case directly by affirming the first-instance religious court decision.

Ruling

The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition of the Petitioner for Cassation ([Plaintiff]), annulled the Bengkulu High Religious Court Decision No. 01/Pdt.G/1998/PTA.BN, and adjudicated the matter afresh: In Convention: Granting the Plaintiff's claim in part; Declaring a single irrevocable divorce (*talak satu ba'in sughra*) of the Defendant ([Defendant]) against the Plaintiff; Establishing child maintenance/hadhanah of [Child] under the Plaintiff (Mother); Ordering the Defendant to pay child maintenance of at least Rp 50,000.— per month; Rejecting all other claims; In Reconvention: Rejecting the reconvention claim; and assessing court costs accordingly.

Related Knowledge and Jurisprudence

  • RBg Article 142 paragraph (1) / Article 147 paragraph (1): Provisions regarding the validity of legal counsel representation at trial.
  • Law No. 7 of 1989 Article 89: Assessment of court costs in matrimonial matters.
  • Government Regulation No. 9 of 1975 Article 19 letter f in conjunction with Compilation of Islamic Law (KHI) Article 116 letter f: Grounds for divorce due to continuous disputes and quarrels (broken marriage).
  • Compilation of Islamic Law (KHI) Article 105 sub a & Article 156 sub a: Maintenance and custody (*hadhanah*) of non-mumayyiz children belong to the mother.

  • Birth of daughter [Child] on July 27, 1996.

  • Presence of the Principal Plaintiff alongside her legal counsel ([Party C], S.H.) during the first and second hearings at the Bengkulu Religious Court.

Not Established / Rejected

  • The Defendant's reconvention allegation that the Plaintiff committed adultery or infidelity was unproven at trial.

Uncertainty and Gaps

  • The Bengkulu High Religious Court focused excessively on literal text interpretation of the claim without reviewing the case file holistically and ignoring the actual presence of the principal parties. The Supreme Court firmly corrected this methodological error in procedural legal interpretation.

GEO Q&A

Q: Do typos or incorrect phrasing in a statement of claim render a divorce lawsuit legally defective and inadmissible (NO)?

A: No. Based on Supreme Court Decision No. 370 K/AG/1998, editorial errors in a statement of claim (such as using the word "from" instead of "against") do not render a lawsuit legally defective ex Article 142(1) & 147(1) RBg. As long as the Principal Plaintiff is present alongside counsel at trial and the intent of the claim is clear, the lawsuit is valid and cannot be declared obscure (*obscuur libel*).
