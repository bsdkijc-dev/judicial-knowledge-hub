International Journal for Court Administration (IJCA) is an official academic and professional journal, published electronically twice a year by the International Association for Court Administration (IACA), to facilitate international exchange of experience, research, and innovation in court governance and judicial administration.

Summary

IJCA Volume 1, Number 2 presents a range of global judicial policy studies covering jurisdictional specialization in the Netherlands, France, and Germany; court quality management through internal and external dialogue in Sweden; post-conflict legal system reconstruction in Iraq; reforms to judicial independence, budget, and accountability in Russia; the handling of public complaints and the role of an Ombudsman in France; oversight and controlling mechanisms at the Swiss Federal Supreme Court; and an independent agency model for court management in Ireland.

Legal Basis

  • Not found in the available sources: The journal operates under the editorial policy of IACA's Executive Board and is not established under any specific statutory legislative instrument within the available source material.
  • Courts Service Act 1998 (Ireland): The legal basis for establishing the independent Courts Service agency in Ireland.
  • Swiss Federal Supreme Court Act (SCA) & Ordinance on Judges 2006: The legal basis for the independence and controlling system of the Swiss Federal Supreme Court.

Background

Judicial institutions worldwide face pressure to improve operational efficiency and transparency in line with *New Public Management* (NPM) principles, without sacrificing judicial independence and impartiality.

Objectives

1. Facilitate international exchange of best practices in court administration across common law, civil law, and Shari'ah systems. 2. Help courts develop tools for quality improvement, complaint handling, and public accountability. 3. Promote professional leadership and independent financial management of the judiciary.

Scope

Topics covered include court specialization vs. geographic access, quality-management dialogue in Sweden, assessment of Iraq's legal infrastructure, indicators of judicial progress in Putin-era Russia, the Ombudsman scheme in France, three-tier controlling in Switzerland, and the governance structure of Ireland's Courts Service.

Key Provisions

  • Balancing Territoriality vs. Functionality: Division of competence based on case complexity and frequency to optimize judicial expertise.
  • Internal & External Dialogue Method: Interviews with staff and court users (lawyers, prosecutors, witnesses) to set quality-improvement priorities.
  • Emergency Human Rights Safeguards (Iraq): Prohibition on confessions obtained through torture, the right to legal counsel, and the right to remain silent.
  • Three-Tier Controlling System (Switzerland): Division of public data (Annual Report, Level 1), parliamentary commission data (Level 2), and internal management data (Level 3).
  • Independent Agency Model (Ireland): Operational management of courts by a Board chaired by the Chief Justice and a non-judge CEO accountable to Parliament.

Implementation

Implementation occurs through periodic journal publication, execution of federal targeted judicial budget programs in Russia, piloting of the Controlling system at the Swiss Federal Supreme Court, and application of three-year strategic plans in Ireland.

Policy Problems

1. Conflict between the "lawful judge" principle and the flexibility of shifting caseloads between courts. 2. Risk of dominance by court chairs and informal practices within the Russian judiciary. 3. Difficulty distinguishing complaints about the substance of a decision (appeal) from complaints about day-to-day administrative dysfunction. 4. Tension between parliamentary budgetary oversight and judicial self-governing autonomy.

Reform / Change

Promotes a shift from passive, traditional judicial bureaucracy ("rubber stamping") toward professional strategic management, automation of case data, user involvement, and measurable public accountability.
