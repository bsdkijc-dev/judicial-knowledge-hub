Overview

Supreme Court Civil Review Decision Reg. No. 220 PK/Pdt/1986 dated October 30, 1986 (pronounced on December 16, 1986), represents a foundational landmark decision in Indonesian Intellectual Property Law (Trademark Law). The Supreme Court established pioneering legal principles holding that:

  • Protection of International Well-Known Marks: The Republic of Indonesia is obligated to maintain international relations by respecting well-known marks belonging to foreign citizens or legal entities, even if they have not been registered at the Indonesian Office of Industrial Property Rights (Directorate of Patents and Copyrights). Indonesian entrepreneurs who are aware of an international well-known mark are prohibited from using or registering identical or similar marks, in order to protect Indonesian consumers from confusion and deception regarding the origin of goods.
  • Judicial Error / Manifest Error (Article 67(f) of Law No. 14 of 1985): A rigid (formalistic) interpretation of Articles 8 and 10 of Law No. 21 of 1961 regarding the time limit for filing a trademark cancellation lawsuit—which allows the registration of a bad-faith imitation mark to remain valid—constitutes a judicial error or manifest error (legal oversight), serving as valid grounds for granting a Civil Review (*Peninjauan Kembali*).

Case Background

The Petitioner for Civil Review / Original Plaintiff (Nike International Ltd., Oregon, U.S.A.) is the sole owner and first user globally and in Indonesia of the trademark and trade name "Nike" alongside the wing device (*swoosh*) for shoes and apparel. However, Respondent for Civil Review I / Original Defendant I ([Party A]) without authorization registered the "Nike" trademark and wing device at the Directorate of Patents and Copyrights under Registration No. 141589 on December 13, 1979, with bad faith to exploit the fame of the "Nike" mark.

The Plaintiff (Nike International Ltd.) filed a lawsuit seeking the cancellation of said trademark registration at the Central Jakarta District Court. The Central Jakarta District Court (Decision No. 315/1983 Pdt.G dated November 22, 1983) granted the Plaintiff's lawsuit in its entirety and annulled the trademark registration in the name of [Party A]. However, on cassation appeal, the Supreme Court (Decision No. 294 K/Pdt/1984 dated July 24, 1985) annulled the Central Jakarta District Court decision and declared the Plaintiff's lawsuit inadmissible on formalistic grounds, holding that the Defendant's trademark registration had not yet been published in the Additional State Gazette (*Tambahan Berita Negara RI*), meaning the 9-month lawsuit time limit ex Article 10 of Law No. 21 of 1961 could not yet be calculated. Dissatisfied with the cassation ruling, Nike International Ltd. filed a petition for Civil Review (*PK*) to the Supreme Court.

Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

  • Ratio Decidendi:
  1. A rigid interpretation of Articles 8 and 10 of Law No. 21 of 1961 declaring the lawsuit time limit rule to be absolute is erroneous. The interpretation that the time limit under Articles 8 and 10 of Law No. 21 of 1961 is not absolute serves as a correction of a judicial error or manifest error pursuant to Article 67(f) of Law No. 14 of 1985.
  2. The Republic of Indonesia, as an independent nation, is obligated to maintain international relations by respecting trademarks belonging to foreign nationals. Indonesian entrepreneurs who are aware of a mark that is famous internationally—although unregistered or not yet registered in the general register of the Industrial Property Office, yet known in Indonesia—cannot use an identical mark, in order to protect Indonesian consumers from confusion as to whether such Indonesian goods originate from an imitated foreign manufacturer.
  3. Indonesian citizens manufacturing Indonesian-made goods are required to use clear brand names reflecting an Indonesian national identity and should, to the extent possible, avoid using brand names that resemble, let alone plagiarize, foreign brand names.
  4. Respondent for Civil Review I ([Party A]) was proven to have acted in bad faith by registering the "Nike" mark under Registration No. 141589, which is identical to the mark owned by Nike International Ltd. Consequently, the Civil Review petition is granted, the cassation decision No. 294 K/Pdt/1984 is annulled, and the Central Jakarta District Court judgment granting Nike's lawsuit is reinstated.
  • Ruling: Granting the civil review petition from Petitioner for Civil Review: NIKE INTERNATIONAL LTD.; Annulling the Supreme Court decision dated July 24, 1985, No. 294 K/Pdt/1984; and Independently Adjudicating: (1) Granting the Plaintiff's lawsuit in its entirety; (2) Declaring the Plaintiff as the sole owner and first user in Indonesia of the trademark and trade name Nike, and therefore holding the exclusive right to use the trademark and trade name Nike in Indonesia; (3) Declaring that the Nike trademark registered under the name of Defendant I under Registration No. 141589 bears an overall similarity to the Plaintiff's Nike trademark (Exhibit P-1)... [and ordering the cancellation of registration No. 141589 from the General Trademark Register]. *(Decided in the deliberation meeting of the Supreme Court on October 30, 1986, and pronounced on December 16, 1986).*
