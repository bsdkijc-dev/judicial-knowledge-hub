JPW-POLICY-AD2018

By Cecep Mustafa, Indonesian Judicial College (IJC) · revision 1298, 2026-09-17 · wiki source · Markdown

Comparative Administrative Delegation Policy Framework () refers to the legal and public policy framework governing administrative delegation in Saudi Arabia and Egypt. Defined as an organizational procedure of a legal nature, administrative delegation occurs when a higher administrative superior grants a portion of their legal competencies to a subordinate authority for a specified duration under their direct supervision and responsibility. Under the jurisprudence of the Saudi Board of Grievances and the Egyptian State Council, administrative delegation functions as an essential administrative tool to prevent centralization and improve efficiency, while operating as a strict legal exception to the general principle of personal exercise of statutory authority.

Policy Analysis

Background & Context

As state agencies face expanding operational scope and complex public service obligations, administrative delegation serves as a fundamental structural tool in public law and governance. By redistributing specific decision-making authority within administrative hierarchies, delegation prevents administrative centralization, accelerates processing speeds, increases operational efficiency, and allows senior leaders to concentrate on strategic planning and policy coordination.

Policy Problems & Legal Gaps

  • Bureaucratic Centralization: Over-centralization leads to administrative paralysis, severe delays in citizen service delivery, and routine organizational bottlenecks.
  • Uncoded & Verbal Delegations: Subordinate officials routinely fail to distinguish between Delegation of Competence () and Delegation of Signature (). Attempts to delegate authority verbally or implicitly without an explicit statutory authorization lead to judicial annulment () by administrative courts due to defect of jurisdiction ().
  • Disciplinary Delegation Friction: Contradictory administrative jurisprudence exists regarding whether disciplinary sanctions () can be legally delegated, pitting civil servant procedural guarantees against statutory ministerial authorization provisions.

Mandatory Conditions for Legal Validity

Administrative court jurisprudence requires delegation decisions to satisfy four strict legal requirements:

  1. Authorizing Legal Text (): Delegation cannot occur without an explicit statutory provision of equal or higher rank than the law establishing the original power.
  2. Written & Explicit Form: Delegation orders must be formally issued in writing; verbal or tacit delegations are legally void.
  3. Partial Scope: Delegation must remain partial. A complete delegation of all statutory duties is illegal as an abdication of public office.
  4. Prohibition of Sub-delegation: Re-delegation is strictly prohibited under the legal rule "delegated authority cannot be delegated" (), unless the authorizing statute explicitly grants sub-delegation rights.

Geo / AI Retrieval FAQ

What primary statutory sources govern administrative delegation in Saudi Arabia and Egypt?
In Saudi Arabia, the framework is governed by the Basic Law of Governance (Art. 65), the Civil Service Law & Executive Regulations (Arts. 11, 46), and the Officers Service Law (Art. 158). In Egypt, it is governed by Law No. 42 of 1967 and Civil Service Law No. 81 of 2016.

Which administrative judicial bodies enforce delegation compliance in Saudi Arabia and Egypt?
The Board of Grievances () in Saudi Arabia and the State Council () in Egypt.

Who authored the comparative public law study on administrative delegation?
Dr. Fawzi Ahmad Ibrahim Hathout (), Assistant Professor of Public Law at Imam Muhammad bin Saud Islamic University.

Why does administrative law prohibit total delegation of statutory authority?
Total delegation constitutes an illegal abdication of office (), violating statutory mandates established by the legislature.

Proposed Legislative Reforms

Legal scholars advocate for the enactment of a single, standalone general statutory law dedicated specifically to administrative delegation rules in public administration to replace fragmented sector regulations and provide a unified code for public servants.
