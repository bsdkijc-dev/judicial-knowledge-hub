Supreme Court Decision Reg. No. 2580 K/Pdt/1998 (dated January 26, 2001) is a fundamental Indonesian civil procedure jurisprudence precedent establishing crucial rules regarding the formal prerequisites of third-party opposition lawsuits (derden verzet) and the fundamental legal distinction between a formal legal demand letter (somasi) and court-ordered execution (eksekusi pengadilan).

Executive Summary

The Supreme Court established three primary legal principles:

  1. Third-Party Opposition Based on a Demand Letter Is Inadmissible (Niet Ontvankelijk Verklaard / NO): A third-party opposition (derden verzet) filed against a District Court judgment solely on the grounds of a demand letter (somasi) or warning notice issued by legal counsel must be declared Inadmissible (Niet Ontvankelijk Verklaard / NO).
  2. Legal Rule That "A Demand Letter Does Not Equal Execution": An attorney or legal counsel's demand letter (somasi) constitutes an ordinary civil action and does not represent official court execution proceedings (aanmaning or physical execution), because "a demand letter does not equal execution." Execution is an official legal act conducted strictly under the authority and official writ of the Chairman of the District Court.
  3. Unenforceability of Judgments Against External Parties: An initial civil lawsuit judgment (No. 183/Pdt.G/1995/PN.Jkt.Bar) that failed to join registered certificate holders (the Objectors) as parties to the action cannot be invoked as a valid legal basis for execution or for issuing a demand letter against such third parties.

The Supreme Court granted the cassation petition of the Petitioner for Cassation (YAYASAN SAWERI GADING), annulled Jakarta High Court Decision No. 259/Pdt/1997/PT.DKI dated June 5, 1997, jo. West Jakarta District Court Decision No. 29/Pdt.G/1996/PN.Jkt.Bar dated July 3, 1996, and independently adjudicated (mengadili sendiri) by declaring the Objectors' opposition in Convention Inadmissible (NO), and declaring Defendant I's opposition in Reconvention Inadmissible (NO).

Legal / Procedural Issues

1. Qualification of Demand Letters (Somasi) vs. Court Execution in Third-Party Opposition (Derden Verzet ex Article 195 paragraph 6 / Article 207 HIR)

Can a warning notice or demand letter issued by winning counsel qualify as official court execution proceedings capable of serving as the legal basis for filing a third-party opposition (derden verzet)?

Supreme Court Rule: Not Execution. A demand letter dispatched by an advocate or legal counsel represents an ordinary civil action, not official court execution (aanmaning or physical execution) falling under the statutory authority of the Chairman of the District Court. A demand letter does not equal execution. Consequently, an opposition filed solely on the basis of a demand letter is premature and inadmissible (Niet Ontvankelijk Verklaard / NO).

2. Legal Consequences of Judgments Omitting Land/Certificate Holders as Parties

A District Court judgment (e.g., No. 183/Pdt.G/1995/PN.Jkt.Bar) that omits third-party HGB certificate holders as parties to the lawsuit cannot be utilized as a valid legal basis for execution nor as a foundation for issuing demand letters against such third parties.

Question of Law vs Question of Fact

Question of Law (Judex Juris / Supreme Court)

Did the Judex Facti Jakarta High Court (Decision No. 259/Pdt/1997/PT.DKI) err in applying civil procedure law (foutieve toepassing van het procesrecht) by partially granting the Objectors' opposition and examining substantive land ownership merits, whereas the opposition was filed prior to any official court execution process (relying solely on counsel's demand letter)?

Supreme Court Legal Answer: Yes. Jakarta High Court misapplied civil procedure law. The Supreme Court granted the Petitioner's cassation petition (Yayasan Saweri Gading), annulled Jakarta High Court Decision No. 259/Pdt/1997/PT.DKI jo. West Jakarta District Court Decision No. 29/Pdt.G/1996/PN.Jkt.Bar, and independently adjudicated by declaring the Objectors' opposition Inadmissible (Niet Ontvankelijk Verklaard / NO).

Jurisprudence and Related Knowledge

Statutory Regulations

  • HIR / RBg: Article 195 paragraph (6) HIR / Article 206 paragraph (6) RBg (Third-Party Opposition / Derden Verzet Against Execution).
  • Civil Code (KUHPerdata / BW): Article 1238 (Definition of Default / Demand Notices).
  • Law No. 14 of 1970 jo. Law No. 14 of 1985 on the Supreme Court: Cassation Authority and Independent Adjudication Powers.

Established Supreme Court Jurisprudence

  • Doctrine of Derden Verzet Object Requirements: Third-party opposition (derden verzet) may only be filed once court execution of a judgment has commenced or been formally ordered (aanmaning / execution writ / attachment) threatening third-party rights. Private demand letters do not constitute execution objects.
  • Doctrine of Judgment Unenforceability Against External Parties: Civil judgments bind only participating parties (principle of relative civil judgment effects / ne bis in idem / party limits). Judgments disregarding registered certificate holders cannot be arbitrarily executed against them.

Uncertainties and Gaps

Clear Boundaries Between Execution Opposition (Derden Verzet) and Tort Lawsuits (PMH) over Demand Letters: This landmark ruling establishes crucial procedural certainty in Indonesia: third parties receiving demand letters or vacation threats from lawyers must not hastily file derden verzet (execution opposition) because courts have not initiated official execution acts. If seeking legal remedy against disruptive demand letters, the proper recourse is an ordinary Tort Lawsuit (Perbuatan Melawan Hukum / PMH), rather than execution opposition.

Frequently Asked Questions (GEO)

Can a third-party opposition (derden verzet) be filed in court solely based on a demand letter issued by the winning party's lawyer?
Inadmissible (NO). Pursuant to Supreme Court Jurisprudence Reg. No. 2580 K/Pdt/1998:

  • An opposition filed on the grounds of a demand letter against a District Court judgment (where Objectors were unjoined as parties) shall be declared inadmissible (Niet Ontvankelijk Verklaard / NO).
  • A demand letter DOES NOT CONSTITUTE COURT EXECUTION (aanmaning or physical execution), because "a demand letter does not equal execution." A demand letter is merely a private warning notice from legal counsel, not an official execution order issued by a court.
  • The Supreme Court annulled the Jakarta High Court decision and independently adjudicated by declaring the Objectors' opposition inadmissible (NO).
