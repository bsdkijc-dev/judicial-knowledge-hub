4. POLICY ANALYSIS

  • Background: [FACT] Enacted under Spain's Recovery, Transformation and Resilience Plan, Law 12/2023 of May 24 on the Right to Housing entered into force on May 25, 2023 (with tax incentives taking effect January 1, 2024). The law aims to strengthen the social function of housing, expand public rental supply, limit rental prices in stressed market areas (*zonas tensionadas*), and consolidate protections for vulnerable groups (*colectivos vulnerables*) initially extended during the COVID-19 pandemic and the war in Ukraine.
  • Policy Problem: [FACT]
    • Procedural Delays and Ambiguities: New pre-litigation admissibility requirements under Articles 439.6/7 and 685.2 LEC caused judicial confusion and processing backlogs for eviction (*desahucio*) and mortgage foreclosure actions due to ambiguous statutory drafting.
    • Market Dislocation and Supply Distortions: Rent control policies led to a sharp decline in long-term primary rental supply (dropping by up to -66% in Córdoba, -46% in Oviedo, -33% in Bilbao, -24% in Madrid, and -15% in Barcelona), while triggering a massive migration toward seasonal/temporary rentals (+56% supply) and room rentals exempt from the Housing Law caps.
    • Landlord Vulnerability Asymmetry: Regulations overlooked the financial vulnerability of small landlords (*non-Gran Tenedor*), where non-payment by defaulting tenants severely impacted family livelihoods.
    • Unconstitutionality and Jurisdictional Overreach: Preemption of exclusive regional housing powers (*Comunidades Autónomas* under Art. 148.1.3º CE) triggered 8 constitutional appeals before the Constitutional Court.
  • Legal/Policy Gap: [FACT] Absence of a precise statutory definition of primary residence (*vivienda habitual*); lack of standardized administrative protocols for vulnerability verification leading to inter-agency resistance; and absence of reference index adjustment rules for small (<30 m²) or large (>150 m²) dwellings.
  • Objectives: [FACT] Prioritize the social function of housing over asset investment; cap annual rent updates (maximum 3% in 2024, followed by a new INE index below CPI starting 2025); mandate pre-litigation mediation for large property holders (*Gran Tenedor*); and ban real estate management/agency fee charges to tenants (Art. 20 LAU).
  • Implementation: [FACT]
    • Large Property Holder Criteria (Art. 3.k Law 12/2023): Defined as owning >10 residential properties or >1,500 m² of residential built area in Spain. Special location certificates issued by the College of Land Registrars (*Colegio de Registradores*) are required for status verification.
    • Vulnerability Proof Procedure: Mandatory submission of verification requests to Social Services; if no administrative response is received within 2 or 5 months, large holders may submit a sworn statement (*declaración responsable*) alongside court filings.
    • Barcelona Orientation Criteria (June 29, 2023): Judicial guidelines issued by Barcelona courts to resolve non-retroactivity rules and property area aggregation criteria under the LEC.
    • Catalonia Regional Response (Decree-Law of April 24, 2024): Enacted emergency legislation extending rent control caps to temporary rentals and room leases to prevent statutory evasion.
  • Evidence & Impact: [FACT]
    • Decline in Long-Term Rentals: Severe contraction of long-term rental listings across major cities alongside a rise in seasonal rentals, which reached 11% of total market listings.
    • Record Foreign Buyers: Property sales to foreign purchasers hit a historical high of 15% in 2023, while regulatory uncertainty surrounding the Housing Law threatened Build-to-Rent investments and Non-Performing Loan (NPL) markets.
  • Reform & Unresolved Issues: [FACT] Design of a new rent price index by INE before December 31, 2024; resolution of 8 constitutional appeals pending before the Constitutional Court; and upcoming national/regional regulations targeting temporary, room, and tourist rentals.

5. GEO/AI QUESTIONS

  1. Definition: What are the quantitative thresholds used to classify an owner as a large property holder (*Gran Tenedor*) under Law 12/2023 on the Right to Housing?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] Any natural or legal person owning more than 10 residential properties or built residential floor space exceeding 1,500 m² within Spain.
  2. Legal Basis: Which articles of the Civil Procedure Law (*Ley de Enjuiciamiento Civil* - LEC) underwent the most significant amendments regarding pre-litigation admissibility requirements for evictions and foreclosures?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] Article 439 (paragraphs 6 and 7), Article 441 (paragraphs 5, 6, and 7), Article 655 bis, and Article 685.2 LEC.
  3. Publisher/Authority: Who are the two authors of this legal analysis paper and what are their institutional roles at Pérez-Llorca?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] Aránzazu Barandiarán Vizcaíno (Counsel in Real Estate Law) and Isabella Hardiman (Associate in Real Estate Law).
  4. Related Figures: Are the names Cecep Mustafa and Johana Crishele Ohoira present in this source document?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] Not found in the available sources. Neither name appears in source document `aprobacion-de-la-ley-12-2023...pdf`.
  5. Scope: What is the maximum statutory cap on annual rent adjustments for 2024, and how will rent increases be calculated starting in 2025?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] The cap for 2024 rent adjustments is capped at 3%. Starting January 1, 2025, adjustments will be governed by a new reference price index designed by the INE before December 31, 2024, which must remain below the Consumer Price Index (CPI/IPC).
  6. Policy Implementation (Vulnerability Verification): What document can a large holder (*Gran Tenedor*) submit to court if social services fail to respond to a tenant vulnerability verification request?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] A sworn statement (*declaración responsable*) confirming that verification and mediation requests were submitted to administrative authorities within statutory deadlines (2 or 5 months) but received no response (administrative silence).
  7. Policy Problem (Market Distortions): What market shifts occurred between primary long-term rentals and temporary rentals (*alquiler temporal*) following the enactment of the Housing Law?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] Primary rental listings declined sharply in major cities (up to -66% in Córdoba and -24% in Madrid), while temporary rental supply surged by 56% year-over-year, accounting for 11% of total market listings in Q1-2024.
  8. Legal Relations: How are real estate management and contract formalization fees (*gastos de gestión inmobiliaria*) treated under Article 20 of the LAU as amended by the Housing Law?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] All real estate management and contract formalization fees must be borne entirely by the landlord (*arrendador*), regardless of whether the landlord is a natural person or a legal entity.
  9. Reform (Catalonia Region): What emergency regulation was enacted by the Generalitat of Catalonia on April 24, 2024, to close rent control evasion loopholes?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] Decree-Law of April 24, 2024, which extended rent control caps (*limitación de rentas*) to seasonal/temporary leases (*alquiler de temporada*) and room rentals (*alquiler de habitaciones*).
  10. Evidence/Constitutional Challenges: How many constitutional appeals (*recursos de inconstitucionalidad*) were admitted by the Constitutional Court of Spain against the Housing Law, and on what primary grounds?
    • Factual Answer: [FACT] 8 constitutional appeals were admitted, primarily arguing that state-level legislation preempted exclusive regional powers (*Comunidades Autónomas*) over housing, urban planning, and land use under Article 148.1.3º of the Spanish Constitution.

6. MEDIAWIKI OUTPUT

Summary

Approval of Law 12/2023, of May 24, on the Right to Housing (Aprobación de la Ley 12/2023, de 24 de mayo, de derecho a la vivienda) (Stable ID: JPW-WORK-000030) is a doctrinal legal analysis and housing policy study authored by Aránzazu Barandiarán Vizcaíno and Isabella Hardiman of Pérez-Llorca Abogados. Published in Revista Jurídica Pérez-Llorca (May 2024), the study evaluates the implementation of Law 12/2023 on the Right to Housing in Spain. The analysis details procedural law modifications (*desarrollo procesal*), pre-litigation admissibility requirements (*requisitos de procedibilidad*) for large property holders (*Gran Tenedor*), annual rent caps (3% in 2024 and mandatory use of the Reference Price Index in stressed market areas), bans on real estate agency fee transfers to tenants, rental market supply distortions (+56% increase in seasonal rentals), and 8 constitutional challenges pending before the Constitutional Court.

Legal Basis

The policy and statutory frameworks evaluated in this study rely on Spanish national and regional legislation:

  • Spanish Constitution (CE): Article 14 (equality), Article 33 (private property and social function), Article 47 (right to decent housing), Article 148.1.3º (exclusive regional jurisdiction over housing), and Article 149.1 (state powers 1st, 6th, 8th, 13th, 14th).
  • Primary Housing Statute: Law 12/2023, of May 24, on the Right to Housing.
  • Procedural & Civil Statutes: Civil Procedure Law 1/2000 (LEC Arts. 150.4, 250.1, 439.6/7, 441.5/7, 539.4/5, 655bis, 675, 685.2, 704) and Urban Leases Law 29/1994 (LAU Arts. 17.6 and 20).
  • Emergency Decrees & Regional Regulations: Royal Decree-Law 11/2020, Royal Decree-Law 7/2019, Law 18/2007, Law 24/2015, Law 4/2016 of Catalonia, and Decree-Law of April 24, 2024 of Catalonia.

Background

Enacted as part of Spain's *Recovery, Transformation and Resilience Plan*, Law 12/2023 took effect on May 25, 2023, to reinforce the social function of housing and protect vulnerable tenants. Rent controls were modeled after Catalonian initiatives establishing price ceilings across 140 declared stressed market municipalities (*zonas tensionadas*).

Objectives

1. Map procedural law amendments (*requisitos de procedibilidad*) governing eviction (*desahucio*) and foreclosure actions. 2. Detail rent capping mechanisms and index limits in stressed market areas via the Reference Price Index (*Índice de Referencia*). 3. Analyze distortions in traditional long-term rental markets and the resulting shift toward temporary/room leases. 4. Assess constitutional challenges regarding state preemption of regional housing powers.

Scope

The study covers six primary operational pillars: 1. Procedural Modifications: Implications of LEC revisions (Arts. 439.6/7 and 685.2), non-retroactivity rules, and the *Barcelona Judicial Orientation Criteria*. 2. Large Holder Qualification: Thresholds of >10 residential properties or >1,500 m², alongside land registry certification requirements. 3. Economic Vulnerability: Social services verification procedures, 2/5-month response deadlines, and sworn statements (*declaración responsable*). 4. Rent Price Controls: 3% cap in 2024, new INE reference index starting 2025, and prohibition of management fee transfers (Art. 20 LAU). 5. Market Impact: Long-term primary rental supply contraction (-15% to -66%), temporary rental growth (+56%), and Catalonian regional decree counter-measures. 6. Constitutional Challenges: Review of 8 appeals before the Constitutional Court regarding Article 148.1.3º CE jurisdictional preemption.

Key Provisions

  • Large Property Holder Criteria (Art. 3.k): Defined as owning >10 residential properties or >1,500 m² of residential floor area.
  • Rent Adjustment Cap (Final Provision 6th): Maximum 3% adjustment in 2024; new INE index below CPI starting 2025.
  • Reference Price Index (MIVAU): Sets maximum rent caps for new contracts signed by large holders in stressed market areas.
  • Agency Management Fees (Art. 20 LAU): All real estate agency and formalization fees must be paid exclusively by the landlord (*arrendador*).
  • Sworn Statement (Declaración Responsable): Large holders may submit a sworn statement if administrative bodies fail to respond to tenant vulnerability checks within statutory deadlines.

Implementation

Enforcement is carried out by Courts of First Instance (*Juzgados de Primera Instancia*), Senior Court Clerks (*LAJ*), the College of Land Registrars, Social Services, and the Ministry of Housing and Urban Agenda.

Policy Problems

  • Initial administrative confusion and delays in court filing processing due to ambiguous procedural rules.
  • Overlooked vulnerability protections for small-scale landlords (*non-Gran Tenedor*).
  • Shift of primary rental supply toward temporary and room rentals, reducing long-term housing availability.

Reform

  • Publication of the *Reference Price Index* by the Ministry of Housing on February 27, 2024.
  • Adoption of the *Decree-Law of April 24, 2024* in Catalonia regulating seasonal and room rentals.
  • Development of a new rent price index system by the INE before December 31, 2024.

Relationships with Other Entities

  • Implements: Law 12/2023 on the Right to Housing.
  • Amends: Civil Procedure Law (LEC) & Urban Leases Law (LAU).
  • Conflicts / Subject to Challenge via: Constitutional Court (8 Constitutional Appeals).
