'International Journal for Court Administration, Vol. 6, No. 1' (IACA, 2014)

Wiki Entity Page

International Journal for Court Administration (Vol. 6, No. 1, Source File: 16-81-PB.pdf) is the 12th official peer-reviewed electronic periodical publication of the International Association for Court Administration (IACA) published on the official IACA website (www.iaca.ws). This issue features 2 professional articles and 6 comparative academic research papers examining judicial accountability, the use of social media in courts, electronic evidence management at the International Criminal Court (ICC), public trust in Swiss conciliation authorities, psychological selection of probation officers (PEPQ/PSR Plus), expectations of "good justice" and Swiss court culture, ethnography of criminal sentencing in the English Crown Courts, the economic benefits of Dutch judicial reforms, and the impact of criminal justice federalism on crime rates in Switzerland.

Summary

This issue is led by Markus B. Zimmer (Executive Editor), Prof. Dr. Philip M. Langbroek (Managing Editor), Prof. Dr. Andreas Lienhard, and Luis Maria Palma (Journal Editors). The publication presents empirical and practical analyses on the modernization of judicial administration in the United States, Netherlands, Switzerland, United Kingdom, Australia, Norway, and international judicial institutions (ICC).

Background & Summary of Main Articles

1. Executive Editorial: Judicial Accountability (Markus B. Zimmer)

  • Asserts that institutional judicial independence can only be achieved if judicial leaders expressly demonstrate professional accountability and enforce codes of conduct with coercive sanctions.

2. Practical Guide to Social Media in Courts (Norman H. Meyer, Jr.)

  • Analyzes the adoption of social media (Twitter, Facebook, YouTube) in 25% of US federal courts and 50% of US state supreme courts. Identifies the risks of "Google mistrials" and develops social media management strategies [21, 43, 45, 70, 86–88].

3. Electronic Evidence Management at the ICC (Mark Dillon & David Beresford)

  • Examines the challenges of Big Data and Electronically Stored Information (ESI) at the ICC, the use of Ringtail software, Bates/ERN numbering, and e-court protocols.

4. Public Trust in Swiss Conciliation Authorities (Christof Schwenkel)

  • A survey of 3,484 citizens across 26 Swiss cantons proves that public trust in conciliation authorities (Friedensrichter, score 6.81/10) is higher than in cantonal governments/parliaments, and is influenced by positive personal experiences rather than the institutional model.

5. Psychological Selection of Probation Officers / PEPQ PSR Plus (Herrmann & Bedwell)

  • Tests the predictive validity of the PEPQ/PSR Plus tool on 262 probation officer candidates. Logistic regression results prove that the Alienation/Perceptual Distortion (AP), Interpersonal Relations (IR), and Intellectual Efficiency (IE) scales significantly predict the risk of performance failure/critical incidents.

6. Expectations of "Good Justice" & Swiss Court Culture (Emery & De Santis)

  • Analyzes Swiss court culture using Boltanski & Thévenot's 4 "reference worlds". Finds that judges and court managers agree on 12 out of 27 core expectations (celerity and client-orientation ranking highest) [282, 309, 314, 321–324].

7. Criminal Sentencing & Indirect Moralization in English Courts (Enescu & Scheffer)

  • Sequential discourse analysis of 7 phases of sentencing speeches in English Crown Courts, explaining how judges apply "indirect moralization" when defendants and victims are not given direct speaking space.

8. 15-Year Evaluation of the Economic Benefits of Netherlands Judicial Reforms (Frans van Dijk)

  • Measures the economic impact of reducing civil trial duration by 6 months (190 days). The study records realized societal gains of €795 million to €970 million per year (0.13%–0.16% of GDP).

9. Criminal Justice Federalism & Disparities of Foreigners in Switzerland (Koller & Fink)

  • Uses the BADAC database to show that resource limitations in cantonal police/judiciary result in extreme disparities in pre-trial detention and unsuspended prison sentences for foreign nationals (comprising 56% of total criminal convictions).

Policy Problems

1. Partisan political campaigns eroding judicial independence due to weak internal accountability enforcement [6, 7–10]. 2. Risk of trial annulments (Google mistrials) due to social media use by jurors. 3. Obstacles in managing massive digital evidence files (Big Data) in courts. 4. Economic losses due to delays in civil and administrative trials. 5. Disparities in the imposition of criminal sanctions and pre-trial detention across cantons against foreigners.

Reform / Change

1. Coercive and structured enforcement of judicial codes of ethics. 2. Adoption of court social media strategies equipped with IT controls and ethical guidelines [21, 86–88]. 3. Simplification of civil trial procedures and full digitalization of case files. 4. Standardization of psychological selection tools for probation officers based on scientific evidence.
