JPAIS:PRECEDENT:1459K Pdt 1986

By Cecep Mustafa, Indonesian Judicial College (IJC) · revision 1749, 2026-09-23 · wiki source · Markdown

[Party D] and [Party C] v. [Party A], Registration No. 1459 K/Pdt/1986, is a decision of the Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia (Mahkamah Agung RI) concerning the legal classification of property acquired by a spouse through a family gift ("hibah") during marriage, and the validity of that spouse's subsequent donation of the property without the consent of their spouse. The Court held that such property constitutes separate property ("harta bawaan") under Article 35(2) of Law No. 1 of 1974 on Marriage, and that the owning spouse retains full authority to deal with it, including by gift, without requiring the other spouse's approval.

Background

The dispute concerned a shop known as Toko Eng Bo Ho, located on Jalan A. Yani, Jayapura, standing on land originally registered as Recht van Opstal (Rvo) No. 15/1954, with an area of 903 m². The plaintiff, [Party A] (the second wife of Defendant I), brought suit in the Jayapura District Court against:

  • Defendant I: [Party D] (her husband)
  • Defendant II: [Party C] (Defendant I's son from his first marriage)

The plaintiff sought to annul Deed of Gift No. 594.4/01/1984, dated 10 February 1984, by which Defendant I had donated the disputed land and building to Defendant II. She argued that the property was joint marital property (harta bersama), having been formalized (via Building Use Right/HGB certificate No. 60) during her marriage to Defendant I, which began in 1972.

The defendants countered that the land had in fact been transferred to Defendant I by his elder brother, [Party E], in 1961 — when [Party E] was preparing to return to Hong Kong — long before Defendant I's marriage to the plaintiff. Defendant I had also been previously married to [Party B] (1958–1971). The plaintiff and Defendant I were married according to Islamic law on 27 May 1972 and divorced by talak on 28 August 1984.

Procedural history

  • The District Court of Jayapura (No. 37/Pdt/G/1985/PN.Jpr, 23 October 1985) partially granted the plaintiff's claim.
  • The High Court of Jayapura (No. 38/Pdt/1985/PT.Jpr, 28 November 1985) affirmed the District Court's decision, holding the gift invalid and the land to be joint marital property.
  • Defendants I and II filed an appeal in cassation (kasasi) to the Supreme Court.

Issues

The Supreme Court considered two principal questions:

  1. Whether land and buildings whose certificate of title (or land-right conversion) was formalized during the marriage automatically become joint marital property (harta bersama), where the underlying origin of the property was in fact a family gift predating the marriage.
  2. Whether the owner of such separate property (harta bawaan) may validly dispose of it — including by donation to a child from a prior marriage — without the consent of the other spouse.

A subsidiary procedural issue concerned whether a general civil court (Pengadilan Negeri) had jurisdiction to adjudicate a claim for spousal maintenance (nafkah) arising from a marriage and divorce conducted under Islamic law.

Ruling

The Supreme Court granted the petition for cassation filed by [Party D] and [Party C], and annulled the decision of the High Court of Jayapura. Adjudicating the case itself ("mengadili sendiri"), the Court:

  • In the exception: Declared that the District Court of Jayapura lacked jurisdiction to hear the plaintiff's claim for maintenance (nafkah), that claim falling within the absolute competence of the Religious Court (Pengadilan Agama); declared the defendants' remaining exceptions inadmissible.
  • On the provisional claim: Rejected the plaintiff's provisional claim.
  • On the merits: Rejected the plaintiff's claim in its entirety; declared the conservatory attachment (sita jaminan) executed by the District Court of Jayapura on 22 July 1985 (No. 37/BA.Pdt.G/1985.PN.Jpr) to be without legal force, and ordered its lifting.

Ratio decidendi

The Court held that the High Court of Jayapura had misapplied the law. Under Article 35(2) of Law No. 1 of 1974 on Marriage, property that either spouse acquires individually as a gift, present, or inheritance remains under that spouse's exclusive control, constituting separate property (harta bawaan), unless the parties agree otherwise. Because the disputed land originated from a gift by Defendant I's brother in 1961 — well before Defendant I's marriage to the plaintiff — it was properly classified as Defendant I's separate property, notwithstanding that formal certification or conversion of the land right took place during the marriage.

Consequently, under Article 36(2) of the same law, Defendant I possessed full legal authority to deal with that property, including donating it to Defendant II (his son from his first marriage), without requiring the plaintiff's consent. The Deed of Gift No. 594.4/01/1984 and the resulting Building Use Right (HGB) Certificate No. 545 in Defendant II's name were accordingly held valid.

The Court further held that the timing of formal registration or certificate conversion during a marriage does not, by itself, alter the underlying legal character of property whose true origin lies in a gift or inheritance predating that formalization; courts must examine the actual moment of original acquisition, not merely the date administrative documentation was issued.

Significance

The decision is cited as a leading authority in Indonesian marital property law for two linked propositions:

  • Property whose substantive origin is a gift or inheritance retains its character as separate property (harta bawaan) under Article 35(2) of Law No. 1 of 1974, even where formal land certification occurs during the marriage.
  • The owner of separate property has an unrestricted right under Article 36(2) to transact regarding that property — including by gift — without the consent of a spouse.

The case is also referenced for its holding on absolute competence (kompetensi absolut), confirming that maintenance claims arising from marriages solemnized under Islamic law fall exclusively within the jurisdiction of the Religious Courts, not the general civil courts.
