Overview

Supreme Court Decision No. 25 K/AG/1984 dated October 31, 1984, establishes two fundamental legal principles in Islamic Civil Law and Civil Servant Divorce Regulations:

  • Prohibition of Conditional Judgments: An appellate religious court's dispositive order commanding the religious court to open a session to witness the talak pronouncement only after obtaining authorization from the appellant's superior official (pursuant to Government Regulation No. 10 of 1983) and the opinion of the BP.4 and close family is legally impermissible because it renders the judicial decision conditional (*menggantung*).
  • Conditions for Talak Validity under Islamic Law: A talak pronouncement uttered by a husband in a state of anger and in the absence of two (2) just witnesses is legally invalid under Islamic law.

Case Background

The Respondent for Cassation / Original Petitioner ([Husband], a Perhutani employee, [age removed]) filed a petition for authorization to pronounce talak against his wife, the Petitioner for Cassation / Original Respondent (Mrs. [Wife], [age removed]), at the Blora Religious Court. The couple married in 1949/1959 and were blessed with 6 children. In 1974, the Petitioner contracted a secondary marriage (polygamy) with [Second wife] without proper procedural consent, triggering domestic discord that led the Petitioner to abandon the shared marital domicile for 8 years. The Petitioner had also previously uttered a unilateral talak while angry and without the presence of witnesses.

The Blora Religious Court (Determination No. 161/1982 dated October 13, 1982) rejected the Petitioner's divorce petition because the marital dispute arose from the Petitioner's own actions and all children and both wives desired reconciliation. However, the Surakarta High Religious Court (Decision No. 12/1983 dated June 14, 1983) annulled the Blora Religious Court determination and granted the divorce authorization with a conditional order linking the execution of talak pronouncement to civil servant superior authorization (PP No. 10 of 1983) and BP.4 opinions. Mrs. [Wife] subsequently filed a cassation petition to the Supreme Court.

Core Legal Principle (Ratio Decidendi)

  • Ratio Decidendi:
  1. The dispositive order of the High Religious Court commanding the Religious Court to witness the appellant's talak pronouncement upon securing superior authorization and BP.4 opinions is legally unjustified.
  2. A talak uttered by a husband while angry, unaccompanied and unwitnessed by two just witnesses, is deemed invalid under Islamic religious law.
  3. Because the domestic discord stemmed from the Petitioner's improper polygamy and his abandonment of the joint domicile, whereas the first wife, the 6 children, and even the second wife all desired to reconcile and preserve household harmony, the Petitioner's divorce petition lacks valid legal grounds and must be rejected.
  • Ruling: Granting the cassation petition from Petitioner for Cassation [Wife]; Annulling the Surakarta High Religious Court decision dated June 14, 1983, No. 12/1983; and Independently Adjudicating: Rejecting the divorce petition filed by Petitioner [Husband], and ordering the Respondent for Cassation / Petitioner to pay all procedural costs across all levels of jurisdiction.
