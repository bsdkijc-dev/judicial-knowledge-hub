International Journal for Court Administration (IACA Bogor Conference Issue) is an official publication of the International Association for Court Administration (IACA), containing an Executive Editorial from Markus Zimmer on the first IACA Asia-Pacific Regional Conference in Bogor, Indonesia, along with a collection of comparative court administration research articles from Europe, the UK, and Macedonia [1–3].

Summary

Co-sponsored by the Supreme Court of the Republic of Indonesia and the Federal Family Court of Australia, the Bogor conference was formally opened by Indonesian President Susilo Bambang Yudhoyono at Bogor Palace. The conference brought together over 200 participants from 19 countries and 40 jurisdictions across the Asia-Pacific region to discuss strategies for maximizing public access to justice. This issue reviews the unique features of Indonesia's judicial system, which operates 343 trial and appellate religious courts (employing both male and female judges), and the history of the Kertha Gosa Hall of Justice in Klungkung, Bali (established in 1700 A.D.). The issue also includes research on courts and media relations (Philip Langbroek), an empirical assessment of European judicial motivation (Zajc & Kovač), the establishment of the UK Supreme Court (Gavin Drewry), the use of IT in dispute resolution (Dory Reiling), and the efficiency of administrative judicial protection in Macedonia (Pelivanova & Dimeski) [1–3].

Background & Analysis of Indonesia's Judicial System

Indonesia is the world's largest archipelagic nation, comprising 17,508 islands (roughly 6,000 inhabited), a 55,000 km coastline, a land area of 2 million square kilometers, and a population of 245 million people speaking hundreds of local dialects. This geographic and cultural diversity presents major challenges for delivering justice. Following independence from Dutch colonial rule and Japanese occupation in 1945, Indonesia built a civil law system that includes 343 religious courts. Uniquely among Muslim-majority nations, Indonesia's religious courts employ both male and female judges.

History of Kertha Gosa (Klungkung, Bali)

Featured on the cover of this IJCA issue, Kertha Gosa is a Hall of Justice in Klungkung, Bali, built in 1700 A.D. (Candra Sengkala). During the feudal era, the King of Klungkung gathered regional rulers every fourth full moon of the Balinese Caka calendar to render judicial guidance. In 1908, the Dutch colonial government took control of Klungkung and transformed Kertha Gosa into a court blending European jurisprudence with Balinese customary law.

1. Courts and Media Relations: An editorial by Philip M. Langbroek on the dynamics of trial communication and media. 2. European Judicial Motivation: An empirical study by Katarina Zajc and Mitja Kovač on what motivates judges across Europe. 3.
