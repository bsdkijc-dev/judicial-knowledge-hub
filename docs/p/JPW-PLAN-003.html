JPW-PLAN-003

By Cecep Mustafa, Indonesian Judicial College (IJC) · revision 1110, 2026-09-16 · wiki source · Markdown

Registry Strategic Plan 2023-2025 is the official strategic policy document of the Registry of the International Criminal Court for the 2023–2025 period, setting out 2 strategic goals, 10 priority objectives, and 31 Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) to support judicial efficiency and organizational governance across the Court.

Summary

The plan is fully synchronized with the concurrent strategic plans of the Court as a whole, the Office of the Prosecutor (OTP), and the Trust Fund for Victims (TFV), aiming to create Court-wide performance synergy. It builds on the achievements of the 2019–2021 Registry Strategic Plan (extended through 2022 as a transition year) and integrates recommendations from the September 2020 Independent Expert Review (IER) Final Report and the December 2021 Independent Oversight Mechanism (IOM) evaluation.

Legal Basis

  • Rome Statute mandate and decisions of the Assembly of States Parties (ASP).
  • Financial Rules and Regulations (FRR).
  • Recommendations of the Independent Expert Review (IER) Final Report, September 2020.
  • Evaluation report of the Independent Oversight Mechanism (IOM), December 2021.

Background

A substantial increase in judicial workload and new investigations opened by the OTP required extensive operational, technical, administrative, and security support. The Registry also had to manage post-COVID-19 recovery, mitigate the effects of external sanctions such as U.S. Executive Order 13928 (June 2020), address gender imbalance at senior management levels, and confront geographical staff representation gaps, with 79 States Parties classified as non-represented or under-represented by the end of 2021.

Objectives

  • Strategic Goal 1: A more efficient Registry delivering more effective services across all areas, reflecting a culture of continuous improvement.
  • Strategic Goal 2: A more engaged Registry workforce, through the promotion of organizational culture and values, and improved geographical representation and gender balance (GRGB).

Key Provisions

The plan sets out 10 Priority Objectives:

  • 1.1: Effectiveness of management, judicial, and external operational services.
  • 1.2: Efficient management of resources and budget.
  • 1.3: Application of continuous-improvement principles.
  • 1.4: Strengthened State cooperation environment and public awareness.
  • 1.5: Effective support for victims and witnesses.
  • 1.6: Strengthened structure for defence teams and victims' legal representation.
  • 2.1: Improved geographical representation and gender balance (GRGB), diversity, and inclusion.
  • 2.2: Staff capacity development and application of the ICC Leadership Framework.
  • 2.3: Flexible workforce framework and talent acquisition.
  • 2.4: Strengthened staff engagement, wellbeing, and accountability mechanisms.

Implementation

Implementation follows a cascade model, with modernization of the Judicial Workflow Platform (JWP) and migration of data to Microsoft Cloud, automation via an ERP system (SAP S/4 HANA), recruitment of an Ombuds position, development of administrative guidance on discrimination and harassment, rollout of the Court-wide Values Project, and integration of the ICC Leadership Framework. Progress is monitored quarterly by the Registry Management Team (RMT) via a digital Registry Dashboard and Activity Tracker.

Evidence

Selected KPIs from Annex 1's 31-indicator framework:

  • Budget Execution Rate (KPI 4): Baseline 98.8% (2021), target 99% (2023).
  • Competitive Procurement Tenders (KPI 5): Baseline 64%, target 65%.
  • Permanent Relocation of Witnesses/Victims (KPI 14): Baseline 701 days, target reduced to 200 days.
  • Geographical Staff Representation (KPI 18): Baseline 61%, target 63%.
  • Gender Ratio in Senior Management Recruitment (KPI 19): Baseline 0%, target at least 50% women.
  • Staff Recruitment Duration (KPI 27): Baseline 209 days, target 160 days.
  • Staff Engagement (KPI 29): Baseline 68%, target toward an external benchmark of 77%.

Policy Issues

  • Long processing times for permanent relocation of protected witnesses/victims, requiring intensive coordination with States Parties.
  • A high number of States Parties remaining non-represented or under-represented among staff.
  • The need to mitigate liquidity crises and arrears in state financial contributions.

Reform / Future Directions

Full implementation of IER recommendations across 2023–2025; adoption of a tenure policy; creation of a National Professional Officer (NPO) category; conduct of a Secondary Trauma Survey; and implementation of a Sustainability Plan to reduce the Court building's carbon footprint through ATES systems, LED lighting, and a paperless-organization model.

Relationships with Other Entities

  • Registry (ICC): Principal implementing organ.
  • International Criminal Court: Parent institution.
  • Office of the Prosecutor: Organ whose strategic plan is synchronized with the Registry's.
  • Trust Fund for Victims: Body whose strategic plan is synchronized with the Registry's.
  • Assembly of States Parties: Oversight body and budget-setting authority.
  • Independent Oversight Mechanism (IOM): Body evaluating implementation of the plan.
  • Risk Management Committee (RMC): Body overseeing organizational risk alongside the plan's implementation.
  • Registrar: Chief executive official leading, overseeing, and accountable for implementation and monitoring of the Registry Strategic Plan.
  • Ombuds: Independent official recruited in 2022 to facilitate informal dispute resolution and handle internal staff grievances.
  • Focal Point for Gender Equality: Court-wide official position established to promote gender equality and an inclusive work culture within the Registry and the ICC.
